## Identity and versions

* **Genie** — D. Gottlieb & Co., October 1979; model **435**, **Gottlieb System 1** solid-state, four-player widebody. Design by **Ed Krynski**, art by **Gordon Morison**; production **6,800**. [S1][S4]
* Five flippers, three pop bumpers, a central 4-bank of yellow/star drops, and a **seven-target upper-left mini-playfield**. No multiball. [S1][S4]
* Genie is generally credited as **Gottlieb’s first widebody**. [S4]
* Original hardware uses game PROM **“L”** and matching L-SND sound ROM; no meaningful software-revision tree was found. Replacement boards should be checked only if the venue uses non-original rules. [S1][S5]
* The rules differ materially between **3-ball and 5-ball** setup in some switch values; tournament play should confirm ball count. [S1]
* Scope follows the supplied commentator-reference specification.

## Thirty-second game plan

1. **Bonus is the game.** Build base bonus toward its **29K maximum**, while watching the center yellow 4-bank for a **lit target**. [S2][S3]
2. Whenever a yellow drop is lit, **shoot it first**: each successful lit target advances Bonus X, ultimately to **5×**. Maximum end-of-ball bonus is therefore **145K**. [S1][S2]
3. Once bonus reaches **20K**, the upper-right kickout hole lights **Score Bonus**. Competitive strategy says delay collecting until **4× or 5×** when practical; collecting resets the base bonus but lets you cash it before draining. **Observed strategy.** [S3]
4. If no multiplier target is currently lit, go **up top** or through the spinner to toggle/rebuild the next opportunity rather than forcing an unlit drop. [S3]
5. Alternate route: on a strong-flipper copy, attack the upper-left red/white drop banks for direct points and Extra Ball/Special qualification—but this is normally secondary to maximizing bonus. [S1][S3]

## Core rules and persistence

* Base bonus reaches **29,000**; Bonus X reaches **5×**. A lit yellow/star drop advances the multiplier. Pop bumpers and upper rollover switches toggle which yellow targets are lit for multiplier advancement. [S1][S2][S3]
* Completing all four yellow drops scores an additional award and resets the bank, but **only a target lit for “Advance Multiplier” gives the Bonus-X advance**. [S1]
* At **20K base bonus**, the kickout hole lights to **Score Bonus**. Collecting awards the current bonus and resets the base value; secondary rules documentation indicates the multiplier remains for the rest of that ball. [S3][S6]
* Thus a 20K collect at 5× is already roughly **100K**, while a fully built 29K × 5× end-of-ball bonus is **145K**—huge relative to ordinary switch scoring. [S2]
* **All playfield features reset after each ball** on original software. This is explicitly stated in the Gottlieb manual; do not describe Genie as having persistent progress. [S1]
* Upper-left mini-playfield: complete the **four red** drops to light Special; complete the **three white** drops to light the right Extra Ball target. Either completed color resets the seven-bank and increases the white targets’ scoring. [S1]
* Completing both sets of **A-B-C-D rollovers** lights the left Extra Ball target. [S1][S2]

## Skill shots

**None found.**

The plunge enters the upper A-B-C-D lanes, but the Gottlieb manual defines no named skill-shot award. Competitive guidance notes that all four lanes have equivalent raw scoring and merely expresses a preference for plunging toward **A** because its rebound can sometimes reach the mini-playfield. That is an **observed plunge preference**, not a verified skill-shot rule. [S3]

## Secondary features

* **Ball save:** none.
* **Extra Ball:** two principal qualifications:

  * complete A-B-C-D → left Extra Ball target;
  * complete the white mini-playfield drops → right Extra Ball target. [S1]
* Original code permits **maximum one Extra Ball per ball in play**. [S1]
* **Special:** complete all four red mini-playfield drops, then hit the lit Special target. Maximum one Special per ball. [S1]
* Original operator setting can choose **Replay or Extra Ball game mode**, so tournament treatment should be verified rather than inferred. [S1]
* **Lit spinner:** either inlane/star rollover can light the spinner. While lit, spins add bonus; the lamp goes out on subsequent switch activity, making it a short-lived scoring opportunity rather than a permanent spinner build. [S3][S6]
* No player buttons beyond the flippers, no lane change, no video/mystery mode.

## What to watch

1. **Lit yellow/star drop:** this is the highest-priority visual cue—it means the next hit there advances Bonus X. [S1][S3]
2. **Bonus-X lamps:** 4× or 5× plus a large base bonus usually explains why the player suddenly turns toward the kickout hole.
3. **Score Bonus hole lit:** base bonus has reached at least 20K; the player now has an in-ball cash-out available. [S3]
4. **White/red mini-playfield bank completed:** white means the right Extra Ball target is lit; red means Special is lit. [S1]

## Match strategy

**Playing ahead:** prioritize **Bonus X over raw points**. Shoot a lit yellow drop whenever available, otherwise send the ball upstairs; once at 4×/5× with a substantial bonus, consider taking the Score Bonus hole rather than continuing into the dangerous mini-playfield. **Observed strategy.** [S3]

**Playing behind:** maximize to **5×**, drive base bonus toward 29K, collect it at the hole, then rebuild it. Because the cash-out resets base bonus but not the multiplier for that ball, a long ball can theoretically score multiple heavily multiplied bonus collects. [S3][S6]

**Key recurring decision:** **collect the bonus now or keep building it?** At 20K/5× the player can bank roughly 100K immediately; waiting toward 29K yields more, but risks losing the opportunity to collect it during the ball.

## Danger zones

1. **Center spinner return:** balls falling back through the spinner can go straight down the middle. [S3]
2. **Gap between the upper mini-playfield flippers:** another direct center-drain path. [S3]
3. **Upper-right saucer-area return:** a ball that fails to hug the nearby rubber can fall centrally. [S3]
4. **Left A-B-C-D / bumper exit:** the lane beneath the left bumper is described as effectively a **50/50 drain path** without timely nudging. [S3]
5. **Right-flipper-side rubber:** a gentle hit can hop over the right flipper and head down the middle rather than transferring safely left. [S3]

## Trivia

* Genie was **Gottlieb’s first widebody**, built on the company’s System 1 architecture. [S4][S5]
* Its cabinet contains **five flippers**, including two small flippers dedicated to the upper-left “game-within-a-game” mini-playfield. [S1][S4]
* Gottlieb advertised that mini-playfield specifically as a **“Game-Within-A-Game.”** [S2]
* The game’s sound board offers **two selectable sound sets**, unusual enough to feature in contemporary descriptions. [S4]
* A contemporary 1979 trade report quoted Gottlieb VP Tom Herrick saying Genie had been under development for “many, many months”; the same report documents its 29K bonus and 5× multiplier. [S2]

### Uncertainties and conflicts

* **Date:** authoritative machine databases use **October 1979**, while some secondary sources say November. Use “Gottlieb, 1979” on commentary. [S4][S5]
* **3-ball versus 5-ball scoring:** the original manual explicitly changes several values: completed red/white banks are **10K in 3-ball versus 5K in 5-ball**, and the lit kickout adds **10K in 3-ball versus 5K in 5-ball** before the bonus collect. [S1]
* The manual establishes that **all playfield features reset after each ball** [S1]. This is stronger evidence than generalized descriptions that may imply progression carries.
* The exact safety of the mini-playfield, left-bumper lane and saucer return is highly **copy-dependent**; Bob Matthews specifically notes that aging Genie flipper strength can materially alter tournament strategy. [S3]
