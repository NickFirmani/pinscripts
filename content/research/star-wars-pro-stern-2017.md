## Identity and versions

* **Star Wars — Pro**, Stern, **2017**, SPIKE 2. Design: **Steve Ritchie**; principal software/rules: **Dwight Sullivan**; engineering/mechanics: **Elliot Eismin**; sound: **Jerry Thompson**. [S1][S2] ([Stern Pinball][1])
* Pro/Premium/LE share the same core rules. The **Pro** has a static illuminated Death Star and Super Speed inner loop; Premium/LE instead add the exploding Death Star plus motorized Hyperdrive/ball accelerator. This matters most for **Hyperspace Multiball**, whose physical setup differs on Pro. [S1][S5] ([Stern Pinball][1])
* **Rules basis: `code` — v1.30.0, released 2025-12-22.** The current Stern-derived changelog lists 1.30; that release adds C-3PO-topper/character-select support and system changes, not a major competitive-rules rewrite. [S2] ([Pinside][2])
* Major scoring multiballs: the four **planet-final multiballs + Victory Multiball**, **TIE Fighter Multiball**, **Hyperspace Multiball**, and the deep **Jedi Multiball** wizard. [S3] ([TiltForums][3])

Research scope follows the supplied commentator-reference specification.

## Thirty-second game plan

1. **Observed tournament strategy:** select **Han**, often with a path that lights Tatooine, and quickly prepare Tatooine plus another planet. The current tournament card uses the short Tatooine progression as a fast route to a planet multiball. [S4] ([JLP's Pinball Cards][4])
2. Before the valuable shots arrive, manage the **shot multiplier**: shoot the center 3-bank to raise it, turn it off with the action button, move the green X arrows with the flippers, then reactivate them over the shots you expect to cash. [S3] ([TiltForums][3])
3. Start the planet-final multiball and use its ball-save time to keep building the multiplier. In normal play it can ultimately reach **20×**, or **40× with Double Multipliers**. [S3] ([TiltForums][3])
4. TIE Fighter and Hyperspace Multiballs are usually best treated as **stacking/ball-save support** during a planet multiball, rather than the main scoring objective. [S3] ([TiltForums][3])
5. **Alternate:** choose **R2-D2** for easier, longer-lasting multipliers and pursue Death Star/Endor scoring; this is a long-established high-level tournament route when survival is good. [S3][S6] ([TiltForums][3])

## Core rules and persistence

Four major shots correspond to the four planets. Normally, **three shots qualify a planet mode**; completing that planet’s available modes eventually qualifies its final multiball. Planet modes do not generally time out, though individual hurry-up stages can. In standard play, two missions from different planets can stack; **Competition Mode disables mission stacking**. [S3] ([TiltForums][3])

The defining scoring system is **shot X**. It begins at 2×; center 3-bank hits raise it, eventually narrowing the multiplied region from three shots to one. The action button toggles the multiplier arrows on/off; while off, flippers move them. Higher X expires faster. The fourth FORCE completion awards **Double Multipliers**. R2 makes this whole system easier. [S3] ([TiltForums][3])

**Competition Mode materially changes this:** Stern’s historic competition install sets multiplier difficulty to Hard, shortens multiplier duration, and disables mission stacking. Competitive documentation also records a **10× base ceiling / 20× doubled ceiling** under that setup rather than normal 20×/40×. [S2][S6] ([Pinside][2])

A planet final leads directly into **Victory Multiball**. Six major shots earn the medal; the multiplier on the final medal shot applies to that medal value. Medals later contribute to the potentially enormous qualification award before Jedi Multiball. [S3] ([TiltForums][3])

## Skill shots

| Skill shot            | How / award                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| --------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **3-Bank Skill Shot** | After launch, hit the flashing target in the center 3-bank. Awards **Bonus X**; repeat successes make the target move faster and raise the Bonus-X award. The middle target is worth the most. |
| **FORCE Skill Shot**  | Hit the roving lit FORCE drop target. It **spots the next FORCE completion award**. It remains available until the player hits the wrong drop target.                                          |

These are the two verified production skill shots; no separate super/secret skill shot was found. [S3] ([TiltForums][5])

## Secondary features

* **Escape:** second FORCE completion lights the **right-outlane save**. On Pro it is a virtual ball save; Premium/LE use a physical return gate. [S3] ([TiltForums][3])
* **Video Mode:** third FORCE completion lights the Millennium Falcon asteroid mode at the right inner loop. Critically, **that shot’s multiplier applies to Video Mode scoring**. [S3] ([TiltForums][3])
* **Action button:** toggles/moves the shot-X system and is mashed during TIE Fighter Assault. Every second press during its seven-second window guarantees another TIE Fighter. [S3] ([TiltForums][3])
* **Mystery:** first FORCE completion lights three simultaneous awards at the right scoop. A multiplier on Mystery multiplies its point awards; **Bonus Hold** can make end-of-ball bonus enormous. [S3] ([TiltForums][3])
* **Extra balls:** several qualifications exist. Factory code supports **10M when an EB is configured as points**; current Competition Install sets **No Extra Balls**. [S2] ([Pinside][2])

## What to watch

1. **Green X arrows:** these show exactly which shots are multiplied. Red/off means the player can reposition them with the flippers. [S3]
2. **Multiplier timer/X value:** a player abandoning mode progress for the center targets is often simply protecting a large multiplier before it expires. [S3]
3. **FORCE completion #4:** “Double Multipliers” just became active; this is one of the game’s largest scoring-leverage moments. [S3]
4. **Planet-final multiball + red/yellow progress:** the player may be deliberately ignoring its main objective to start TIE Fighter or Hyperspace MB underneath it and refresh ball-save time. [S3] ([TiltForums][5])

## Match strategy

**Playing ahead:** **strategic inference:** prefer the fast Han/Tatooine route, take an available planet multiball, and keep the multiplier alive rather than gambling on perfect repositioning. Use add-a-ball late, after draining toward one ball, instead of immediately.

**Playing behind:** build X aggressively during ball save, park the remaining multiplied shot on the largest objective, and pursue **Double Multipliers + planet/Victory MB**. Bonus Hold is also a real comeback mechanism on a developed game; tournament players have documented hundreds of millions or more carrying through bonus. [S6] ([TiltForums][6])

**Key recurring decision:** **progress the mode or spend time building/moving shot X?** Star Wars can make an ordinary jackpot at 10×–40× more valuable than blindly advancing a nominally “bigger” feature.

## Danger zones

* **Center 3-bank:** essential but dangerous; direct standup rebounds can punish multiplier greed.
* **Tatooine/right-scoop eject:** documented copies can kick toward center or the left outlane; highly adjustment-sensitive. [S7]
* **Left orbit:** some machines return a full-speed orbit essentially straight down the middle unless guides are adjusted. [S7]
* **Auto-plunge:** owners document rebounds from the upper area directly toward the left outlane; manual soft plunging may materially change risk. [S7]
* **Inner-loop rejection:** Stern’s own v1.10 notes specifically address balls bouncing out of the Hoth/inner loop if its center post is not flush. [S2] ([Pinside][7])

## Trivia

* Stern released the game for the franchise’s **40th anniversary** in 2017. [S1] ([Stern Pinball][8])
* It was designed by **Steve Ritchie**, whose layouts are famously fast; Stern calls the Pro’s inner loop the **Super Speed Loop**. [S1] ([Stern Pinball][1])
* The game uses footage from **Episodes IV, V and VI** plus original **John Williams** music. [S1] ([Stern Pinball][9])
* Pro’s Death Star is **static**, whereas Premium/LE physically explode it and feed balls into a separate Hyperdrive accelerator. [S1][S5] ([Stern Pinball][1])
* Stern returned to this same rules/layout package for the later **Comic Art** versions; their gameplay is substantially the same. ([Stern Pinball][10])
