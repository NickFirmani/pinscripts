## Identity and versions

* **Grand Prix** — Williams Electronics, 1976; model **468**, four-player electro-mechanical (EM) game. Design by **Steve Kordek**, art by **Christian Marche**. Current archival production figure: **10,554 units**. A sample run began in June 1976, with main production later that year. [S4] ([Pinball Serial Number Database][1])
* Hardware: two flippers, two high-powered DC pop bumpers, **two long spinner shots**, four drop targets, and three eject holes. **No multiball.** [S4]
* Five extremely rare **solid-state Grand Prix prototypes** were built in 1977 using Williams’ experimental System 1 electronics and digital displays. They are not the normal 1976 tournament game. [S5] ([Pinside][2])
* As an EM, there is no software revision. Ball count, Extra Ball/Special handling, physical post settings, and mechanical feed quality matter far more. [S1][S2]
* Scope follows the supplied commentator-reference specification. 

## Thirty-second game plan

1. **Shoot either spinner repeatedly.** Each spinner builds the bonus on its corresponding side; the side bonus reaches **50K**, then that spinner becomes worth **1K per spin** instead of 100. This is the dominant tournament scoring engine. **Observed strategy.** [S3] ([Silverball Rules][3])
2. Complete either drop-target pair—**A+B or C+D**—to advance the center Star unit. **Star 1 lights Double Bonus.** [S2]
3. With Double Bonus lit, a full 50K side bonus becomes a **100K collect**. Shoot that side's eject hole to cash it, reset that bonus, then build it again through the spinner. [S3]
4. Pay attention to the **red end-of-ball bonus arrow**. Only one side pays automatically when the ball drains, so the other side's saucer may be the more urgent collect. [S3][S6] ([Pinside][4])
5. If Double Bonus is not lit, simply continuing to rip a maxed 1K spinner can be as attractive as cashing the bonus. With Double Bonus lit, repeated **build → collect → rebuild** is the high-ceiling route. [S3]

## Core rules and persistence

* Grand Prix has **two independent side bonuses**, each maxing at **50K**. Every five spinner revolutions advances that spinner's bonus by 5K; the top eject hole and some other switches also advance bonus. [S3] ([Silverball Rules][5])
* Once one side reaches 50K, its spinner becomes **1K/spin**. A strong rip can therefore score 20–30K directly. Collecting that side bonus at its eject hole resets the bonus and spinner value, but the player can immediately rebuild it. [S3] ([Silverball Rules][5])
* Completing **A+B or C+D** advances the four-step Star feature. Star 1 = Double Bonus; Star 2 qualifies Extra Ball at the inner bottom rollovers; Special is qualified on Star 3 or 4 depending on adjustment. [S2] ([Pinball Nirvana][6])
* At end of ball, only the bonus corresponding to the **lit red arrow** is scored. Mechanical/owner evidence shows the stock alternator uses **left bonus on odd-numbered balls and right bonus on even-numbered balls**; the other bonus is reset rather than safely carried forward. [S6] ([Pinside][4])
* Easy commentary trap: **collecting bonus is not the end of the scoring engine.** It resets that side, allowing another spinner build and another collect during the same ball. [S3]

## Skill shots

**Top Saucer Skill Shot** — plunge directly into the top eject hole.

* Scores **5K** and advances bonus.
* The saucer kicks toward the right pop bumper; competitive guidance recommends nudging that exit toward the nearby **A/B drops**, potentially setting up Double Bonus almost immediately. [S3] ([Silverball Rules][5])
* The skill shot remains the same on later balls; no separate super or secret skill shot was verified.

## Secondary features

* **Ball save:** none found.
* **Extra Ball:** Star 2 lights one of the two inner bottom rollover lanes for Extra Ball. Tournament handling matters considerably because subsequent Star progress has little competitive value if Extra Balls and Specials are disabled. [S2][S3]
* **Special:** normally lights at an outer bottom rollover on Star 3 or 4; the threshold is operator-adjustable. [S1][S2] ([Wikipedia][7])
* **Player controls / video / mystery:** none.
* **3- or 5-ball play:** mechanically adjustable. Contemporary Williams advertising explicitly offered both; tournament ball count should be verified. [S1] ([World Radio History][8])

## What to watch

1. **Center Star 1 / Double Bonus lamp:** once lit, every side-bonus collect has doubled leverage.
2. **50K bonus ladder + lit/high-value spinner:** that side's spinner has reached **1K per spin**; expect repeated attacks rather than immediate cash-out. [S3]
3. **Red left/right end-of-ball arrow:** tells which bonus is already insured if the player drains—and therefore which opposite-side saucer may be worth collecting first. [S3][S6]
4. **Star 2+ / lit lower lane:** indicates Extra Ball or Special progression; relevant only if the event gives those awards value. [S2]

## Match strategy

**Playing ahead:** get Double Bonus, then favor clean spinner shots. If a side is already maxed, a 1K spinner produces excellent low-complexity scoring without requiring a dangerous saucer kickout. Dead-bounce transfers are preferred over marginal live catches because of the unusual gaps beside the return lanes. **Observed strategy.** [S3]

**Playing behind:** repeatedly max and **collect doubled 50K bonuses**, then rebuild them. If Extra Balls have value, deeper Star progression also becomes a legitimate comeback route. A few strong 1K spinner rips plus repeated 100K collects can change the game very quickly. [S2][S3]

**Key recurring decision:** **keep ripping the 1K spinner, or collect and rebuild the bonus?** Undoubled, the spinner itself produces roughly the same direct value needed to rebuild 50K; with Double Bonus lit, cashing and rebuilding creates substantially more scoring. Also favor collecting the side **opposite the end-of-ball arrow**, because the arrowed side is already paid if the saucer kickout drains. [S3][S6]

## Danger zones

1. **Short spinner shot:** can fall back onto a sling or directly toward center. [S3]
2. **Side-saucer kickouts:** the biggest copy-dependent danger; poor ejects can go straight down the middle or arrive at the very tip of the same-side flipper. [S3]
3. **Return-lane gaps:** an attempted live catch can roll too far up the flipper and disappear through the unusual gap beside the inlane. [S3][S6] ([Silverball Rules][5])
4. **Pop-bumper exits:** can exit toward either outlane, slings, or glance down the center. [S3]
5. **Fast scoring mechanism:** Grand Prix is documented as occasionally **skipping 100K increments** during very fast spinner or bonus-count activity; tournament directors should verify the reels keep up. [S3] ([Silverball Rules][5])

## Spoken commentary cues

* “That pair of drops gets Star One—**Double Bonus is lit**.”
* “That side is at fifty thousand, so its spinner is now worth **a thousand a spin**.”
* “They can take a hundred thousand at the saucer, reset it, and start building the same bonus again.”
* “Watch the red arrow—the other side is the bonus they really need to cash before a drain.”
* “That spinner came up short; this is exactly the dangerous sling-and-center return.”
* “Extra Balls are off, so once Double Bonus is established there’s very little reason to keep chasing those drops.”

## Trivia

* Grand Prix was designed by **Steve Kordek**, one of pinball's longest-serving designers; he later described the game as a particularly successful design. [S4] ([Wikipedia][7])
* Its backglass depicts the **Monaco harbor/race-course area**, fitting the Grand Prix theme. [S4] ([Pinside][9])
* Contemporary reviewer **Roger Sharpe** called 1976 the “year of the spinner” and praised Grand Prix's fast two-spinner gameplay. [S4] ([Wikipedia][7])
* Williams later converted **five Grand Prix machines into experimental solid-state prototypes**, an important step toward its first production electronic games. [S5] ([World Radio History][10])
* The closely related **Liberty Bell (1977)** reworks essentially the same basic playfield concept, but its rules and lower-lane geometry differ enough that strategy should not be transferred blindly. [S3]

### Uncertainties and conflicts

* **Production count:** current IPSND/Pinside/Kineticist records give **10,554** [S4], while several older virtual-recreation pages repeat **15,554** [S2]. The modern serial-number/archive figure is better supported; use **10,554**.
* **Manufacture date:** archival sources distinguish a **June 1976 sample run** from main production beginning in December. “Williams, 1976” is safest on air. [S4]
* Accessible primary/reproduction documentation confirms the Star sequence, but I did **not** find sufficiently clear primary evidence for whether every Star/Double-Bonus state is mechanically retained between balls. Treat it as ball-local for commentary unless the venue copy demonstrates otherwise.
* The side-saucer, spinner and return-lane risk varies dramatically with flipper strength, eject power and post settings. [S3][S6]
