## Thirty-second game plan

1. Early observed strategy: choose Maximus for extended Power Armor or The Ghoul for quest-oriented play. Lucy favors Vault progression. Build health with yellow shots, then start a quest at the right scoop once HP reaches 50. [S3][S4]
2. Qualify Deathclaw Multiball: hit the four flashing weak points on the Deathclaw. During the first multiball, spend 20 seconds building jackpot value, then repeatedly bash the Deathclaw during the 12-second collection window. [S3]
3. Maximize playfield multiplier: collect junk to upgrade weapons (+0.2× per upgrade), charge fusion cores at the pop bumper, and hold the action button to activate Power Armor (+1× playfield) for your biggest scoring opportunities. [S3]
4. Stack for points: early strategy documentation recommends combining a quest with Deathclaw Multiball, then activating Power Armor for multiplied scoring. This remains a tentative observed strategy, not a verified v0.81 tournament consensus. [S4]
5. Alternate route: spell VAULT, open the physical door, complete a Magna-Flip Vault experiment, and collect a lunchbox to reach Vault Multiball. Wasteland Multiball is another major route with progressively increasing Super Jackpots. [S3]

## Core rules and persistence

- Quests: require at least 50 HP, collected through yellow food shots. Start at the right scoop. HP also determines the mode timer: 1 HP = 1 second, capped at 100 seconds. Radiation reduces maximum health; Radaway restores it. [S3]
- Deathclaw: four differently timed weak-point hits qualify the first multiball. On Prem/LE, hitting an already-disabled limb triggers the Deathclaw's physical kickback. Later Deathclaw Multiballs require a weapon upgrade before qualification. [S3][S5]
- Deathclaw MB1: 20-second build phase, then 12-second collection phase. Build shots raise the 2M-base jackpot; consecutive Deathclaw hits multiply collections. Four jackpots qualify a Super Jackpot. The cycle repeats. [S3]
- Wasteland MB: defeat all three upper-gallery enemy types using the Fat Man. Jackpot starts at 3M + 500k per shot. Super starts at 15M × wave number, plus 2.5M for each collected jackpot. The Fat Man collects the Super. [S3]
- Vault progression: spell VAULT, hit the center door/ramp to open it, then enter the lower playfield. Completing a Vault experiment awards a lunchbox: odd-numbered lunchboxes grant Vault Multiball; even-numbered ones grant a Legendary Weapon. [S3]
- Multiplier and bonus: each weapon upgrade adds +20% to playfield scoring and +1 bonus multiplier. Power Armor adds a timed +100% playfield boost; these boosts are additive. End-of-ball bonus includes character level, caps and junk, multiplied by weapon level and remaining tilt warnings. [S3]
- Persistence: S.P.E.C.I.A.L. and other progression can carry across games through Insider Connected. Ordinary multiplayer tracks individual progress; v0.81 newly shares quest progress in co-op/team play. The precise Competition-mode handling of saved character progression needs confirmation. [S1][S3][S5]

## Skill shots

Cap Stash Skill Shot — confirmed: time the plunge so the ball rolls through the flashing right inlane, lowering the Nuka-Cola spinner briefly. Immediately rip the spinner with the right flipper. [S3]

- Award: 3M base + 75k per spinner spin, plus 3 seconds of ball save.
- Repeat progression: each successful Cap Stash increases the base by 1M and the per-spin award by 50k.
- Why choose it: instant points, caps and valuable protection at the start of a ball.
- Cue: flashing Nuka-Cola inlane insert, followed by the lowered bottle-cap spinner.

No separate normal, super, alternate or secret skill shot was verified for v0.81. [S3][S4]

## Secondary features

- Stimpak outlane save: complete designated white Wasteland-item shots to light an outlane rescue. With Perception level 2, the right flipper can move the lit Stimpak between outlanes. [S3]
- Power Armor: hold the action button to activate, consuming fusion-core charge. Charge through pop-bumper activity or Sundries purchases. Deactivate to conserve charge for later jackpots. Maximus gets two extra seconds per fusion-core level. [S3]
- Companions: yellow Wasteland shots qualify companions with passive and action-button abilities. Mr. Handy spots Deathclaw progress; Dogmeat collects lit junk; Victor can immediately activate Cap Stash. [S3]
- Fat Man / V.A.T.S.: complete the three mini-nuke targets, then load the Fat Man through its designated ramp/loop. Fire with the action button. Building Action Points (AP) from switches unlocks Vault-Tec Assisted Targeting System, allowing flipper-button target selection and automatic aim. [S3]
- Sundries: spend caps on fusion cores, healing, junk and useful equipment, or attempt theft. A failed attempt triggers a 10-second escape challenge with serious resource penalties if missed. [S3]
- Mystery / Hacking: the white-lit target beside the left ramp can award points, XP or other rewards, including failure. Competition mode changes the mystery sequence, and Legendary Weapons follow a fixed order. [S3][S5]
- Red Menace: v0.81 adds a standalone challenge mode and a video-mode version obtained through a Holotape. The video version ends after two levels, with scoring converted to pinball points and a remaining-lives bonus. [S5]
- Extra Balls: lit through Radroach progression, currently documented at four and then 25 qualifying targets. Collection is at the target beside the left ramp. Tournament conversion or suppression is configuration-sensitive. [S3]

## What to watch

1. Deathclaw limb inserts / FIRE: moving lights indicate the weak point currently vulnerable. Four disabled limbs means multiball is ready; an already-disabled limb can produce a violent kickback. [S3]
2. Fusion-core charge / Power Armor: a lit action button and visible fusion charge mean multiplied scoring is available. Watch for activation immediately before jackpots. [S3]
3. Vault door and Pip-Boy: completed VAULT letters light the door. Once open, the action moves to the lower magnetic playfield, with remaining time on the Pip-Boy. [S3]
4. Fat Man / shooting gallery: the mechanical launcher moving into position announces target combat; V.A.T.S. qualification allows the player to select targets. Clearing all gallery enemies starts Wasteland Multiball. [S1][S3]

## Match strategy

Playing ahead: Prioritize attainable Deathclaw jackpots and completed quests, preserve Stimpak protection, and spend Power Armor charge when meaningful scoring is already available. Avoid unnecessary shop theft and risky attempts to multiply every possible jackpot. Strategic inference. [S3][S4]

Playing behind: Stack a quest with Deathclaw Multiball where permitted, activate Power Armor, and pursue repeated Super Jackpots. Wasteland's escalating Super Jackpot or Vault Multiball under a multiplier offers alternative comeback potential. A successful Radaway Jackpot loop sequence can also compound points rapidly during its 10-second window. [S3][S4]

Key recurring decision: Build resources now or cash scoring now? Weapon upgrades permanently improve scoring and bonus; Power Armor provides a temporary boost. Strong players may choose either immediate multiball scoring or additional setup based on ball control, score differential and current resources. Strategic inference. [S3]

## Danger zones

- Left outlane pop bumper: Keith Elwin's Paragon-inspired design is unusually punishing; early hands-on play documented alley passes going directly into the outlane. [S6]
- Deathclaw kickback: an already-crippled limb can cause a forceful ball rejection on Prem/LE. [S3]
- Inverted upper flipper: its unusual rotation changes expected catches and returns; failed control can quickly surrender the ball. Strategic inference.
- Vault door bash/reject: the central entrance demands a committed shot and can return toward the flippers if missed. Strategic inference.
- Sundries escape hurry-up: a failed theft creates a time-critical shot; missing it sacrifices valuable resources even without draining. [S3]

## Trivia

- Stern released 770 Limited Editions, referencing Fallout's Great War of 2077. [S1]
- The fully functional playfield Pip-Boy was developed with The Wand Company. [S1]
- The inverted Mutated Flipper revives an unusual mechanical arrangement that Stern describes as absent from pinball for over 75 years. [S1]
- The Magna-Flip Vault is reminiscent of the magnetic Powerfield from Twilight Zone, but adds more targets and an orbit. [S6]
- Actor Johnny Pemberton, who portrays Thaddeus in the television series, recorded custom callouts. [S1]

### Uncertainties and conflicts

- Deathclaw MB1 build timer: current v0.81 community rulesheet gives 20 seconds [S3], while a v0.80 guide gives 15 seconds [S7]. Use 20 for v0.81, but verify installed code.
- Quest stacking: early competitive guidance proposes Deathclaw Multiball + quest + Power Armor [S4]. Precise restrictions under v0.81 are not conclusively documented.
- Radiation trigger: [S3] says previously killed Radroach targets inflict RAD upon another hit; [S7] describes radiation from killing Radroaches more generally. The exact trigger needs verification.
- Sundries escape shot: [S3] identifies the right orbit, whereas [S4] and [S7] describe a right-ramp escape shot. Confirm on current code.
- Insider Connected competition behavior: character progression is explicitly persistent in regular play [S1], but I could not verify precisely which saved advantages v0.81 Competition mode suppresses.
- Incomplete code: What Makes YOU Special, Observatory and Cold Fusion are listed as not implemented [S3]. Some Vault mode details and late Deathclaw behavior remain incompletely documented.
- Source revisions: [S4] dates to launch-era code and [S7] explicitly covers v0.80. Values changed in v0.81, notably Quest scores, Mind Control stages, Vault jackpots and Deathclaw requalification. [S5]
