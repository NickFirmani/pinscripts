## Identity and versions

* **The Mandalorian (Pro)** — Stern Pinball, **2021**, SPIKE 2. Lead design **Brian Eddy**; lead game development **Dwight Sullivan**; mechanics **Tom Kopera**; art **Randy Martinez**; sound **Jerry Thompson**; music includes Ludwig Göransson. [S1][S3]
* Pro-specific hardware: **static one-flipper Encounter mini-playfield**. Premium/LE use a motorized tilting two-flipper version. Pro also uses the **U-Turn** where Premium/LE have the Ambush scoop, and its relevant equipment is **Rising Phoenix** rather than Jet Pack. [S1][S3]
* Latest verified code: **1.45, July 1, 2026**. The maintained detailed rulesheet is based on **1.42**, so small post-1.42 changes remain possible. [S3][S5]
* Main multiballs: three **Razor Crest Multiballs** — Ice Spider, Jetpack, Pirates — plus later mini-wizard multiballs. [S2][S3]

## Thirty-second game plan

1. **Observed strategy:** light a **Mission** with the two ramps, start it at the left scoop, then stack it with a **Razor Crest Multiball** from the center ramp. This is the simplest reliable tournament scoring package. [S2][S3]
2. Before multiball, try to complete **Hunter and/or Scope** for playfield multipliers. Boba Fett can add another multiplier during multiball. All three together produce **6× playfield scoring**. [S3]
3. Use the starting **Flamethrower** strategically: each flaming major shot scores **2× once**, multiplicative with playfield X. With 6× running, the affected shot can score **12× base**. [S3]
4. For maximum ceiling, choose **Jetpack Multiball**, build its Super Jackpot toward 50M, return to single-ball play, then Flamethrower the U-Turn collect. The documented theoretical collect is **600M** at 6× PF × 2× Flamethrower. [S3]
5. Alternate route: repeatedly complete **Hunter**, earning Beskar for the Foundry and another 75-second multiplier; this sacrifices immediacy for equipment, multiplier, and progression flexibility. [S3]

## Core rules and persistence

* Missions are normally lit by making **both ramps**, then started at the left scoop. They last 60 seconds and can run during Razor Crest Multiball. Completed missions add **2.5M to end-of-ball bonus**. [S2][S3]
* Razor Crest qualification is the hanging **center target**, followed by a full center-ramp shot. There are three different multiballs; Boba Fett targets select which numbered one is next. Each later Razor Crest feature requires more target hits. [S3]
* After a multiball, completing **Hunter** relights Razor Crest for a Hurry-Up and later multiball. If the player drains with Razor Crest unavailable, it is automatically re-enabled next ball. [S3]
* Playfield X has three components: **Hunter +1× for 75s; Scope +1× for 75s; Boba Fett +1× initially 30s**. All three do **not** total 4× — completing the trio gives a special **6× total playfield multiplier**. [S3]
* Hunter and Scope generally must be earned **before multiball**; Boba Fett is the one multiplier that can start during multiball. This explains why expert players sometimes delay an already-ready Razor Crest. [S3]
* **Flamethrower:** one use starts every game. Holding the action button progressively ignites major shots from right to left; the next hit to each lit shot scores that shot's associated value at 2×. Lit flames disappear at drain. [S3]
* Encounter level progress **carries between attempts**. Mission failures cannot normally be replayed until This Is The Way. [S3]
* Beskar is retained throughout the game and can be banked for later Foundry visits. A topper adds separate cross-game Beskar behavior; ignore that for ordinary tournament strategy unless the topper features are enabled. [S3]

## Skill shots

**Regular Skill Shot:** plunge into the cycling lit **C-H-I-L-D top lane**.

* Scores 1M, increasing 1M on each successive skill shot.
* Rotating secondary award: **+2 Bonus X, Light Child, or +1 Super Pops**. [S3]

**Super Skill Shot:** hold the **left flipper** during plunge, then make **U-Turn → right ramp → chosen final shot**, roughly five seconds per step.

Final shot chooses the reward:

* **Left scoop:** starts lit Mission.
* **Left ramp:** starts lit Encounter.
* **Center ramp:** starts lit Razor Crest Multiball/Hurry-Up. [S3]

This is materially valuable because it can skip qualification/cash-out steps and immediately establish the player's intended feature.

## Secondary features

* **Equipment/action button:** Flamethrower = one-shot 2×; **Amban Rifle** = add-a-ball after opening multiball save expires; **Whistling Birds** = collect all currently flashing mission/jackpot shots; **Rising Phoenix (Pro)** = complete one Encounter wave; Armor extends ball saves. [S3]
* **Foundry:** earned Beskar buys equipment, Razor Crest qualification, Mission/Encounter starts, Scope, Heal, Extra Ball, or +1 playfield multiplier. Costs rise by 50% each repeated purchase. [S3]
* **Heal:** an outlane rescue that can be earned from Child/Foundry. Its lit state matters on a drain-heavy tournament setup. [S3]
* **Extra Ball:** lit at left scoop after starting two Missions, purchased at Foundry, or awarded from Child. **Competition Mode disables Extra Balls and disables Child Mystery.** [S3]
* **Child Mystery:** orbit-qualified random awards including equipment, Beskar, Super Spinner, multiplier and Heal; absent in Competition Mode. [S3]
* **Ambush:** on Pro, the U-Turn can trigger it. Competition settings remove its random start ambiguity: the second candidate shot starts the mode. Its cash-out can reach **720M with all multipliers active**. [S3]

## What to watch

1. **Three multiplier inserts above the right sling:** purple = active; blue/purple strobing = timer nearly dead. All three lit means **6×**, not 4×. [S3]
2. **Orange action button:** Flamethrower is available; expect the player to preserve it for a valuable mode/multiball shot.
3. **Center Razor Crest insert flashing:** multiball or Hurry-Up is ready; if the player ignores it, they may be preparing Hunter/Scope first.
4. **Mission flashing at left scoop + Razor Crest ready:** classic tournament stack is fully prepared.

## Match strategy

**Playing ahead:** use the low-complexity **Mission + Razor Crest** stack and cash available jackpots. A single Hunter or Scope multiplier is often enough; avoid spending a whole ball constructing the full 6× Fire Triangle.

**Playing behind:** build **Hunter + Scope**, start multiball, then complete Boba Fett for 6×. Add Flamethrower to create 12× shot opportunities. Jetpack's post-multiball Super Jackpot and a deeply extended Ambush are the clearest blow-up plays. [S3]

**Key recurring decision:** **start the ready multiball now, or delay it for multipliers?** Immediate multiball protects the ball and produces reliable score; Hunter/Scope setup exposes the player to single-ball risk but dramatically multiplies the payoff.

## Danger zones

1. **Razor Crest target/ramp rejects:** a weak center attempt returns toward the middle quickly. **Strategic inference from layout.**
2. **Hunter/Boba standups:** valuable multiplier targets but direct standup rebounds can create immediate loss of control. **Strategic inference.**
3. **Pro Encounter exit:** the static one-flipper upper area can return the ball quickly if the player fails to make progress.
4. **Scope/diverter feed:** a documented Pro issue on some copies can mistakenly feed a weak left-ramp shot onto the Encounter area while Scope is armed, leaving the lower playfield dark until the state clears. **Setup-dependent.** [S6]
5. **Fast orbits/top-lane return:** Pro owner reports consistently characterize the game as fast; imperfect post/feed setup can turn clean orbit shots into difficult recoveries. [S7]

## Trivia

* The Mandalorian was released **May 11, 2021** and uses material from the first two seasons of the Disney+ series. [S1][S4]
* **Carl Weathers** recorded exclusive Greef Karga speech for the pinball machine. [S1][S4]
* Brian Eddy's design uses a hanging target beneath a sculpted **Razor Crest** to qualify the game's three central multiballs. [S1]
* Premium/LE's Encounter area physically **tilts** and has two flippers; Pro intentionally simplifies this to a fast static **single-flipper** mini-playfield. [S1]
* Dwight Sullivan described the equipment economy as mirroring Mando's progression in the show: earn weapons and armor, then use them to improve later encounters. [S4]
