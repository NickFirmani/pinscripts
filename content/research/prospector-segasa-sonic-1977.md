## Identity and versions

* **Prospector** — Segasa, sold under its **Sonic** brand, March **1977**; model **995**. Four-player electro-mechanical (EM) game with two flippers, three pop bumpers and two central spinners. Production quantity is unknown. [S1][S5] ([Museum of the Game][1])
* No designer or artist credit could be verified from reliable archival sources; omit rather than guess.
* No multiball and no software revision. Tournament differences are primarily Extra Ball/Special handling, ball count and physical feed behavior.
* The central concept is two independent scoring resources: **Gold Bonus**, paid at end of ball, and **Silver Bonus**, collected during play and used to light 1K spinners. [S2][S3] ([Silverball Rules][2])
* Scope follows the supplied commentator-reference specification.

## Thirty-second game plan

1. **Observed strategy:** from the right flipper, repeatedly shoot the **left Gold lane/orbit** to build Gold Bonus toward **100K**. [S2] ([Silverball Rules][2])
2. From the left flipper, hit standups **5 and 6** to light **Double Gold Bonus**. A maxed Gold Bonus is therefore worth **200K at the drain**. [S2][S3] ([Silverball Rules][2])
3. Build **Silver Bonus to 10K**, mainly with the two spinners. Once maxed, one spinner is always lit for **1K per spin**, alternating between them. [S2][S3] ([Silverball Rules][2])
4. **Do not automatically collect maxed Silver.** Bob Matthews' tournament strategy is to preserve the 1K-spinner state and rip whichever spinner is lit. [S2]
5. Alternate/safety route: some players max Gold before risking the 5–6 standups; when protecting a lead, the repeatable left orbit can be preferable to dangerous target rebounds. [S2] ([Silverball Rules][2])

## Core rules and persistence

* **Gold Bonus:** advances in **10K steps to 100K**. The reliable scoring shot is the left orbit/Gold lane; lit gold-star rollovers elsewhere also advance it. [S2][S4] ([Kineticist][3])
* **5 + 6 = Double Gold Bonus**, making the maximum end-of-ball award **200K**. Targets 1–4 instead qualify Extra Ball; completing all six can qualify Special. [S2][S3] ([Lower Playfield][4])
* **Silver Bonus:** builds primarily from spinner activity to **10K**. It does **not** pay automatically at end of ball; it is collected at the center or upper-right saucer. [S2][S3] ([Silverball Rules][2])
* At maximum Silver, the game alternates a **1K-per-spin** lamp between the two spinners. Collecting Silver gives up that mature state and requires building it again, which is why the primary tournament guide recommends spinner farming instead. [S2] ([Silverball Rules][2])
* A successful left Gold orbit often continues into the **upper-right saucer**, adding 5K plus a Silver advance before kicking into the bumpers—a particularly efficient compound shot. [S2] ([Silverball Rules][2])
* **Persistence is insufficiently documented.** Reliable sources establish what Gold and Silver do within a ball but I did not find primary documentation clearly establishing which bonus/doubler states carry between balls. Verify the venue copy before stating persistence on air.

## Skill shots

**Gold-star skill shot** — plunge through a **lit top gold-star rollover**.

* Advances Gold Bonus, making it materially valuable immediately. [S2] ([Silverball Rules][2])
* The same lit gold-star switches can later be collected from bumper action.
* The useful visible cue is simply the **lit gold-star rollover** before the plunge.
* I found no separately documented super/alternate skill shot.

## Secondary features

* **Ball save:** none found.
* **Extra Ball:** targets **1-2-3-4** qualify Extra Ball; a documented modern tournament setup disables it entirely. [S3] ([Lower Playfield][4])
* **Special:** completing **1 through 6** can light Special. In tournament play it is commonly assigned no value. [S3]
* Extra Ball is mechanically awkward to disable on some copies rather than a simple software setting, so event configuration genuinely needs verification. [S7] ([Pinside][5])
* No video mode, mystery award, lane change, action button, Magna-Save or conventional timed save.

## What to watch

1. **Gold Bonus ladder:** approaching 100K means the player may switch from the left orbit to targets 5–6.
2. **5 and 6 completed / Double Bonus lit:** the player's Gold Bonus is now worth twice its displayed base at end of ball.
3. **Silver at 10K + a lit spinner:** the game has entered its second major scoring state; watch which spinner is currently worth 1K/spin.
4. **Extra Ball/Special lamps:** strategically important only if the tournament awards them value.

## Match strategy

**Playing ahead:** favor the repeatable **left Gold orbit**, get Gold to 100K, then decide whether exposing the ball to 5–6 is necessary. Once a spinner is lit for 1K, farming it avoids unnecessary Gold-star/standup rebounds. **Observed strategy.** [S2]

**Playing behind:** complete 5–6 for the full **200K Gold Bonus**, then attack the lit spinner aggressively. Silver can also be collected directly if an immediate cash-out rather than long-term spinner value is what the score requires. [S2][S4] ([Silverball Rules][2])

**Key recurring decision:** **collect maxed Silver or preserve the 1K-spinner state?** Bob's tournament guide strongly favors leaving Silver uncollected and ripping spinners; owner/player commentary notes that a difficult Silver collect can still be worthwhile when its immediate points matter. [S2][S4]

## Danger zones

1. **Left Gold-lane return:** the game's key machine-specific feed—safe at some speeds/copies, dangerous at others. [S2]
2. **#6 standup:** can rebound into the post immediately to its left and produce a fast drain threat. [S2]
3. **Pop-bumper exits:** Bob Matthews identifies bumper action as the principal source of trouble after otherwise successful shots. [S2] ([Silverball Rules][2])
4. **Playfield gold-star rollovers:** especially the left-side ones, produce sufficiently random rebounds that deliberately shooting them is discouraged. [S2]
5. **Silver saucer attempts:** owner reports describe the center collect as requiring a softer/lob-style shot; full-power attempts can bounce straight over it. **Copy-dependent.** [S4] ([Pinside][6])

## Trivia

* Prospector was built in **Spain by Segasa** and marketed under the company's **Sonic** name; archival records identify model **995** and a March 1977 release. [S1][S5] ([Museum of the Game][1])
* Historical pinball literature identifies the men on the backglass as **Stan Laurel and Oliver Hardy**, fitting their prospecting comedy *Way Out West*. [S6] ([Scribd][7])
* Sonic's late-1970s games were frequently described as having a distinctly **Williams-like feel**, even down to similar chimes. [S6]
* The game is unusual in having **two entirely different bonus economies**: Gold is an end-of-ball award while Silver is an in-play collect/spinner-enabler. [S2][S3]
* Despite its obscurity, Bob Matthews describes Prospector as a recurring **Classics tournament favorite**. [S2] ([Silverball Rules][2])

### Uncertainties and conflicts

* **Bonus persistence:** I did not find sufficiently authoritative documentation specifying the exact between-ball behavior of Gold Bonus, Double Bonus and Silver Bonus. This should be tested on the tournament machine.
* **Silver cash-out strategy:** [S2] recommends **not collecting** maxed Silver because the resulting 1K spinner is more valuable. [S4] contains experienced-owner commentary favoring occasional Silver collects. This is a genuine tactical choice rather than a rules conflict.
* **Extra Ball collection details:** reliable tournament documentation confirms that 1–4 qualifies Extra Ball [S3], but surviving secondary sources disagree in wording about precisely which lane/saucer finally collects it. If EBs are disabled—as is common—the distinction is irrelevant.
* **Production quantity:** IPSND explicitly lists the production run as **unknown**. [S5]
* Saucer catches, Gold-lane return, spinner feeds and bumper exits are highly **copy-dependent**.
