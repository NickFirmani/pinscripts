## Identity and versions

**Spanish Eyes** — Williams, commonly catalogued as **1972**, model **408**, single-player electromechanical game. Design: **Norm Clark**; artwork: **John Craig**. Production is consistently listed as **3,905**. The game has two flippers, four pop bumpers, six numbered standups, one center kick-out hole and the distinctive **pop bumper between/below the flippers**. [S1][S4]

Date records conflict: Pinside gives **March 1, 1972**, while IPDB-derived records and later recreations cite **November 1971**. “Williams 1972” is the safest commentary identification. [S4]

The factory manual confirms selectable **3- or 5-ball** play and **Replay / Add-A-Ball / Novelty** configurations. Those settings materially change tournament scoring. [S1]

No multiball.

## Thirty-second game plan

1. **Build numbers 1–6 up top.** Each lit number raises the center saucer from 500 to as much as **6,000**. [S3]
2. If the center horseshoe gives a survivable return, **shoot it repeatedly for 5,000**; otherwise keep going **up top all day (UTAD)** until the saucer is valuable. [S3]
3. Completing **1–6** lights the horseshoe for an Extra Ball and both bottom rollovers/outlanes for **5,000**. Taking the EB resets the numbers, sacrificing the 6K saucer value. [S2][S3]
4. Completing **A-B-C-D-E** directly earns another Extra Ball under normal/add-a-ball rules. [S1][S2]
5. The recurring choice is therefore **cash repeatable 5K/6K scoring versus reset progression for another ball**. If EBs are converted to points, that decision changes dramatically.

## Core rules and persistence

The six numbered standups are the primary scoring setup. The center hole scores **500 with no numbers lit, otherwise 1,000 for each lit number**, reaching **6,000** with all six. [S2][S3]

Completing **1-2-3-4-5-6** lights the center horseshoe/hoop for an Extra Ball and lights both bottom rollovers for **5,000**. Collecting the horseshoe EB resets the six-number sequence; the 5K bottom rollovers remain lit for that ball but reset on the next ball. [S2][S3]

Completing **A-B-C-D-E** awards an Extra Ball and resets that letter sequence. Competitive documentation treats the letter and number progress as persistent enough across balls to make plunge choices meaningful until each sequence is completed. [S3]

There is **no end-of-ball bonus**. All meaningful scoring is immediate, so a tilt does not erase a large accumulated bonus—but it does obviously end the ball. [S3]

Configuration is crucial: the Williams manual explicitly provides **Replay, Add-A-Ball and Novelty** settings. In **Novelty**, completion of either A–E or 1–6 advances the **10,000-point drum** instead of awarding another ball. [S1]

## Skill shots

**Competitive two-part Skill Shot:** [S3]

* First, plunge through the small **“ON BUMPERS” rollover** above the center C lane. This lights the side upper bumpers for **100 points** instead of 10.
* Second, choose an **A–E lane**. The recommended opening plunge is often **C**, because its feed can interact with the center upper bumper and potentially bounce back through another letter.
* On later balls, plunge for whichever A–E letter is still missing.

This is established tournament terminology/strategy rather than a factory-named scored “Skill Shot.”

## Secondary features

**Extra Balls:** two independent routes exist: complete **A–E**, or complete **1–6 then shoot the lit horseshoe**. [S1][S2]

**Novelty conversion:** factory Novelty configuration converts either completed sequence to **10,000 points** instead of another ball. This is large enough to materially alter tournament strategy. [S1]

**Bottom rollover payoff:** completion of 1–6 lights the two lower side rollovers/outlanes for **5,000** for the remainder of that ball. [S2][S3]

No ball save, kickback, mystery, action button or player-controlled rescue exists.

## What to watch

* **Number lamps 1–6:** immediately reveal center-saucer value; six lit means the saucer is at its 6K maximum.
* **Horseshoe lit for EB:** the player must decide whether another ball is worth resetting the profitable number state.
* **A–E lamps:** show progress toward the other Extra Ball/Novelty award.
* **Side bumpers lit:** means the plunge crossed **ON BUMPERS**, making upper-playfield action substantially more valuable. [S3]

## Match strategy

**Playing ahead:** favor the safer of **horseshoe versus saucer** based on the actual machine's return. If both are dangerous, stay upstairs and accumulate numbers rather than forcing center shots. With all six numbers lit, repeatedly taking the **6K saucer** can be preferable to collecting an EB that resets it. **Observed strategy.** [S3]

**Playing behind:** accept center-shot variance. The horseshoe's **5K** and maxed saucer's **6K** dwarf ordinary switches. If Extra Balls are enabled, cashing the 1–6 EB creates more time for a comeback; if Novelty is configured, both complete sets become immediate **10K** targets. [S1][S3]

**Key recurring decision:** **horseshoe or saucer—and is the Extra Ball worth resetting 1–6?** Good players will choose differently depending on which center feed the physical game handles safely.

## Danger zones

* **Bottom center pop bumper:** the defining hazard. Horseshoe and saucer returns frequently land directly on it; the resulting rebound may save the ball or accelerate it into the drain. [S3][S5]
* **Under-flipper drains:** there are open spaces beneath both flippers; a mistimed defensive flip can send the ball literally **under and out**. [S3]
* **Horseshoe return:** can bank repeatedly off the powerful bottom bumper and go straight down the middle. [S3]
* **Saucer kickout:** often heads toward the right flipper/bottom pop but is not reliably catchable. [S3]
* **Bottom-bumper side kick:** the bumper can propel a ball past a raised flipper and directly into an outlane, requiring “flipper defense.” [S3]

## Trivia

* Spanish Eyes is one of pinball's most famous **middle-pop** layouts: the fourth pop bumper sits directly between and below the flippers. [S5]
* The factory manual states that the game operates on **24 volts**, with rectified DC powering components including the bumpers/kickers; the unusually strong bumper action is central to its reputation for speed. [S1]
* **Norm Clark** designed the game; current archival consensus credits **John Craig**, correcting older publications that attributed the art to Christian Marche. [S4]
* The related **Granada** uses the Spanish Eyes visual family and add-a-ball concept but has a **different playfield layout**, so it should not be treated as merely a renamed Spanish Eyes. [S3][S5]
* Spanish manufacturer **Segasa** later produced its own Spanish Eyes version in 1973 using the same basic layout with different artwork. [S4][S5]

### Uncertainties and conflicts

* **Manufacture/release date:** sources disagree between **November 1971** [S4] and **March 1972** [S4]. The game is conventionally identified as *Williams 1972*, matching the requested machine.
* **Extra Ball configuration:** this is not merely a tournament policy. The factory machine itself can be set to Replay, Add-A-Ball or **Novelty**, with Novelty converting the A–E and 1–6 reset awards into **10K points**. [S1]
* **Persistence:** detailed competitive sources treat A–E/1–6 progression as carrying across balls until collected/reset [S3], but the factory manual text recovered online is primarily schematic/adjustment material rather than a clean printed rules description. Verify venue behavior if the lamps unexpectedly reset.
* **Center-feed danger:** Bob's tournament guide explicitly makes strategy conditional on the individual game's horseshoe, saucer and bottom-bumper feeds. [S3] Do not print one route as universally optimal.
