## Identity and versions

* **Dealer’s Choice** — Williams, 1974; model **433**, electro-mechanical (EM), four-player score-reel game. Design by **Norm Clark**, art by **Christian Marche**; production **8,850**. [S3][S8] ([Pinside][1])
* Four flippers: two at lower-left, one lower-right, and a distinctive **upper-right flipper** used to shoot cross-playfield toward the card targets. No multiball. [S3][S5] ([Pinside][2])
* **Lucky Ace** is the related one-player version; archival sources describe it as sharing Dealer’s Choice’s playfield/rules family. [S1][S4] ([Kineticist][3])
* Date sources disagree slightly: Williams-era references treat it as a **1974 game**, while archival databases variously list **December 1973** manufacture or **March 1974** release. [S3][S8]
* Scope follows the supplied commentator-reference specification. 

## Thirty-second game plan

1. **Build the base bonus to 10,000**, principally through the top lanes and three yellow standups. Bonus is the game's main scoring resource. [S1] ([Kineticist][3])
2. Complete the center **1–9 rollover-button matrix once for 2× bonus, twice for 3×**. A maxed 10K bonus at 3× means **30K waiting at the drain**, enormous on this scoring scale. [S1][S2] ([kineticist.com][3])
3. **Observed strategy:** use the upper-right flipper to shoot crosswise through the 1–9 buttons into **10-J-Q-K-A**. One shot can advance both the multiplier and card-bank objectives. [S1]
4. Completing the five cards lights the **right return lane for Extra Ball** if enabled; later card completions and repeated 1–9 sweeps can light Special. [S1][S2]
5. Once 3× bonus is secured, stop unnecessarily exposing the ball to the center matrix unless the remaining Special/Extra-Ball value justifies it. **Strategic inference.**

## Core rules and persistence

* Base bonus tops at **10K**. Either top lane and each of the three yellow standups advances it. [S1]
* First complete sweep of **1 through 9 = double bonus**; second = **triple bonus**. Further completed sweeps can light Special. [S1][S2]
* Complete **10-J-Q-K-A** once to light the right-bottom rollover for Extra Ball. Additional completions light the side Special rollover. [S2] ([VPForums.org][4])
* In replay configuration, Special awards a replay; rules transcriptions document **5,000 points instead in add-a-ball mode**. Tournament handling must therefore be checked. [S2]
* **Progress resets between balls:** technical documentation of the EM reset cycle identifies separate **1–9 and 10–Ace reset banks firing after the drain**. The end-of-ball bonus is counted down, and the following ball starts with the base bonus reset to 1K. [S7] ([Pinball Nirvana][5])
* Easy commentary trap: this is therefore a **ball-by-ball build**, not a multi-ball progression system. Missing one center number near the drain can erase almost an entire multiplier setup.

## Skill shots

**None found.**

The plunge can enter either top lane and advance bonus, so plunge strength matters, but I found no reliable source identifying a formal normal, alternate, super, or secret skill shot. I would not call the top-lane plunge a named skill shot on air.

## Secondary features

* **Ball save:** none.
* **Extra Ball:** complete 10-J-Q-K-A, then collect the lit **right return lane**. This can completely change tournament strategy if extra balls are played. [S1][S2]
* **Special:** available from repeated 1–9 completions or after subsequent card-bank completions, then collected at the appropriate lit lane. Replay-versus-points behavior is configuration-sensitive. [S1][S2]
* **Player controls:** no lane change, action button, Magna-Save, or other special player control.
* **Video/display modes:** none.

## What to watch

1. **Lit 1–9 center buttons:** shows exactly how close the player is to the next 2×/3× multiplier completion.
2. **Double / Triple Bonus state:** once triple is lit, the player's end-of-ball equity may be more important than another few switch points.
3. **10-J-Q-K-A lamps:** five completed cards mean the right-bottom Extra Ball lane is live if enabled.
4. **Lit side/right lane:** this is a cash-out shot—Extra Ball or Special—not merely another return lane.

## Match strategy

**Playing ahead:** prioritize completing a clean **2× or 3× bonus**, then reduce unnecessary center-button attacks. Crosswise card shots generally give a more controllable scoring route than repeatedly sending the ball directly through the middle. **Observed strategy.** [S1]

**Playing behind:** aggressively finish the missing 1–9 numbers for **3× bonus**, then pursue the card-bank Extra Ball/Special if the tournament configuration makes those awards meaningful. One 30K bonus can overturn a large percentage of a typical game score.

**Key recurring decision:** **chase the one missing rollover button, or protect the bonus already built?** The missing number may turn 10K into 20K/30K, but the center matrix feeds directly into the game's most chaotic area.

## Danger zones

1. **Center 1–9 → pops:** Bob’s Guide specifically warns that the resulting bumper action can exit down the center or either side. [S1]
2. **Gap between the two lower-left flippers:** historical design commentary warns that trying to trap on the lower-left flipper can lose the ball through this unusual gap. [S4] ([Scribd][6])
3. **Right outlane:** owner reports note that its adjustment dramatically changes ball times. [S5] ([Pinside][7])
4. **Card-target rebounds:** usually return toward a flipper, but can occasionally arc directly down the middle. [S1]
5. **Fast side-to-side play:** a well-tuned copy is unusually quick for a 1974 EM; the four-flipper layout leaves little recovery time after lateral misses. [S5]

## Trivia

* Dealer’s Choice was designed by **Norm Clark**, with art by **Christian Marche**. [S3]
* Its unusual four-flipper geometry includes **two adjacent lower-left flippers** plus the upper-right cross-playfield flipper. [S4]
* Greg Kmiec later said **Dealer’s Choice inspired the playfield layout of Bally’s Capt. Fantastic**; he credited Clark with teaching him complete game design. [S4] ([Scribd][6])
* Williams produced **8,850**, a large run for an EM of the period. [S3][S8]
* A Dealer’s Choice appears in the bar scenes of the original **Rocky** (1976), beginning around 22 minutes into the film. [S6] ([Pingeek][8])

### Uncertainties and conflicts

* **Manufacture/release date:** IPSND lists **December 1973** [S8], while Pinside and *The Pinball Compendium* use **March 1974** [S3][S4]. “Williams, 1974” is the safest commentator identification.
* I found **no formally documented skill shot**. Norm Clark later stated that he believed every design should have a skill shot [S4], but that is not sufficient evidence to invent a named Dealer’s Choice skill shot.
* Replay/Add-A-Ball/Special behavior is mechanically configurable and materially affects tournament strategy. The exact event configuration must be verified.
* Physical risk is especially copy-dependent on this EM; right-outlane width, bumper strength and flipper setup can change the safest strategy substantially. [S5]
