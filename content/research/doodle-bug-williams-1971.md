## Identity and versions

* **Doodle Bug** — Williams Electronics, March 18, 1971; model **390**, one-player electro-mechanical (EM) game. Requested scope is the original Williams machine. 
* Design: **Norm Clark**; art: **Christian Marche**. Williams produced **3,006** Doodle Bugs. [S1] ([IPDB][1])
* Hardware: two flippers, five pop bumpers, five standups, one top eject hole, a player-protecting **center up-post**, a right-outlane return gate, and the game's signature under-playfield **Doodle Bug captive-ball mechanism**. [S1]
* No multiball, jackpot or end-of-ball bonus system.
* **Related versions matter:** Doodle Bug is generally documented as the **replay** version; **Love Bug** is the one-player add-a-ball version, while **Dipsy Doodle** is the earlier four-player version. [S1] ([IPDB][1])
* Early-production Doodle Bugs had a higher reel/display range; common production games top their normal display at **199,990**, after which **OVER THE TOP** lights. [S1]

## Thirty-second game plan

1. **Go up top first.** Make the upper saucer and side lanes to advance the required 1-2-3-4 target, light valuable bumpers, and—most importantly—raise the **center post**. **Observed strategy.** [S2][S3] ([Silverball Rules][2])
2. With the post up, use tiny controlled **micro-flips** to hit the lit **1 → 2 → 3 → 4 standups in order**. Each complete sequence multiplies the Doodle Bug value by ten: **10 → 100 → 1,000 → 10,000 per stroke**. [S2][S4] ([Silverball Rules][2])
3. Shoot the **center standup** to start the under-playfield Doodler. It repeatedly scores its current value while the live ball remains in play without hitting a stop switch. [S2]
4. Once the Doodler reaches **1,000 or 10,000**, forget most other objectives: keep it running and send the live ball into relatively safe upper-playfield action. A few seconds can swing the entire game. **Observed strategy.** [S2]
5. Alternate when the Doodle value is still low: take safe **1,000-point Stop Doodle Bug switches** and 5K saucers rather than exposing the ball solely to build the feature. [S2][S5]

## Core rules and persistence

* The four numbered standups must be collected **in sequence**. The currently needed number is lit; the top eject can advance the sequence for you. [S2][S4] ([Silverball Rules][2])
* Each completed 1-2-3-4 sequence raises Doodle Bug scoring by **10×**, maxing at **10,000 per captive-ball switch hit**. [S2][S4]
* Shooting the center target starts the Doodle Bug mechanism and **immediately lowers the center post**. [S2]
* The Doodler runs independently while the player's normal ball continues. Certain lower/side rollover switches and the upper horizontal slings stop it; going through the right-outlane switch also stops it even if the return gate saves the live ball. [S2]
* **Doodle value resets on the drain.** A large 1K/10K value is therefore a ball-specific scoring opportunity, not a resource carried through the game. [S4] ([JLP's Pinball Cards][3])
* Upper side lanes raise the center post. When the appropriate bumper color has been lit, those lanes also open the **right outlane return gate**. Lighting both yellow and green bumper sets lights the center bumper for **1,000**. [S2][S3]
* Easy commentator trap: the center post is **not permanently available once earned**. Starting the Doodler and several lower switches drop it again. [S2][S3]
* There is no conventional bonus multiplier or drain bonus; the game's leverage is almost entirely the escalating Doodle value.

## Skill shots

* **Top-saucer Skill Shot:** plunge for the upper eject hole while its rotating award shows **5,000**, the strongest immediate award. Upper switches cycle the lit award, so the player may try to time one or two changes before the ball enters. [S2][S4] ([Silverball Rules][2])
* Alternative useful saucer awards **light the yellow or green bumpers**, which helps toward the center-post/right-gate protection. The 500-point selections are comparatively poor. [S2]
* The saucer also **spots/advances the current 1-2-3-4 number**, so even a non-5K plunge can materially shorten the Doodle setup. [S2]

## Secondary features

* **Center up-post:** raised by the upper side lanes. This is the game's key survival device and enables the micro-flip strategy between the two flippers. [S2][S3]
* **Right-outlane gate:** lit upper-side-lane progress can open a ball-return gate at the right outlane. It can save the live ball, but tripping that outlane switch stops an active Doodler. [S2]
* **Extra-ball / replay configuration:** this is historically messy. IPDB identifies Doodle Bug as the replay version and Love Bug as the add-a-ball version [S1], but contemporary Williams trade coverage describes Doodle Bug as configurable for replay/novelty play and advertises a lit center-target extra ball. [S6] Tournament setup must therefore be checked rather than inferred from the title. ([IPDB][1])
* No video mode, mystery, player button, Magna-Save or modern timed ball saver.

## What to watch

* **Center post visibly raised:** the player now has a much safer chance to micro-flip into the numbered targets.
* **Lit 1/2/3/4 target:** this tells you exactly which standup the player needs next; hitting the others does not advance the sequence. [S2]
* **Captive ball visibly hammering under the lower playfield:** the Doodler is active. If its value is already 1K or 10K, ordinary-looking survival is generating enormous score continuously.
* **Yellow/green bumper lamps:** both colors lit means the center pop is worth 1,000 and both side-lane routes can provide valuable protection/progression. [S2][S3]

## Match strategy

**Playing ahead:** Keep the post up, prioritize top play and 5K saucers, and avoid forcing naked standups when protection is gone. At low Doodle values, steady 1K switches can be more sensible than risking the ball to chase another full sequence. **Strategic inference.**

**Playing behind:** Build the Doodler aggressively to **1,000 or 10,000**, start it, then maximize safe upper-playfield dwell time while avoiding stop switches. At those values the scoring rate dwarfs ordinary switches and can erase a large deficit almost instantly. **Observed strategy.** [S2]

**Key recurring decision:** **Build one more 10× step, or turn the Doodler on now?** Higher value raises the ceiling exponentially, but every additional numbered-target attempt risks draining and resetting the value completely.

## Danger zones

* **Flipper-to-sling gaps:** the game was deliberately built so a raised flipper cannot hold a normal cradle; the ball can roll *behind* the flipper and drain. [S1][S2]
* **Micro-flip too hard:** the ball can climb the opposite flipper and fall through that same gap. [S2]
* **#1 and #4 target misses:** nearby rubbers operate **DOWN POST** switches, removing center-drain protection. [S2][S3]
* **Upper-playfield exit with post down:** a descending ball frequently heads straight through the middle or into a sling/outlane. [S2]
* **Weak old pop bumpers:** on a tired machine they may no longer recycle the ball into the saucer, making the intended up-top strategy much weaker. [S2]

## Trivia

* The moving **Doodle Bug** is a real captive pinball beneath the playfield, repeatedly knocked across a scoring rollover rather than a purely decorative animation. [S1]
* Designer **Norm Clark** also created the four-player predecessor **Dipsy Doodle**; Doodle Bug reused the concept in a one-player format. [S1]
* Artwork is by **Christian Marche**, whose exaggerated psychedelic figures are especially characteristic of Williams games from this period. [S1]
* Williams produced **3,006** Doodle Bugs. [S1]
* Williams revisited a mechanically similar captive-ball scoring idea ten years later in **Solar Fire (1981)**. [S1] ([IPDB][1])

### Uncertainties and conflicts

* **Extra-ball configuration:** IPDB explicitly calls **Love Bug the add-a-ball version** and Doodle Bug the replay version [S1], while contemporary April 1971 Williams trade coverage says Doodle Bug could be adjusted for replay or novelty and states that a lit center target awarded an extra ball [S6]. The machine's actual instruction card/wiring setup should control tournament commentary. ([IPDB][1])
* **Early vs. regular production:** display/reel arrangement differs substantially. Early examples could display much higher scores; common production uses the illuminated “1” and OVER THE TOP treatment. [S1]
* **Physical strength matters unusually much:** competitive sources explicitly note that weak 55-year-old pop bumpers can remove the intended saucer-recycling strategy. [S2]
* **Dipsy Doodle is not rules-identical:** its Doodle-value range is documented as only 10/100, so its instruction card should not be blindly applied to Doodle Bug. [S2]
