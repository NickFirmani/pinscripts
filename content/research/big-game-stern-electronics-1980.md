## Identity and versions

* **Big Game** — Stern Electronics, March 1980; model 121, Stern **M-200 MPU**, four-player solid-state widebody. Scope is the original Stern Electronics game requested here. 
* Design: **Harry Williams**; artwork: **Doug Watson and Gerry Simkus**; software credited to **Alan McNeil**. Production: **2,713**. [S3][S4] ([Kineticist][1])
* Four flippers, three 3-bank drop-target banks, two spinners, three pop bumpers and one upper eject pocket; **no multiball**. [S3][S4]
* No numbered factory software revision was verified. Rules below use the **original Stern factory rules**. A materially different unofficial **Big Game 2022/custom ROM** exists and has appeared in tournament setups; it changes card behavior and scoring, including adding 5× bonus. [S5][S6] ([Lower Playfield][2])

## Thirty-second game plan

1. **Work the numbered drops while the right bingo card is selected.** Each 1–9 target spots that number only on the currently active X/Y/Z card(s). Completing lines/cards builds the game's powerful **2×/3× end-of-ball bonus**. **Confirmed rule.** [S1]
2. **When X is lit, rip the far-left spinner.** It becomes **2,000/spin** and is widely regarded as the safest and strongest repeatable tournament shot. It also feeds the upper eject and returns the ball toward the top lanes. **Observed strategy.** [S1][S2] ([Pinball Rebel][3])
3. **Build toward full cards:** one full card—or one line on every card—lights 2×; two full cards—or two lines on every card—lights **3×**. Filling all three establishes a 27K **Reserve Bonus** and allows another 27K to be built: **54K base × 3 = 162K**, before any additional line bonus. [S1] ([manualzz.com][4])
4. Spell **BIG GAME** while doing this. The upper-left eject scores for collected letters and a completion award, adding useful value to the already-safe spinner route. [S1][S2]
5. **Alternate:** when the spinner is not valuable, attack drop banks. Once a bank's numbers have been completed across all three cards, later bank completions score **25K**, making repeated drops a legitimate scoring route. [S1]

## Core rules and persistence

* The center has three bingo-style cards, **X, Y and Z**, each containing numbers 1–9. Hitting a numbered drop target spots that number only on the selected card(s). Card selection changes from the top bumper, slingshots, center rollover and top B-I-G lanes. [S1] ([manualzz.com][4])
* An operator adjustment determines whether **one card or potentially two cards** can be active simultaneously. That materially changes how efficiently every drop-target hit advances bonus. [S1]
* **2× Bonus:** one completed card OR one completed line on each of X/Y/Z. **3× Bonus:** two completed cards OR two completed lines on each card. [S1]
* Each spotted card number contributes **1K bonus**. Filling all 27 positions stores **27K as Reserve Bonus** and permits another 27K to be built, for 54K before multiplier. [S1]
* **Important persistence:** Stern describes the bonus system as **memory**. The bonus is scored at the drain but its built state is restored for subsequent balls rather than starting over. Big Game is therefore a game-long build, not a ball-by-ball reset. [S1]
* Completing numbered banks eventually converts them into repeatable scoring: after a bank's three numbers have been spotted on all three cards, subsequent completions of that bank award **25K**. [S1]
* **BIG GAME letters:** B-I-G come from the top lanes; M/E from the return lanes; the G/A standups supply the remaining letters. The upper eject pays **5K per lit letter** plus a completion award. [S1][S2]
* Easy commentator mistake: the lit card matters as much as the numbered target. A player hitting “7” may be trying to finish a line on **X**, not simply completing the 7-8-9 bank.

## Skill shots

`None found.`

The plunge enters the B-I-G top lanes and can therefore supply a letter and change card selection, but the factory rules do not document a separate skill-shot award or plunge objective. [S1]

## Secondary features

* **No ball save** or modern rescue feature exists.
* **Extra Ball:** with factory extra balls enabled, completing BIG GAME can light/award an Extra Ball through the left-center side lane. A separate Extra Ball can light at the **right outlane** through advanced bingo-card patterns. [S1] ([manualzz.com][4])
* Extra balls can be globally bypassed by MPU adjustment. For tournament commentary, whether this switch is on is important: a live extra ball is far more valuable than its nominal scoring opportunity. [S1]
* The **Special** award is operator-selectable as no award, Extra Ball, **100K**, or replay. If Specials remain reachable in tournament play, its setting can therefore matter. [S1]
* An adjustable **5K-per-completed-line** bonus feature can begin on a selected ball. The unofficial custom ROM can behave differently here. [S1][S5]
* No mystery, video mode, lane-change buttons, Magna-Save, action button, kickback or multiball was found.

## What to watch

* **X lamp lit:** the far-left spinner is now worth 2K/spin. A player trapping on the right is probably preparing to shoot it. [S1][S2]
* **2× / 3× Bonus insert:** immediately tells you the value of the player's accumulated bingo progress; 3× plus Reserve Bonus can make the next drain score surprisingly large. [S1]
* **Reserve Bonus lit:** all three cards have been filled once; 27K has effectively been banked and the player is building a second set. [S1]
* **BIG GAME letters / upper eject:** a nearly complete word explains continued top-lane or letter shooting and raises the value of the far-left spinner/eject route. [S1][S2]

## Match strategy

**Playing ahead:** Favor the **far-left spinner whenever X is lit**, especially from a controlled right-flipper trap. Let incidental drops and upper-playfield action build the persistent bonus rather than aggressively forcing dangerous banks. **Observed strategy.** [S2]

**Playing behind:** Attack the drop banks to reach **3× and Reserve Bonus** faster. A mature 54K bonus at 3× is 162K before extra-line additions, and repeat bank completions can become 25K each. If Extra Balls are enabled, completing BIG GAME becomes much more attractive. **Strategic inference** from confirmed rules. [S1]

**Key recurring decision:** **Take the safe lit spinner now, or deliberately shoot missing drop numbers to accelerate the persistent bonus?** The spinner scores immediately; cards create larger delayed leverage and improve future balls.

## Danger zones

* **Side drains:** Big Game produces lots of oblique side-to-side motion; balls can bypass both sling and outlane post very quickly. [S2]
* **Upper-right chute return:** Silverball specifically warns that the wrong exit speed can head directly toward the center drain. [S2] ([Silverball Rules][5])
* **Center-left upper feed:** another speed-sensitive return that can suddenly turn safe upper play into an SDTM threat. [S2]
* **Drop-target rebounds:** all three banks throw the ball sideways into a very wide lower playfield, frequently toward the outlanes. **Observed physical behavior.** [S2]
* **Upper/lower flipper gap:** stock metal barriers help prevent a “scissors” drain between paired flippers; if those guides have been removed, that normally-safe geometry becomes dangerous. [S2]

* “He's not just knocking drops down; he's trying to put that number onto the currently selected bingo card.”
* “There's 3× bonus — now all that accumulated card progress is worth triple at the drain.”
* “Reserve Bonus is up, so he's already filled all three cards once and is building the second 27,000.”
* “With the spinner dark, this is where the drop banks become the better scoring route.”
* “That bonus carries forward — the drain scores it, but it doesn't erase all the work he's done.”

## Trivia

* **Harry Williams**, co-founder and namesake of Williams Electronics, designed Big Game while working with Stern Electronics. [S3][S4]
* It was **Stern Electronics' first widebody**, although the playfield itself was only about 24 inches across—narrower than many contemporary widebodies. [S4] ([Pinside][6])
* Big Game is credited as the **first electronic pinball machine with seven-digit player score displays**. Stern even demonstrated this in attract mode by displaying `1234567` and then `7777777`. [S4] ([Virtual Pinball Universe][7])
* Production was **2,713 machines**. [S3][S4]
* Its central X/Y/Z scoring system borrows the visual logic of **bingo cards**, but integrates those grids directly with conventional drop-target and bonus play. [S1][S2]

### Uncertainties and conflicts

* **Upper eject completion award:** the surviving factory-manual text [S1] says an additional **25K** for completed BIG GAME, while current competitive rules [S2] and the Big Game 2022 documentation [S6] state **35K**. This may reflect an original manual correction, ROM difference, or documentation error; the final page should avoid printing a value until the venue ROM is known. ([manualzz.com][4])
* **Custom ROM:** [S5][S6] document major non-factory changes including automatic card rotation, altered line-bonus behavior and **5× Bonus**. Those rules should not be used for a stock machine. ([Lower Playfield][2])
* **Physical feeds** are highly setup-sensitive. In particular, outlane-post position is an explicit factory adjustment. [S1][S2]
