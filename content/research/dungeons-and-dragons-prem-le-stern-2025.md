## Identity and versions

**Dungeons & Dragons: The Tyrant’s Eye — Premium / Limited Edition**, Stern Pinball, 2025. Lead design: **Brian Eddy**; code/rules: **Dwight Sullivan, Elizabeth Gieske, Corey Stup, David Sullivan**; lead mechanical engineering: Thomas Malcolm; artwork: Vince Proce; music: Cris Velasco. Stern SPIKE 2, three flippers. [S2][S4]

Premium and LE have the same gameplay playfield: both use the full multi-axis **Rath the Relentless**, an eight-ball Dragon Multiball system, and the rising **Dungeon** ball-capture mechanism. LE differences are cosmetic/presentation; 740 LEs were produced. Pro is materially different. [S2][S4]

- **Rules basis: `code` — v1.10.0, released 2026-09-22.** Stern’s public game-code ledger uses September 22; the embedded README heading says September 21. [S1][S6]
- Main scoring multiballs: **Dragon Multiball → Town Celebration**, **Gelatinous Cube Multiball**, and mini-wizard **Tavern Brawl**. [S2]
- v1.10 adds official-topper support, **Mimic Mania**, and **Rowan Freewander / Fighter**; Fighter’s action-button ability is one Add-A-Ball per ball during multiball. [S1][S7]

## Thirty-second game plan

1. **Current score-first route: prioritize Gelatinous Cube Multiball and Dungeon Crawl.** Gel Cube is the more lucrative normal multiball; Dungeon Crawl can produce a large low-commitment foundation score. **Observed strategy.** [S4]
2. Still start **Dragon Multiball**, because Dragon + Gel Cube + one Dungeon Crawl qualify **Tavern Brawl**, the main repeatable tournament mini-wizard target. [S2][S4]
3. Stack a normal town quest before starting a multiball whenever practical; multiball protection lets you finish otherwise dangerous battle shots. [S4]
4. Build **20-second 2x scoring** at the Hidden Temple and carry it into a high-value multiball or mode. [S2]
5. Use **Critical Hits, ranged attacks, and the Shield** instead of forcing dangerous lit shots. This game rewards utility management more than blindly shooting arrows. [S2][S4]

## Core rules and persistence

A new game chooses a character and starting city, then begins in **Travel**. Three blue shots—or a drain—reach town; yellow “choose action” shots then start quests or travel. Modes are campaign progression: once a quest has been played, success or failure normally removes it from that campaign. [S2]

**Competition Mode** is materially different: no saved campaign progress, constant random seed, legendary equipment disabled, unlocked campaign characters unavailable, and Dungeon Crawl can only be started once per ball. Since v1.01, Dungeon Crawl’s normal 20-second ball save is also disabled in Competition/Prolonged Competition. [S2][S6]

**Dragon Multiball:** spell DRAGON at Rath’s three-bank, then start at the right orbit or eligible side ramp. Premium/LE uses **eight balls** and an unlimited ball save until either Rath is defeated or the allowed number of drains is exhausted. Jackpots progress 1x→2x→3x; collecting the required 1x shots lights the upper-loop Super. Defeating Rath flows directly into Town Celebration Multiball. [S2]

Dragon difficulty is now persistent campaign progression through **levels 1–10**. Higher levels require more dragon damage and eventually make Premium/LE Rath physically block the Super Jackpot route. v1.10 also makes a **Critical Hit collect a Dragon Multiball jackpot** in addition to its other benefits. [S1][S6]

**Gelatinous Cube Multiball:** backhand the left orbit to lower its ramp, then shoot the ramp. Qualification is 1–4 hits by adjustment; normal Medium chooses a weekly-fixed value, while Competition guidance uses a deterministic requirement. The locked hurry-up becomes the jackpot base; jackpots rise by 3M each to 25M, then the upper loop collects a 20M Super. Completing a map mode requalifies Gel Cube after it has been played. [S2][S4]

**Tavern Brawl:** start Dragon Multiball, Gel Cube Multiball and Dungeon Crawl, then shoot the center spinner. Premium/LE can light the Super Jackpot via the rising Dungeon; completing DRAGON additionally lights a one-time Add-A-Ball at the shortcut loop. [S2]

## Skill shots

- **Upper Loop Skill Shot:** short plunge, then upper loop. Base **2M × current ball number**.
- **Gelatinous Cube switch Skill Shot:** short plunge directly toward the cube-area switch or use the shortcut loop. Same scaling.
- **Super Skill Shot:** left eject → side ramp → right ramp; starts at **6M**.
- **MXV Skill Shot:** hidden Temple lane behind the upper flipper; starts at **6M**. [S2]

Extra balls increase the skill-shot multiplier as though another ball number had been reached. No strategic progression reward beyond the documented points was verified. [S2]

## Secondary features

- **Shield:** action button raises the metal bar between the lower flippers. Besides saving straight-down-the-middle drains, good players deliberately “shield stage” balls to make the left orbit/left ramp or right ramp easier. Normally up to two uses can be stored. [S2][S4]
- **Ranged attack:** during battles, hold the action button; a blue target roves across shots. Release to damage the currently selected shot, avoiding a dangerous direct attempt. A normal lane/ramp reloads it. [S2]
- **Critical Hit:** hit the captive ball twice within its window. It spots battle/mode progress, advances major multiball qualification, and on 1.10 also collects a Dragon-MB jackpot. [S1][S2]
- **2x scoring:** Secret Door/Hidden Temple qualification starts **20 seconds of 2x playfield**; repeat Temple hits add five seconds. [S2]
- **Extra Ball:** multiple routes light it at the right ramp, including two map modes, 50 gems, Fizmo and repeated Dragon MB. No universal disabled-EB conversion was verified. [S2]
- **Topper:** outside Competition, treasure chests can build 13 letters toward the topper-exclusive **Mimic Mania**; those letters persist globally between games. v1.10 explicitly says letters are **not awarded during Competition play**. [S1][S7]

## What to watch

1. **Shield action button flashing:** the player has both a safety resource and a shot-staging tool available.
2. **Left orbit ramp lowered:** Gelatinous Cube progression is active; expect the player to backhand the orbit/ramp sequence. [S4]
3. **Rath lowered / DRAGON letters flashing:** Dragon MB damage opportunity; the display also reflects remaining dragon life.
4. **Center spinner lit for Tavern Brawl:** Dragon MB, Gel Cube and Dungeon Crawl have all been started—the next spinner can launch the major tournament mini-wizard.

## Match strategy

**Playing ahead:**  
**Observed strategy:** favor Wizard/Helenir for the longer Shield, backhand the left orbit, use Critical Hits/ranged attacks rather than forcing bad battle shots, and take Gel Cube scoring without overcommitting to deep campaign progression. [S4]

**Playing behind:**  
Build **2x + Gel Cube**, or finish the Dragon/Gel Cube/Dungeon trio and enter **Tavern Brawl**. Its jackpot multiplier loop plus Add-A-Ball gives substantially more variance and comeback ceiling. [S2][S4]

**Key recurring decision:**  
**Advance the campaign, or stay focused on Tavern Brawl qualification.** Town Dungeons can score and provide long-term rewards, but they lock out other features while active; score-focused tournament strategy generally avoids them until the Dragon + Gel Cube + Dungeon Crawl path is secured. [S2][S4]

## Danger zones

- **Left orbit / left ramp:** strategically crucial but physically difficult; the orbit can be backhanded, while the ramp requires the right flipper. [S4]
- **Right ramp:** another explicitly difficult major shot; Shield staging can reduce the risk. [S4]
- **Rath three-bank:** center standups are necessary progression but surrender control on rebounds.
- **Dungeon Crawl in Competition:** **no ball save**, despite the normal rules granting 20 seconds—an easy commentary mistake on current code. [S6]
- **Shield timing:** the bar has a short activation delay; pressing the action button after the ball is already committed SDTM can be too late. [S4]

## Trivia

- The game celebrates **50 years of Dungeons & Dragons**, and the LE run of **740** references the original game's 1974 debut. [S3]
- Premium/LE Rath is a multi-axis animatronic dragon with omnidirectional hit detection and can **shoot pinballs from its mouth**. [S3]
- The Premium/LE Dungeon entrance literally rises from and disappears into the playfield. [S3]
- PinSave lets logged-in players preserve character XP, items and campaign position between machines/games; Competition deliberately disables this advantage. [S2][S3]
- v1.10’s official Treasure Chest topper is unusually rules-significant: it adds an exclusive wizard mode and a new playable Fighter class, not merely animations. [S1][S7]
