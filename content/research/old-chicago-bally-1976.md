## Identity and versions

* **Old Chicago** — Bally Manufacturing, April 1976; model **1042**, four-player electro-mechanical (EM) game with mechanical reels, two flippers, five pop bumpers, one spinner, two kickout holes and a five-bank of drop targets. Production: **7,155**.  [S2][S3] ([Pinside][1])
* Design: **Greg Kmiec**; production artwork credited to **Dave Christensen**, with **John Youssi** contributing the original concept art. [S3][S5] ([ppmuseum][2])
* No multiball, software revisions, jackpot system or modern playfield multiplier.
* **3-ball versus 5-ball matters materially.** On 3-ball, a successful lit top rollover advances bonus **three steps**, rather than one. Double Bonus can also be adjusted to apply on balls **1 & 3 or ball 3 only** in a 3-ball game; 5-ball options are 1/3/5 or 3/5. [S1][S2] ([GameFAQs][3])

## Thirty-second game plan

1. **Build bonus with the left spinner.** Every completed five-light spinner sequence advances the OLD CHICAGO bonus one step; on a good spinner shot this can happen repeatedly. [S1][S4] ([Scribd][4])
2. **Collect the built bonus repeatedly at the center Collect Bonus hole.** Unlike draining, this collect **does not reset the bonus**, so once bonus is large the player can loop the saucer for repeated cash-outs. [S1][S4]
3. In **3-ball**, prioritize the lit top lane on the plunge: it scores 3K and advances bonus **three steps**, a huge jump toward the 10K maximum. [S1]
4. Once bonus reaches **10K**, the upper-right feature saucer itself becomes **5K** rather than 500, making saucer control even more valuable. [S1]
5. Alternate route: attack the drop bank for 5K completions / Extra Ball-Special qualification, but competitive guidance generally treats it as secondary to spinner → bonus → saucer. **Observed strategy.** [S4] ([Silverball Rules][5])

## Core rules and persistence

* Bonus starts at **1K** and climbs to **10K**, one lit letter of OLD CHICAGO per 1K. It advances from the lower-left target, completed five-light spinner sequences, the upper-right feature saucer, and the lit top rollover. [S1] ([Scribd][4])
* **Center Collect Bonus hole:** awards the currently lit bonus **without stepping it down**. This is the fundamental exploit/strategy—build once, collect repeatedly. [S1][S2] ([GameFAQs][3])
* **Drain:** awards the bonus and then resets it. If Double Bonus is lit for that ball, the **outhole only** awards 2× bonus; the center Collect Bonus hole does **not** double. Tilt forfeits the drain bonus. [S1] ([Scribd][4])
* **Spinner:** each spin itself is adjustment-sensitive—**10 or 100 points**—but each five-light sequence advances bonus, so raw spinner value is much less important than bonus progress. [S1]
* **Upper-right feature saucer:** advances bonus by 1K; scores **500 until bonus reaches 10K, then 5K**. [S1]
* **Drop bank:** each target = 500; completing all five = **5K + reset**. First completion that ball lights the Special/Extra-Ball system; later completions can score Specials. [S1][S2] ([Scribd][4])
* Easy commentator trap: **Double Bonus is not something the player builds.** It is automatically active on predetermined balls according to an operator jumper. [S1][S2]

## Skill shots

* **Lit top rollover:** plunge into the currently lit upper lane. It scores **3K and advances bonus**; in a 3-ball game it advances bonus **three steps**, making this one of the most consequential plunges on the machine. [S1][S4]
* The lit lane shifts when the ball hits the upper rubber switches, so players may lag-plunge or nudge to preserve/steer the desired lane. **Observed strategy.** [S4] ([Silverball Rules][5])
* No super/secret alternate skill shot found.

## Secondary features

* **Extra Ball:** first drop-bank completion enables the right-outlane Extra Ball opportunity. Depending on adjustment, the right Extra Ball and left Special lamps either alternate or remain available together. Maximum is **one Extra Ball per ball in play**. [S1][S2] ([GameFAQs][3])
* **Special:** first bank completion also enables the left-outlane Special; subsequent bank completions can themselves score Special. [S1][S2]
* Bally advertised Old Chicago as **convertible to add-a-ball**, and the high-score/credit circuitry is adjustable, so tournament handling of these awards should be verified. [S2] ([GameFAQs][3])
* No ball saver, kickback, video mode, mystery award or player-controlled utility.

## What to watch

* **OLD CHICAGO letters/bonus lamps:** the more letters lit, the more every center-saucer collect is worth; at 10K the upper-right saucer also jumps to 5K.
* **Spinner lights climbing:** every fifth spinner-light step advances bonus, so a long rip can increase the value of all subsequent saucer collects.
* **Double Bonus lamp:** means the eventual **drain** bonus is doubled—not center-saucer collects.
* **Drop bank completed once:** Extra Ball/Special outlane opportunities are now active; this can radically alter tournament risk if Extra Balls are live.

## Match strategy

**Playing ahead:** Build the bonus enough to make the center saucer worthwhile, then **repeat the saucer rather than attacking drops**. Dead bounces and patience are particularly useful because many saucer/pop feeds naturally fall toward a flipper. **Observed strategy.** [S4] ([Silverball Rules][5])

**Playing behind:** Aggressively rip the spinner to reach 10K, then loop center-saucer collects. On a Double Bonus ball, preserve the ball long enough to leave the full 10K for the eventual **20K drain collect** as well. **Strategic inference** from confirmed rules.

**Key recurring decision:** **Keep building bonus or start collecting it repeatedly?** Competitive guidance puts the crossover around roughly **6K**: below that, spinner/building tends to dominate; above it, repeat saucer collects become attractive. [S4] ([Silverball Rules][5])

## Danger zones

* **Lower pop bumpers:** competitive references identify this area as the main drain generator; weak bumpers can make it substantially worse. [S4]
* **Spinner return:** unlike upper-right feeds, it may bypass the lower pops entirely and come directly toward the center drain. [S4]
* **Upper-right descent:** can head center, though on many copies it falls into the lower bumpers first. [S4]
* **Drop-bank rebounds:** secondary strategy requires repeated direct target hits near uncontrolled lower-pop action.
* **Saucer kickouts:** strategy depends heavily on whether the center/upper saucer ejects can be dead-bounced, live-caught or trapped cleanly. [S4]

## Trivia

* Old Chicago was designed by **Greg Kmiec**, who also designed Bally classics including *Wizard!*, *Captain Fantastic* and *Night Rider*. [S3] ([Pinside][6])
* Production totaled **7,155 machines**. [S3] ([Pinside][1])
* The backglass gangster is intended to represent **John Dillinger**; the woman in red references **Anna Sage**, associated with his 1934 ambush outside Chicago's Biograph Theater. [S5] ([ppmuseum][2])
* Old Chicago was **John Youssi's first pinball art project**: he produced the concept art, which Dave Christensen substantially reworked for production. [S5]
* The game has **no conventional slingshots**; instead, two unusually low pop bumpers occupy the space just above the flippers/inlanes. [S3] ([Pinside][7])

### Uncertainties and conflicts

* **Double Bonus defaults:** Bally documentation establishes adjustable 3-ball choices of **1 & 3 or 3 only**, and 5-ball choices of **1/3/5 or 3/5**. Secondary recreations often simply print “1st, 3rd and 5th,” so the actual jumper must be checked. [S1][S2]
* **Spinner raw value:** factory adjustment is 10 or 100 points/spin. This barely changes the dominant strategy because the spinner's more important job is advancing the five-light sequence toward bonus. [S1]
* **Extra Ball/Special lamps:** conservative adjustment alternates them; liberal leaves both lit. That materially changes whether completing the drop bank creates a useful survival opportunity. [S1]
* **Physical feeds:** competitive strategy assumes a reasonably controllable center-saucer kickout and functional lower pops; weak coils can change the preferred spinner/saucer loop. [S4]
