## Identity and versions

**High Speed** — Williams, 1986. Designed and conceived by **Steve Ritchie**; software by **Larry DeMar**; art by **Python Anghelo and Mark Sprenger**; music by Ritchie and Bill Parod. Williams **System 11**, three flippers, alphanumeric display, physical hideout locks and a backbox police beacon. [S2][S4]

* **Rules basis: `rom` — L-4**, the final recognized Williams production ROM. L-3 and L-1 are earlier revisions. [S4]
* An unofficial **L-4C Competition MOD** appeared in 2018 and changes rules/scoring while running on unchanged hardware. Do not assume an L-4C machine behaves like factory L-4. [S4]
* One main feature: **3-ball Multiball** with a single **Hideout Jackpot** available per multiball. [S2][S3]
* Production is commonly documented at **17,080 units**. [S5]

## Thirty-second game plan

1. **Shoot the side ramp.** It spots stoplight progress, advances Bonus X, scores Ramp Bonus, starts Police Chase, starts Multiball, and later collects the Jackpot. On a well-playing game, it is essentially the whole competitive strategy. **Observed strategy.** [S2]
2. Complete **green → yellow → red** stoplights, then make the ramp to **Run the Red Light**. Make the ramp again during Police Chase to **Getaway** and start multiball. [S2][S3]
3. During Multiball, make the ramp quickly for the **Hideout Jackpot**. It starts at 250K, can reach **2M**, and every point scored before collecting it adds to the progressive. [S3]
4. If the ramp is unreliable, switch to **Freeways**: timed orbit combos climb toward 100K, light useful features, and can generate Hold Bonus. **Observed strategy.** [S2][S3]
5. Central decision: **force the high-value ramp or play safer orbit/freeway control** based on the particular machine’s upper-flipper strength and ramp reject behavior. [S2]

## Core rules and persistence

Stoplight progression runs **green → yellow → red**, normally requiring all three targets of each color. A successful ramp or upper-right eject-hole shot can spot a missing target on factory/default-style settings. Once red is complete, a ramp shot starts **Police Chase**. [S1][S3]

During Police Chase, another ramp shot scores **Getaway**: the ball locks in a hideout, another ball is served, and completing the sequence begins 3-ball Multiball. Alternatively, completing all nine stoplights during Police Chase lights **Escape** at the upper-right eject hole, which starts multiball through a different physical locking sequence. [S3]

The **Hideout Jackpot is global/progressive rather than player-specific**. It starts at **250K**, carries across games, increases by all points scored during Multiball before the jackpot is collected, caps at **2M**, and resets to 250K when collected. That makes the displayed jackpot value important match context before anyone plunges. [S3]

Only **one Jackpot** is available per multiball. After collecting it, continuing to complete the nine stoplights can instead light an outlane Special/extra feature depending on setup. [S2][S3]

Side-ramp shots also advance **Bonus X to 5x**; another ramp after reaching 5x lights Hold Bonus. Three Freeways in one ball can also qualify Hold Bonus. The detailed rulesheet says Hold Bonus carries both base bonus and multiplier into the next ball. [S2][S3]

## Skill shots

**None found.**

High Speed has no conventional plunge skill shot with a dedicated scoring award. A full plunge can travel up the side ramp and may spot a stoplight depending on adjustment, but it does not score the normal ramp award or Bonus X. Players can also attempt to plunge into a flashing stoplight target, but that merely advances progression. [S2][S3]

## Secondary features

* **Kickback:** the left outlane has a one-use kickback. A lit 1–6 standup relights it; after one Freeway in the current ball, lit inlanes also relight it. The factory setting lights it at the start of each ball, but this is adjustable. [S1][S2]
* **Freeways:** an inlane or orbit sequence lights a timed opposite-orbit Freeway. Awards progress through 50K, 75K, 100K/Hold Bonus and Extra Ball on a normal 3-ball setup; 5-ball play inserts an initial 25K stage. [S3]
* **Extra Ball:** Freeway progression lights Extra Ball at the upper-right eject hole. During Multiball, completing all nine stoplights after the Jackpot can also qualify an additional award. Tournament treatment is configuration-dependent; no universal factory point conversion was verified. [S2][S3]
* **No normal ball save** or player action button applies.
* **No video mode or mystery award** was found.

## What to watch

1. **Traffic light reaches red:** the next side ramp starts Police Chase; another successful ramp starts the main Multiball. [S3]
2. **Police beacon / chase audio:** the player is one meaningful ramp shot from Getaway and multiball. [S2]
3. **Jackpot value before Multiball:** because it is a machine-wide carried progressive, a near-2M jackpot changes the match immediately. [S3]
4. **Freeway arrow flashing:** the player has only a short window to repeat the corresponding orbit and advance the Freeway ladder. [S2][S3]

## Match strategy

**Playing ahead:**
**Observed strategy:** if the ramp is comfortable, keep shooting it rather than introducing new target risk; it advances nearly every major objective. If it is rejecting badly, switch to Freeways and preserve the kickback. [S2]

**Playing behind:**
Push for Multiball and the Jackpot—especially if the inherited progressive is already high. Because Multiball scoring itself raises the Jackpot until collection, surviving and scoring before finally making the ramp can increase the cashout, albeit with obvious drain risk. **Strategic inference.** [S3]

**Key recurring decision:**
**Take the ramp again or abandon it for Freeways.** The ramp has overwhelmingly better progression and Jackpot upside; the orbit strategy exists because individual High Speed machines can have weak upper flippers or savage ramp rejects. [S2]

## Danger zones

* **Early upper-flipper ramp attempt:** can fire the ball straight down the middle. [S2]
* **Weak ramp reject:** can dump quickly toward the right outlane. [S2]
* **Center stoplight bank:** intended target work can redirect the ball toward the left outlane. [S2]
* **1–6 standups:** useful for Kickback but produce uncontrolled lateral rebounds. [S2]
* **Freeway returns:** orbit strategy is safer in shot selection but demands control of very fast feeds. [S2]

## Trivia

* High Speed’s police-chase theme was inspired by **Steve Ritchie’s real-life high-speed encounter with police**. [S5]
* The game is widely credited as the first pinball machine to use a **Multiball Jackpot** in the modern sense, and its jackpot carries from game to game. [S5]
* It was the first Williams production game using the company’s **System 11** architecture and new alphanumeric display style. [S4][S5]
* The backbox police beacon activates during the chase/multiball presentation. [S4]
* Williams followed it in 1992 with **The Getaway: High Speed II**, another Steve Ritchie design. [S5]
