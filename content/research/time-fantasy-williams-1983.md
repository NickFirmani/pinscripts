## Identity and versions

**Time Fantasy** — Williams Electronics, **1983**, model **515**, four-player **System 7** solid-state game. Design: **Barry Oursler**; artwork: **Seamus McLaughlin**; software: **Ed Suchocki**; sound credits include Suchocki and **Eugene Jarvis**. Archival records give **608 total units**. [S3][S4] ([Kineticist][1])

A very small **two-player test/sample version** also exists; Pinside estimates roughly eight of the 608 were early two-player machines before production changed to four-player. [S7] ([Pinside][2])

Latest production ROM verified: **L-5**. Planetary Pinball's Williams System 7 checksum table explicitly lists game #515 *Time Fantasy* as L-5. [S2] ([Planetary Pinball][3])

No multiball. The game's equivalent headline feature is **Snail Time**, a timed unlimited-ball scoring mode. [S3] ([Silverball Rules][4])

## Thirty-second game plan

1. **Complete 1–5 before Snail Time** to build the playfield multiplier toward **5X**. [S3] ([Silverball Rules][5])
2. Shoot the tight **TIME tunnel** when the needed letter is lit; spelling **T-I-M-E starts Snail Time**, minimum 30 seconds. TIME progress **carries between balls**. [S3] ([Silverball Rules][4])
3. During Snail Time, drains are saved and relaunched. Keep attacking **1–5**: at the mature 90K set value under **5X**, a completion is worth **450K**. [S3] ([Kineticist][1])
4. Before starting, optionally build more time—#1 lane adds 10 seconds, lit return lanes 5, FANTASY completion 5—up to **99 seconds**. More setup means a much larger scoring window, but risks never starting it. [S3]
5. Alternate route: build the unusual **horseshoe** toward 100K or pursue **FANTASY → 10X bonus**, whose maximum 39K base yields a 390K end-of-ball bonus. [S3]

## Core rules and persistence

**Snail Time:** completing TIME immediately starts the timed feature. It begins at **30 seconds minimum**, can be pre-built to **99**, and has continuous ball-save behavior: draining does not subtract time, and another ball is returned. Additional time cannot be earned once Snail Time has begun. [S3] ([Silverball Rules][4])

That makes a drain during Snail Time fundamentally different from a normal drain: competitive strategy can deliberately exploit rapid re-plunges to get additional shots at the top 1–5 lanes. **Observed strategy.** [S3][S5] ([Pinside][6])

**1–5:** completed sets score **10K, then 30K, 60K, 90K**, while also advancing Snail Time's playfield multiplier up to **5X**. The sets can be made before or during Snail Time. [S3]

**TIME persistence:** TIME letters survive from ball to ball, so a player at T-I-M can realistically plan the next ball around one tunnel shot. The **playfield multiplier does not carry**. [S3]

**FANTASY:** completing the seven standups advances ordinary end-of-ball Bonus X through **2X → 3X → 5X → 10X**. Base bonus maxes at **39K**; bonus and Bonus X reset every ball. [S3]

**Horseshoe:** starts at **10K** and can be built to **100K**. Left-orbit shots and making the horseshoe in the arrow's indicated direction raise it; going through opposite the arrow lowers it one step. [S3]

## Skill shots

**1–5 Skill Shot**

* Plunge through the **flashing top 1–5 lane**.
* The lit lane can be manipulated via lane change; the apron explicitly identifies the **right flipper** as advancing lanes.
* Making the flashing lane spots a **TIME letter**; #1 is especially valuable because it also contributes **10 seconds toward the next Snail Time**. [S3]
* Later plunges remain strategically valuable because TIME progress carries and, during Snail Time, every returned ball creates another chance at 1–5.

No separate Super or Secret Skill Shot was verified.

## Secondary features

**Snail Time ball save:** this is the game's defining survival mechanic. For the entire timed feature, drained balls are replaced without reducing the timer. It is therefore much more powerful than a conventional short ball save. [S3][S5]

No conventional player-activated save, kickback, video mode or mystery feature was found.

No conventional Extra Ball qualification was found in the detailed rules sources reviewed; contemporary players specifically describe Snail Time as the game's unusual alternative to the usual Extra Ball concept. [S5] ([Pinside][6])

## What to watch

* **TIME letters:** three lit with one missing means Snail Time is one dangerous tunnel shot away; this progress survives a drain.
* **Snail Time clock / bell:** once it starts, drains are harmless until the timer expires—expect fast plunge-and-score play rather than ball control. [S3][S5]
* **1–5 progress / playfield X:** a player who delays TIME may be deliberately preparing 4X–5X before starting the clock.
* **Horseshoe arrow/value:** tells whether the player is building the 10K→100K alternate scoring engine or risking a value decrease. [S3]

## Match strategy

**Playing ahead:** get TIME close, build a reasonable 1–5 multiplier, then **start Snail Time rather than greedily waiting for a perfect 99-second/5X setup**. Once Snail Time runs, exploit the guaranteed returns and top lanes rather than taking unnecessary FANTASY-target rebounds. **Strategic inference** from the documented scoring/risk structure.

**Playing behind:** maximize leverage before TIME—push toward **5X**, bank more seconds, then start Snail Time and aggressively cycle balls through 1–5. A mature set is **450K at 5X**. A built 100K horseshoe or **390K maximum bonus** offers separate match-swinging routes. [S3]

**Key recurring decision:** **start Snail Time now or keep building it?** Thirty seconds of safe scoring is already valuable; 5X plus a long timer is devastating, but every setup shot before TIME remains a normal-ball drain risk.

## Danger zones

* **TIME target rebound:** both forehand and backhand attempts can return straight down the middle; unavoidable risk because TIME is mandatory. [S3]
* **FANTASY standups:** Bob's Guide specifically recommends not forcing them because of dangerous rebounds. [S3]
* **Pop-bumper exits:** unpredictable feeds can run down the back side of the horseshoe toward center. [S3][S5]
* **Horseshoe setup:** experienced owners describe dialing this shot in as a “side-drainer” problem; it is much safer to experiment once Snail Time protects the ball. [S5]
* **Post-pass/shatz transfer:** moving possession to the preferred right flipper can be useful for TIME, but on a fast example failed transfers expose the wide lower playfield. **Observed strategy.** [S3]

## Trivia

* Time Fantasy was built during a bleak period for Williams pinball. Programmer **Ed Suchocki recalled the team had less than three weeks** to get a game ready so the factory line would not shut down. [S6] ([dokumen.pub][7])
* Historian Ken Horowitz describes it as intended to be a **low-cost game with limited play time** while Williams was concentrating heavily on video games. [S6]
* Only **608 machines** were produced. [S4][S6] ([Pinball Serial Number Database][8])
* Williams initially experimented with a **two-player version** to reduce manufacturing cost before distributors pushed for the four-player production configuration. [S7] ([Pinside][2])
* The game's surreal Seamus McLaughlin artwork—especially the giant snail—has made “**Acid Trip Snail**” a recurring collector nickname, though that is community terminology rather than an official title. [S5]

### Uncertainties and conflicts

* **Snail Time terminology:** the detailed current competitive rules [S3] and players [S5] agree that completing TIME immediately begins the feature during the current ball. One generic pinball-programming article describes Time Fantasy as having a “post-game” timed mode; that description conflicts with machine-specific documentation and is not used here.
* **Production split:** archival sources give **608 total** [S4], while Pinside estimates approximately eight early two-player test games and roughly 600 four-player games [S7]. Exact test-sample count is not firmly documented.
* **Designer/programmer credits:** current four-player archives credit Barry Oursler / Ed Suchocki [S4]; the separate two-player prototype archive uses a different programmer attribution. Do not generalize prototype credits to the production game.
* **Horseshoe versus Snail-Time meta:** strong-player evidence supports both a built horseshoe strategy and the dominant 1–5/Snail Time route. Which is viable depends considerably on how safely the venue game's horseshoe and upper feeds behave. [S3][S5]
