## Identity and versions

* **Avatar** — Stern Pinball, **2010**, standard model; later databases often call it “Pro.” Designed by **John Borg**; software by **Lonnie D. Ropp and Lyman F. Sheats Jr.**; art by **Kevin O’Connor**; sound/music by **David Thiel**. Stern SAM/DMD platform. [S1][S2] ([Stern Pinball][1])
* Stern also produced a **250-unit Limited Edition**, but it is **not gameplay-equivalent**: LE adds a Powerball sensor with double-scoring rules, motorized Link pod, more animated AMP Suit and other hardware. It should be cataloged separately. [S6] ([Pinside][2])
* **Rules basis: `code` — v1.10, released 2011-11-10.** Stern still provides downloadable game code; v1.10 is the latest standard-game release. Its gameplay changes after v1.06 were essentially fixes rather than a scoring rewrite. [S2] ([Pinside][3])
* Major multiballs useful to commentary: **Link Multiball, AMP Suit Multiball, Na’vi Multiball**, and the 4-ball **Final Battle** wizard. [S1][S3] ([Stern Pinball][1])

Research scope follows the supplied commentator-reference specification. 

## Thirty-second game plan

1. **Observed tournament strategy:** complete the R-D-A top lanes and put the first **2× shot multiplier on Link**. Multipliers last for the ball, and Link is the dominant tournament scoring shot. [S3][S4] ([Pinball News][4])
2. Lock a ball in Link; optionally start **Valkyrie/Bomber Battle or Banshee** first, then start Link Multiball. [S4] ([Tilt Forums][5])
3. In Link MB, collect **15 jackpots → 3M Super at Link → hit the trapped ball for Double Super**. With Link at 2×, that Super/Double-Super pair totals **18M**; catching the roving 3× can produce **27M**. [S3][S4] ([Pinball News][4])
4. Use Link’s extra balls/ball save to prepare **AMP Suit MB**, but strong players often delay actually stacking it until the Link Double Super is secured—or use AMP afterward to keep a dying Link MB alive. [S4]
5. **Alternate:** 2× the Banshee ramp and repeatedly loop it. Deep-game Final Battle is normally too costly to chase from zero, but once nearly qualified it has match-destroying scoring. [S4] ([Tilt Forums][5])

## Core rules and persistence

Completing **R-D-A** lights a 2× multiplier for the next unmultiplied major shot. Repeat until all seven major shots have permanent 2× for that ball; the next R-D-A completion starts a **roving 3×** instead. These multipliers reset on the next ball. [S3] ([Pinball News][4])

**Link MB:** first lock requires one Link shot, then two hits to the trapped ball release it for 2-ball MB. Fifteen alternating jackpots light a 3M Super at Link; Link traps the ball again and hitting it during the timer scores the Double Super. Afterward, Link enters victory laps. [S3] ([Pinball News][4])

**AMP Suit MB:** lower the A-M-P bank, then complete AMP Suit Battle; the final hit must be the center bullseye. MB progresses through six jackpots, three double jackpots, then AMP bullseye Super jackpots. The second Super completes the feature and increases the next jackpot cycle substantially. Shot multipliers apply throughout. [S3] ([Pinball News][4])

Easy commentary trap: **stacking is not always desirable.** Eywa can award either MB add-a-ball or timed-mode extension; running Seeds/Bomber/Banshee may cause **Extend Time to take priority over Add-A-Ball**. Competitive players therefore frequently avoid indiscriminate stacks. [S4] ([Tilt Forums][5])

For Final Battle qualification, starting a feature lights its ladder insert **solid**; solid progress disappears next ball. Fully completing the feature makes it **flash**, which persists between balls. Bomber’s remaining required awards and Seeds’ remaining switch hits also carry forward between attempts. [S3] ([Pinball News][4])

**Final Battle:** collect increasing switch-hit quotas, then Unobtanium lights for 5M. Deliberately draining one lit outlane scores 10M and raises the target to 15M; the second scores 15M and raises it to **30M**. Current competitive documentation confirms the center-shot multiplier applies, making a perfectly prepared **3× Unobtanium worth 90M**. Collecting it restores lost balls and restarts ball save. [S3][S4] ([Pinball News][4])

## Skill shots

**R-D-A Skill Shot:** plunge through the flashing top lane; flippers move the flashing lane. It starts at **250K** and rises **25K per subsequent ball**. It exists only on a new-ball plunge, not the relaunch after a Link lock. [S3] ([Pinball News][4])

The points are secondary to where the plunge leaves the ball: completing R-D-A is what creates the strategically important shot multipliers.

No separate production super/secret skill shot was verified.

## Secondary features

* **Eywa Mystery:** accumulated switch hits light the captive ball; multiple uncollected awards can be banked. Awards include points, Bonus X, timer extension, and crucially **add-a-ball during multiball**. [S3] ([Pinball News][4])
* **Tournament sensitivity:** expert tournament documentation says the first Eywa during Link MB is not an add-a-ball in tournament mode, while the first during AMP MB is. I did not find the factory adjustment table that independently enumerates this behavior, so treat it as **observed tournament behavior**. [S4] ([Tilt Forums][5])
* **Extra ball:** a rare Eywa award can light Extra Ball at the ramp. Its occurrence is percentage/adjustment driven; no universal tournament point conversion was verified. [S3] ([Pinball News][4])
* **Unobtanium outlane awards:** hits to the center target light/build consolation awards over the outlanes; during multiball ball-save time these can effectively become free points. [S3]
* No player action button or production video mode was verified.

## What to watch

1. **Green X flashing/solid:** flashing means a shot multiplier is ready to be assigned; solid means that shot has 2× for the rest of the ball. A roving flash after all seven are doubled is 3×. [S3]
2. **Ball physically trapped in Link:** setup is banked; repeated hits to that ball either start Link MB or create the Double-Super opportunity. [S3]
3. **AMP 3-bank drops:** the player has exposed the AMP Battle targets; once only the bullseye remains, multiball is imminent. [S3]
4. **Feature ladder solid vs flashing:** solid is temporary Final-Battle progress; flashing survives the drain. [S3]

## Match strategy

**Playing ahead:** **strategic inference:** grind Link, put 2× there early, take the Double Super, and avoid unnecessary Seeds or deep Final-Battle work. Don’t stack AMP merely because it is available if doing so makes Link scoring harder to control.

**Playing behind:** bring Banshee/Bomber into Link, chase 3× Link timing, or use AMP to extend the multiball after reaching Link’s Double Super. If Final Battle is already close, its **30M × 2×/3×** Unobtanium collect is far larger than normal-game scoring. [S4]

**Key recurring decision:** **stack now or preserve control/Eywa priority?** Avatar unusually rewards *not* throwing every ready feature together.

## Danger zones

* **Pop-bumper exit:** documented standard games can throw the ball straight down the middle; later examples received a small guide/spring intended to improve this. **Machine-sensitive.** [S3][S5] ([Pinball News][4])
* **AMP Suit targets/magnet:** arguably the game’s most dangerous required area; rebounds and magnet throws can immediately become drains. [S3][S5]
* **Banshee ramp reject:** underpowered attempts can come back into the center quickly. **Observed/setup-sensitive.** [S5]
* **Left outlane:** specifically noted as unusually hungry in contemporary play testing. [S3] ([Pinball News][4])
* **Center Unobtanium standup:** huge potential value, but a dangerous direct target when the ball is under control.

## Trivia

* Stern commissioned **Stephen Lang** to record original Colonel Quaritch callouts rather than relying entirely on movie clips. [S1] ([Stern Pinball][1])
* The distinctive backglass is a **3-D lenticular design**, one of the machine’s most recognizable presentation features. [S1]
* The Link mechanism traps a ball between **two rising posts**, then makes the player physically strike the captive ball to free it. [S3]
* The standard version used an unusually sparse one-ramp layout; much of the mechanical budget went into the AMP Suit, magnet and 3-D presentation. [S2][S3]
* The 250-game LE added a **white Powerball with sensor-triggered double scoring**, enough of a rules change that it should not share this standard-game reference sheet. [S6] ([Pinside][6])
