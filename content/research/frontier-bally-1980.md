## Identity and versions

* **Frontier** — Bally Manufacturing, 1980; model **1217/1217-E**, Bally MPU **AS-2518-35** solid-state platform. Design by **George Christian**, software by **Rehman Merchant**, art by **Greg Freres, Kevin O’Connor, and Margaret Hudson**. Production: **1,850 units**. [S1][S4][S5]
* No multiball. The game’s scoring is built around two simultaneous resources: **Frontier Bonus** and **Den of Predators Bonus**, each with its own multiplier. [S1][S2]
* A related **Bell Games Frontier (1981)** exists; this brief covers the original Bally machine. [S4]
* Scope follows the supplied commentator-reference specification.

## Thirty-second game plan

1. Build **A-B-C / predator rows**: every set containing one A, B and C advances **Frontier Bonus by 10K**, while individual animals build the separate Predator Bonus. [S1][S2]
2. Shoot the **left inline drops**. The first opens **Frontier Falls** and lights the right spinner; deeper shots raise Frontier Bonus to **2×, 3×, then 4×** at the Grizzly target. [S2]
3. Rip the lit **right spinner** and try to fall into **Frontier Falls** at upper right. It collects the multiplied Frontier Bonus **without resetting its value**; reopen the gate and collect it again. This is the primary tournament scoring loop. **Observed strategy.** [S2]
4. If the Predator grid has grown large, complete the **right 3-bank** to raise Predator Bonus as high as **4×**. A max 90K Predator Bonus becomes **360K at end of ball**. [S2]
5. Alternate strategy: if the spinner/Falls feed is poor, emphasize Predator progress and its end-of-ball multiplier rather than repeatedly gambling on the collect loop. **Strategic inference.**

## Core rules and persistence

* A/B/C lanes and their corresponding standups light animals in three columns. Each animal is worth **3K Predator Bonus**; all 15 produce a 45K completed set, and a second set can raise the base Predator Bonus to **90K**. [S2]
* Completing each horizontal row—one animal from each column—adds **10K Frontier Bonus**, normally up to **60K**. A documented liberal adjustment can raise the maximum to **110K**. [S1][S3]
* **Left inline sequence:** first target lights the spinner and opens Frontier Falls; second = **2× Frontier**, third = **3×**, rear Grizzly standup = **4×**. [S2][S3]
* **Right 3-bank:** advances the Predator multiplier through **2×, 3×, 4×**. [S2][S3]
* **Frontier Falls can be collected repeatedly.** Collecting closes its gate but does **not** reset the Frontier Bonus; another left-side target reopens it. [S2]
* Both Frontier and Predator **base bonus progress carry between balls** in the documented standard strategy/rules implementation. [S2]
* At end of ball, both bonuses are awarded; **Predator Bonus cannot otherwise be collected during the ball**, while Frontier Bonus can be cashed repeatedly at Frontier Falls. [S2]
* Easy commentary trap: a Frontier Falls collect is **not a cash-out/reset**. The same built bonus remains available for another collect.

## Skill shots

**A-B-C lane skill shot** — plunge into whichever top lane is least advanced.

* There is no lane change, so plunge strength/angle determines the choice.
* Each lane advances its corresponding Predator column and can complete another horizontal row for Frontier Bonus.
* Competitive guidance specifically recommends targeting the least-complete lane rather than merely plunging for raw switch points. **Observed strategy.** [S2]
* Completing A-B-C once lights the return lanes for **15K** and enables the next A-B-C completion to award Extra Ball; subsequent completion can light/award Special depending on configuration. [S3]

No separate super or secret skill shot found.

## Secondary features

* **Ball save:** none found.
* **Extra Ball:** A-B-C progression can qualify/award Extra Ball; original rules limit the game to **one Extra Ball per ball in play**. [S1][S3]
* **Special:** available through later A-B-C completions, the completed Predator grid, and other deep progression. Replay/novelty/tournament treatment is configuration-dependent. [S1][S3]
* **Player controls:** no lane change, action button, Magna-Save, or equivalent.
* **Video/mystery features:** none.

## What to watch

1. **Frontier 2×/3×/4× lamps:** show how much every Frontier Falls collect is currently worth. [S3]
2. **Gate arrow + lit spinner:** the primary scoring route is open; expect a shot through the spinner toward Frontier Falls. [S2]
3. **Predator grid / 45K lamp:** a nearly or fully completed grid makes the right-side multiplier bank much more important. [S2]
4. **Frontier Bonus ladder:** once it is large, repeated Falls collections can dominate the game even without further bonus-building.

## Match strategy

**Playing ahead:** establish Frontier Bonus and multiplier, then repeatedly use the spinner/Falls route if its feed is trustworthy. Avoid the right drop bank unless the Predator Bonus is already large enough to justify the rebound risk. **Observed strategy.** [S2]

**Playing behind:** maximize both systems: push Frontier toward 4× for repeated collects, and attack the right bank for **4× Predator Bonus**. A completed second Predator set can put **360K** into end-of-ball alone. [S2]

**Key recurring decision:** **stay on the repeatable Frontier Falls engine, or invest in the dangerous right bank to multiply Predator Bonus?** The answer depends heavily on score differential and how safely the spinner/Falls route plays on that copy.

## Danger zones

1. **Short spinner shot:** falling backward through the spinner can head directly toward the center drain. [S2]
2. **Right drop bank:** specifically identified as riskier than the left inline targets; direct rebounds can become immediate drain threats. [S2]
3. **Pop-bumper exits:** much of the uncontrolled danger starts after an otherwise successful trip upstairs. [S2]
4. **Predator standups:** unnecessary direct shots add rebound risk; expert guidance recommends avoiding them unless strategically useful. [S2]
5. **Left-inline rebounds:** generally safer than the right bank, but a hard reject can still head toward the middle. [S2]

## Trivia

* Frontier was designed by **George Christian**, with programming by **Rehman Merchant** and artwork shared by **Greg Freres, Kevin O’Connor, and Margaret Hudson**. [S4]
* Bally produced only **1,850 units**. [S4][S5]
* The machine was designed in May 1980 and manufactured in late **1980**, with archival sources generally placing production in October/November. [S3][S5]
* The playfield has **two completely separate bonus systems with separate multipliers**, unusually making end-of-ball bonus and an in-ball collect equally central to strategy. [S2]
* Bally’s contemporary trade description advertised Frontier as **“rugged” and “down to earth”**, matching its mountain-man/wilderness artwork. [S6]

### Uncertainties and conflicts

* **Frontier maximum:** standard/conservative documentation gives **60K**, while a liberal adjustment permits **110K**. [S1][S3] This substantially changes the value of repeated Falls collects.
* Bob’s Guide states that both **base bonus values persist ball-to-ball** [S2]. I did not obtain a readable primary-manual page establishing whether any venue-selectable memory adjustment can change this; verify if the machine uses unusual settings.
* Extra Ball and Special behavior is adjustment/tournament dependent. Original Bally settings can be configured differently from modern tournament handling.
* The spinner return, Frontier Falls entry, pop exits, and target rebounds are highly **copy-dependent**, making the safest strategy less universal than the rules alone suggest.
