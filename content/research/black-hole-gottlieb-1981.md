## Identity and versions

* **Black Hole** — D. Gottlieb & Co., October 1981; model **668**, Gottlieb **System 80**, widebody. Design by **John Buras and Adolf Seitz Jr.**; art by **Terry Doerzaph**. Production: **8,774**. [S1][S5] ([Pinside][1])
* The signature mechanism is a full **lower playfield sloping away from the player**, with reversed-orientation flippers, reached through the Gravity Tunnel. The game has one elaborate **3-ball multiball** using physical locks on both levels. [S1][S2] 
* Domestic games have Votrax speech and the mirrored/animated “black hole” backbox; many export games omit speech and use a simpler backglass. Core gameplay is otherwise the same. [S5] ([Pinside][2])
* No authoritative “latest ROM” history was found. **Replacement System 80 CPUs matter more:** Ni-Wumpf and other boards can implement non-original tournament behavior. [S6] ([Ni-Wumpf][3])
* Scope follows the supplied commentator-research specification. 

## Thirty-second game plan

1. **Two tournament routes are legitimate.** If the upper-left captive-hole saucer returns safely, repeatedly shoot it for **5K** while deliberately **not completing the four yellow standups**, which would light it to lock. This exact grind won the deciding Black Hole game in the **2025 Pinburgh A final**. **Observed strategy.** [S1][S4] ([GameFAQs][4])
2. For the scoring-ceiling route, complete **B-L-A-C-K and/or H-O-L-E in order**: one completed sequence gives **2× G-Force** scoring downstairs; both give **3×**. [S1] 
3. Before committing downstairs, get the **Re-Entry Gate open** if possible. Otherwise a lower-playfield drain becomes a real drain unless the player completes either lower target bank first. [S1][S3] 
4. Downstairs, build the **Bonus Multiplier to 5×**, score heavily, and lock a ball in the always-active lower captive hole. Light and lock the upper captive hole to set up multiball. [S1][S2] ([GameFAQs][4])
5. With both balls locked, send the third through the **Gravity Tunnel**: first comes 2-ball play downstairs, then—if re-entry succeeds—3-ball play upstairs with the gate held open. This is the high-risk/high-ceiling route. [S1]

## Core rules and persistence

* The four **yellow standups** light the upper captive hole and the right-return “Open Gate” arrow. The upper lock qualification is player-specific and remembered. [S1] 
* The **lower captive hole is always active**. On original software its physical locked ball persists from ball to ball and can therefore effectively be **inherited/stolen by another player**. The upper locked ball, by contrast, is ejected at the end of that player's turn. This is extremely easy to mis-call. [S1][S6] 
* All actual **bonus is earned only on the lower playfield**, displayed in the separate lower-field display, then multiplied and added at end of ball. Bonus X runs up to **5×**. [S1][S2] 
* One completed B-L-A-C-K or H-O-L-E sequence gives persistent **2× G-Force**. Completing both gives **3× for the current ball**, but both sequences reset for the next ball. [S1][S2] 
* In normal single-ball play, pops and minor switches can **close the Re-Entry Gate**. During the final 3-ball phase, the gate stays open. [S1][S2] ([GameFAQs][4])
* A particularly strong technique is to **immediately shoot back down the Gravity Tunnel after successful re-entry**: until another upper-playfield switch closes the gate, the next basement trip remains safe. **Observed strategy.** [S3] ([Tilt Forums][5])

## Skill shots

**None found.** Neither the Gottlieb manual nor the detailed rulesheet defines a normal, alternate, super, or secret plunge skill shot. The top rollovers can be influenced by plunge strength, but that alone is not enough to label one a formal skill shot. [S1][S2]

## Secondary features

* **Ball save:** no conventional ball save found. Re-entry is the game's survival mechanism, but it must be earned.
* **Extra balls:** can be lit at the upper captive hole by completing the lit lower four-bank, and through progression of the upper rollunder. The original game allows only one extra ball per ball-in-play. [S1] ([Digital Pinball Fans][6])
* **Novelty/tournament treatment:** original System 80 settings can substitute points for Extra Ball/Special; modern replacement boards may add entirely different “Tournament Mode” behavior. Venue configuration is materially important. [S6] ([Pinside][7])
* **Player controls:** no action button, Magna-Save, lane-change system, or equivalent found.
* **Video/display modes:** none.

## What to watch

1. **Blue captive-hole lamp flashing:** the upper-left saucer will now physically lock the ball. If it remains dark and the player keeps shooting the saucer, they may intentionally be running the safe 5K grind. [S1][S4]
2. **One or two G-Force lamps:** tells you lower-playfield scoring is at **2× or 3×**. The speech-equipped version announces “G-Force accelerated.” [S1]
3. **Re-Entry Gate / green Open Gate arrow:** this is survival state. Gate closed means entering the basement carries much more risk. [S1]
4. **“Enter Gravity Tunnel” callout with both locks occupied:** multiball is ready; the next successful tunnel shot releases the lower lock. [S1]

## Match strategy

**Playing ahead:** if the upper captive-hole saucer feeds predictably, the **5K saucer grind** is a proven low-variance choice. Avoid completing all four yellow standups so the saucer does not become a lock. Jason Zahler used approximately 50 saucer shots to put up more than 450K in the deciding 2025 Pinburgh final. **Observed strategy.** [S4] ([Kineticist][8])

**Playing behind:** pursue **3× G-Force + 5× Bonus X + lower-playfield/multiball scoring**. The lower bonus can reach hundreds of thousands before its end-of-ball multiplier, making it the obvious match-swinging route. **Observed strategy.** [S2][S3]

**Key recurring decision:** **keep safely looping the upper saucer, or light the lock and commit to Black Hole progression?** The former banks steady points; the latter sacrifices that grind for the game's vastly larger but riskier lower-playfield ceiling.

## Danger zones

1. **Entering downstairs with Re-Entry closed:** if you fail to complete a lower target bank, the eventual lower-field drain becomes a lost ball. [S1]
2. **Upper pops after opening the gate:** they can immediately close it again, turning what looked like a safe Gravity Tunnel shot back into a risk. [S1]
3. **Upper drop-target rebounds:** many dangerous returns are straight toward the center gap. [S3]
4. **Lower-right control:** balls deflected toward the gate can push through it, have the gate close behind them, and drain. [S3]
5. **Lower-left cradle attempts:** balls can climb the rail above the flipper and dribble through the corner gap; drop catches/dead bounces are preferred by the strategy guide. [S3] ([Silverball Rules][9])

## Trivia

* Black Hole's **reversed lower playfield** was a major novelty: the flippers sit at the far end and propel the ball back toward the player. [S1][S5] ([Arcade History][10])
* Gottlieb produced **8,774** machines, a substantial run for an elaborate System 80 widebody. [S5]
* The domestic machine's robotic speech uses a **Votrax SC-01** synthesizer. [S5] ([Arcade History][10])
* Domestic cabinets used the famous **infinity-mirror backglass with a rotating black-hole effect**; export versions were often simplified. [S5]
* A separate **fifth display** visible through the playfield shows the bonus accumulated on the lower level. [S1][S5] ([Pinside][2])

### Uncertainties and conflicts

* **Bonus rollover persistence:** the Gottlieb manual says activated upper rollovers are remembered by player [S1], while Peter Hall's detailed rulesheet explicitly says this manual statement is incorrect [S2]. Later rules references state **Bonus X resets to 1× each ball**. Treat rollover-progress persistence as machine/ROM verification-required. ([GameFAQs][4])
* **Replacement CPU tournament behavior:** original software has no modern “Tournament Mode,” while Ni-Wumpf added one in later replacement-board code. Reports indicate it can change what happens to the shared lower lock, which materially changes multiplayer strategy. [S6] ([Ni-Wumpf][3])
* **Physical strategy is highly copy-dependent.** The 5K saucer grind is demonstrably tournament-viable [S4], but only if that machine's kickout/feed is repeatable.
