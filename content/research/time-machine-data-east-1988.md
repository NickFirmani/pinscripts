## Identity and versions

* **Time Machine** — Data East USA, December 1988; model 04, four-player solid-state/alphanumeric game on **Data East/Sega Version 2** hardware. Design: **Ed Cebula and Joe Kaminkow**; software: **Jerry Koraganis and Lonnie D. Ropp**; art: **Kevin O'Connor and Margaret Hudson**; sound/music: **David Thiel**. Production: **2,896**. [S1][S3]  ([manualzz.com][1])
* Three-ball multiball is the principal multiball. The game physically locks balls while traveling backward from the 1980s through the **1970s → 1960s → 1950s**. [S1] ([manualzz.com][2])
* The celebrated 1950s state switches from contemporary electronic sound to **real electromechanical chimes mounted in the cabinet**. [S1][S3] ([manualzz.com][1])
* **Latest surviving ROM verified: 2.4.** PinMAME and the maintained code-revision list both identify 2.4; Pinside still labels the official “latest software” unknown, and I found no Data East change log establishing when/how 2.4 changed rules. [S4] ([GitHub][3])

## Thirty-second game plan

1. **On factory-ish settings, shoot ramps—especially the right ramp—to do almost everything.** Each ramp spots a STARWARP letter and, with factory General Spotting, can also spot the required decade targets. **Observed strategy.** [S1][S5] ([manualzz.com][1])
2. Spell **STARWARP**, then shoot the center ramp during its factory **20-second** timer for **1,000,000**. This is enormous relative to normal scoring and is a recognized tournament priority. [S1][S7] ([manualzz.com][1])
3. Keep traveling backward: complete the three shapes for each decade and shoot an outside ramp. Reaching the **1950s starts 3-ball multiball**. [S1][S2] ([manualzz.com][1])
4. During multiball, light decade targets to grow the center-ramp **Jackpot from 100K toward 1M**, then cash it. [S1][S5] ([manualzz.com][1])
5. Alternate tournament route: simply **loop STARWARP** rather than prioritizing multiball. Experienced owners and tournament discussion explicitly note that repeated million-point Starwarp collects can outperform the multiball route. **Observed strategy.** [S6][S7] ([Pinside][4])

## Core rules and persistence

* **Time Travel:** triangles qualify the 1970s, circles the 1960s, squares the 1950s; once the current decade is qualified, either outer ramp travels backward. Factory General Spotting also lets top lanes and ramps spot these standups, while **Hard turns that assistance off entirely**. [S1] ([manualzz.com][1])
* **STARWARP:** top lanes and all three ramps spot letters. Complete all eight, then center ramp scores the adjustable Starwarp value; factory = **1M**, adjustment range **500K–1.5M**. Factory timer = 20 seconds. [S1] ([manualzz.com][1])
* STARWARP persistence is separately adjustable:

  * **HOLD:** progress carries ball-to-ball **and player-to-player**.
  * **1 PLAYER:** carries ball-to-ball for each individual player.
  * **RESET:** starts unlit every ball.
    This can materially change multiplayer tournament fairness/strategy. [S1] ([manualzz.com][1])
* Factory **General Memory** retains lit decade/standup and top-lane progress between balls; Hard does not. [S1] ([manualzz.com][1])
* **Multiball Jackpot:** center ramp = 100K base plus 100K for each lit 1970s/1960s/1950s standup, max **1M**. Standups can therefore be deliberately added during multiball before the collect. [S1][S5] ([manualzz.com][1])
* **Mini-Jackpot:** three consecutive center-ramp shots within its timer; instruction card gives 100K / 200K / 300K on balls 1/2/3. [S2] 
* Top-lane completions also award a semi-random back-panel feature. Factory probabilities include **100K, Bonus Hold, Extra Ball and 20 seconds of 2× All Scores**. That 2× is a major opportunistic scoring lever. [S1] ([manualzz.com][1])
* Easy commentator trap: **2× All Scores is not the ordinary 2×–5× end-of-ball bonus multiplier.**

## Skill shots

**None found.**

The plunge into the three top lanes is strategically useful because lit lanes score the back-panel value and, on factory settings, spot both STARWARP and decade targets. But neither Data East's manual nor instruction card identifies a separate coded plunge skill-shot award. [S1][S2] ([manualzz.com][1])

## Secondary features

* **Laser Kick:** left-outlane kickback. Factory setting starts it lit each ball; completing either outside ramp relights it. Hard starts it only on ball 1 and requires the currently flashing outside ramp. Protecting/re-lighting this is important because the left outlane is particularly dangerous. [S1][S6] ([manualzz.com][1])
* **Einstein Shot:** pop bumpers build an Energy value; roll through one return lane, then shoot the **opposite ramp** to collect it before the factory 12-second timer expires. [S1] ([manualzz.com][1])
* **Extra Balls:** completing top lanes six times or traveling to the 1950s twice on the same ball can light an outlane EB; right flipper moves the lit side. Both routes are individually operator-disableable. [S1] ([manualzz.com][1])
* **Novelty mode** disables Extra Balls and free-play awards and converts Special to score. [S1] ([manualzz.com][1])
* No video mode or player action button.

## What to watch

* **STARWARP letters complete / center ramp flashing:** there is a timed 500K–1.5M collect available; factory is 1M.
* **1970 / 1960 / 1950 ramp insert flashing:** current decade targets are complete; the next outside ramp advances time and may lock another ball.
* **Actual music/chimes change by decade:** the soundtrack itself tells the commentator how deep into multiball progression the player has gone.
* **2X ALL SCORES lit:** immediately expect the player to abandon setup and cash STARWARP, Jackpot or another large value before the ~20-second timer expires.

## Match strategy

**Playing ahead:** On factory spotting, favor the **ramp/STARWARP route** rather than attacking raw standups. Take each million promptly and keep Laser Kick lit. Multiball is useful protection, but there is little reason to overbuild its Jackpot if another Starwarp collect covers the opponent. **Strategic inference.**

**Playing behind:** Push toward **1M Jackpot and repeated 1M STARWARP**, and exploit any random **2× All Scores** award immediately. A 2× Starwarp-sized collect is capable of changing the match on one shot. Multiball provides the highest-variance chance to raise the Jackpot while keeping balls alive.

**Key recurring decision:** **Keep traveling toward multiball, or divert to the center ramp for STARWARP?** Strong players may prioritize the immediate million because it is timed; others use the outside-ramp loop to keep advancing both objectives.

## Danger zones

* **Left outlane:** repeatedly identified by owners as the major drain; Laser Kick status matters immediately. [S6] ([Pinside][5])
* **Left ramp:** notably harder than the other flowing shots on some well-maintained copies. [S6] ([Pinside][5])
* **Decade standup banks:** on Hard settings these become mandatory, exposing the ball to direct rebounds instead of benign spotting.
* **Center-ramp pressure shot:** STARWARP is timed, encouraging rushed shots at a tight central entrance.
* **Pop exits:** Einstein value encourages bumper time, but the eventual feed back to the lower playfield is uncontrolled.

## Trivia

* The 1950s mode uses **real physical EM-style chimes**, not merely sampled chime sounds; the operator can choose OFF, STREET or louder SUPER intensity. [S1] ([manualzz.com][1])
* The game changes music and sound as it travels from the 1980s backward through the three earlier decades. [S1][S6]
* Time Machine was an early Data East title, using the company's **Version 2 MPU** architecture. [S1][S3] ([manualzz.com][1])
* The center STARWARP ramp was unusually lucrative for 1988: its factory **1M value is itself operator-adjustable as high as 1.5M**. [S1]
* The manual's factory setup includes an automatic system intended to maintain roughly a **65-second average ball time** by periodically adjusting game difficulty. [S1] ([manualzz.com][1])

### Uncertainties and conflicts

* **Dominant scoring strategy:** factory documentation emphasizes both time travel/multiball and STARWARP [S1], while tournament/player sources explicitly report that repeatedly farming the **1M STARWARP** can outscore prioritizing multiball [S6][S7]. Therefore this brief treats ramp → STARWARP as the dominant short-game scoring line and multiball as the main alternate/high-variance route. ([Pinside][4])
* **ROM:** preservation sources identify **2.4** as the latest known ROM [S4], but Pinside says latest software is unknown and no manufacturer change log was located. Do not imply 2.4's exact rule changes are known.
* **STARWARP shared state:** HOLD explicitly carries progress player-to-player [S1]. If a tournament machine is actually set this way, players can inherit another player's nearly completed STARWARP; 1 PLAYER avoids this.
* **General Spotting is unusually consequential:** Factory lets ramps/top lanes spot decade standups; Hard disables that assistance completely. Much online “right ramp all day” advice assumes the easier/factory behavior. [S1][S5]
* Exact physical lock ownership behavior between players was not verified well enough to call it lock stealing; check the actual machine before printing that claim.
