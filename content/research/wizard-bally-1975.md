## Identity and versions

**Wizard!** — Bally Manufacturing, **May 1975**, model **1027**, four-player electromechanical game. Design **Greg Kmiec**; art **Dave Christensen**. Production is documented at **10,005** units. Two flippers, three pops, four mechanical **Flip Flags**, one spinner, one top saucer and end-of-ball bonus; no multiball. [S3][S4]

The machine was factory-adjustable for **3 or 5 balls**. Surviving factory instruction cards also show distinct **Replay and Extra-Ball award versions**, which matters much more to tournament play than cosmetic production variations. [S1][S2]

## Thirty-second game plan

1. **UTAD — Up Top All Day — through the spinner from the right flipper.** The goal is to land in the top saucer rather than fall back through the pops. **Observed strategy.** [S3]
2. The **top saucer scores 3K + three bonus advances**, then ejects through the four-position Flags lane, safely building/collecting the game's scoring features. [S1][S3]
3. The important features are **Flag 3 → Double Bonus** and **Flag 4 → spinner 100/spin instead of 10**. Max base bonus is **19K**, so doubled bonus is a huge **38K**. [S3][S5]
4. The core risk decision: **keep looping safely upstairs, or deliberately attack a Flag standup** to light Double Bonus / spinner when the payoff justifies the rebound risk. [S3]
5. Alternate route: if the left flipper can reliably **backhand the spinner**, the player can farm the same UTAD strategy without repeatedly transferring to the right flipper. [S3]

## Core rules and persistence

Wizard's four mechanical flags form the main rules engine. The corresponding standups qualify them: **Flag 1** upgrades pops from 10 to 100; **Flag 2** upgrades the center target from 500 to 3K; **Flag 3** enables Double Bonus; **Flag 4** upgrades the spinner from 10 to 100. Top star rollovers can also qualify Flags 1 and 4. The feature is then activated as the ball travels through the **upper-right Flags lane**. [S3]

The top saucer is unusually efficient: **3K + three bonus advances**, followed automatically by passage through all four feature-lane buttons. Competitive strategy therefore values saucer entries far above balls merely rattling through the pop area. [S1][S3]

Bonus reaches **19K**. When Flag 3 has been activated, that end-of-ball bonus is doubled, making **38K** available at drain. Technical owner evidence confirms the bonus unit counts 19K and Double Bonus doubles the countdown. [S3][S5]

At **19K bonus**, the **right Special target and Extra Ball lane light**. Factory cards specify only **one Extra Ball per ball in play**. [S1]

## Skill shots

**Top-saucer / Flag-4 Skill Shot — observed tournament terminology**

* Plunge so the ball crosses the **right top star rollover**, qualifying Flag 4, then falls into the **top saucer**.
* Saucer gives **3K + three bonus advances** and ejects into the Flags lane, which immediately turns on the 100-point spinner if the Flag-4 qualification was made.
* If the right star cannot be reached cleanly, the left star/Flag 1 is still useful, but the saucer itself takes priority. [S3]

No separate factory-named Super or Secret Skill Shot was found.

## Secondary features

**Extra Ball:** when bonus reaches **19K**, the Extra Ball lane lights. The factory card awards an EB when the ball passes through it, limited to one EB per ball in play. [S1]

**Special:** 19K also lights the right Special target. Two surviving factory cards disagree intentionally because they represent different configurations: card **M-1508-43-B awards a replay**, while **M-1508-43-C awards an Extra Ball**. [S1]

No ball save, kickback, player control, mystery or video feature.

## What to watch

* **Flag 3 qualified/collected:** Double Bonus is the major end-of-ball leverage; a 19K base becomes 38K.
* **Flag 4 / spinner lit:** the spinner has jumped from **10 to 100 per spin**, making the normal UTAD loop much more lucrative.
* **Bonus at 19K:** Extra Ball lane and right Special are now lit. [S1][S3]
* **Ball enters the top saucer:** expect 3K, three bonus steps, then an immediate Flags-lane feed; this is essentially the ideal Wizard possession. [S3]

## Match strategy

**Playing ahead:** stay with **spinner → top saucer → Flags lane**, prioritize ball control on the lane exit, and avoid deliberately shooting standups. If the base bonus is already large and not doubled, Flag 3 may be the one exception. **Observed strategy.** [S3]

**Playing behind:** deliberately qualify **Flag 3** for Double Bonus and **Flag 4** for the 100-point spinner. A maxed 38K doubled bonus plus repeated lit-spinner trips can erase a large deficit. If the tournament honors the Extra Ball version, reaching 19K has even more leverage. [S1][S3]

**Key recurring decision:** **take another safe trip upstairs, or risk a standup to improve that trip?** Wizard rewards patience, but Double Bonus becomes worth the target risk as the base bonus approaches 19K.

## Danger zones

* **Balls falling through the pops:** much less controllable than a successful saucer → Flags-lane exit and can head directly center. [S3]
* **Flags-lane exit:** normally the preferred feed, but individual games may require a dead bounce, drop catch, tap pass or nudge to regain the right flipper. [S3]
* **Any Flag standup:** direct EM target rebounds make intentionally qualifying features hazardous.
* **Recessed 5K target:** competitive documentation calls it a classic sucker shot; even a successful hit can drain. [S3][S6]
* **Top-edge wear groove:** heavily played examples can develop a track above the saucer that prevents balls from dropping into it, badly damaging the intended UTAD strategy. [S3]

## Trivia

* Bally explicitly launched *Wizard!* as a tie-in to **Ken Russell's 1975 film *Tommy***, with **Roger Daltrey and Ann-Margret** depicted on the backglass. Contemporary *Cash Box* quoted Bally describing the movie connection at launch. [S7]
* **10,005 machines** were produced, making it Bally's highest-production flipper game to that point. [S4]
* Wizard is widely credited as an early landmark in **officially licensed pinball themes**. [S4][S8]
* It was the first Bally title commonly credited with the unusual mechanical **Flip Flag** feature; the associated target-device patent was filed in May 1975. [S8]
* It was also the **last Bally EM with the knocker mounted in the lower cabinet** before Bally moved it into the backbox. [S8]

### Uncertainties and conflicts

* **Replay versus Extra-Ball Special:** this is genuine factory variation, not a documentation error. Card B awards a **replay** at the lit Special; card C awards an **Extra Ball**. [S1]
* **Flag-number typo in strategy documentation:** Bob's detailed rule mapping correctly identifies **Flag 3 = Double Bonus**, and independent PinTips advice says the lowest left standup is Flag 3. One later Bob strategy sentence refers to “Flag 2” while discussing setting up Double Bonus; this appears to be a typo. [S3][S6]
* **Flag persistence between balls:** I did not find sufficiently authoritative documentation specifying every flag's ball-to-ball reset behavior. Avoid asserting persistence on commentary unless verified on the venue machine.
* **3-ball versus 5-ball:** the factory manual supports both configurations [S2], but I found no reliable evidence that core switch values change between them. Do not import 3/5-ball scoring differences from other Bally EMs.
