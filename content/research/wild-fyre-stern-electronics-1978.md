## Identity and versions

**Wild Fyre** — Stern Electronics, **October 1978**, model **106**, four-player solid-state game on Stern's **M-100** platform with six-digit numeric displays. Design: **Harry Williams**. Production: **2,400**. Two flippers, three pop bumpers, three top eject holes, an upper-right bonus-collect eject, 3-bank and 4-bank drops, and a spinner; no multiball. [S1][S3]

No authoritative production ROM revision number was found; current archives list the software version as unknown. An aftermarket **Wild Fyre: Reignyted** rules conversion exists and radically changes scoring, 5X and ball-save behavior—it is **not** the stock game discussed below. [S3][S6]

## Thirty-second game plan

1. **UTAD — Up Top All Day.** Survive, build the base bonus from **2K toward 20K**, and make the upper-right **Collect Bonus** eject when the value is worthwhile. [S1][S2]
2. Get **Double Bonus (2X)** either by completing the upper 3-bank twice or by making three Arrow advances. The multiplier survives a mid-ball bonus collect. [S2]
3. Watch the base bonus carefully: at **2K, 8K, 14K and 20K**, the spinner jumps from **100 to 1,000 per spin**. Rip it while the lamp is on. [S1][S2]
4. Collect Bonus resets the base to **2K** but keeps 2X, so the core scoring loop is **build → collect → rebuild**. [S1][S2]
5. **Final-ball twist:** once Double Bonus is earned, stock Wild Fyre alternates **2X and 5X** with switch hits. A max 20K bonus is therefore worth **100K at 5X**, and deliberately ending the ball while 5X is showing can actually be correct. [S1][S2]

## Core rules and persistence

Base bonus begins at **2K**, advances in 2K steps and maxes at **20K**. It is collected either at the upper-right eject during play or at the drain; afterward the base returns to 2K. [S1]

**Double Bonus:** the competitive rules document two routes—complete the upper 3-bank twice, or advance the right-side Arrow feature three times. The latter also makes its rollover worth **10K**. [S1][S2]

**Spinner:** ordinarily 100/spin; it becomes **1K** only at base-bonus values 2K, 8K, 14K and 20K. At 20K, the manual says the 1K lamp remains lit for the rest of that ball. This unusual relationship means advancing bonus can actually turn a valuable spinner **off** temporarily. [S1]

**3-ball/5-ball:** pop bumpers score **1K in 3-ball, only 100 in 5-ball**. The manual supports either ball count. [S1]

The **WILD FYRE** drop bank is not part of the core bonus engine. It primarily creates Extra Ball/Special opportunities; don't mistake those drops for ordinary bonus progress. [S1][S2]

## Skill shots

**None found.**

No factory-documented scored plunge Skill Shot was found. A plunge can obviously influence which top eject/bumper area the ball reaches, but calling that a formal Skill Shot would be inference.

## Secondary features

**Extra Ball:** spelling **WILD** lights the inner-right Extra Ball lane. Extra Ball itself is operator-disableable; tournament treatment must therefore be checked. [S1][S2]

**Special:** completing **WILD FYRE** lights the inner-left Special lane, and the top-eject sequence provides another Special route. The factory software allows Special awards to be adjusted. Bob's tournament guide specifically notes configurations where a Special is worth **100K**; under such a setup these otherwise-secondary features become enormous scoring objectives. [S1][S2]

No stock ball save, kickback, mystery, video mode or player-controlled rescue was found. The aftermarket Reignyted conversion adds such features; do not confuse it with original software. [S6]

## What to watch

* **Bonus = 2K / 8K / 14K / 20K:** spinner should be lit for **1K/spin**; a player suddenly changing to spinner shots is responding to that lamp. [S1]
* **Double Bonus lit:** Collect Bonus is now worth up to 40K, and on the final ball the 2X/5X alternation becomes possible. [S2]
* **2X versus 5X flashing on final ball:** every harmless switch may change the eventual bonus by **60K** at max base bonus. [S2]
* **WILD / WILD FYRE progress:** largely irrelevant unless Extra Ball or Special has tournament value; then it can immediately change shot selection. [S2]

## Match strategy

**Playing ahead:** favor long-ball **bonus build → Collect Bonus** cycles. Avoid forcing WILD FYRE or drop targets after 2X is established unless Special has meaningful point value. When the spinner happens to be 1K, take the temporary efficiency rather than blindly advancing bonus. **Observed strategy.** [S2]

**Playing behind:** maximize 20K bonus, secure 2X, and exploit every 1K-spinner window. On the final ball, manipulate the stock **5X alternation** aggressively; if 5X is showing and its bonus is enough to pass the opponent, even a deliberate drain can be mathematically superior to another switch hit. If Special = 100K, pivot hard toward Special qualification. [S2]

**Key recurring decision:** **collect now or continue building?** A collect safely banks the accumulated value but resets base bonus to 2K; continuing may reach 20K or another lucrative spinner window, at the cost of exposing the ball to Wild Fyre's chaotic upper feeds.

## Danger zones

* **Central return-lane dividers:** balls can ricochet from them directly into an outlane or center drain. [S2]
* **Upper 3-bank:** balls dropping freely from this area can arrive at the flippers with little warning. [S2]
* **Right-side returns:** specifically identified by competitive documentation as a common uncontrolled feed. [S2]
* **Drop-target rebounds:** direct attacks can immediately turn into center-drain danger. [S2]
* **Collect-eject kickout:** strategically mandatory, but exact control after the eject is restoration/setup-dependent. **Strategic inference** supported by owner reports describing the upper holes as requiring nudging. [S5]

## Trivia

* Wild Fyre was designed by **Harry Williams**, founder of Williams Manufacturing and later a designer for his old colleague Sam Stern's Stern Electronics. [S3][S7]
* Stern produced **2,400** examples. [S3]
* Its artwork/theme depicts **Roman-era chariot racing**; contemporary *Cash Box* specifically called out the Roman graphics at launch. [S7]
* It uses Stern's early **M-100** electronics and SB-100 electronic sound hardware. [S4]
* The modern **Wild Fyre: Reignyted** project was explicitly created to change two controversial stock behaviors: uncontrollable spinner lighting and final-ball-only alternating 5X. [S6]

### Uncertainties and conflicts

* **Stock versus Reignyted is critical.** Reignyted adds a Skill Shot, ball save, controllable spinner qualification and earnable 5X on ordinary balls; almost every strategic statement above assumes the **original Stern rules**. [S6]
* **Production ROM revision:** no reliable authoritative revision identifier was found; current game archives simply list software as unknown. [S3]
* **Top-eject Special sequence:** the factory manual's surviving OCR is difficult to read. It clearly establishes 3K ejects, a 7K center value after completing the set and a later Special opportunity [S1]; secondary recreations describe the exact 2-1-3 sequence somewhat differently. Because this only becomes strategic when Special has value, verify on the venue game before printing detailed sequencing.
* **Special point value:** 100K is documented as a competitive/operator configuration [S2][S5], not a universal factory scoring value. Confirm locally.
* **5X behavior:** the stock manual, Bob's Guide and the Reignyted author's explanation all agree that 5X is a **final-ball-only alternation with 2X** once qualified. [S1][S2][S6]
