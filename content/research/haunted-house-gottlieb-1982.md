## Identity and versions

**Haunted House** — D. Gottlieb & Co., **June 1982**, model **669**, System 80 solid-state widebody. Design: **John Osborne**; software: **Allen Edwall**; artwork: **Terry Doerzaph and Richard Tracy**; sound: **Craig Beierwaltes**. Production is documented at **6,835 games**. [S1][S2]

The machine has **three playable levels**—attic, main floor and cellar—**eight flippers**, no multiball, and a dedicated fifth display used for bonus. [S2][S3]

Factory manual reviewed: **Game PROM 669/1**. No later scoring revision was verified. [S1]

## Thirty-second game plan

1. **Get upstairs or downstairs and stay there.** Competitive guidance favors farming the attic/cellar targets rather than loose main-floor play. The **cellar bank is generally the easier/high-value route**. [S3][S4]
2. Every productive transition into another level advances **Bonus X**, up to **5X**. Moving between attic and cellar efficiently can therefore build the multiplier very quickly. [S1][S3]
3. Repeated cellar/attic work lights **double scoring on other floors**; two cellar-bank completions or three attic-bank completions also turn on **double scoring for the main floor**. [S1]
4. Keep building the large end-of-ball **Monster Bonus** while exploiting whichever floor is doubled. At 5X, bonus becomes the game's primary scoring leverage. [S2][S3]
5. Alternate route: complete the numbered **1-2-3-4-5 sequence** on the main floor for 5K/10K target scoring, Extra Ball qualification and cellar Double Bonus—but it is less controlled than simply staying upstairs/downstairs. [S1]

## Core rules and persistence

**Bonus X:** changing levels advances the multiplier, except for two non-qualifying transitions: entering the cellar up-kicker directly from the main floor and rolling down the attic ramp to the main floor. Shooting *up* the attic ramp only earns the advance after hitting an attic target. The playfield lamps explicitly show **1X–5X**. [S1]

**Double scoring is floor-specific.** Eleven qualifying hits upstairs light double scoring downstairs; eleven downstairs hits light double scoring upstairs. Two cellar drop-bank completions or three attic-bank completions light double scoring on the **main floor**. Entering the attic hole turns off attic double scoring; using the cellar up-kicker turns off cellar double scoring. [S1]

**Double Bonus is separate from Double Scoring.** Two cellar-bank completions qualify the left top hole to light attic Double Bonus; three attic-bank completions qualify the right top hole for main-floor Double Bonus; completing 1-2-3-4-5 in order qualifies the center hole for cellar Double Bonus. [S1]

Drop and spot targets across the three floors add to bonus; flashing 1-5 targets add **two bonus increments**. Bonus is paid when the ball drains and multiplied by the current Bonus X. [S1][S2]

**3-ball versus 5-ball materially changes scoring:** pop bumpers are **1,000 in 3-ball but only 100 in 5-ball**; the attic hole also resets its drop bank in 5-ball play. [S1]

## Skill shots

**None found as a distinct scored skill-shot rule.**

Competitive references call the plunge choice a “skill shot”: the ball falls into one of three top-left holes—**left → cellar, center → main floor, right → attic**. Choosing attic or cellar is strategically preferable because it immediately gives access to bonus-building targets and starts working Bonus X. This is **observed terminology**, not a manufacturer-defined Skill Shot award. [S3][S4]

## Secondary features

**Extra Ball:** completing **1-2-3-4-5** or either drop-target bank lights Extra Ball at the right-side kicker. Oddly, once lit, **every main-floor switch toggles the Extra Ball lamp on/off**, so its apparent availability can change rapidly during live play. [S1]

**Novelty Mode is tournament-significant:** operator switch 20 converts playfield **Specials and Extra Balls into 50,000 points** instead of replay/extra-ball awards. Fifty thousand is large enough to materially affect strategy on Haunted House. [S1]

No ball save, kickback, action button, mystery, video mode or player-controlled rescue was found.

## What to watch

* **1X–5X lamps:** immediate read on the player's end-of-ball bonus leverage. [S1]
* **DOUBLE SCORING lamp for a floor:** target farming there is temporarily worth twice as much. [S1]
* **Ball remaining in attic/cellar:** usually deliberate; those areas build bonus while keeping the ball away from the dangerous main-floor drain geometry. [S3]
* **Trap door open:** the main-floor mini-orbit can now drop the ball into the cellar instead of continuing across the playfield. [S1][S2]

## Match strategy

**Playing ahead:** stay in the attic/cellar, especially the cellar if its drops are behaving well. Those floors build bonus and Bonus X while avoiding much of the open main-floor chaos. Once 5X is established, survival becomes increasingly valuable. **Observed strategy / strategic inference.** [S3][S4]

**Playing behind:** pursue repeated bank completions to activate **double scoring plus Double Bonus**, and attack 1-2-3-4-5 if a 50K Novelty Extra Ball/Special is available. The larger multiplier stack can change the game much faster than low-risk switch scoring. [S1]

**Key recurring decision:** **stay on a sub-playfield and farm controlled target/bonus value, or return to the main floor to qualify additional features?** Strong play generally prefers staying upstairs/downstairs until the state makes leaving worthwhile.

## Danger zones

* **Small center drain between the two right-side main-floor flippers:** a uniquely awkward drain path. [S3]
* **Main-floor target rebounds:** multiple direct standups/kicking targets can fire the ball unpredictably across the widebody. [S3]
* **Attic ramp return:** balls descending from the upper playfield can arrive fast and uncontrolled. [S3]
* **Pop-bumper exits:** documented as a major source of sudden loss of control. [S3]
* **Flipper-button transition:** main-floor flippers use the forward cabinet buttons; attic/cellar flippers use the **second/rear pair**. Forgetting to switch controls as the ball changes floors can produce an instant drain. [S4]

## Trivia

* Haunted House was the **first production pinball machine with three playable levels**, including a visible playfield beneath the main floor. [S2][S5]
* Its **eight flippers** were a record when the game appeared. [S2]
* The center **Secret Passage** is a standup that physically drops away and swallows the ball into the cellar. [S2]
* The game uses a **fifth six-digit display as a live bonus display** rather than another player score. [S2]
* Its organ soundtrack incorporates Bach's **Toccata and Fugue in D minor** and Gounod's *Funeral March of a Marionette*, familiar as the *Alfred Hitchcock Presents* theme. [S6]

### Uncertainties and conflicts

* **Release date:** IPSND/Pinside document **June 1982** [S2], while some secondary sources print later dates. June is used here because it is supported by the stronger archival databases.
* **“Skill Shot”:** current competitive references label the three-hole plunge choice a skill shot [S4], but the Gottlieb manual contains no separate Skill Shot scoring award. The final page should describe it as **plunge/level choice**, not a formal scored feature.
* **Double-scoring shorthand:** some secondary summaries say “complete the opposite drop bank” to light Double Scoring [S3]. The manufacturer rule is more precise: **11 hits** on a sub-playfield light the other sub-playfield; only the main-floor double requires two cellar-bank or three attic-bank completions. [S1]
* Physical Haunted Houses are notably maintenance-sensitive, especially their inter-level mechanisms; tournament feed notes should be verified on the actual venue machine. [S3][S6]
