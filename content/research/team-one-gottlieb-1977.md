[A## Identity and versions

* **Team One** — D. Gottlieb & Co., 1977, model **384A**; single-player electro-mechanical wedgehead with two flippers, two pop bumpers and two five-bank drop-target banks. Design: **Jeff Brenner**; art: **Gordon Morison**. [S1][S3][S4] 
* It is the **Add-A-Ball sibling of Abra Ca Dabra (1975)**, with substantially different scoring/progression: Team One adds six numbered objectives and explicit WOW/add-a-ball rules. [S1]
* Production is disputed: the current International Pinball Serial Number Database derives a run of **666** machines; Pinside lists **650**. [S3][S4] ([Pinball Serial Number Database][1])
* Team One can be wired for either **Add-A-Ball** or **Novelty** play. In Novelty, each WOW becomes **50,000 points** instead of an added ball; this is the configuration specifically recommended by the main tournament rules source. [S1][S2] ([Kineticist][2])
* No multiball, software, conventional end-of-ball bonus or playfield multiplier.

## Thirty-second game plan

1. **In tournament/Novelty setup, finish numbers 1–6 first.** That scores **50K** and permanently raises the drop-target values from 500/5K to **5K/50K**. [S1]
2. After that, **shoot the currently lit drop target**—the soccer-ball lamp above it marks the lucrative target. It is now worth **50K per hit**. [S1]
3. Complete a drop bank, then prefer the **center standup** to reset it: in Novelty it cashes **50K per completed bank**; both banks down makes that a **100K shot**. [S2]
4. Numbers and completed banks create the setup-versus-cash-out decision: sometimes finish an unlit target just to complete/reset a bank; otherwise wait for the lit 50K target. **Observed strategy.** [S1]
5. If the game is left in stock **Add-A-Ball mode**, strategy changes dramatically: the 1–6 sequence and completed drops instead lead to WOW/extra-ball opportunities, making survival more important than point farming. [S2][S5]

## Core rules and persistence

* Numbers **1–4** are upper lanes; **5 and 6** are lower lanes near the flippers. Additional 5/6 pad targets are exposed behind the upper drop targets, providing another way to finish those numbers. [S1] ([Kineticist][2])
* **Novelty:** completing 1–6 scores **50K** and raises target scoring by 10×: unlit drops become **5K**, lit drops **50K**. The lit target moves with switch activity. [S1]
* **Drop-bank resets:** completed banks do **not automatically reset between balls**. They reset via the center standup or one of the two outer return lanes. [S2] ([Pinside][3])
* In Novelty, the **center target pays 50K per completed bank** before resetting it: one bank = 50K, both = **100K**. Outer return lanes reset completed banks for only **5K per bank**, while leaving the richer center WOW available. [S2]
* This creates an easy commentator mistake: an outer return-lane reset is usually **far less valuable** than deliberately collecting the center target first.
* **Novelty 1–6 progress carries across balls** according to current schematic/owner analysis. In Add-A-Ball mode, recent technical discussion says the numbered sequence likely resets each ball; that point remains less firmly verified. [S2]
* A selectable adjustment may cause **2 or 3 to spot both 2 and 3**, making those lanes especially valuable. [S1]

## Skill shots

* **Missing-number plunge:** plunge whichever of upper **1–4** is still needed. On ball one, competitive guidance recommends something other than 4 because 4 can be obtained later with a soft plunge. [S1]
* On some copies, plunging 1 or 4 can feed **straight down the middle**; if so, favor 2 or 3—particularly when the 2/3 spotting adjustment is active. [S1]
* No separate super/secret skill shot found.

## Secondary features

* **WOW / Add-A-Ball:** stock Team One is explicitly designed as an Add-A-Ball machine. Finishing 1–6 can light drop targets for a WOW; completing the required drop-bank progression can light the center target for another WOW. [S2][S5]
* **Novelty conversion:** backbox connectors change WOWs into **50K point awards**. This is not an event-rule approximation; it is a native machine configuration. [S2] ([Pinside][3])
* **3- or 5-ball play:** both are supported. Competitive owners often use 3-ball Novelty to prevent extremely long Add-A-Ball games. [S2][S6]
* No ball saver, kickback, mystery, video mode or player-controlled special device.

## What to watch

* **Six numbered lamps:** once all six are complete in Novelty, ordinary-looking drop shots become dramatically more valuable.
* **Soccer-ball lamp over a target:** that's the moving lit target—5K initially, **50K after 1–6**.
* **One or both banks completely down:** the center target has become the preferred reset/cash-out; both banks means a potential **100K** collect in Novelty.
* **WOW/backglass ball indicators:** if the game is in Add-A-Ball rather than Novelty, the entire scoring strategy has changed.

## Match strategy

**Playing ahead:** Complete 1–6, then take **lit 50K drops** and safe center-reset cash-outs. Avoid knocking down random unlit targets unless they finish a bank or improve access to a missing number. **Observed strategy.** [S1]

**Playing behind:** Finish both banks and preserve them for the **100K center collect**, then exploit the raised **50K lit-target value** repeatedly. In Add-A-Ball configuration, chase WOWs aggressively instead; extra balls can dwarf ordinary scoring.

**Key recurring decision:** **Shoot the valuable lit target, or finish an unlit target to complete/reset the bank?** Good players sometimes sacrifice immediate target value to reopen a bank and create more future 50K opportunities.

## Danger zones

* **1/4 plunge feeds:** documented on some Team Ones as occasional straight-down-the-middle returns. [S1]
* **Drop-bank rebounds:** the dominant scoring route requires precision hits into two banks; misses and hard rebounds are the game's primary tournament risk. [S1]
* **Lower pop bumpers:** unusually positioned in the lower playfield rather than conventional sling-only geometry, producing unpredictable lateral/center returns. [S4][S5]
* **Center standup:** strategically necessary for the premium bank reset, but a direct center target can return immediately toward the flipper gap.
* **Wide flipper gap / posts:** owner and gameplay documentation consistently characterize Team One as unforgiving despite the simple rules. [S5]

## Trivia

* Team One is the soccer-themed **Add-A-Ball evolution of *Abra Ca Dabra***, adding numbers 5 and 6 to the earlier game's four-number structure. [S1]
* It was one of the relatively few Gottlieb playfields credited to **Jeff Brenner**. [S1][S4]
* An export sibling, **Kicker**, was produced in the same period with different artwork/configuration. [S1]
* The moving high-value target is indicated unusually: a **soccer-ball lamp above the physical drop**, rather than a conventional insert immediately in front of it. [S1][S6]
* Team One is a very late Gottlieb single-player EM wedgehead, built shortly before solid-state games became dominant.

### Uncertainties and conflicts

* **Production:** IPSND's serial-number project gives **666** [S3]; Pinside reports **650** [S4]. Use “~650” if the final page needs a production number.
* **Release date:** IPSND/Kineticist place it in **February 1977**, while Pinside gives **March 2, 1977**. “1977” is safest. [S1][S3][S4]
* **1–6 persistence:** Novelty carryover is supported by recent schematic discussion [S2]. The statement that Add-A-Ball resets the sequence each ball is qualified by its source as a belief rather than a confirmed schematic conclusion; verify on the venue game.
* **Add-A-Ball WOW logic differs materially from Novelty:** in Novelty one completed bank can generate a 50K center WOW and both banks generate 100K; schematic analysis says stock Add-A-Ball requires **both banks down** for the center WOW/reset. [S2]
* No factory operations manual was located; contemporary owners note Gottlieb's Add-A-Ball games generally did not ship with the normal rule manuals, so the detailed rules here rely primarily on schematic-based technical analysis and Bob's competitive guide. [S1][S2][S6]
