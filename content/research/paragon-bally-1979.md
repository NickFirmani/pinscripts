## Identity and versions

* **Paragon** — Bally Manufacturing, **June 1979**, model **1167-E**, Bally **AS-2518-35** solid-state platform. Design by **Greg Kmiec**, art by **Paul Faris**. Production: **9,120 units**. [S1][S4]
* Bally's **first production widebody** and the first production pinball game generally credited with **in-line drop targets**, the four-target Valley of Demons. [S4][S5]
* Standard domestic game has **four flippers**, including the unusual stacked/scissor right pair and upper-left mini-flipper. Some European export games used a three-flipper layout. No multiball. [S4]
* No meaningful production ROM/version tree was found. The strategically important differences are operator settings: 3/5 balls, Extra Ball/Special treatment, PARAGON-letter behavior, Valley bonus advances, Waterfall values, and physical outlane posts. [S1]

## Thirty-second game plan

1. **Observed strategy:** attack the **Valley of Demons** in-line drops. Target 3 gives **2× bonus**, target 4 gives **3×**, and the Treasure Chamber behind them gives **5×** on its first visit that ball. [S1][S2]
2. Build base bonus aggressively to **20K**. Reaching **20K, 30K, or 40K** locks that Super Bonus floor in for every remaining ball. This early persistent equity is the main tournament objective. [S1][S3]
3. Maximum end-of-ball bonus is **49K × 5 = 245K**, a game-changing score on Paragon. Do not tilt with a large bonus. [S1][S2]
4. If the spinner/feed is unusually good, use the spinner to build bonus instead; otherwise Valley is preferred because it also advances multiplier and usually offers more controllable returns. [S2][S3]
5. Alternate scoring: build **Golden Cliffs** to its persistent **20K per visit**, or finish PARAGON for **25K + Special** when the letter settings make that practical. [S1][S3]

## Core rules and persistence

* Base bonus is **1K–49K**. The spinner, several targets/saucers, and Waterfall advance it. [S1]
* Super Bonus floors at **20K / 30K / 40K persist between balls** for that player. This is easy to underestimate: hitting 20K on ball 1 materially improves every later-ball bonus. [S1]
* Valley of Demons: targets 1–2 advance points/bonus; target 3 adds **2×**, target 4 adds **3×**; first Treasure Chamber collect gives **5×**. [S1]
* A second Treasure Chamber visit on the same ball awards **Same Player Shoots Again / Extra Ball** and resets the Valley; completing the four drops again can then light a Special there. [S1]
* Valley targets can be configured for **1 or 2 bonus advances each**, a major difficulty difference. They can also either spot PARAGON letters or not. [S1]
* **Golden Cliffs:** begins at 2K, rises 2K per visit to 20K, and **its value persists ball-to-ball**. At 20K it also advances bonus. [S1]
* **PARAGON letters persist**. The right saucer spots the lit letter; letters may advance sequentially or scan continuously. Completing the word lights the saucer for **25K + Special**. [S1]
* Waterfall **drop-bank value persists**, but the Waterfall lane's current value resets each ball. [S1]

## Skill shots

**Saucer plunge choice — observed skill-shot strategy.** [S2]

* If PARAGON letters are **sequential**, plunge toward the **right Paragon saucer** to spot a useful letter and advance bonus.
* If the letters are **scanning**, competitive guidance prefers **Golden Cliffs**, whose 2K→20K value is deterministic and persists for the game.
* This is configuration-dependent rather than a dedicated coded “skill shot” award. [S2]

No separate super or secret skill shot found.

## Secondary features

* **Beast's Lair Save-A-Ball:** the lower-left area replaces a conventional left outlane. Bally describes an escape ramp intended to return the ball to the flippers, but in practice the Lair is notoriously difficult to survive and requires nudging/mini-flipper intervention. It is passive, not a timed ball save. [S1][S3]
* **Extra Ball:** second Treasure Chamber visit during one ball. Original switch settings can award the ball or substitute **25K** in novelty mode. [S1]
* **Specials:** PARAGON, Waterfall, right drop progression and Treasure Chamber can award Specials. Stock operator configuration can turn these into replay, Extra Ball, or **50K**. [S1]
* No video mode, mystery award, lane change, action button, or conventional kickback.

## What to watch

1. **20K / 30K / 40K Super Bonus lamp:** that value is now protected for later balls.
2. **Valley drops:** three down = 2×; all four = 3×; a clear path to Treasure Chamber means 5× is available.
3. **Golden Cliffs value:** once it reaches 20K, every future visit is a substantial repeatable score.
4. **PARAGON letters:** if nearly complete—especially on sequential settings—the right saucer suddenly becomes worth pursuing.

## Match strategy

**Playing ahead:** secure the earliest Super Bonus floor possible, then favor Valley shots and controllable returns. Avoid deliberately shooting the center Advance Bonus standup or Waterfall just for marginal value. **Observed strategy.** [S2][S3]

**Playing behind:** pursue full **5×** and push toward 40K–49K base bonus. A 245K end-of-ball count can reverse almost any normal Paragon game. A matured 20K Golden Cliffs or point-converted Special also provides useful immediate comeback scoring.

**Key recurring decision:** **Valley versus spinner.** Valley is slower but builds multiplier and can be relatively controlled; a powerful spinner can build Super Bonus faster but throws the ball into unpredictable bumpers. The correct choice is highly copy-dependent. [S2]

## Danger zones

1. **Beast's Lair:** lower-left balls can become trapped in bumper/rubber chaos and drain despite the escape passage. [S3]
2. **Right scissor flippers:** with both held up, the ball can drain between them. Keep them down unless needed. [S3]
3. **Waterfall exit:** one trip may reach a flipper cleanly; the next can hit a sling or go straight down the middle. [S2][S3]
4. **Valley/Treasure rejection:** hard shots can brick off the in-line drops or the Treasure Chamber blocker and return extremely fast. [S3]
5. **Top-right bumper → rubber:** documented common ricochet directly toward the center drain. [S2]

## Trivia

* Paragon was Bally's first production **“Super Size” widebody**, advertised as **6½ inches wider** than a conventional game. [S5]
* The **Valley of Demons** introduced the in-line drop-target mechanism to production pinball. [S4][S5]
* Contemporary advertising specifically promoted the **Beast's Lair Save-A-Ball Ramp** as a signature feature. [S5]
* Paul Faris reportedly depicted **himself and his wife** in the fantasy artwork, something he also did on other Bally art. [S4]
* Golden Cliffs plays an increasingly long musical phrase as its value rises; archival museum documentation identifies the tune as derived from **“Funky Worm.”** [S3][S4]

### Uncertainties and conflicts

* Secondary strategy sources sometimes describe completing PARAGON as **50K + Special**; Bally's primary manual says the lit Paragon saucer awards **25K + Special**. [S1][S3] Use the manufacturer value. In novelty configuration, the Special itself can be worth another 50K.
* The exact value/utility of Valley shots depends heavily on **switch #30**: 1 bonus advance per target versus 2. [S1]
* PARAGON strategy changes substantially depending on whether its letters are **sequential or scanning**, and whether Valley targets spot letters. [S1]
* Waterfall and both saucer feeds, Valley rejections, Beast's Lair survival and right-side scissor drains are extremely **copy-dependent**.
* Standard Paragon is four-flipper; archival sources document rarer European export versions lacking one upper flipper. [S4]
