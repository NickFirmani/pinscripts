## Identity and versions

* **TRON: Legacy (Pro)** — Stern Pinball, **2011**, Stern **S.A.M.**, DMD, three flippers, two ramps, spinning identity disc and fixed Recognizer three-bank. Design: **John Borg**; software: **Lonnie D. Ropp and Lyman Sheats**; art: **John Youssi**; sound/music: **David Thiel**. [S1][S2]  ([Stern Pinball][1])
* **Latest verified Pro code: V1.74, February 11, 2013.** Among other changes, 1.74 substantially reworked Disc Multiball and added ramp-based Extra Ball qualification. [S2] ([Pinside][2])
* Three core multiballs: **Light Cycle, Quorra, Disc**. **Light Cycle + Quorra can stack; Disc cannot stack with or advance alongside them.** [S3] ([Kineticist][3])
* Pro versus LE: the Pro has **standup TRON targets, fixed Recognizer and no End of Line/“Daft Punk” Multiball**. LE adds drop targets, moving Recognizer, fiber-optic lighting and the exclusive End of Line Multiball rule. [S3][S5] ([Kineticist][4])

## Thirty-second game plan

1. **Live on End of Line.** After a side-ramp shot, immediately one-time the ball into Flynn’s Arcade scoop. The jackpot starts at **500K**, grows with combos, and does **not reset when collected**. This is widely identified as the game's dominant scoring shot. **Observed strategy.** [S3] ([Kineticist][4])
2. Prepare **Light Cycle + Quorra**, then stack them. Quorra gives two Recognizer **add-a-balls**, making the combined multiball far more durable. [S3][S4] ([Kineticist][3])
3. During any multiball, keep taking End of Line opportunities rather than blindly chasing small jackpots. [S3]
4. Opportunistically start **Double Scoring** from the TRON bank before a major collect; it doubles essentially everything, including End of Line and multiball scoring. [S3] ([Kineticist][4])
5. Alternate comeback path: **Disc Multiball** is harder and more dangerous to qualify but can be lucrative and has a restart if you fail to reach its Super Jackpot. [S2][S3]

## Core rules and persistence

* **End of Line Jackpot:** make a qualifying combo ending at the **side ramp**, then quickly shoot the scoop. Starts 500K, increases as combos accumulate, and stays at its built value after collection. [S3] ([Kineticist][4])
* **Light Cycle MB:** hit flashing Light Cycle shots, then start at scoop. Two-ball; jackpots at major shots, with multi-shot combos producing Supers. It can be advanced and stacked with Quorra. [S3]
* **Quorra MB:** repeated Quorra/left-spinner shots qualify it at the scoop. Two-ball; Quorra scores recurring jackpots, Gem scores Super. The Recognizer provides **two add-a-balls**. [S3][S4] ([Kineticist][3])
* **Disc MB:** repeatedly hit the dangerous center three-bank to expose the spinning disc, then work the disc. In V1.74, major blue-disc jackpots stay lit; spinning-disc hits advance the stages. If the multiball ends before a Super Jackpot, a temporary **Disc restart** becomes available. [S2][S3] ([Pinside][2])
* The vertical **feature ladder** tracks nine objectives. **Flashing = played; solid = completed.** All flashing qualifies **Sea of Simulation**; all solid qualifies **Portal Multiball**. [S3] ([Kineticist][4])
* Sea of Simulation is a **single-ball, untimed** second-chance mode to make unfinished flashing features solid. Portal is a four-ball wizard multiball. [S3]
* End-of-ball bonus can become very large: flashing/solid ladder features contribute, and every **Gem** shot adds **+1 Bonus X**. Avoid unnecessary tilts deep into a game. [S3] ([Kineticist][4])

## Skill shots

* **Flynn’s Arcade skill shot:** soft plunge so the ball rebounds from the shooter-lane gate into the scoop. **500K**, increasing by 50K per success according to current competitive documentation. Preferred tournament option. [S3][S6] ([Kineticist][4])
* **Left-ramp alternate:** super-soft plunge, then immediately shoot the left ramp; **200K** and Light Cycle progress. [S6] ([JLP's Pinball Cards][5])
* **Side-ramp alternate:** hold the left flipper on a full plunge to send the ball around to the upper flipper, then shoot the side ramp; **300K** plus Light Cycle progress. Hardest option. [S3][S6]

## Secondary features

* **Double Scoring / Super Bumpers / Super Spinners:** complete the TRON bank while the desired award is flashing. Double Scoring is the important one. Rolling through the nearby **Zen lane** lets the next TRON hit instantly start the flashing feature. [S3] ([Kineticist][4])
* **Flynn’s Arcade Mystery:** relit via the right orbit. Awards are randomized normally; Competition Mode standardizes random/global elements on this S.A.M.-era platform. [S7]
* **Extra Ball:** V1.74 added qualification from ramp counts; tournament references list the normal conversion as **3M points** when EBs are not played. [S2][S8] ([Pinside][2])
* No video mode, kickback, Magna-Save or action button. [S1][S2]

## What to watch

* **Side ramp → scoop attempt:** that's almost certainly an **End of Line** cash-out, even though the game gives it surprisingly little fanfare.
* **Light Cycle + Quorra both flashing at scoop:** a major stack is ready; Quorra's add-a-balls can turn two fragile 2-ball MBs into a long scoring opportunity.
* **TRON bank showing Double Scoring:** expect the player to prioritize it before a large End of Line, Disc Super or wizard-mode collect.
* **Feature ladder:** flashing means merely played; solid means completed. All flashing → Sea of Simulation; all solid → Portal.

## Match strategy

**Playing ahead:** Favor repeatable **End of Line** scoring and Light Cycle/Quorra rather than pounding the center bank for Disc. Take Quorra add-a-balls early and protect a large accumulated EOB bonus. **Observed strategy.** [S3]

**Playing behind:** Stack Light Cycle + Quorra with Double Scoring, or commit to Disc Multiball and its Super/restart path. Deep feature progress also creates the possibility of a huge Sea of Simulation payout; entering it with everything already solid awards **50M**, doubled to 100M under Double Scoring, before Portal. [S3] ([Kineticist][4])

**Key recurring decision:** **Keep shooting the comparatively safe combo/End-of-Line loop, or abandon it for ladder completion and Disc/Portal progress?** The former is usually the short-game tournament score; the latter has the bigger deep-game ceiling.

## Danger zones

* **Recognizer three-bank:** central standups can send hard rebounds immediately toward the drain; Disc is intentionally a higher-risk route. [S3]
* **Gem:** difficult upper-flipper shot; a miss frequently gives away valuable upper-playfield control.
* **High-speed ramp returns:** TRON is exceptionally fast; missed one-timers often turn directly into uncontrolled lower-playfield action. [S3]
* **Right orbit:** sends the ball through the pops rather than slowing it down; poor bailout when control is already marginal. [S3]
* **Scoop kickout/reject:** central to MB starts and End of Line, so a bad feed can punish the game's best shot.

## Trivia

* TRON was designed by **John Borg**, with art by **John Youssi** and sound/music programming by **David Thiel**. [S1] ([Stern Pinball][1])
* Stern produced only **400 LE machines**; its major rule difference is the exclusive End of Line/Daft Punk Multiball. [S5] ([Pinside][6])
* The Pro's Recognizer is **fixed** and hides a standup bank; the LE replaces those with drop targets and moves the Recognizer. [S5]
* The game deliberately uses the film's **Daft Punk score**, one of its most celebrated presentation elements.
* Stern's final 1.74 code arrived nearly two years after release and materially revised Disc Multiball. [S2]

### Uncertainties and conflicts

* **“Pro” naming:** Stern's contemporary materials mostly called it standard TRON: Legacy; modern databases conventionally label the non-LE machine **Pro**. [S1][S5]
* **Skill-shot exact values:** current competitive sources agree on the three options and rank scoop highest; the most useful current card gives **500K / 200K / 300K** [S6]. Earlier community rules summaries occasionally omit or vary the lesser values.
* **Extra-ball tournament conversion:** the maintained Points-for-Extra-Ball table lists **3M** [S8], but event directors may instead use plunge-and-drain or a complete Novelty/Competition install.
* Physical difficulty varies dramatically. Recent tournament setup documentation shows TRON commonly configured with **EB off, Competition Mode on**, but that is a venue example, not a Stern universal setting. [S9] ([Lower Playfield][7])
