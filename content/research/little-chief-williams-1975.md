## Identity and versions

* **Little Chief**, Williams, **1975**; four-player electro-mechanical score-reel game, model 458. Designed/mechanically engineered by **Steve Kordek**, with artwork by **Christian Marche**. Approximately **6,300** were produced. [S1][S2] ([Kineticist][1])
* **Rules basis: `fixed`.** This is an electromechanical game with no gameplay ROM or downloadable software revision. Its important variation comes from physical operator adjustments, especially the **Advance Bonus** and **Eject Hole** difficulty plugs. [S2][S3] 
* No multiball. The game is fundamentally a repeated **build bonus → double bonus → collect bonus without draining** loop. [S3][S4] 

Research scope follows the supplied commentator-reference specification. 

## Thirty-second game plan

1. **Observed strategy:** shoot **up top**, especially from the right flipper, to collect **A and B** and build the base bonus toward its **10,000 maximum**. [S4][S5] ([Silverball Rules][2])
2. Completing A+B **opens the right-side collect gate** and lights the top-center rollover. Make that lit center lane—or the riskier center saucer—to turn on **Double Bonus**. [S3][S4] 
3. Once bonus is large and doubled, shoot the **middle-right open gate** from the left flipper. It awards the accumulated bonus **without ending the ball**, then resets bonus/A/B so the whole cycle can start again. [S3][S4] 
4. **Central decision:** cash a good doubled bonus now, or keep building toward the 10K base maximum and risk draining before collection.
5. **Alternate:** repeated center-saucer/lit-center-lane completions advance the Eject Hole unit toward Extra Ball/Special opportunities, but tournament strategy sources generally regard this as secondary to the bonus loop. [S3][S5] 

## Core rules and persistence

Base bonus starts at **1,000** and builds to **10,000** through top lanes, rollovers and bonus-advance targets. [S4][S5] ([Silverball Rules][2])

**A + B** does two crucial things: it **opens the collect gate** and lights the **top-center rollover**. Either the center saucer or that lit rollover turns on Double Bonus and advances the Eject Hole unit. [S3] 

The open right-side gate is the game's defining cash-out: sending the ball through it scores the accumulated bonus, returns the ball to the shooter lane, resets bonus to 1,000, resets A/B and removes Double. It is therefore possible to collect multiple full bonuses on one ball. [S3][S4] 

**No meaningful state carries between balls.** A/B, bonus, Double and Eject-Hole progress reset; long balls are disproportionately valuable because the player can cycle the bonus repeatedly before draining. [S6] ([MAACA][3])

The Eject Hole progression is adjustment-sensitive: factory settings permit Special/Extra-Ball qualification at **3/4, 4/5, or 5/6 advances** for liberal/medium/conservative settings. [S3] 

## Skill shots

**A-lane Skill Shot:** plunge for the **A top lane**.

* A and B both contribute toward opening the collect gate.
* Under the factory **conservative Advance Bonus adjustment**, A specifically lights the **upper-right bonus-advance target**, while B lights the lower-left target.
* **Observed strategy:** A is preferred because the upper-right target is much safer and more likely to be hit incidentally. [S3][S4] 
* Under the **liberal** adjustment, both bonus-advance targets are always lit, reducing A's special advantage. [S3] 

No super/alternate/secret skill shot was found.

## Secondary features

* **Extra Ball / Special:** advancing the center Eject Hole unit eventually lights the two bottom Special lanes alternately for **Extra Ball**. A later threshold lights the extra Special lane for a **credit, adjustable to Extra Ball**. Exact thresholds are operator-adjustable. [S3] 
* In a tournament where Extra Balls/credits are suppressed, I found **no factory point-conversion rule**; do not assume those lamps score substitute points.
* No ball save, player action control, mystery, video mode, kickback or other modern utility feature applies.

## What to watch

1. **A and B both made:** the right-side gate opens and the top-center rollover lights; the scoring loop is set up. [S3]
2. **DOUBLE lit:** the next major decision is whether the accumulated bonus is large enough to cash through the gate.
3. **Open middle-right gate:** this is not an outlane—the player is trying to deliberately send the ball through it to collect bonus and relaunch. [S3]
4. **Lit alternating bottom Special lane:** the Eject Hole advance has reached its Extra-Ball threshold; strategically relevant only if tournament settings honor it. [S3]

## Match strategy

**Playing ahead:** **Strategic inference:** cash doubled bonus somewhat earlier rather than insisting on a perfect 10K base. The gate gives guaranteed large value while retaining the same ball, whereas an unnecessary saucer or transfer risks losing the entire uncollected opportunity.

**Playing behind:** maximize the cycle—push base bonus toward **10K**, double it, collect **20K**, and immediately rebuild. If Extra Balls are enabled, deliberately advancing the Eject Hole becomes more attractive. [S3][S4] 

**Key recurring decision:** **cash the doubled bonus now or keep building it?** That is essentially Little Chief's tournament game.

## Danger zones

* **Center saucer miss:** the posts around the saucer can send the ball outside toward an outlane or through the center. [S4] ([Silverball Rules][2])
* **Ball descending beside the saucer:** if it gets outside either lower post, the next contact can be a direct side drain or sling-to-outlane. [S4]
* **Saucer kickout:** useful, but the return feed requires immediate control and varies by machine. [S4]
* **Left-to-right transfer:** strategy guides explicitly flag transfers as risky; use them only when the desired shot genuinely requires the left flipper. [S4]
* **Uncollected bonus:** the largest strategic danger is simply draining with a large doubled bonus still sitting on the inserts instead of having cashed it.

## Trivia

* *Little Chief* was released in **September 1975** and Williams produced roughly **6,300** examples. [S1][S2] ([Kineticist][1])
* The game was designed by prolific Williams designer **Steve Kordek**, with artwork by **Christian Marche**. [S1]
* Its unusual mid-playfield **return-to-shooter-lane gate** effectively lets the player “collect bonus without draining,” the mechanism around which the entire strategy is built. [S2][S3] ([Pinside][4])
* Williams allowed the operator to choose **three different Eject Hole difficulty thresholds** with a physical plug under the playfield. [S3]
* The title arrived almost exactly a decade after Williams' **Big Chief** (1965), but *Little Chief* is a separate design rather than a remake. [S2] ([Pinside][4])
