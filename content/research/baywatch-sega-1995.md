## Identity and versions

**Baywatch** — Sega Pinball, 1995. Design: **Joe Balcer and Joe Kaminkow**; engineering/mechanics: **John Borg**; software credits include John Carpenter, Lonnie D. Ropp, Neil Falconer and Orin Day; art by Jeff Busch and Markus Rothkranz; music/sound by Brian Schmidt. Data East/Sega Version 3b hardware with an unusually large 192×64 DMD and four flippers. [S2]

* Single production model; no gameplay-equivalent edition split is needed.
* **Rules basis: `rom` — official CPU ROM v4.00 (`BAYCPUA.400`).** [S2][S3]
* A materially different **unofficial v4.02**, released 2025-12-16 by Chad Hendrickson, patches v4.00 and changes skill-shot values, mode scoring, lock advancement, bonus calculation, tournament usability and several bugs. It should not be silently treated as factory rules. [S3]
* Major multiballs: the central **5-ball Multiball** and **Tourist Season** 2-ball multiball. **Earthquake** is the timed all-balls wizard mode. [S1]

## Thirty-second game plan

1. **Prioritize the main Multiball.** Clear the center 3-bank to light locks at the left-ramp trap door; three locks start 5-ball play. **Observed strategy:** competitive references identify this as Baywatch’s primary repeatable scoring engine. [S1][S4][S5]
2. During Multiball, collect the **five major-shot jackpots**. A ball held in the Upper Ball Launch temporarily makes regular jackpots **2x**. [S1]
3. Five jackpots light the **Super Jackpot at the Shark Scoop**, then the **Double Super at the Side Ramp**; completing both exposes the match-changing **500M Baywatch Jackpot**. [S1]
4. In single-ball play, keep advancing all five GUARD/mode shots. **CPR** is especially lucrative; **Copter Rescue and Shark Cove** are commonly timed out rather than deliberately attacked because of their dangerous standups. **Observed strategy.** [S4][S5]
5. Alternate route: **Tourist Season** offers relatively straightforward 2-ball scoring; longer play can reach **Earthquake**, where every major shot recollects built mode value. [S1][S4]

## Core rules and persistence

The five central “mode shots” are **Left Ramp, Center Ramp, Upper VUK, Right Ramp and Spinner Lane**. Repeated hits both build horizontal GUARD progression and ultimately start a shot-specific vertical rescue mode. Progress is cumulative through the game. [S1]

There is a source conflict on the exact count: Sega’s manual says a vertical mode starts on the **fourth** hit to a shot, while the current competitive card says the **third** hit starts it. [S1][S4] Do not call an exact count on-air unless the installed ROM has been verified.

**Main Multiball:** complete the 3-bank to light left-ramp locks; lock three balls. Five balls launch with ball save. Regular jackpot value rises with balls in play, previous jackpots and drop-target hits. Holding a ball in the Upper Ball Launch for roughly 10 seconds doubles regular jackpots. [S1]

After all five regular jackpots, the **Shark Scoop Super Jackpot = 50M × balls in play**; the Side Ramp then offers a Double Super worth twice that. Completing both starts a roving **500M Baywatch Jackpot** on the five major shots. If multiball ends, a short “last chance” at the Baywatch Jackpot can persist. [S1]

**SPF bonus:** completing the top lanes advances the end-of-ball multiplier to **2x/4x/6x/8x/10x** on the documented factory rules. It resets every ball. Mode lamps and Sharks contribute cumulatively to bonus; standup-target bonus is ball-specific. [S1]

**Earthquake:** complete the five vertical modes plus the two horizontal modes—or reach the final Shark-feature tier—to light it at the Upper VUK. For 30 seconds, balls continually auto-launch, major shots recollect accumulated mode values, and the game supplies enhanced pops/shark/kickback features. When time expires, the flippers shut off and the balls drain. [S1]

Factory v4.00 also contains generous automatic lock advancement across balls; v4.02 deliberately reduces this so players are no longer effectively guaranteed all locks by Ball 2. [S3]

## Skill shots

* **SPF / Top-Lane Skill Shot:** select it with the flippers, then let the auto-launch feed the flashing SPF lane. Factory base value **10M**, increasing 10M after each successful skill shot. Safest tournament choice and also advances SPF bonus. [S1]
* **Shark Scoop Skill Shot:** select the Shark option and use the upper-right shark-fin sequence to reach the scoop. Factory base **20M**. Harder and more volatile. [S1]
* **Side Ramp Skill Shot:** select the ramp and make the Side Ramp with the upper-left flipper. Factory base **50M**, the hardest and largest immediate award. [S1]

Making the same skill shot on three balls **or** collecting all three varieties gives an additional **50M** on factory rules. [S1]

**v4.02 sensitivity:** Shark rises to 40M and the Variety Award rises to **150M**, materially improving the risk/reward of taking all three different shots. [S3]

## Secondary features

* **Laser Kick:** left-outlane kickback is lit at the start of each ball and can be relit from the two standups near the Shark flipper. Shark progression can award a longer-lasting Super Laser Kick. [S1]
* **Search & Rescue:** complete the five vehicle standups, then collect at the Spinner. It is random in normal play but follows a **fixed award order in Tournament Mode**. [S1]
* **Shark Flips:** completing SHARK enables the software-controlled shark-fin flipper to automatically attempt the Shark Scoop. It is useful progression but can produce less predictable feeds than simply letting the ball fall through. [S1][S5]
* **Video modes:** factory documentation includes Waterskiing and Ironman. Ironman is qualified through Side Ramp progress; these can provide useful secondary points but are not the primary tournament plan. [S1]
* **Extra balls:** available from SPF, rescue modes and Shark progression. No verified universal point conversion exists; tournament policy must be handled separately.

## What to watch

1. **Left-ramp trap door opening:** a physical lock is lit; the player has shifted toward main Multiball.
2. **Five major jackpot arrows during Multiball:** completing the set transitions the game to the Shark Scoop Super → Side Ramp Double Super sequence.
3. **Ball held in the Upper Ball Launch:** regular Multiball jackpots are temporarily **doubled**—a clear reason to shoot another lit jackpot immediately. [S1]
4. **Surfboard/roving jackpot indication:** the **500M Baywatch Jackpot** is active; virtually any intentional major-shot choice is now about finding that roving award. [S1]

## Match strategy

**Playing ahead:**
**Strategic inference:** favor the safe SPF skill shot, take straightforward major-shot progress, start Multiball once available, and avoid deliberately shooting the vehicle or SHARK standups during Copter Rescue/Shark Cove. Time those modes out if necessary.

**Playing behind:**
Push the main Multiball all the way through **Super → Double Super → 500M Baywatch Jackpot**. Outside Multiball, CPR offers a compact high-value three-ramp sequence, while Earthquake is justified when already close to qualification. [S1][S4][S5]

**Key recurring decision:**
**Mode progress or Multiball locks?** The left ramp advances both CPR-related progression and physical locks, but taking a lock removes the ball and auto-launches another. Strong players may therefore prioritize left-ramp mode progress early before incidental drop-target completions force the game toward locks. [S5]

## Danger zones

* **Copter Rescue vehicle targets:** competitive sources specifically recommend timing this mode out; the standups produce dangerous center rebounds. [S4]
* **Shark Cove / SHARK bank:** similarly high drain risk for relatively exposed target work. [S4][S5]
* **Shark-fin flipper:** automatic/upper-flipper scoop attempts can be less controllable than simply accepting the safe inlane feed. [S5]
* **Center-ramp entrance target:** a rejected shot can come directly back between the lower flippers.
* **Physical lock replunge:** every successful lock gives up settled control and sends another auto-launched ball through the upper playfield.

## Trivia

* Baywatch uses Sega’s oversized **192×64 DMD**, substantially taller than the more common 128×32 display. [S2]
* Its **shark-shaped upper-right flipper is software controlled**, an idea comparable to Thing Flips on *The Addams Family*. [S2]
* Approximately **2,000 machines** were produced. [S2]
* Future Stern designer **John Borg** is credited for Baywatch’s engineering/mechanics. [S2]
* The machine supports up to **six players**, unusual compared with the standard four-player format. [S2]
