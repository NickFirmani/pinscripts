## Identity and versions

* **Space Time** — Bally Manufacturing, **September 1972**, model **953**; four-player electro-mechanical (EM) game. Design by **Jim Patla**, art by **Christian Marche**. Production is consistently listed as **5,000**. [S3][S4]
* Two flippers, four pop bumpers, two upper kickout holes, two side return gates, and the distinctive illuminated **3-D Bonus/Time Tunnel** beneath the lower playfield. No multiball. [S1][S2]
* **Time Zone (Bally 1973)** is the two-player production version with essentially the same rules/playfield. The rare prototype predecessor **Time Tunnel** lacks Space Time's center up-post and has fewer bumpers, so its tournament control strategy is materially different. [S2][S6]

## Thirty-second game plan

1. **Time the plunge** so the Tunnel stops on **5,000**, ideally collecting it through the top Collect Tunnel lane immediately. [S2]
2. **Observed strategy:** with a good 3K–5K Tunnel value, repeatedly shoot the **center red Collect Tunnel target**. It scores the Tunnel and raises the center post, creating the game's main control position. [S2]
3. Settle the ball against the raised post/flipper and use tiny taps or transfers to repeat the center target. This is the dominant tournament grind. [S2]
4. If Tunnel is only **1K–2K**, don't keep cashing it: use a Start Tunnel feature or upper play to restart/change the value, then wait for a better collect. [S2]
5. Opportunistically take an **open side gate**: it also collects Tunnel and returns the ball to the shooter lane for another timed plunge. [S2]

## Core rules and persistence

* The **Time Tunnel cycles 1K → 5K**. The shooter-alley stop switch freezes the cycling value; designated Start Tunnel features can restart it. Tunnel is then collected at several features including the center target and side gates. [S2][S3]
* **Center red target:** collects the lit Tunnel value and raises the post between the flippers. Two white rollover buttons above the slings lower that post again. [S2]
* The raised post is crucial strategically: it can create a cradle-like pocket between the flipper tip and post, making repeated center shots possible. **Observed strategy.** [S2]
* Each upper kickout hole opens one of the two side gates. An open gate scores and **collects the Tunnel**, then sends the ball back to the shooter lane. [S2]
* Easy commentary trap: **“Tunnel Bonus” is an in-play collect, not a modern accumulated end-of-ball bonus.** The reliable rules documentation does not describe a conventional bonus ladder or multiplier.
* No strategically meaningful ball-to-ball scoring progression was verified. The important state—Tunnel value, gate state and up-post—is transient mechanical game state rather than a deep persistent progression system.

## Skill shots

**Collect Tunnel Skill Shot**

* Time the plunge while the Tunnel lights cycle, cross the shooter-lane stop switch when **5,000** is lit, and plunge with enough force to reach the top **Collect Tunnel lane**. [S2]
* Award: the current Tunnel value, ideally **5K**.
* Bob Matthews notes a possible two-stage soft-plunge technique—first stopping the value, then plunging into the lane—but considers a well-timed near-full plunge more repeatable. **Observed strategy.** [S2]
* Visible cue: the five Tunnel-value lamps beneath the lower playfield.

No alternate, super or secret skill shot found.

## Secondary features

* **Double free-ball gates:** each upper saucer opens one side gate. “Free ball” here means the ball physically returns to the **shooter lane** rather than draining; it is not automatically an Extra Ball. [S1][S2]
* **Center up-post:** activated by the center Collect Tunnel target and lowered by either white rollover near the slings. It is a survival/control mechanism, but not a timed ball save. [S2]
* **Extra Ball:** Space Time has a dedicated target that is lit on a **mystery/mechanical stepper basis**. Technical documentation from owners identifies the match stepper as also controlling the intermittent Extra Ball lamp. Tournament treatment therefore matters. [S5]
* No video mode, player button, Magna-Save, lane change or modern mystery feature.

## What to watch

1. **Tunnel value:** 4K–5K means repeated center collecting is attractive; 1K–2K often explains why the player goes back upstairs.
2. **Center post raised:** expect controlled soft taps and repeated center-target attempts.
3. **Open side gate:** a side return can both score Tunnel and hand the player another plunge.
4. **Extra Ball target lit:** potentially meaningful only if the tournament honors or converts it.

## Match strategy

**Playing ahead:** once the Tunnel is 3K–5K and the center post is raised, stay disciplined: settle the ball and repeat the center collect rather than deliberately shooting dangerous Start Tunnel standups. **Observed strategy.** [S2]

**Playing behind:** maximize **5K Tunnel cycles**—take gate returns and other opportunities that restart/replunge the ball rather than settling for repeated 1K–2K collections. The game has no huge jackpot; comeback variance comes from stringing together many full-value Tunnel collects while surviving the center rebounds.

**Key recurring decision:** **cash the current Tunnel value or restart it?** At 3K–5K, competitive guidance says collect; at 1K–2K, accept extra risk to cycle it toward a better value. [S2]

## Danger zones

1. **Center Collect target:** the best shot is also the main drain generator; off-center hits deflect toward slings and outlanes. [S2]
2. **Start Tunnel standups:** tempting when the value is poor, but their direct rebounds are severe. [S2]
3. **White “down-post” rollovers:** accidentally lowering the center post removes the player's primary control/safety resource. [S2]
4. **Upper-playfield falls:** balls leaving the four-bumper area arrive fast and frequently enter sling/outlane action. [S2]
5. **Outlanes:** experienced-owner accounts consistently describe them as unusually punishing; gate state radically changes the danger. [S4]

## Trivia

* Bally produced **5,000 Space Times**; the two-player **Time Zone** followed in 1973 with the same essential game concept. [S3][S4]
* The earlier **Time Tunnel** prototype reportedly stopped after only about **70 test machines** following a naming/copyright conflict; Bally reworked it into Space Time. [S6]
* That redesign added the **center up-post**, which dramatically changed the competitive game by enabling controlled repeated shots. [S2][S6]
* Bally's original flyer prominently marketed the unusual **three-dimensional Bonus Tunnel** and double return gates. [S1]
* The game combines dinosaurs, space travel and futuristic imagery in Christian Marche's artwork—a deliberately broad time-travel theme rather than a licensed property. [S3]

### Uncertainties and conflicts

* **Release month:** major machine databases say **September 1972** [S3][S4], while later historical summaries sometimes describe an October release. Use **Bally 1972** on the final sheet unless the exact month matters.
* **Extra Ball qualification:** contemporary material describes the target as lighting on a “mystery” basis, and technical evidence ties it to the match stepper [S5], but I found no sufficiently authoritative sequence describing exactly which switch counts light it. Do not predict when it will light.
* No reliable primary rules transcription was accessible enough to establish detailed between-ball reset behavior. Treat Tunnel/gate/post state as live observable state rather than announcing persistence.
* Center-target rebounds, saucer kickouts and side-gate feeds are strongly copy-dependent.
