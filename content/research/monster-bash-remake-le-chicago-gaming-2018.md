## Identity and versions

**Monster Bash Remake — Limited Edition**, Chicago Gaming Company, 2018. Straight remake of Williams’ 1998 *Monster Bash*, retaining **George Gomez’s design** and the original Lyman Sheats rules/software foundation while running on modern remake electronics with colorized presentation. [S2][S4]

- Three remake trims: **Classic, Special, Limited**. Core playfield and gameplay are shared. SE/LE add XL color display, enhanced sound/RGB lighting and upgraded monster presentation; LE additionally has the sculpted topper, blue trim, cabinet mirrors, plasma disc and shaker. LE production was **1,250**. [S2][S7]
- **Rules basis: `code` — v1.0.3**, still the current downloadable Chicago Gaming build. Its documented changes are presentation/audio and a right-popper adjustment, not new scoring rules. **Exact release date: unresolved**; CGC’s current update page does not state one. [S1]
- Major multiballs: **Frankenstein Multiball, Mosh Pit Multiball, Monster Bash**, and wizard mode **Monsters of Rock**. [S4][S5]

## Thirty-second game plan

1. **Start monster modes aggressively rather than finishing them one at a time.** Starting all six lights **Monster Bash**, a lucrative four-ball multiball. [S4]
2. **Stack modes before a multiball.** Frankenstein, Mosh Pit and Monster Bash freeze active mode timers while multiball remains alive, making difficult instruments much easier to finish. [S4][S5]
3. **Collect all six instruments** by completing their monster modes to light **Monsters of Rock (MoR)**, the real scoring payoff. [S5]
4. **Observed strategy:** leave difficult modes such as Creature/Bride running into Frankenstein or Monster Bash rather than attacking them naked; “start two things instead of finishing one” is the modern tournament shorthand. [S4][S6]
5. Alternate route: if **Mosh Pit Multiball** becomes conveniently close, take it—it is lucrative—but deliberately shooting the center spinner six times is much riskier than normal wizard-mode progression. [S4]

## Core rules and persistence

There are six monster modes. **Starting** all six lights Monster Bash; **completing** each awards that monster’s instrument, and all six instruments light Monsters of Rock. Those are different milestones and easy to confuse on commentary. [S5]

Modes may overlap. During Frankenstein Multiball, Mosh Pit Multiball or Monster Bash, active monster-mode timers stop while two or more balls remain, but the mode objectives can still be completed. This stacking is the central competitive rule. [S4][S5]

**Monster Bash:** four-ball multiball. Each monster shot scores, and the center Mosh Pit lane collects a Bash Jackpot that increases with monsters hit since the previous collect, up to **7.5M**. [S5]

**Monsters of Rock:** four-ball wizard multiball after all six instruments. The first start immediately pays at least **55M** from six instruments plus the complete-set bonus, then each monster becomes a high-value shot; completing the set during the multiball awards an MoR Jackpot. Reaching MoR without previously playing Monster Bash also earns a large additional bonus. [S5]

If the final instruments are completed while **Monster Bash is running**, Monsters of Rock can replace it immediately and refresh the multiball. Conversely, starting MoR terminates remaining ordinary monster modes rather than automatically completing them. [S5]

**Monster Bombs** are banked resources. Pressing the launch button during the corresponding monster mode spots one required hit/shot; they are especially valuable for awkward late-mode requirements. [S5]

## Skill shots

- **D-I-G Skill Shot:** use the flipper buttons before/plunge to move the flashing D-I-G rollover so the launched ball passes through it.
- Award: **100K + 50K per previous successful skill shot**, plus a **Monster Bomb**. [S5]
- The points are secondary; the Bomb is the tournament value because it can later spot progress toward an instrument.
- No separate verified super/secret skill shot exists.

## Secondary features

- **Monster Bombs / launch button:** player-controlled spot awards; one distinct Bomb exists for each monster. Use it while that monster’s mode is active. [S5]
- **Extra Ball:** multiple mode/progression paths can light Extra Ball at the scoop. Tournament handling is event/configuration dependent; no universal remake point conversion was verified.
- **Scoop/Mystery:** when no higher-priority feature occupies it, the scoop can give miscellaneous awards. I found no authoritative remake-specific Competition Mode sequence, so commentators should not promise a deterministic award.
- **Ball save:** the remake has operator-adjustable start-of-ball saving; owner documentation points to separate enable/time adjustments. v1.0.3 also added an adjustment intended to reduce center drains from the right popper. [S1]
- No video mode or Magna-Save-style control materially affects tournament strategy.

## What to watch

1. **Monster portrait lit, instrument unlit:** that mode has merely been *started*—good enough for Monster Bash, not Monsters of Rock.
2. **Instrument insert lit:** the mode was completed; count these to know how close MoR is.
3. **Multiple mode inserts flashing when multiball starts:** exactly what a strong player wants—the timers now freeze while they finish those objectives.
4. **Monster Bash versus Monsters of Rock lit at the scoop:** MoR is the deeper cashout; if both conditions converge, expect the player to prioritize the instrument/wizard progression.

## Match strategy

**Playing ahead:**  
**Observed strategy:** keep accumulating mode starts and take Monster Bash once qualified rather than risking six intentional Mosh Pit spinner shots. Under multiball, finish whatever instruments are already close instead of forcing dangerous Frankenstein/Dracula hits unnecessarily. [S4]

**Playing behind:**  
Stack aggressively. Start Bride/Creature/Dracula and then bring them into Frankenstein or Monster Bash; completing the remaining instruments there can turn Monster Bash directly into **Monsters of Rock**, creating the game's largest comeback opportunity. [S5][S6]

**Key recurring decision:**  
**Finish a mode now, or start another feature first?** Good tournament play usually favors **more simultaneous modes plus multiball protection** over cleanly finishing each monster in isolation. [S4]

## Danger zones

- **Mosh Pit spinner:** the clearest strategic danger; misses on the narrow center lane can drain immediately. [S4]
- **Frankenstein:** necessary but centrally exposed; repeated direct hits can rebound straight toward the flippers.
- **Dracula standups/toy:** another forward-facing target sequence that trades control for progress.
- **Scoop/right popper:** CGC specifically added a v1.0.3 coil-time setting to mitigate center drains from this eject behavior. [S1]
- **Weak ramp shots:** both Bride ramps can reject quickly back toward the middle rather than completing their controlled return.

## Trivia

- The remake reproduces Williams’ **1998 George Gomez layout** rather than redesigning it. [S2][S4]
- Original software/rules were by **Lyman F. Sheats Jr.**, with art by **Greg Freres and Kevin O’Connor**. [S4]
- Chicago Gaming released the remake in **three trims simultaneously in 2018**; all contain the complete ruleset. [S2]
- The LE’s sculpted topper depicts **Frankenstein, Dracula and the Bride performing as a band**. [S2]
- The hidden **Lyman’s Lament** easter egg from the original is retained in the remake. [S7]
