## Identity and versions

**Cover Girl** — D. Gottlieb & Co., **July 1962**, model **190**. Single-player electro-mechanical wedgehead with two flippers, three pop bumpers, a split center standup and two ball-ending gobble holes. **Wayne Neyens** designed the game; **Roy Parker** supplied the artwork. Production is documented at **2,100 machines**. [S2][S3]

No software or edition differences apply. No multiball. Maximum displayed score is **1,999**. [S2]

## Thirty-second game plan

1. **Hit the top-center 50-point rollover.** It is unusually valuable by this game's scale and lights the center **split target**. Experienced-owner commentary specifically identifies this as the key scoring shot. [S1][S4]
2. When the split target is lit, use its **red or white half to spot matching numbered rollovers/Cover Girls**, accelerating completion of the corresponding 1–6 row. A dead-center hit may register both sides on a well-set-up game. [S1][S4]
3. **Farm the currently lit pop bumper for 10 points** when the ball is up top; the scoring light rotates between the three pops. [S1][S4]
4. Completing either red 1–6 or white 1–6 normally awards a **Special/replay**; completing all 12 lights the side holes for **1, 2 or 3 Specials**. Whether this matters in tournament play depends entirely on replay conversion. [S1][S4]
5. **High-variance alternate:** the side gobble holes are documented by experienced owners as **50/100/150-point cash-outs**, but taking one ends the ball. Behind late, 150 points can be a real match swing; ahead, avoid them. [S4]

## Core rules and persistence

**Confirmed rule:** there are six red and six white numbered rollovers, corresponding to the two rows of Cover Girls on the backglass. Completing either entire row awards a **Special**; completing all 12 lights the side holes for 1, 2 or 3 Specials. [S1]

**Confirmed rule:** the **top-center rollover lights the split target**. Once lit, hitting the red target half spots red rollover/Cover-Girl progress; hitting the white half does the same for white. [S1]

**Confirmed rule:** the three pop bumpers have a rotating lit state; a lit bumper scores **10 points**. [S1]

Ordinary numbered rollover scoring is **5 points**, corroborated by repair documentation from an actual machine. [S5]

**Important tournament distinction:** Cover Girl was built around **replay awards**, not modern point jackpots. If the event simply ignores Specials, completing rows has much less direct value than the 50-point center rollover, lit pops and point-scoring gobble holes. If Specials convert to tournament points, the optimal strategy changes materially.

**Persistence is unresolved:** I did not find sufficiently reliable documentation establishing whether partial red/white Cover-Girl progress resets after each ball or carries forward. Do not call that on stream without checking the venue game.

## Skill shots

**Top-center rollover — observed “50-point center rollover skill shot.”**

* **Shot:** make the top-center rollover.
* **Award:** **50 points** and lights the split target. [S1][S4]
* **Why choose it:** 50 is enormous relative to ordinary 5-point rollovers and 10-point lit pops, while also enabling faster number collection.
* **Plunge:** experienced-owner documentation calls this a skill shot, but I found no authoritative source specifying the exact plunge technique; avoid claiming a particular plunge strength.
* **Cue:** the split-target feature becomes lit. [S1][S4]

No separate super/alternate/secret skill shot was found.

## Secondary features

**Specials / replays:** completing all red 1–6 or all white 1–6 awards one replay; completing all 12 lights the side gobble holes for **1, 2 or 3 Specials**. [S1][S4]

This is strategically significant only if the tournament assigns value to Specials. There is **no verified built-in point conversion**.

**Match:** a normal Gottlieb match feature can award another replay after the game; irrelevant to match score unless the event has an unusual conversion rule. [S1]

No ball save, kickback, Extra Ball feature, player-controlled save, mystery award, video mode or lane-change control was found.

## What to watch

* **Backglass Cover Girls:** two rows of six give an immediate visual read on red/white completion progress. [S1]
* **Split target lit:** the player has made the top-center rollover and can now use the center target to spot missing red/white numbers. [S1]
* **Which pop is lit:** that pop is currently worth **10 points**, explaining why the player may try to keep the ball in that region. [S1]
* **Player lining up a side hole:** this is not an accidental scoop—it is a **ball-ending 50/100/150-point cash-out** and may be intentional when behind. [S4]

## Match strategy

**Playing ahead:** **strategic inference** — avoid both gobble holes and unnecessary direct-center standup shots. Favor repeat attempts at the 50-point top-center rollover and opportunistic lit-pop scoring. If Specials have no tournament conversion, do not risk the ball merely to finish a Cover-Girl row.

**Playing behind:** the center split target becomes more attractive because it rapidly spots missing numbers. On a final ball or large deficit, an available **100/150-point gobble-hole cash-out** can be rational despite immediately surrendering the ball. [S4]

**Key recurring decision:** **continue playing safely for repeatable points, or deliberately terminate the ball in a high-value gobble hole?** A second decision—whether Cover-Girl completion is worth chasing at all—depends on tournament treatment of Specials.

There is no substantial published competitive meta for this rare game; these match recommendations are **strategic inference from verified rules and documented owner observations**, not tournament consensus.

## Danger zones

* **Side gobble holes:** literal instant drains; their scoring value is the compensation. [S2][S4]
* **Center split target:** direct standup shot described by an experienced owner as a **“sucker shot”**; a square reject can return very quickly. [S4]
* **Outside number lanes:** useful feature progress sits close to the edges and gobble-hole entrances.
* **Upper pops:** good scoring when lit, but their exits surrender ball control.
* **Short-era flipper geometry:** owner reviews identify Cover Girl as part of Gottlieb's two-inch-flipper generation; late saves are correspondingly less forgiving. [S4]

## Trivia

* **Wayne Neyens** designed Cover Girl and **Roy Parker** illustrated it, one of the classic designer/artist pairings of Gottlieb's wedgehead era. [S2]
* Gottlieb's original 1962 advertising specifically promoted its **“new split target skill feature.”** [S1]
* The backglass contains **12 Cover Girls**, matching the game's twelve red/white numbered objectives. [S1][S6]
* Only **2,100** were produced, according to IPSND/Pinside records. [S2][S3]
* The score reels only display through **1,999**, a useful reminder of how significant a single 50- or 150-point shot is on this game. [S2]

### Uncertainties and conflicts

* **Progress persistence:** no sufficiently authoritative source found establishing whether partially completed red/white rows carry between balls.
* **Gobble-hole point-value progression:** experienced owners consistently identify the holes as **50/100/150-point shots** [S4], while Gottlieb's primary advertising confirms that all 12 Cover Girls light them for **1/2/3 Specials** [S1].
