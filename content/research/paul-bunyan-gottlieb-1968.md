## Identity and versions

**Paul Bunyan** — D. Gottlieb & Co., **1968**, model **245**, two-player electromechanical game. Production: **1,900**. Modern archival credits list **Wayne Neyens, Ed Krynski and Steve Kirk** in the design/concept team, with **Art Stenholm** artwork. It has **six 2-inch flippers**, three pop bumpers, seven standups, two kick-out holes and one upper rollunder; no multiball. [S1][S2][S3]

A closely related **Big Jack** followed in October 1968 as an Italian-market Add-A-Ball version. Do not assume its award behavior is identical to Paul Bunyan. [S3][S7]

## Thirty-second game plan

1. **Work A-B-C.** Completing the sequence lights a rotating/running **500-point** A/B/C target opportunity; 500 is a huge award on this scoring scale. [S1][S4]
2. **Hit whichever A/B/C target is currently lit** to take the 500 and reset the sequence. Strong-player reports identify this as the game's primary deliberate scoring objective. [S4][S6]
3. Use the **upper flippers** to attack the top rollunder once its Shoot Again/high-value state is qualified; the six-flipper layout is intentionally built around “relay recovery shots” back toward the top. [S1][S4]
4. **Lit kick-out holes score 600**, the largest clearly manufacturer-documented single scoring award, so a lit hole is an immediate match-changing opportunity. [S1]
5. Tournament wrinkle: stock A-B-C state appears to **carry between players and even between games until completed**, so the starting state may matter as much as the player's own setup. [S5][S6]

## Core rules and persistence

The central progression is the **A-B-C Rotation Sequence**. Contemporary Gottlieb advertising says the sequence relights a “running light” rollover/target for **500 points**; surviving playfield instructions and experienced-player reports clarify that a lit A, B or C target scores the 500 and resets the A-B-C sequence. [S1][S4]

A technically important 3-ball/5-ball distinction is documented by schematic analysis: on a **5-ball game A-B-C must be completed in order**, while **3-ball play accepts them in any order**. This is secondary technical evidence rather than wording recovered directly from the factory cards, but it materially changes difficulty. [S5]

Most unusually, the A-B-C sequence bank does **not normally reset on player change or new game**; it resets when the sequence itself is completed. Multiple owners, schematic analysis and hands-on demonstration agree on this behavior. This means Paul Bunyan has **globally shared state across competitors and potentially across matches** unless the venue manually resets/modifies it. [S5][S6]

Period advertising separately confirms **bullseye targets can light the upper rollunder for Shoot Again** and that either kick-out hole can become worth **600**. The exact intermediate lamp logic for the holes is not sufficiently documented in the sources reviewed, so do not invent a qualification sequence on commentary. [S1]

There is no end-of-ball bonus, jackpot ladder or scoring multiplier comparable to later games; leverage comes from turning ordinary tens-of-points scoring into repeated **500/600-point awards**.

**None found.**

No reliable source documents a separate plunge-based Skill Shot award. The plunge merely feeds the dense upper playfield; describing a particular top lane as a formal skill shot would be inference.

## Secondary features

**Shoot Again:** bullseye-target progress lights the **top rollunder** for Shoot Again. Secondary owner documentation describes the rollunder as alternating between a high-value 500-point state and an Extra Ball/Shoot Again state following successful A-B-C progression. Because the precise sequencing is less clear than the factory promotion, verify it on the venue game before giving a detailed call. [S1][S6]

**Kick-out holes:** two side kick-out holes can each light for **600 points**. On this score scale that is not a side feature—it is potentially the largest single scored switch on the playfield. [S1]

No conventional ball save, kickback, mystery, video mode or player control beyond the six flippers was found.

## What to watch

* **A/B/C progress lamps:** tell you how close the shared sequence is to producing the 500-point cash-out.
* **One A/B/C target flashing/lit:** that's the immediate 500-point objective; making it resets the sequence. [S4]
* **Top rollunder lit:** a Shoot Again/high-value upper-flipper opportunity is ready. [S1][S6]
* **Either kick-out hole lit:** **600 points** are available—an enormous swing on Paul Bunyan. [S1]

## Match strategy

**Playing ahead:** prioritize survival and **relay recovery**—use each pair of flippers to send the ball back upward rather than forcing every available standup. When a 500 target or 600 hole is already lit, cash it; otherwise avoid giving away control solely to manufacture a feature. This is **strategic inference** from the scoring scale and observed physical play. [S4]

**Playing behind:** attack the 500-point A/B/C cash-out and any lit 600-point kick-out aggressively. A few such awards dwarf ordinary switch scoring and can change the match immediately. [S1][S4]

**Key recurring decision:** **take the risky high-value target now, or use that possession to recover upstairs?** The short flippers and segmented layout make continued survival valuable, but 500–600 points are too large to ignore when a comeback is needed.

A separate tournament decision exists before the ball is plunged: if the venue preserves stock shared A-B-C state, the player may inherit a nearly finished sequence—or leave one for the next competitor. [S5]

## Danger zones

* **Below the middle flippers:** experienced players describe survival time as extremely short once the ball falls into the bottom section. [S4]
* **Dual outlanes:** both sides have two drain lanes, making lateral rebounds particularly punishing. [S4]
* **A/B/C standup rebounds:** the key scoring targets sit in the lower-middle area; failed shots can immediately remove the chance to recover upstairs.
* **Two-inch flippers:** trapping is possible on some upper flippers but inconsistent; many possessions become rebound/deflection play rather than controlled cradles. [S4]
* **Kick-out ejects:** they preserve the ball but exact return direction and strength are restoration-dependent; watch the first player before assuming they are safe feeds.

## Trivia

* Gottlieb advertised the six flippers specifically as enabling **“relay recovery shots”** from one section of the playfield to the next. [S1]
* Modern historical research identifies **Paul Bunyan / Big Jack as Wayne Neyens' final conventional Gottlieb design** before his later experimental *Challenger*. [S7]
* The project combined three notable designers: **Wayne Neyens, Ed Krynski and a young Steve Kirk**; Kirk later became known for Stern games including *Stars*, *Meteor* and *Nine Ball*. [S3][S6]
* Art Stenholm's backglass depicts the giant lumberjack being groomed with absurdly oversized tools, including an axe and pitchfork. Contemporary *Cash Box* coverage called specific attention to the artwork. [S1]
* Gottlieb produced **1,900 Paul Bunyans**; the related Italian Add-A-Ball *Big Jack* followed only months later. [S2][S7]

### Uncertainties and conflicts

* **A-B-C persistence is tournament-critical.** Schematic/owner evidence strongly supports stock carryover between players **and games** [S5][S6], but many tournament organizers would reasonably add a manual/reset modification. The actual venue policy must be known before publishing the final page.
* **3-ball vs. 5-ball sequence rule:** schematic analysis says 3-ball accepts A/B/C in any order while 5-ball requires order [S5]. Factory 3-ball and 5-ball instruction cards are archived [S8], but their images were not readable through the research interface, so this remains strong technical evidence rather than directly transcribed primary wording.
* **Top-rollunder sequence:** the period flyer definitively says bullseye targets light it for Shoot Again [S1]. A detailed owner demonstration says the post-A-B-C payoff progresses through the lit 500 target to the upper rollunder [S6]. The exact alternation between 500 and Shoot Again should be checked on the venue copy.
* **Kick-out qualification:** factory advertising confirms lit kick-out holes score 600 but does not fully document how their lamps advance. Omitted rather than guessed.
