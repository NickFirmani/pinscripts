## Identity and versions

* **GoldenEye** — Sega Pinball, March 1996; model 42, early **Sega/Stern Whitestar**, 128×32 DMD, two flippers, three ramps. About **2,200 produced**. Scope is the Sega game specified in the supplied brief.  [S1][S4] ([Kineticist][1])
* Design: **Ward Pemberton**; mechanics: Rob Hurtado; software: John Carpenter, Lonnie Ropp, Neil Falconer and Orin Day; art: Jeff Busch and Paul Faris; music/sound: Brian Schmidt. [S1]
* Signature mechanisms are the rotating magnetic **GoldenEye satellite**, a pop-up jump ramp used to shoot it, the tank ball launcher, and an under-playfield magnet used for the unusual **Eject or Die** rescue. [S1][S2] ([Pinside][2])
* **Latest software verified: CPU 4.04 / display A4.00.** The detailed contemporary rulesheet is based on **CPU 4.02**, so exact 4.04 changes are unresolved. [S2][S3] ([Museum of the Game][3])
* Important multiballs: **Satellite Multiball**, **Tank Multiball**, the Train/Tank 007 Encounter, and the deep **GOLDENEYE!** wizard multiball.

## Thirty-second game plan

1. **Build Satellite Multiball:** hit the center Eject target to light virtual locks, collect two at the Lock Ramp, then use the left-inlane-triggered pop-up ramp to stick the ball onto the satellite. [S2]
2. **Prefer Satellite over Tank Multiball.** Contemporary competitive strategy explicitly considers Satellite the richer route; Tank can even be cancelled with the gun trigger during its start animation. **Observed strategy.** [S2]
3. During Satellite MB, collect the four jackpot shots, then hit the raised satellite ramp to **knock the captured ball loose for 200M**; afterward a moving **150M Super Jackpot** cycles among the four jackpot shots. [S2]
4. Independently, shoot ramps in single-ball play to spell **GOLDENEYE**. Three 50M countdown ramp hurry-ups followed by the satellite shot can approach **310–320M**, another match-swinging route. [S2]
5. Deeper alternative: complete the wizard checklist for **GOLDENEYE!**; contemporary strategy calls this the game's primary long-game objective, but Satellite MB is the much more realistic short-tournament cash-out. [S2] ([GameFAQs][4])

## Core rules and persistence

* Center Eject lights the Lock Ramp; **two virtual locks** light Satellite Multiball. The left inlane then raises the pop-up ramp for the satellite shot. [S2]
* Satellite MB jackpots are the **far-left ramp, Lock Ramp, Mode Start lane and right ramp**. Guard targets cycle the jackpot value through **10M/25M/50M/75M/100M**. [S2]
* If Satellite MB drops to one ball before the satellite payoff, the physically held satellite ball is released to **continue multiball**—an important built-in survival feature. [S2]
* Features and 007 Encounters can interact unusually: starting a regular multiball generally **pauses the running Encounter**, which resumes afterward rather than truly stacking its scoring. [S2]
* Ramp shots during single-ball/non-mode play spell GOLDENEYE. Finishing all three hurry-ups lights the satellite for a second collection of the accumulated hurry-up value plus 10M. [S2]
* The wizard mode requires: all five 007 Encounters started/ended, 007 lanes completed, Shootout completed, Q's Pen completed, and GOLDENEYE spelled. In **GOLDENEYE!**, switches are 3M, ramps 20M and every 50 switches adds another ball; it ends at one ball. [S2]
* Bonus is based on ramp + Mode Start shots ×1M, multiplied by Bonus X from the 007 lanes. [S2]

## Skill shots

`None found.`

GoldenEye uses an automatic launcher/gun-style shooter, but neither the contemporary 4.02 rulesheet nor modern owner documentation identifies a coded plunge skill shot; a current owner specifically notes that the game has no skill shot. [S2][S5] ([Pinside][5])

## Secondary features

* **Eject or Die:** finish Shootout to light both outlanes. An outlane drain during single-ball play is caught by the lower magnet and thrown back into play; the player then has about **6 seconds to hit the center Eject target** or the flippers shut off and the ball is lost. Awards can be stacked. [S2] ([GameFAQs][4])
* **Shootout:** clear the three white standups, then hit the yellow target. The resulting display/video feature is completed by firing the cabinet's gun trigger six times; finishing it lights Eject or Die. [S2]
* **Gun trigger:** besides Shootout, it can **cancel Tank Multiball** during its start animation, preserving Satellite MB qualification. [S2]
* **Xenia Extra Ball:** one of the five Encounters; center Eject is briefly lit for an Extra Ball. This is the only normal Extra Ball source documented by the contemporary rulesheet. [S2]
* **Mystery:** right inlane briefly lights Mode Start for Mystery; 4.02 documentation observed 10M or 2×. Exact tournament-mode normalization was not verified. [S2]
* Short automatic saves also protect some dangerous center-target/Mode Start kickouts. [S2]

## What to watch

* **Satellite ramp rises after the left inlane:** Satellite MB or another satellite collect is ready; the next shot is literally airborne toward the rotating dish.
* **Ball visibly stuck to the satellite:** the player has a reserve multiball ball and is hunting four jackpots before knocking it loose. [S2]
* **GOLDENEYE letters nearly complete:** repeated ramp shooting is about to turn into the three-ramp hurry-up sequence worth roughly 300M.
* **Eject or Die lit at the outlanes:** an apparent drain may not be over; watch for the magnet throw and immediate six-second center-target shot.

## Match strategy

**Playing ahead:** Take Satellite MB when ready and collect four jackpots without obsessing over cycling every jackpot to 100M. A quick 200M satellite collect plus 150M Super is safer than extended setup. Avoid the right ramp if this copy has the classic outlane-hop problem. **Observed strategy.** [S2]

**Playing behind:** Pursue both major leverage routes: Satellite MB and the **GOLDENEYE ramp hurry-up/satellite collect**. Going all the way to GOLDENEYE! offers the highest sustained ceiling, but Q's Pen is a dangerous bottleneck. [S2]

**Key recurring decision:** **Take Tank Multiball now, or cancel it and risk the satellite shot?** Tank gives immediate multiball safety; Satellite offers the 200M satellite payoff and repeatable 150M Supers.

## Danger zones

* **Right-ramp return:** documented from the original rulesheet as capable of hopping over the inlane and draining without even registering the outlane. [S2]
* **Satellite miss:** the jump ramp launches the ball airborne; a failed magnetic catch can ricochet almost anywhere. [S2]
* **Q's Pen targets:** six total target hits—three to qualify, then three within ~14 seconds—make this both a wizard-mode bottleneck and a dangerous repeated standup attack. [S2]
* **Center Eject:** essential but rebound-prone; several immediate drains are explicitly compensated by code. [S2]
* **Pop exits:** the 007/top-lane area feeds back between the center Eject and satellite target bank with little control.

## Trivia

* GoldenEye was **Ward Pemberton's final documented pinball design** after games including *Fathom*, *Mousin' Around!* and *Riverboat Gambler*. [S1] ([GameFAQs][6])
* Sega reportedly debuted production GoldenEye machines at a **Flint, Michigan pinball tournament in January 1996**, before its March release. [S6] ([WikiMili.com][7])
* The satellite shot is unusual because the ball leaves the playfield and must be caught by a **magnet mounted in the rotating dish**. [S1][S2]
* Sega issued a March 1996 service bulletin because the original game overloaded one GI circuit and required **five bulbs to be removed**, plus a power-source change for the magnet processor board. [S7] ([Pinside][8])
* There is no mechanical knocker; a distinctive electronic **“BLOP”** announces a replay. [S2]

### Uncertainties and conflicts

* **Code:** latest verified CPU ROM is **4.04** [S3], while the authoritative detailed rulesheet was written on **4.02** [S2]. I found no sufficiently detailed 4.04 changelog proving which gameplay rules changed.
* **Multiball ball counts:** contemporary sources are inconsistent—the 4.02 rulesheet itself uses question marks for Tank/Satellite counts, while later summaries describe Tank as three-ball and Satellite as four-ball. For commentary, the important verified behavior is the jackpot structure and satellite-held reserve ball, not a printed starting count. [S2][S6]
* **Tournament Mode:** GoldenEye has a Sega Tournament Mode adjustment, but I did not find reliable documentation establishing its exact effect on Mystery or Extra Balls. Do not assume modern Stern-style Competition Install behavior.
* **Physical mechanisms matter unusually much:** both the satellite and lower rescue depend on magnets and their dedicated processor hardware; a weak/broken magnet materially changes strategy. [S7]
