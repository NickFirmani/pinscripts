## Identity and versions

* **South Park** — Sega Pinball, **1999**; Sega/Stern Whitestar DMD platform. Game design/concept: **Joe Kaminkow**; engineering/mechanics: **Joe Balcer and Rob Hurtado**; software credits include **Jon Norris, Lonnie D. Ropp, Neil Falconer and Orin Day**; art by **Dave Link and Kevin O’Connor**. Approximately **2,200** produced. [S1][S2] ([Pinside][1])
* **Rules basis: `rom` — factory CPU A1.03** (U210, checksum `$58FF`), with display A1.01. Stern’s Whitestar firmware appendix and current ROM archives identify A1.03 as the latest production gameplay ROM. [S2][S3] ([Stern Pinball][2])
* Prototype **0.90/0.96 “profanity” ROMs** exist and materially differ: uncensored audio, unfinished presentation and a Cartman video mode absent from production software. This brief covers **factory A1.03**, not prototypes. [S4] ([Action Pinball][3])
* Relevant multiballs: normal **5-ball Multiball**, **Stan’s 4-ball mode**, and the 5-ball wizard modes **Spooky Vision** and **Volcano**. [S5][S6] ([JLP's Pinball Cards][4])

Research scope follows the supplied commentator-reference specification.

## Thirty-second game plan

1. **Observed tournament strategy:** play all five character modes but **intentionally leave at least one incomplete**. That qualifies **Spooky Vision**, generally the more exploitable wizard: all five character shots become repeatable jackpots, and clearing the set raises their value. [S5] ([JLP's Pinball Cards][4])
2. Meanwhile qualify normal Multiball with **five Mr. Hankey/toilet hits**, then start it at the **Kyle ramp**. Completing character modes first is valuable because that character’s shot becomes a **double jackpot** during Multiball. [S5][S6] ([JLP's Pinball Cards][4])
3. In normal Multiball, collect five jackpots to light the timed **Super Jackpot** at the Kyle ramp. Repeated Supers raise both future Super value and base jackpot value. [S5] ([JLP's Pinball Cards][4])
4. **Central choice:** deliberately fail one mode for Spooky Vision’s repeatable scoring, or complete all five to reach **Volcano**, where fifteen 10M shots plus completion produce about **200M+** in one successful round. [S5][S6] ([JLP's Pinball Cards][4])
5. **Alternate short-game strategy:** repeatedly attack **Kenny**. Competitive discussion documents “Kenny all day” as a viable simple tournament route when deeper progression is unattractive. [S9] ([TiltForums][5])

## Core rules and persistence

The five characters are **Stan** (left lane through the pops), **Chef** (center VUK), **Kenny** (upper-right toy/hole), **Kyle** (right ramp), and **Cartman** (far-right scoop). Initially, four shots start each character mode; successfully completing a character makes later qualification harder. Solid character faces mean **completed**; flashing faces mean **played but incomplete**. [S6][S8] ([GameFAQs][6])

Qualification persistence is asymmetric: a detailed long-term player guide reports **Kenny and Cartman qualification counts carry across drains**, while Stan, Chef and Kyle qualification counts reset at the end of the ball. Completed/attempted character status remains relevant toward the wizard modes. [S8] ([Riley MacDonald][7])

Normal Multiball can be started while a timed character mode is running, but the character mode **pauses until the game returns to single-ball play**. That makes Multiball protection less useful for directly finishing timed modes than on many modern games. [S5] ([JLP's Pinball Cards][4])

During normal Multiball, completed-character shots score **double jackpots**. Five jackpots light a roughly 10-second Super at the Kyle ramp; collecting it raises the jackpot base by 1M and subsequent Super value by 5M. [S5] ([JLP's Pinball Cards][4])

## Skill shots

**Normal Skill Shot:** plunge to the **flashing yellow townsperson standup** below/around the toilet area; the flipper buttons move the lit target. The original instruction card explicitly documents the movable flashing target. [S7] ([Pinball Rebel][8])

Award is documented in the contemporary rulesheet as **5M + 5M per previous successful skill shot**. A full-power plunge can instead strike the toilet targets and advance Multiball, trading guaranteed skill-shot value for strategic progress. [S6][S8] ([GameFAQs][6])

No separate super/secret skill shot was verified in production A1.03.

## Secondary features

* **Ball save:** observed production behavior provides a short save after the initial plunge and after Kenny/Cartman cellar-hole returns. It is brief enough that a slow outlane roll can outlast it. [S8] ([Riley MacDonald][7])
* **Mystery:** collected at the Chef lane. Documented awards include points, Bonus X and **Mr. Mackey**, a 30-second frenzy where switches score heavily. Sources disagree on the exact qualifying target set and point awards, so do not call those specifics without seeing the machine state. [S6][S8] ([GameFAQs][6])
* **Extra ball:** Mystery/consolation behavior can light one at Chef, including a low-score ball-3 “charity” extra ball. Tournament handling is external configuration/policy; no modern competition-install normalization exists for this Sega-era title. [S6] ([GameFAQs][6])
* **Video mode:** **none in factory A1.03**. The prototype profanity ROM has a Cartman video mode, which is one reason ROM identification matters. [S1][S4] ([Pinside][1])

## What to watch

1. **Character face flashing vs solid:** flashing = played but failed; solid = completed. Five played characters with at least one flashing is the setup for **Spooky Vision**. [S5][S7] ([JLP's Pinball Cards][4])
2. **Chef lane lit for wizard:** after all five modes are played, Chef starts either Spooky Vision or Volcano depending on completion state. [S5][S6] ([JLP's Pinball Cards][4])
3. **Toilet opens / Multiball arrow at Kyle ramp:** five Hankey hits have qualified the 5-ball Multiball. [S5][S7] ([JLP's Pinball Cards][4])
4. **Kyle ramp flashing during Multiball:** likely the timed Super Jackpot; this is the major cash-out shot. [S5] ([JLP's Pinball Cards][4])

## Match strategy

**Playing ahead:** **strategic inference:** use Kenny and Cartman for easier value, start normal Multiball once qualified, and cash Supers rather than taking risky Stan shots solely to chase a wizard. Completed easy characters also improve normal-MB scoring.

**Playing behind:** pursue **Spooky Vision** deliberately: get all five modes played while allowing one difficult mode—often Stan—to time out. Once there, repeatedly cycle the five character jackpots to raise their value. If all five are already complete, Volcano offers the bigger fixed wizard payoff. [S5] ([JLP's Pinball Cards][4])

**Key recurring decision:** **complete the fifth mode or intentionally fail it?** Completing everything gives Volcano; leaving one incomplete gives Spooky Vision, whose repeatable escalating jackpots are the currently documented tournament-preferred route. [S5] ([JLP's Pinball Cards][4])

## Danger zones

* **Mr. Hankey/toilet rebound:** direct shots—including full plunges—can ricochet immediately toward the **right outlane**. [S8] ([Riley MacDonald][7])
* **Stan miss/return:** the through-the-pops shot is inconsistent and its upper-ramp return can fall into several different feeds. [S8] ([Riley MacDonald][7])
* **Kyle ramp reject:** the shot needs sufficient power; failed climbs return into open play, and the Super is timed during crowded 5-ball play. [S8] ([Riley MacDonald][7])
* **Cellar/VUK returns:** Kenny and Cartman return directly toward the right-flipper side; expect immediate action rather than a cradle. [S10] ([GameFAQs][9])
* **Slings/outlanes:** experienced-player documentation particularly identifies the right outlane as the machine’s common punishment for uncontrolled rebounds. [S8] ([Riley MacDonald][7])

## Trivia

* *South Park* was one of the final games carrying the **Sega Pinball** name before Gary Stern’s organization continued the Whitestar line under Stern Pinball. [S1] ([Pinside][1])
* Production is estimated at **2,200 machines**. [S1] ([Pinside][1])
* The game’s signature mechanical joke is a **toilet that physically opens and “flushes” the ball** to start Multiball. [S1][S6] ([Pinside][1])
* Prototype ROMs contained substantially more profanity and an unfinished **Cartman carpet-licking video mode**; those features were removed from factory production code. [S4] ([Action Pinball][3])
* Entering **LLRRLLRR** in attract mode displays the design-team credits. [S6] ([GameFAQs][6])
