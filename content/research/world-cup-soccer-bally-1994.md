## Identity and versions

* **World Cup Soccer** / **World Cup Soccer ’94** — Bally/Midway, **1994**, model **50031**, **WPC Security (WPC-S)**. Design by **John Popadiuk and Larry DeMar**; software by **Larry DeMar and Matt Coriale**; art by **Kevin O’Connor**; engineering by **Jack Skalon**; callouts by **Tim Kitzrow**. Production: **8,743**. [S1][S4]
* Latest verified factory game ROM is **L-2, July 7, 1994**. Its documented change from L-1 was primarily display/pricing fixes, not a rules overhaul. [S5]
* Main scoring multiball is **Final Draw Multiball**; the deep wizard mode is the **World Cup Final Match** against Germany.
* Scope follows the supplied commentator-reference specification.

## Thirty-second game plan

1. **Observed strategy:** prioritize **Multiball**. Hit flashing **BUILD LOCK** shots until all five soccer-ball panels are lit, then lock a ball on either ramp and hit **Final Draw** to start. [S1][S3]
2. In Multiball, **Goal = Jackpot**; then shoot either ramp or the Assist saucer to relight Goal. Jackpots climb from **20M to Germany at 250M**, followed by **50M Victory Laps**. [S1][S2]
3. Scoring goals *before the first Multiball* matters: every two Goals improves your starting rank, and physically locking a ball adds another four ranks. [S1]
4. Meanwhile, take lit Goals for **Ultra** scoring and work Cities when convenient; Ultra points are paid in **end-of-ball bonus**, so **do not tilt**. [S1][S3]
5. Alternate high-ceiling route: travel through all **nine Cities** to Los Angeles and start **Final Match**—75M per Goal plus **500M for beating Germany**. [S1][S3]

## Core rules and persistence

* **Goal:** lit at the start of each ball. A normal Goal scores **10M** and starts one of four Ultra features. Relight Goal by completing the four central star rollovers; the upper Striker target spots an unlit rollover. [S1]
* Ultras are **Spinner 1M/spin, Jets 2M/hit, Ramps 5M/shot, Goalie 5M/hit**. Each runs until 30M of feature scoring is accumulated, then adds a 10M completion bonus. [S1]
* **Important commentary trap:** Ultra scoring is not fully banked immediately; it is included in end-of-ball bonus. Bonus also gives **5M per Goal and 5M per City accumulated over the whole game**. Tilt forfeits the bonus. [S3]
* **Multiball:** five BUILD panels qualify Lock; either ramp locks the ball; Final Draw starts Multiball. First-Multiball rank improves one position per two pre-MB Goals plus **four positions for having locked a ball**. [S1]
* In Multiball, Goal scores the current opponent Jackpot; a ramp or **Assist** relights Goal for the next team. Germany is **250M**; defeating Germany lights 50M Victory Laps. [S1]
* If Multiball ends **without scoring any Goal Jackpot**, a quick rematch can be started at a ramp, Final Draw or Assist. Later Multiballs resume higher on the ranking ladder. [S1][S2]
* **Cities:** complete both ramps or both top BUY/TICKET lanes to light Travel; shoot the **left spinner/orbit** to travel. Nine Cities culminate in Los Angeles and Final Match. [S1]
* Built-in **Tournament Play** equalizes Multiball/Jackpot state among players; it does **not by itself mean “Extra Balls off.”** [S1]

## Skill shots

**Coin Toss Skill Shot**

* Plunge into one of the **three flashing Coin Toss holes** above the shooter lane.
* First new hole: **5M**; second: **10M**; completing all three: **30M + advance one City**. Already-collected holes score only 1M. [S1][S3]
* Strategically useful because completing the set shortcuts City progression toward Final Match. [S3]
* Visible cue: flashing versus solid Coin Toss lamps.

No separate super or secret skill shot found.

## Secondary features

* **Corner Kickback:** protects the left outlane. Up to **three charges** can be stacked depending on adjustment. After the right inlane, shoot the temporarily lit lower-left target to add a charge. [S1][S3]
* **Magna-Goal:** player-activated magnet at the tip of the left flipper, fired with the second left cabinet button to rescue/control a center-bound ball. Only one charge can be held. [S2][S3]
* **Extra Balls:** available from the second TV round, Washington D.C., and Striker awards. With EBs disabled, the TV-round collect and Washington D.C. award **50M instead**. [S1]
* **Striker Awards:** can give Multiball, three Cities, Extra Ball, three Goals, Unlimited Kickback, Super Free Kick, Penalty Kick or points. Tournament organizers may normalize/order these separately from the built-in Tournament Play option. [S1][S2]

## What to watch

1. **Five BUILD panels complete / Lock lit:** player is one ramp lock away from Final Draw Multiball.
2. **Current ranking/opponent:** Jackpot value rises sharply near France (**100M**) and Germany (**250M**).
3. **Soccer ball spinning / GOAL lamp on:** a normal Goal is available and will start an Ultra.
4. **City lamps:** Los Angeles flashing/complete means **Final Match** is one Final Draw shot away.

## Match strategy

**Playing ahead:** favor the repeatable **Multiball route**, cash Goal Jackpots, maintain the left kickback, and protect accumulated Ultra bonus. Avoid aggressive nudging when hundreds of millions may be waiting in bonus. **Observed strategy.** [S2][S3]

**Playing behind:** pursue deeper rankings toward **Germany's 250M Jackpot + 50M Victory Laps**, or commit to the nine-City Final Match. A reasonable five-goal Final Match is **375M in Goals**, with another **500M** for the win. [S1][S3]

**Key recurring decision:** **repeat Multiball or chase Cities?** Modern competitive guidance calls Multiball the most consistent scoring route; Final Match has the larger single-feature ceiling but requires substantially more setup. [S3]

## Danger zones

1. **TV / Final Draw scoop:** famous for being difficult precisely when Multiball is ready; repeated misses can destroy a good setup. [S3]
2. **Left outlane:** sufficiently dangerous that expert tournament guidance says to relight Corner Kickback immediately after use. [S2][S3]
3. **Goal / goalie rejection:** the moving goalie can turn the game's most desirable shot into an uncontrolled rebound.
4. **Left-ramp diverter:** soft versus hard ramp shots can be sent to different feeds, including pop-bumper traffic. [S2]
5. **Final Match goal congestion:** multiple balls entering the Goal nearly together can register as only one Goal; space Goal shots rather than blindly flooding the net. [S2]

## Trivia

* World Cup Soccer was **John Popadiuk's first design to reach full production**; Larry DeMar co-designed and programmed it. [S3]
* It uses **Striker**, the official mascot of the 1994 FIFA World Cup in the United States. [S4]
* The game was among the first titles using Williams' new **WPC Security CPU**, whose security chip tied the board to a game's software family. [S1]
* Announcer **Tim Kitzrow** came to the game immediately after his famous work on *NBA Jam*. [S3]
* Final Match has **no autoplunger**: the player manually launches replacement balls throughout the 45-second mode. [S1][S3]

### Uncertainties and conflicts

* **Release timing:** Pinside lists **February 1994** [S4], while the factory operations manual is dated **May 1994** [S1]. Use *Bally 1994* rather than implying the manual date is the commercial release date.
* **Tournament configuration:** built-in Tournament Play only equalizes multiplayer **Multiball and Jackpot state** [S1]. The classic PAPA tournament rules additionally specify **no Extra Balls and a fixed Striker Award order** [S2]; those are event settings, not automatically implied by the game's Tournament Play switch.
* Final Match difficulty is adjustable: default Germany typically scores roughly **3–5 Goals**, and repeat Final Matches can become harder. [S3]
* Ramp shots can spot **0, 1, or 2 Goal rollovers**, which changes how strong the Goal/Ultra strategy is. [S3]
* Goal registration, Final Draw acceptance, ramp diverters and Magna-Goal strength are all physically copy-dependent.
