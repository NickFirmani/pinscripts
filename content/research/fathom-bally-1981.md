## Identity and versions

* **Fathom** — Bally Manufacturing, August 1981; model **1233**, Bally AS-2518-35 solid-state platform, four players, three flippers. Production: **3,500**. Scope is the original Bally game requested in the supplied brief.  [S1][S3] 
* Design: **Ward Pemberton**; software: **Rehman Merchant**; artwork credited to **Greg Freres and Kevin O'Connor**. [S3] ([Pinside][1])
* Two color-coded multiballs: **Lagoon Multiball (blue)** and **Cave Multiball (green)**. They can overlap for **3-ball play**. [S1][S2]
* No numbered stock-ROM revision was verified; current archives simply list the factory software version as unknown. An unofficial **Home ROM V4** exists and changes the plunge/skill-shot behavior plus callouts, so it matters for commentary. [S3][S6] ([Pinside][1])

## Thirty-second game plan

1. **Light the spinner.** Hit the standup beside the right-side green channel; the spinner becomes **5,000/spin**. It is the game's primary repeatable scoring shot. **Confirmed rule / observed strategy.** [S1][S2] 
2. **Build Cave Multiball first when practical.** Lock a ball in the right/green Cave Trap, then finish its three inline drops; competitive guides regard green as substantially easier than blue. [S2]
3. The same inline-drop work advances **3× → 4× → 5× end-of-ball bonus**, so multiball setup is also bonus setup. [S1]
4. Best payoff: get **both locks running for 3-ball**, where all playfield scoring is tripled. A lit spinner is then effectively **15,000 per spin**; a strong rip can swing the match immediately. [S1][S2]
5. **Alternate tournament line:** if multiball setup looks dangerous but the spinner is already lit, simply shoot spinner repeatedly. In short match play this can outperform elaborate setup. **Observed strategy.** [S2]

## Core rules and persistence

* Shoot a ball into either **Lagoon or Cave saucer** to trap it and raise that color's three inline targets. Knock down all three to release the locked ball and start 2-ball multiball. Lock/release both colors together for 3-ball. [S1] 
* Completing the matching three blue or green targets in the **left six-bank** can also spot one corresponding inline target; this simultaneously advances the relevant bonus multiplier. [S1]
* **2 balls = 2× all playfield scoring; 3 balls = 3×.** This is separate from the **3×/4×/5× end-of-ball bonus multiplier**. Easy commentator trap: Fathom has two different multiplier systems. [S1] 
* Blue and green bonus can together reach **110,000 base**; with both at 5×, maximum end-of-ball bonus is approximately **550,000**. Tilt forfeits the bonus. [S1][S2]
* **Bonus recall is adjustable.** Bally's recommended setup has Bonus Recall ON; the built bonus can therefore survive ball-to-ball, while the bonus multiplier itself does not. Inline-drop and A-B-C recall are independently adjustable. [S1] 
* **Physical locks are shared.** In multiplayer play, a later player can inherit/release a ball trapped by an earlier player—classic “lock stealing.” [S5] ([Tilt Forums][2])
* Relocking a ball during multiball removes it from play; when the inline targets are not raised, the corresponding color's three targets in the left bank are required to release it. **Observed strategy:** avoid accidental relocks when you should be exploiting the playfield multiplier. [S1][S2]

## Skill shots

* **A-B-C lane skill shot:** plunge into an **unlit** top lane; the right flipper button rotates the lit A-B-C lamps. Completing A-B-C once lights one return lane for **50K**, twice lights both. [S1][S2]
* If all lanes are equally useful, **B is slightly preferable**: A advances blue bonus, C advances green bonus, while B advances **both**. [S1][S4] 
* A-B-C progress is operator-adjustable for ball-to-ball recall. [S1]
* **Home ROM V4 caveat:** it adds a more explicit plunge skill shot with an automatically cycling A-B-C lamp and temporarily disables normal lane change. [S6]

## Secondary features

* **Extra Ball:** hit center **1-2-3 drops in order**, then hit the flashing yellow target within **6 or 10 seconds**, depending on adjustment. Maximum is one Extra Ball per ball in play. [S1] 
* In Bally's **Novelty** configuration, that Extra Ball target can instead award **25K**; Specials can be converted to **50K**. This is tournament-relevant because these are nontrivial point awards on Fathom. [S1]
* An earned Extra Ball resumes almost all prior playfield state; the manual specifically excepts the A-B-C routine. [S1]
* **No standard ball save, kickback, video mode or action button.** A tilt immediately ends the ball and forfeits its bonus. [S1]

## What to watch

* **Spinner lamp on:** this is the clearest scoring tell. It is 5K/spin before the 2×/3× multiball multiplier. [S1][S2]
* **Ball visibly sitting in a Cave/Lagoon saucer + three inline drops raised:** a multiball is being built; count remaining inline targets.
* **Both locks occupied:** the player is one release sequence away from 3-ball and triple playfield scoring.
* **Blue/green 3×–5× lamps:** these show both end-of-ball leverage and how far each inline sequence has progressed.

## Match strategy

**Playing ahead:** Light the spinner and take repeatable spinner points; favor the easier **green/Cave** multiball rather than forcing blue. If the spinner is already valuable enough to win, there is little reason to expose the ball to extra drop-bank setup. **Observed strategy.** [S2]

**Playing behind:** Build **both** locks, enter 3-ball, then attack a lit spinner. At **15K/spin**, a single excellent rip can be worth several hundred thousand; meanwhile the lock work has also built a potentially huge end-of-ball bonus. [S1][S2]

**Key recurring decision:** **Spinner now versus multiball first.** The lit spinner scores extremely well in single-ball play; delaying it for 3× creates the higher ceiling but adds several setup shots and drain opportunities. [S2]

## Danger zones

* **Pop-bumper exits:** spinner shots feed uncontrolled upper-playfield action; this is one of the game's principal random-drain sources. [S2][S4]
* **Left and center drop-bank rebounds:** direct hits can kick violently toward center or either outlane. [S2]
* **Reversed return/outlane geometry:** Fathom's lower lanes behave opposite the layout many players instinctively expect; the wrong nudge can turn a saveable ball into a drain. [S2]
* **Green-side return:** often ideal for a dead bounce right-to-left, but trying to trap it conventionally can create unnecessary danger. **Observed strategy.** [S2]
* **Relock during multiball:** a ball disappearing into a saucer immediately reduces the active playfield multiplier until the player earns its release.

## Trivia

* Fathom was designed by **Ward Pemberton**, with software by **Rehman Merchant** and its celebrated underwater artwork credited to **Greg Freres and Kevin O'Connor**. [S3]
* Bally produced **3,500** machines in 1981. [S3]
* Its original rules are unusually symmetrical: separate **blue and green locks, bonuses and target progress** eventually combine into 3-ball play. [S1]
* Fathom includes synthesized **speech**, unusual enough in 1981 that Bally's contemporary promotion specifically called out its voice mode. [S3] ([Retro CDN][4])
* Taito later produced the closely related **Hawkman (1983)**, while Haggis Pinball created the modern **Fathom Revisited** remake decades later. [S3] ([Kineticist][5])

### Uncertainties and conflicts

* **Spinner base value:** current tournament references consistently give **500 unlit / 5,000 lit** [S2][S4]. Bally's feature manual says the spinner “scores 1,000 to 5,000 when lit” and explicitly confirms the right target lights it for 5,000 [S1], but does not clearly document the unlit value in that paragraph. Use **5K lit** confidently; the unlit value is comparatively unimportant to commentary. 
* **Persistence is adjustment-dependent:** bonus recall, inline-drop recall and A-B-C recall each have factory switches. Bally's recommended settings enable recall, but a tournament operator can materially change the game by disabling them. [S1]
* **Lock stealing:** stock physical locks can be inherited by another player in multiplayer games. This is established competitive behavior but may be mitigated by tournament format or modified hardware/software. [S5]
* **Home ROM:** V4 changes skill-shot behavior and callouts. It should not be treated as merely a cosmetic revision. [S6]
