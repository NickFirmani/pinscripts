## Identity and versions

* **Space Odyssey** — Williams Electronics, **June 1976**, model **462**; two-player electro-mechanical game with score reels, two flippers, two pop bumpers, two kickout holes, two lower ball kickers, a spinner and the distinctive motor-driven **swinging target**. Design: **Steve Kordek**; art: **Christian Marche**; approximately **4,300 produced**. [S1][S3]  ([Pinball Rebel][1])
* It is the two-player companion to Williams' four-player **Space Mission**; the core playfield/rules architecture is shared. [S1][S2] ([Kineticist][2])
* Factory advertising offered **3- or 5-ball play** and replay/extra-ball configuration; Williams explicitly promoted 3-ball pricing. [S3] ([World Radio History][3])
* No multiball or software revision.

## Thirty-second game plan

1. **UTAD — up top all day.** Send the ball through the left spinner/top area and use the two lower kickers rather than making unnecessary dangerous direct shots. **Observed strategy.** [S2] ([Kineticist][2])
2. Build the end-of-ball bonus toward its **20K maximum**; the upper-right **Super Bonus Advance** horseshoe adds three bonus steps. [S4] ([McGraw Commons][4])
3. Advance the swinging-target sequence to collect **A/B/C** and qualify the side eject-hole route to **Double Bonus**; then get a side saucer to light the doubler. [S2][S4] ([Kineticist][2])
4. Once Double Bonus is lit, protect the ball and keep building: **20K ×2 = 40K** at drain, a huge fraction of a tournament score. [S4] ([McGraw Commons][5])
5. Alternate swing route: when the **spinner is lit for 1K/spin**, attack it. A strong rip can be worth **20K+** by itself. **Observed strategy.** [S2] ([Kineticist][2])

## Core rules and persistence

* The center swinging target has a **10-position scoring/feature sequence**. Hitting it—or collecting its value through a lit outer top lane—steps the sequence one position and may award 5K or A/B/C progress. [S4] ([McGraw Commons][4])
* A/B/C progress qualifies the eject-hole/doubler sequence. Shooting a qualified side eject hole lights **Double Bonus** for that ball. [S2][S4] ([Kineticist][2])
* Bonus advances from the two yellow standups, the rollover behind the spinner, and the **Super Bonus Advance horseshoe**, which advances it three steps. Maximum verified bonus is **20K / 40K doubled**. [S4] ([McGraw Commons][4])
* **A-B-C complete** lights the bottom outlanes alternately for Special/replay. [S4] ([McGraw Commons][4])
* The swinging-target unit itself is unusually **global mechanical state** rather than player-specific; its current position can persist between players/games. That can leave the next player closer to—or farther from—the valuable spinner/Extra-Ball positions. [S2][S5] ([Kineticist][2])
* Double Bonus and ordinary bonus are ball-level objectives; tilt ends the ball and **forfeits bonus**. [S4] ([McGraw Commons][5])

## Skill shots

* **Collect Swinging Target Value:** plunge through whichever **outer top lane is lit**. It scores/advances the swinging-target sequence exactly as though the target had been hit. [S2][S4] ([Kineticist][2])
* The lit outer lanes toggle with the upper-right rubber/switch action, so a controlled plunge can intentionally change their state before collection. **Observed strategy.** [S2] ([Kineticist][2])
* Do **not** confuse the center top lane with the skill shot: when lit, that is the **Extra Ball** lane.

## Secondary features

* **Extra Ball:** when the swinging-target unit reaches one of the operator-qualified feature positions, the center upper rollover and 1K spinner can light together. The exact set of qualifying positions is **liberal/medium/conservative adjustable**. [S5] ([Pinside][6])
* Only **one Extra Ball at a time** can be earned, and it cannot be collected directly from the initial plunge; the opportunity expires when the ball drains. [S4] ([McGraw Commons][5])
* **Special:** completing A-B-C alternates the lit left/right outlane for a replay. Tournament value depends entirely on event handling. [S4]
* No ball saver, kickback, video mode, mystery or player-controlled utility.

## What to watch

* **Double Bonus lamp:** once on, the player's priority usually changes from qualification to simply building/protecting bonus.
* **Swinging-target position:** if it is on a favorable 5K feature position, the **spinner may be lit for 1K/spin**—an immediate scoring opportunity. [S2][S5]
* **Bonus ladder near 20K:** doubled max bonus means a possible **40K drain award**.
* **Lit outer top lane:** tells you where the player wants the plunge/top feed to go to advance the swinging-target state without directly attacking it.

## Match strategy

**Playing ahead:** Get Double Bonus, then play conservative **UTAD** and accept natural kicker/saucer feeds. A 30–40K pending drain bonus may be worth far more than forcing another dangerous center target. **Strategic inference.**

**Playing behind:** Hunt the lit **1K spinner** and max doubled bonus. The spinner can produce tens of thousands immediately, while 40K at drain gives a second major swing opportunity. If Extra Balls are live, favorable swinging-target state is even more valuable. [S2][S4]

**Key recurring decision:** **Directly hit the swinging target to manipulate the feature state, or let safer top-lane/kicker circulation advance it indirectly?** Strong tournament play generally prefers patience unless the needed state is worth the rebound risk. [S2]

## Danger zones

* **Swinging target:** Bob's guide and owner commentary treat direct center-target work as a major drain risk. [S2] ([Pinside][7])
* **Side-saucer kickouts:** strategically valuable but must be dead-bounced, trapped or allowed into a lower kicker based on the copy. [S2]
* **Lower kicker → center target:** often creates a useful loop, but an unfavorable rebound can immediately become center-drain action. [S2]
* **Upper-area exits:** horseshoe/top play is relatively safe for scoring, but feeds return uncontrolled through the two pops.
* **Tilt:** especially expensive here because it erases a potentially **40K** doubled bonus. [S4]

## Trivia

* Space Odyssey is the **two-player version of Space Mission**, which Williams produced in much larger numbers. [S1][S3] ([Kineticist][2])
* Williams promoted it in June 1976 as retaining Space Mission's “Moving Bonus Target,” “Twin Turn Around,” Extra Ball and replay features. [S3] ([World Radio History][3])
* Steve Kordek developed the original Space Mission concept around the **1975 Apollo-Soyuz mission**. [S6] ([Scribd][8])
* Christian Marche's Space Odyssey backglass adapts imagery associated with **Robert McCall's space artwork**; Princeton's restored machine documents the visual connection. [S4] ([McGraw Commons][4])
* Williams sold the two-player game to distributors for about **$780** according to surviving company-cost research cited by Princeton. [S4]

### Uncertainties and conflicts

* **Spinner qualification:** factory-derived restoration documentation and experienced repair analysis say the 1K spinner is controlled by **specific positions of the swinging-target unit**, with liberal/medium/conservative adjustments. [S4][S5] An older 2018 edition of Bob Matthews' *EM-cyclopedia* instead says the spinner lights when bonus reaches **29K**. [S7] That conflicts with the verified **20K maximum bonus** and with later/current Bob's Guide material, so the 29K statement appears erroneous and should **not** go on the final page. 
* **Exact A/B/C → eject-hole relay behavior:** factory summaries and current strategy guides agree that A/B/C progress enables the Double-Bonus saucer route [S2][S4], while individual repair threads report nuances about which A/B/C relay lights which pair of holes [S5]. Use the visible saucer lamps on the actual machine rather than narrating a more specific letter rule.
* **Release month:** contemporary trade coverage places Space Odyssey in release in **June 1976** [S3], while some modern combined Space Mission/Odyssey databases display January for both models. Prefer June for Space Odyssey. ([World Radio History][3])
