## Thirty-second game plan

1. **Observed strategy:** start by spelling **GROOVY** in order: current code awards a **20% boost to all scoring for the rest of that ball**. [S1][S4] ([Spooky Pinball][2])
2. Qualify a **story mode** with white-lit ramps and start it at the scoop beneath the right ramp. Ideally prepare a multiball first, then stack **Cabin or Double Deadite Multiball underneath the mode**. [S3][S4] ([TiltForums][3])
3. Before the scoring barrage, activate **Blood Flood** for playfield multiplication and exploit the **Dagger multiplier**: an inlane lights the opposite orbit; current code gives a roughly 10-second build-up window after activation. [S1][S4] ([Spooky Pinball][2])
4. **Cash-out:** use the resulting multiplied mode/jackpot shots rather than continuing setup indefinitely. Double Deadite is especially stack-friendly; Boomstick offers a more complicated, higher-risk multiball route. [S3][S4]
5. **Alternate line:** concentrate on Cabin locks and straightforward multiball scoring rather than deeper movie progression. Cabin progress persists unusually well, but the lock shot itself can be dangerous. [S1][S5] ([Spooky Pinball][2])

## Core rules and persistence

At game start the player chooses **Movie 1 or Movie 2**, determining the available four story modes. White-lit ramps qualify modes; the first needs one qualifying ramp, with later starts requiring more. The scoop beneath the right ramp starts the selected mode. The Necronomicon captive ball changes selection; the action button can defer the queued mode and force another qualification cycle. [S3] ([TiltForums][3])

**Current major leverage:**

* **GROOVY:** complete the six inline drops in order for **+20% all scoring for the remainder of the ball**. Added in v2025.11.26. [S1] ([Spooky Pinball][2])
* **Blood Flood:** three targets in the pop-bumper nest activate a playfield multiplier. Current authoritative release notes confirm a multi-stage “1/2/3” Blood Flood display, but do not document the exact current multiplier progression. [S1][S3] ([Spooky Pinball][2])
* **Dagger multiplier:** inlanes light the opposite orbit; current code added a timed multiplier build-up activated with the Launch button. [S1] ([Spooky Pinball][2])
* **Hail to the King:** 25 Deadite hits qualify this wizard; completing it grants a **global scoring multiplier**, but that reward is **disabled by default in Tournament Mode**. [S1] ([Spooky Pinball][2])

**Cabin persistence is unusual:** since v2025.05.28 the Cabin drops retain state between most modes, balls **and players**; physical locks can therefore be stolen in multiplayer. [S1][S3] ([Spooky Pinball][2])

Double Deadite Multiball explicitly stacks broadly with other modes/multiballs. Cabin is likewise strategically useful under a running story mode. v2026.07.15 fixed an illegal Delta 88 stack, so commentators should not assume everything stacks with everything. [S1][S3]

## Secondary features

* **Mystery:** spelling GROOVY lights Mystery at the right orbit; the player uses the action/launch control to stop a moving display award. Documented awards include points, Deadite progress, outlane save, Bad-Linda boosts and an extra tilt warning. Since v2025.12.19, Mystery difficulty increases after each attempt. [S1][S3] ([Spooky Pinball][2])
* **Good Linda / Bad Linda:** right scoop rotates Linda. Good Linda makes mode rules easier for reduced scoring; Bad Linda increases difficulty/reward. [S3] ([The Pinball Archive][4])
* **Linda Tricks:** pop activity can trigger positive or negative effects; current code formalized direction-dependent positive/negative awards. Reverse flippers can be disabled by adjustment. [S1] ([Spooky Pinball][2])
* **Video Mode:** added in v2025.10.03 via hidden “lost tapes”; v2026.04.01 subsequently adjusted scoring and added brief respawn invincibility. [S1] ([Spooky Pinball][2])
* **Extra ball:** old rulesheets’ “seven Hand hits” rule is stale. v2025.10.03 moved Extra Ball to the **second Hand award**. Maximum EBs are adjustment-sensitive. [S1]
* **Action button:** among other state-dependent functions, can lower the cellar door during single-ball play, change queued modes and collect Boomstick button jackpots. [S1][S3]

## What to watch

1. **GROOVY completed:** the player now has +20% scoring until the drain; a valuable signal that setup is turning into scoring. [S1]
2. **White ramps / right-ramp scoop:** white ramps mean mode qualification; a lit scoop means the player can convert that setup into the selected story mode. [S3]
3. **Balls visibly parked in the Cabin:** Cabin Multiball progress is banked—but in multiplayer those physical locks are not sacred to the player who made them. [S1][S3]
4. **Deadites rise / Blood Flood area lights:** pop-up Deadites signal Double Deadite progression; the three targets inside the pop cluster are the separate Blood Flood multiplier setup. [S3]

## Match strategy

**Playing ahead:** **Strategic inference:** secure GROOVY, take a prepared story-mode/multiball stack, and favor the easier Cabin/Deadite progression over repeatedly exposing the ball to the Hand, Deadites or lower playfield. In Tournament Mode, do not plan around Hail to the King’s persistent global-X reward because it is disabled by default. [S1]

**Playing behind:** build **GROOVY + Blood Flood + Dagger**, then bring a story mode and multiple stackable multiballs together. Deadite Frenzy or Boomstick inside that multiplier package offers much greater variance than taking an isolated multiball. [S3][S4]

**Key recurring decision:** **start the available multiball now or save it until a mode and multipliers are ready?** The game rewards stacking heavily, but every additional setup shot—especially Cabin, Blood Flood or bash-toy work—risks losing the ball before collecting anything.

## Danger zones

* **Cabin lock:** owners repeatedly report tight lock attempts and lock releases capable of going straight down the middle; strongly setup-sensitive. [S5] ([Pinside][5])
* **Normal plunge into GROOVY:** documented machines can rebound from the drops directly toward the right outlane; Spooky Luke specifically recommended adjusting launch power for this behavior. [S5] ([Pinside][6])
* **Pop-up Deadites:** central bash shots can produce uncontrolled rebounds; especially dangerous when attacked from a fast ball. [S4]
* **Evil Ash Hand:** another central moving bash target where a miss/rebound can immediately turn toward the drain. **Observed strategy**, not a deterministic feed. [S4]
* **Fruit-cellar lower playfield:** failure to find Lock & Load within its limited window/drain allowance loses the Boomstick setup opportunity. [S3]

* It combines the first two films, with **new Bruce Campbell Ash recordings** alongside original movie clips/audio. [S2]
* Spooky marketed it as the company’s **most mechanism-heavy game to that point**, including a moving Hand, two rising Deadites, rotating Linda, lower playfield and swinging Cabin-ball release. [S2]
