## Identity and versions

* **Black Knight** — Williams Electronics, 1980; model 500, **Williams System 7**, four-player solid-state game. Scope is the original 1980 machine requested here. 
* Design/mechanics/concept: **Steve Ritchie**; software: **Larry DeMar**; art: **Tony Ramunni**; Ritchie also voiced the Black Knight. Standard production is documented at **13,075**. [S3][S4] ([Pinball Archive][1])
* A 1981 **Limited Edition** run of 600 followed. Gameplay is essentially the same; the LE mainly adds cosmetic changes and revised trough/lock switches. [S4] ([Pinside][2])
* No numbered factory software revision was verified; the authoritative factory booklet is dated **December 1980**. A modern unofficial replacement ruleset, **BK25**, exists and completely changes strategy; verify that it is not installed. [S1][S6] ([manualzz.com][3])
* One core feature: **2- or 3-ball Multiball**, with playfield scoring multiplied by balls remaining.

## Thirty-second game plan

1. **Go upstairs immediately.** Competitive strategy is essentially “up top all day”: use the left ramp/upper-playfield entrances, then control with the two upper flippers. [S3] ([Silverball Rules][4])
2. **Lock three balls via the upper mini-orbit** whenever possible. Depending on adjustments, completing the timed upper drop bank may be required to light each lock. [S1][S3]
3. **Start 3-ball from the upper lock rather than the lower eject hole.** All scoring becomes **3× while three balls live, then 2× with two**; beginning upstairs keeps the balls safely contained there longer. **Observed strategy.** [S1][S3] ([manualzz.com][3])
4. During multiball, exploit the timed **Mystery** and **spinner** opportunities: left inlane → 20K–99K Mystery at the center ramp; right inlane → lit spinner at **2,500/spin**. Mystery is also multiplied in multiball. [S1]
5. **Alternate strategy:** if locking is difficult, repeatedly complete the upper drop banks while keeping the ball upstairs; this can score steadily and also earns Magna-Saves. [S3]

## Core rules and persistence

* Locking a ball while a LOCK arrow flashes makes that lock permanent and lights the **lower eject hole**. Three upper locks start 3-ball; with one locked ball, the lower eject starts 2-ball; with two locked, it starts 3-ball. [S1][S2] ([manualzz.com][3])
* **3-ball = 3× scoring; 2-ball = 2× scoring.** There are no jackpots—the multiplier itself is the payday. [S1][S2]
* Lock qualification is adjustment-sensitive. Factory **LOCK lamp memory is OFF** by default, while physically locked balls themselves are remembered. This can make the difference between trivially repeatable and genuinely difficult multiball. [S1] ([manualzz.com][3])
* All four 3-banks are **timed**: after the first target falls, finish the bank before it resets. Factory timing is adjustable from roughly **6–15 seconds**. Completion scores 10K, advances its arrow and lights a Magna-Save. [S1]
* Bonus builds to **49K**; the lower turnaround raises Bonus X through **5×**, making maximum end-of-ball bonus **245K**. [S1][S3]
* **Last Chance:** on the final regular ball, if balls remain locked, lit outside rollovers can release them back into play—but **without Multiball scoring**. Easy commentary trap. [S1]
* Factory multiplayer games can award the leader a postgame **Bonus Ball** timed session; this is highly inappropriate for normal tournament scoring unless intentionally enabled. [S1]

## Skill shots

`None found.`

A controlled plunge can be used to feed the upper flippers and quickly attack the lock orbit, but stock code has no distinct plunge-based skill-shot award. [S1][S3]

## Secondary features

* **Magna-Save:** completing drop banks lights player-controlled magnets above the outlanes. Press the corresponding red cabinet button to energize one; factory duration is **5 seconds**, adjustable 3–9. It attempts to hold the ball and drop it into the inlane, but is not guaranteed. [S1][S3]
* **Mystery Score:** left inlane briefly lights the center-ramp rollunder for **20K–99K**. In 3-ball this can become as much as **297K**. [S1]
* **Super Spinner:** right inlane briefly raises the spinner from 100 to **2,500 per spin**. This is one of the best repeatable multiball shots. [S1][S2]
* **Extra Balls:** earned through repeated drop-bank completions and collected at the upper left-ramp rollover or turnaround. Factory adjustment can set maximum EBs to **0** for tournament use. [S1]
* **Bonus Ball:** with 2+ players, factory default awards the winner **30 seconds** of replenishing-ball play with both Magna-Saves lit. Verify disabled. [S1]
* **BK25 warning:** the unofficial 2025 code adds skill shots, modes, jackpots, playfield multipliers and radically different multiballs. None of this page applies if BK25 is installed. [S6] ([Pinball Refresh][5])

## What to watch

* **Balls physically stacked in the upper lock:** the player is approaching 3× scoring; three locks launch multiball.
* **Magna-Save lamp lit:** the corresponding outlane has an armed, manually triggered rescue.
* **Right inlane → spinner flashes:** expect an immediate left-side spinner attempt at 2,500/spin. [S1]
* **Left inlane → Mystery flashes:** expect the player to shoot the center ramp before the short timer expires. [S1]

## Match strategy

**Playing ahead:** Stay upstairs, lock deliberately, and prioritize ball survival once 3-ball begins. Do not chase the lower turnaround purely for Bonus X; let incidental multiball action advance it. Armed Magna-Saves make conservative control even stronger. **Observed strategy.** [S3]

**Playing behind:** The highest leverage is **3-ball + timed Mystery/spinner scoring**. If there is not enough time or ball control to earn the third upper lock, taking the lower eject with one lock for immediate 2-ball can be justified. **Strategic inference.**

**Key recurring decision:** **Take multiball now from the lower eject, or keep risking single-ball play for the superior three-ball upper start?** Strong players generally prefer three locked upstairs because all three balls begin on the safer upper level. [S3]

## Danger zones

* **Upper-level exit:** balls dropping down the ramp in front of the upper flippers can head straight down the middle. [S3]
* **Half-figure-eight / turnaround:** misses or exits can feed the left outlane. [S3]
* **Lower drop banks:** direct rebounds frequently turn into center or outlane danger. [S3]
* **Partial ramp shots:** a ball that climbs but fails to make an upper ramp can return violently toward the flippers. [S3]
* **Magna-Save failure:** the magnet may merely alter the ball's path or drop it poorly and still produce an outlane drain. [S3]

## Trivia

* Black Knight was the first **solid-state pinball machine with a two-level playfield**; earlier mechanical games had experimented with multiple levels. [S3][S4] ([Pinball Archive][6])
* It introduced Williams' famous **Magna-Save**, a player-controlled electromagnet placed above each outlane. [S1][S4]
* **Steve Ritchie** designed the game and also supplied the Black Knight's taunting voice. [S4]
* Williams produced roughly **13,075 standard machines**, followed by 600 cosmetically upgraded Limited Editions in 1981. [S4]
* The original game's multiball predates the modern jackpot model: its major scoring mechanic is simply **2×/3× everything**, which is why longevity matters so much. [S1][S2]

### Uncertainties and conflicts

* **Spinner value:** the factory December 1980 switch chart [S1] and contemporary Williams advertising [S5] specify **2,500/spin when lit**. Several later secondary rulesheets claim **5,000/spin**. The final page should use **2,500**, since the primary documentation is explicit. ([manualzz.com][3])
* **Lock difficulty/memory:** both are operator-adjustable and substantially change tournament difficulty. [S1]
* **Physical feeds:** upper-ramp exits, flipper strength and Magna-Save effectiveness vary significantly by machine. [S3]
* **Custom code:** BK25 is a complete rules replacement, not a minor ROM revision. [S6]
