## Identity and versions

**Fish Tales** — Williams, 1992. Designed by **Mark Ritchie**; software by Mark Penacho; artwork by Pat McMahon; music/sound by Chris Granner. Williams **WPC Fliptronics II**, DMD, two “lightning” flippers. Production: **13,640**. [S4] ([Pinside][1])

* **Rules basis: `rom` — L-5**, the final recognized Williams production game ROM. Earlier production revisions include L-3/L-4; later D-series/text patches are community modifications, not later factory rules. [S1][S4] ([PinMAME Machine Reference][2])
* One major feature: **Caster’s Club 3-ball Multiball**. “Instant Multiball” is an alternate way to start the same multiball rules. [S1][S2] ([MameChannel.it][3])

## Thirty-second game plan

1. **Safer tournament route: alternate the lit boat ramps.** Six lit ramp shots qualify **Monster Fish**; shoot the left-orbit spinner to collect its hurry-up. The first starts at 20M, later catches can start progressively higher up to 50M. [S2][S3]
2. **High-ceiling route: Caster’s Club Multiball.** Lock three balls, then during multiball shoot Caster’s Club to light the eject-hole jackpot: **20M → 30M → 40M**. [S2][S3]
3. After all three jackpots, the **captive ball becomes a repeatable 100M Super Jackpot** for the rest of that multiball. Jackpot progress carries between multiballs; completing a sequence raises the next jackpot set’s multiplier, eventually to **6x**. [S2][S3]
4. **Rock the Boat can stack with multiball:** every boat ramp/captive-ball shot scores 10M, making a ramp-heavy multiball especially valuable. [S2]
5. Central decision: **reliable Monster Fish ramp grinding versus riskier Caster’s Club setup for enormous Supers.** Strong tournament sources document both approaches depending on game difficulty and required score. [S5][S6] ([Pinball Info][4])

## Core rules and persistence

Only **lit** ramp shots advance the six-fish school toward Monster Fish. Ramp lighting encourages alternating sides. Monster Fish is collected at the left spinner and falls toward a 5M floor; collecting it increases the starting value of the next opportunity, so even a late 5M catch can be strategically worthwhile. [S2][S3] ([GameFAQs][5])

Multiball jackpot progress is unusually persistent: the current 20M/30M/40M stage survives between multiballs, and once Super Jackpot has been reached, later sequences receive increasing jackpot multipliers up to 6x. This makes a merely mediocre first multiball potentially valuable setup for later balls. [S2][S3] ([GameFAQs][5])

**Tournament Play is materially important in multiplayer.** Williams adjustment A.1-26 says Tournament Play keeps multiball and jackpot progress equal between players rather than allowing it to carry to the next player. [S1] ([MameChannel.it][3])

Spell **L-I-E** to advance Bonus X to 7x; flippers lane-change the lit letters. Hold Bonus, the first captive-ball ladder award, preserves bonus and multiplier into the next ball. [S1][S2] 

## Skill shots

**Fast Cast** — effectively Fish Tales’ skill-shot/launch combo. After a normal ball launch, quickly shoot **Caster’s Club**; it starts at **2M** and increases 1M per success to 10M. It can also be scored immediately after a Long Cast and after certain lock launches, but **not after an Auto Cast ball-save launch**. [S2][S3] ([Pinball GX][6])

There is no separate verified Super or secret skill shot.

## Secondary features

* **Auto Cast:** factory Ball Save Time is **7 seconds**; the flashing Auto Cast insert indicates protection. It activates at ball start and multiball start. Outlane Auto Cast is a separate factory-adjustable feature. [S1][S3] ([Coin Operator Shop][7])
* **Video Mode:** second captive-ball award lights the upper-right eject hole. The CAST button on the fishing-rod launcher fires torpedoes at boats; a perfect 20/20 run earns a 20M completion bonus. [S1][S2] ([MameChannel.it][3])
* **Extra Ball:** lit either by the fourth captive-ball award or Fish Finder; Video Mode can also award one directly. L-5 tournament/EB-disabled configurations can substitute a **20M “BS” / Big Score**, but community testing documents quirks in exactly which EB opportunities convert or disappear. [S2][S7] ([Tilt Forums][8])
* **Fish Finder:** right inlane lights a timed left-orbit/eject-hole award. Normal awards are state-aware/random: Extra Ball Lit, Super Jets, Instant Multiball, Captive Ball advance, Bonus X, or points. [S2][S3] ([Pinball GX][6])
* **Player control:** flippers lane-change L-I-E; the cabinet CAST button operates Video Mode. [S1]

## What to watch

1. **Six school-of-fish inserts completed:** Monster Fish is lit; expect an immediate left-orbit spinner attempt before the hurry-up falls. [S2]
2. **Tackle-box jackpot lamps:** show 20M/30M/40M multiball progression; after all three, the captive ball becomes the repeatable Super Jackpot. [S2]
3. **Captive-ball ladder:** tells whether the player is building toward Rock the Boat, Extra Ball or Instant Multiball via Long Casts. [S2]
4. **Flashing Auto Cast:** the player still has ball-save protection and may intentionally take a normally dangerous shot. [S1]

## Match strategy

**Playing ahead:**
**Observed strategy:** live on the ramps and Monster Fish, particularly on a brutal tournament setup. A 20–50M Monster Fish has much less setup risk than a Caster’s Club multiball that may drain before producing a jackpot. [S5][S6] ([Pinball Info][4])

**Playing behind:**
Commit to multiball. The payoff curve changes completely once the three jackpots are banked: **100M repeatable Supers**, eventually multiplied as high as 6x. If Rock the Boat is available, running it simultaneously adds 10M boat shots while protecting the ball with multiball. [S2][S3]

**Key recurring decision:**
**Monster Fish now or build multiball?** Ramps give consistent medium scores and controlled returns; Caster’s Club is harder and can produce nothing, but reaching Super Jackpot can decide the match in a handful of shots.

## Danger zones

* **Caster’s Club:** the key high-value shot is narrow and difficult; tournament players often avoid forcing it when already ahead. [S5]
* **Captive ball:** powerful center rebound; dangerous outside ball-save/multiball.
* **Stringer targets:** historical strategy specifically warns that deliberately attacking them can produce drains; let incidental hits build Feeding Frenzy unless close. [S2] ([GameFAQs][5])
* **Upper L-I-E / pops:** surrender controlled possession and can produce fast exits.
* **Lightning flippers:** Fish Tales uses shortened Williams flippers, widening the center gap versus standard bats and making marginal saves less forgiving. [S3] ([Pinball GX][6])

## Trivia

* Fish Tales sold **13,640 units**, making it one of Williams’ highest-production DMD-era games. [S4] ([Pinball Database][9])
* The physical **fishing reel is the three-ball lock mechanism**; balls are carried across the top of the game and released back into play. [S1][S3]
* The cabinet launcher is styled like a **fishing-rod handle**, and its button doubles as the Video Mode firing control. [S3]
* Mark Ritchie later reused a Fish Tales reference as the **“Fish of Tales”** jackpot in *Indiana Jones: The Pinball Adventure*. [S4]
* The animated fish topper’s moving tail is one of the game's signature mechanical presentation features. [S3]
