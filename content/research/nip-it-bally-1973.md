## Identity and versions

* **Nip-It** — Bally Manufacturing, July 1973; model **940**, four-player electro-mechanical game with mechanical score reels. Design/mechanics/concept by **Ted Zale**; art by **Dick White**. Bally produced **4,580**. [S1][S4] ([Kineticist][1])
* Scope is the Bally game specified in the supplied brief. 
* Signature mechanisms: **zipper flippers**, the player-controlled **Balligator** ball grabber, a right-outlane **Free Ball Gate**, and a physical captive-ball **two-ball multiball**. [S1][S2] 
* Factory manual allows **3- or 5-ball play**. There is no software/version issue, but ball count and physical mechanism condition materially affect tournament play. [S1] 
* No bonus multiplier is documented: the strategically important bonus simply builds from **1K to 10K** and can be collected repeatedly before the ball drains. [S1][S2] 

## Thirty-second game plan

1. **Zip the flippers closed immediately** by hitting the center mushroom bumper. The reduced center gap makes controlled play dramatically safer. **Observed strategy.** [S3] ([Silverball Rules][2])
2. Build bonus toward **10K**, preferably by catching balls with the Balligator: its right alley scores 1K and advances bonus 1K. [S1][S3] 
3. Put a ball in the **Bonus Hole**. This physically locks it and serves a second ball to the shooter lane; the two Release Captive Ball targets light. [S1] 
4. Once bonus is valuable, hit either lit Release target: it **collects the full bonus without resetting it and releases the captive ball**, starting two-ball play. Re-lock and repeat for repeated bonus cash-outs. [S1][S3] ([Pinside][3])
5. Alternate safer route: with zipped flippers, repeatedly work the left alley/top saucer → Balligator loop rather than forcing multiball. **Observed strategy.** [S3] ([Silverball Rules][2])

## Core rules and persistence

* **Bonus:** starts at 1K, advances 1K from the Advance Bonus target and Balligator/right alley, and tops at **10K**. [S1] 
* **Repeated collect:** with a ball captive in the Bonus Hole, either lit Release target awards the current bonus and ejects the locked ball. **The bonus does not reset.** Drain also awards bonus, but then resets it to 1K. [S1] ([Pinside][3])
* **Physical lock:** entering the Bonus Hole does **not**, according to the factory instructions, itself collect bonus; it captures the ball, serves the second ball and lights the Release targets. This distinction matters because some later strategy summaries phrase the sequence imprecisely. [S1][S3] 
* **Zipper flippers:** center mushroom closes them; the white rollover beside it opens them. They also reopen at end of turn or tilt. [S1] 
* Physical locked balls are **shared state**: stock Nip-It permits hard lock stealing, and a ball can remain locked across players—and even across games. [S3][S5] ([Tilt Forums][4])
* No modern stacking, jackpots or EOB multiplier system exists. The scoring leverage is repeated 10K bonus collection plus the survival/control advantage of zipped flippers.

## Skill shots

* **Balligator skill sequence:** plunge through one of the two upper white rollovers, which lights either the red or yellow bumpers; ideally fall into the **500-point top saucer**. On its eject toward the upper-right, time the separate Gator button so the Balligator catches the ball. The resulting right alley scores **1K + advances bonus 1K** and normally feeds toward the right flipper. Bob's Guide explicitly identifies this as the skill shot. [S1][S3] 
* No separate super/secret skill shot was found.

## Secondary features

* **Balligator button:** a second right-side cabinet button operates the grabber throughout play. It can catch a correctly positioned ball or, if triggered early, physically deflect a near-miss to the left. This is genuine player-controlled ball interaction, not automatic animation. [S1][S3] 
* **Free Ball Gate:** when the upper-left Open Gate target is **lit**, hitting it opens the right-outlane gate for one use; it remains open until a ball passes through or that player's turn ends. [S1] 
* **Left alley kickback:** entering the left alley scores 1K and mechanically kicks the ball back to the top for another scoring opportunity. [S1][S2] 
* No conventional Extra Ball award, video mode, mystery award or automatic ball saver was verified.

## What to watch

* **Flippers visibly zipped together:** the player has dramatically reduced the center gap and will often settle the ball before making micro-flips. [S3] ([Silverball Rules][2])
* **Bonus approaching 10K:** expect the player to prioritize the Bonus Hole → Release-target cycle.
* **Ball sitting in the right-side Bonus Hole:** the second ball is now in play and both Release targets are lit; the player may deliberately delay multiball while building bonus. [S1] 
* **Open Gate target lit / right gate open:** an otherwise fatal right-outlane trip has a physical rescue available.

## Match strategy

**Playing ahead:** Get the flippers zipped and favor controlled Balligator/left-alley circulation. Once bonus is respectable, take the guaranteed Release-target collect rather than extending single-ball setup solely to reach 10K. **Strategic inference.**

**Playing behind:** Build the full **10K bonus**, lock a ball, release it for 10K, then repeatedly **re-lock → release → collect another 10K**. A sustained two-ball cycle is the game's main comeback engine. **Observed strategy.** [S3] ([Silverball Rules][2])

**Key recurring decision:** **Release the captive ball now, or leave it parked while continuing to build bonus?** Releasing gives multiball and the cash-out; waiting keeps play simpler and makes every later release more valuable.

## Danger zones

* **Open zipper flippers:** the center gap is enormous compared with the zipped position; closing them is the first survival priority. [S3] ([Silverball Rules][2])
* **Pop-bumper exits:** Bob's Guide says they are more likely to find a sling or drain than a flipper. [S3] ([Silverball Rules][2])
* **Balligator return:** on some copies the supposedly useful right-chute feed can head straight down the middle. [S3] ([Silverball Rules][2])
* **Standup rebounds:** nearly every important non-saucer shot carries direct rebound danger on this open EM layout. [S3]
* **Multiball with flippers open:** two balls plus the wide center gap changes a controlled scoring opportunity into immediate chaos.

## Trivia

* Nip-It was **Ted Zale's final design for Bally**, ending a career that introduced or popularized several of Bally's signature mechanical ideas. [S6] ([Scribd][5])
* It is generally identified as the **last electro-mechanical pinball to use Bally's zipper flippers**. [S6] ([Scribd][5])
* Its Balligator was marketed as a major new player skill device: the player activates it with a dedicated cabinet button behind the right flipper button. [S2] ([World Radio History][6])
* Nip-It famously appeared in **Arnold's on *Happy Days***, despite the 1973 machine being anachronistic for the show's 1950s setting. [S4] ([Pinside][7])
* Bally produced **4,580** machines. [S4] ([Pinside][8])

### Uncertainties and conflicts

* **When is bonus actually collected?** Bally's factory instructions [S1] are clear: locking the ball **lights the two Release targets**, and hitting either lit target scores the current bonus and ejects the captive ball. Bob's Guide [S3] contains one sentence saying the lock “will score your bonus,” although its quick strategy and subsequent text describe the release/relock cycle. For commentary, follow the **factory rule: collect happens on the Release target**. 
* **Ball count:** the factory manual tells the operator to install **two balls**, and modern game archives classify Nip-It as a two-ball multiball. A contemporary 1973 Cash Box promotional passage appears in OCR to suggest “up to three balls,” conflicting with the factory hardware documentation. Treat Nip-It as **two-ball multiball** unless this specific machine is demonstrably modified. [S1][S4][S7] 
* **Physical lock stealing:** well documented for stock games, including locks persisting across games, but tournament organizers may deliberately intervene. [S3][S5] ([Tilt Forums][4])
* Balligator feed, outlane opening and zipper-flipper effectiveness are highly setup/mechanism-condition dependent.
