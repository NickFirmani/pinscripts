## Identity and versions

**Mars Trek** — **Sonic / Segasa**, Spain, **August 1977**, model **993**. Four-player electromechanical game with mechanical reels, **three flippers**, two spinners and no multiball. Sonic's period flyer identifies the manufacturer as Segasa “d.b.a.” Sonic and advertises adjustable **3- or 5-ball** play. [S1][S3]

No reliable designer, programmer or artist credits were found; current archives likewise leave the design team unidentified. [S3]

## Thirty-second game plan

1. **UTAD — up top all day.** Repeatedly send the ball through the left/top route to build bonus toward its **100K maximum**. [S2]
2. Collect **E and K** naturally through the return lanes; together they light **Double Bonus**. Only shoot the dangerous alternating E/K center target when it completes the doubler and the bonus is already worth protecting. [S1][S2]
3. At max bonus, decide whether to **cash the Collect Bonus saucer** or preserve the 100K for a potential **200K end-of-ball Double Bonus**. The saucer itself does **not** double. [S2]
4. Once bonus is maxed, the spinner becomes a strong repeatable scoring route; experienced-owner evidence documents **1K per spin** when lit. [S4]
5. Alternate high-risk scoring: complete **MARS** for 10K and **TREK** to make the center target worth 10K, but competitive guidance generally considers these shots worse than bonus play because of the rebounds. [S1][S2]

## Core rules and persistence

**Bonus** advances from the top saucer, E/K return lanes, upper-left star rollover, and the spinner behind the right-side saucer; it maxes at **100,000**. [S2]

**E + K lights Double Bonus.** This applies to **end-of-ball bonus only**. Shooting the separate Collect Bonus saucer scores the current bonus at normal value even if Double Bonus is lit. [S1][S2]

That creates Mars Trek's defining tournament decision: at 100K with Double Bonus lit, draining safely is worth **200K bonus**, while deliberately collecting gives only 100K immediately and forces the player to rebuild. Bob's tournament guide notes the collect leaves/respots one bonus step afterward. [S2]

**MARS** completion scores **10K**. Completing **TREK** makes the center target score **10K**. Completing **MARS TREK** lights **Special**. [S1]

At exactly **50K bonus**, Extra Ball lights; passing through the lit semi-circular **Runway** awards it. Because collecting or advancing bonus changes that 50K state, the qualification can be fleeting. [S1][S5]

No game-to-game persistence exists; this is a conventional EM. Player-specific mechanical state such as bonus and letters lasts through the current ball/game as implemented by the score/player units.

## Skill shots

No manufacturer-named scored skill shot was found.

**Observed tournament skill shot:** plunge for the **top saucer**. It advances bonus and also spots a top **T or R** letter. [S2]

The correct plunge can be machine-specific because the saucer kickout and the alternative R/T lane feeds differ dramatically in danger. A commentator should describe this as the competitive plunge choice, not an official named “Skill Shot.”

## Secondary features

**Extra Ball:** at **50K bonus**, the semi-circular Runway lights; passing through it awards an Extra Ball. The game is operator-adjustable for replay/Extra Ball behavior, so tournament handling must be confirmed. [S1]

**Special:** completing **MARS TREK** lights Special. Tournament value depends on event/operator handling. [S1]

## What to watch

* **Bonus ladder at/near 100K:** the player is approaching the central cash-versus-hold decision. [S2]
* **E and K lit:** Double Bonus is active; a drain can now be worth twice the stored bonus. [S1][S2]
* **Spinner lit:** bonus is maxed and repeatable spinner scoring has become attractive. [S4]
* **50K bonus / Runway Extra Ball lit:** a temporary Extra Ball opportunity exists if tournament rules allow it. [S1]

## Match strategy

**Playing ahead:** build bonus safely, get Double Bonus naturally, and **do not automatically collect 100K**. If the ball is controllable and E/K are complete, protecting a potential 200K end-of-ball bonus may be worth more than taking the immediate 100K saucer. **Observed strategy.** [S2]

**Playing behind:** once bonus reaches 100K, the Collect Bonus saucer can provide a guaranteed large cash-out and let the player rebuild. A strong lit spinner also offers much faster immediate scoring. Deliberately shooting the center E/K target becomes more defensible if it completes Double Bonus on a large stored bonus. [S2][S4]

**Key recurring decision:** **collect bonus now, or preserve it for Double Bonus at drain?** This is the rule that most often explains apparently contradictory decisions by good players.

## Danger zones

* **Top R/T lane feeds:** both can ricochet through the center/MARS area toward the left outlane; T normally carries more speed than R. [S2]
* **E/K center target:** its left-facing angle produces the game's most notorious left-outlane rebound. [S2]
* **Upper-right flipper:** competitive guidance recommends using it mainly for defensive deflections; attacking MARS from there creates dangerous cross-playfield motion. [S2]
* **Top saucer kickout:** can head toward the right sling/post and may be either controllable or terrible depending on the individual machine. [S2]
* **Runway/Collect Bonus miss:** a player trying to spinner-loop can accidentally fall into the saucer and cash an undoubled bonus they intended to preserve. [S2][S4]

## Trivia

* Mars Trek was built in **Madrid by Segasa under the Sonic name**, and its flyer explicitly uses the unusual wording Segasa “d.b.a.” Sonic. [S1]
* Its **two spinners sit in front of the same horseshoe/runway**, an unusual geometry even among 1970s EMs. [S4]
* Sonic advertised a **polyurethane playfield**, and surviving examples are often noted for unusually durable playfield finishes. The polyurethane claim itself is period manufacturer evidence. [S1]
* Mars Trek appeared in August 1977 alongside a run of Spanish Sonic games whose rules and hardware had a distinctly American/Williams-style feel. Contemporary historical coverage specifically groups it with Segasa's 1977 output. [S6]
* The machine's asymmetric, fast three-flipper layout is unusually aggressive for an EM and has made it a recurring tournament selection despite its rarity. [S2][S4]

### Uncertainties and conflicts

* **Exact bonus persistence after Collect Bonus:** [S2] states that collection effectively leaves the player at one bonus step afterward, but the period flyer does not document the reset/respotted value. Treat this as strong tournament documentation rather than manufacturer-confirmed wording.
* **Spinner value:** experienced-owner evidence says the spinner becomes **1K/spin at max bonus** [S4]; the primary flyer does not state its lit value. Verify on the venue machine before printing the exact 1K number.
* **MARS value after completion:** one owner report says individual MARS targets subsequently rise from 1K to 10K [S4], while the manufacturer flyer only guarantees **10K for making MARS** [S1]. The more conservative manufacturer-supported interpretation is used above.
* **Design credits:** no sufficiently reliable designer/art/programming attribution was found. [S3]
* Physical feed behavior is exceptionally machine-specific; Bob's guide explicitly advises learning the R lane, T lane and saucer kickout separately before choosing the safest UTAD route. [S2]
