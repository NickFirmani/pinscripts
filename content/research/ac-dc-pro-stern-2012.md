## Identity and versions

* **AC/DC (Pro)** — Stern Pinball, March 2012; Stern S.A.M. system, DMD, 2 flippers. Design: **Steve Ritchie**; software: **Lyman F. Sheats Jr.**; art credited to **Kevin O'Connor**. [S1][S4][S5] ([Stern Pinball][1])
* Scope is specifically the 2012 Pro requested in the supplied brief. 
* **Original 2012 Pro:** standup target banks; Bell is a center lane/standup rather than the Premium/LE swinging-bell/saucer mechanism; no lower “Hell” mini-playfield. Premium/LE also use drop targets and a crossover diverter. [S1][S5] ([Stern Pinball][1])
* **Hardware caveat:** Stern introduced an **AC/DC LED Pro in 2014 with an actual swinging bell**, while stating the other game features/rules were unchanged. Do not assume every “Pro” has the 2012 fixed Bell arrangement. [S7] ([Stern Pinball][2])
* **Latest code actually verified:** **V1.70, Feb. 12, 2018.** It materially reduced first Jam qualification from 6→5 ramps and Tour from 8→7 loops, among other balancing changes. [S4] ([Pinside][3])
* Three main multiballs matter for commentary: **Jam** (ramps), **Album** (target banks), **Tour** (loops). [S3] 

## Thirty-second game plan

1. **Pick a song whose key shot matches what you can safely repeat.** Completing that song's key task lights the five white Musical Note shots — left loop, left ramp, Bell, right ramp, right loop. Song scoring feeds a potentially enormous **Song Jackpot**. **Confirmed rule.** [S3][S5] 
2. **Build a controllable multiball**, most commonly Jam: in V1.70 the first Jam takes **5 ramps**; Tour takes **7 loops**; Album starts after **3 target-bank completions**. All start at the right ramp. [S3][S4] ([Pinside][3])
3. **Bring the Bell multiplier into the scoring window.** Three Bell hits starts **2× playfield for 20 seconds**; the sixth hit reaches **3×**; additional Bell hits refresh the timer. Multiplier progress itself survives the drain. [S3] 
4. **During multiball, complete all five Note shots for an add-a-ball**, using banked VIP Passes to spot difficult remaining shots, while scoring jackpots and maintaining 2×/3×. [S3][S4] 
5. **Cash the Song Jackpot before the ball gets away:** load the Cannon via the right ramp and fire at its assigned AC/DC target, ideally at 2×/3×. It resets on a drain, so waiting for a better multiplier is genuine risk/reward. **Observed strategy:** Jam/ramp play is the conventional controlled route; **War Machine/spinner + Tour** is a viable alternative on a spinner-friendly machine. [S3][S5][S6] 

## Core rules and persistence

* A song is effectively an untimed mode: it continues until the player changes songs. Its key task lights the five white Note shots; completing those shots also adds **2.5M** to the Song Jackpot. [S3][S5] 
* **Song Jackpot:** song-shot scoring is accumulated into it; changing songs does **not** wipe it, but **collecting it or draining does**. It is available initially and re-lights through repeated FIRE-lane completions. [S3] 
* **Bell multiplier:** 3 hits → 2× for 20s; hits 4–5 refresh; hit 6 → 3×; later Bell hits refresh 3×. Cannon awards, jackpots and mode scoring are included. Bell-hit qualification carries ball-to-ball; the active timer does not. [S3] 
* **VIP Passes carry between balls.** Pressing the action/fire button spends one to award game-selected current-song progress; during multiball the game prioritizes missing Note shots needed for add-a-ball. [S3][S4] 
* **Easy commentator trap:** Bowen Kerins's excellent rulesheet covers V1.68, so its “6 ramps / 8 loops” first-multiball numbers are outdated under V1.70; use **5 ramps / 7 loops** if the venue is current. [S3][S4] 
* Album is a 3-ball multiball but normally asks for hazardous standups; its strategically important upside is the possibility of collecting the accumulated Song Jackpot value as a **Song Bonus without resetting the underlying jackpot**. [S3] 

## Skill shots

* **Skill Shot:** plunge into the Jukebox and let the ball drop toward the **A-X-E top lanes**; lane-change to the lit lane. Award: 250K base, +2 Bonus X and **1 VIP Pass**. Useful when the guaranteed VIP or top-lane progress matters more than variance. [S3] 
* **Super Skill Shot:** hold the **right flipper** as the ball leaves the Jukebox; it is kicked down the right loop, then make any white Note shot within 5 seconds. Award: 350K base, +5 Bonus X and **2 VIP Passes**. **Observed strategy:** usually preferred because VIPs are exceptionally valuable during multiball. [S3][S6] 
* These values increment slightly on later successes; both are available on subsequent plunges rather than being ball-1-only. [S3] 

## Secondary features

* **Standard ball save:** factory manual shows **5 seconds**; multiball also begins with a short ball saver. This is highly adjustment-sensitive in tournaments. [S2][S3] ([Stern Pinball][4])
* **Multiball add-a-ball:** first completion of all five lit Note shots during a multiball adds a ball. VIP Passes can spot those shots, making them primarily a survival resource rather than incidental mode points. [S3][S4] 
* **Extra balls:** qualified by target-bank milestones and combo milestones; Kerins documents tournament-mode collection as **2.5M points**, itself eligible for playfield X. Verify the venue's actual tournament configuration. [S3] 
* **Action/fire button:** fires the Cannon and spends VIP Passes. Cannon misses/timeouts can erase most lit Cannon awards, making Cannon selection and execution consequential. [S3] 
* **Tunes N Stuff / “STUFF,” V1.70:** completing the Devil Horn sequence (Bell → right loop → left loop) can light the Jukebox for awards including **7.5M and 10M points**, large enough to swing a short tournament game. V1.70 also lists Hold Bonus, Extra Ball and other awards. Award-selection/competition normalization was not sufficiently verified here. [S4] ([Pinside][3])
* No meaningful video mode or Magna-Save/kickback-style player rescue was verified for the 2012 Pro.

## What to watch

* **Five white arrows lit:** the active song's setup task has been achieved; these shots now score song value and build Song Jackpot. [S3]
* **Rapidly flashing 2×/3× lamps:** playfield multiplier is active; faster flashing means the 20-second timer is nearing expiration. [S3] 
* **Jam / Album / Tour insert flashing:** that multiball is qualified; expect the player to head for the **right ramp** to start it. [S3][S4]
* **Cannon display / callout:** the game previews available Cannon awards and their values. A large displayed Song Jackpot immediately explains why the player may delay collection to establish 2×/3× first. [S3][S5]

## Match strategy

**Playing ahead:** Favor controlled backhand ramps, take a ready Jam rather than forcing target-bank progress, and cash a meaningful Song Jackpot instead of risking several naked Bell shots chasing 3×. **Strategic inference** from the game's jackpot-reset and shot-risk rules. [S3][S5]

**Playing behind:** The volatility button is **Song Jackpot × playfield multiplier**. Build a large jackpot through song/multiball scoring, attack Bell for 2×/3×, then take the Cannon collect. Tour/War Machine or stacked multiball scoring can provide another high-variance route. [S3][S5]

**Key recurring decision:** **cash now versus build/multiply.** A drain wipes the uncollected Song Jackpot, but one more Bell sequence can double or triple it; this is the central strategic tension strong players repeatedly face. **Observed tournament strategy.** [S3] ([Tilt Forums][6])

## Danger zones

* **Bell:** narrow, dangerous on a miss and sometimes dangerous even when made. [S5]
* **AC/DC/TNT/ROCK standups:** hard direct rebounds can turn a scoring attempt into an immediate center/outlane threat. [S3][S5]
* **Cannon into AC/DC targets:** potentially wild ricochet toward the right side/outlane; a complete miss may also erase the award. [S3][S5]
* **Cross-field ramp shots:** higher lateral risk than repeatable backhands; safer players often backhand the ramps. [S3][S5]
* **Weak right-loop/top-lane feed:** can trade controlled orbit return for chaotic lanes/bumpers. [S3]

## Trivia

* Steve Ritchie designed the game and Lyman F. Sheats Jr. wrote the software; the rules are unusually deep for the relatively open five-major-shot layout. [S4][S5] ([Pinside][7])
* The game lets the player choose among **12 AC/DC songs**, and the song choice actually changes scoring rules rather than merely the soundtrack. [S1][S5] ([Stern Pinball][1])
* The original 2012 Pro omitted the Premium/LE lower playfield and drop targets. [S1][S5]
* Stern's unusual **2014 LED Pro refresh added a swinging Bell to the Pro**; Stern said it was doing a small run as the AC/DC license approached expiration. [S7] ([Stern Pinball][2])
* V1.70 arrived almost six years after the game's debut and still contained meaningful balance changes, including easier initial Jam and Tour qualification. [S4] ([Pinside][3])

### Uncertainties and conflicts

* **Version-specific:** [S3] documents V1.68's first Jam/Tour requirements as **6 ramps / 8 loops**; V1.70 explicitly changes these to **5 / 7**. [S4] is controlling if the venue runs current verified code. 
* **Edition-specific:** secondary modern guides often describe “the Pro” as having the fixed Bell lane [S5], while Stern officially added a swinging Bell to the 2014 LED Pro [S7]. For a true 2012 Pro, the fixed arrangement is expected. ([Kineticist][5])
* **Adjustment-dependent:** tournament extra-ball behavior and standard ball-save time require venue confirmation. [S2][S3]
* **STUFF:** V1.70 verifies potentially match-changing 7.5M/10M awards, but the precise award-selection/competition-mode behavior was not established strongly enough to describe as deterministic. [S4]
