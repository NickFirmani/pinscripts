## Identity and versions

* **Cactus Canyon Remake — Special Edition**, Chicago Gaming Company, **2021**. Based on Bally/Williams’ 1998 *Cactus Canyon*, designed by **Matt Coriale and Tom Kopera**, with art by **John Youssi** and music/sound by **Rob Berry**. CGC’s remake software was led by **Sam Zehr**, with Lyman Sheats contributing to completion of the intended original rules. [S1][S2] ([Chicago Gaming Company][1])
* **Special Edition (SE)** has the same core playfield/rules as SE+ and LE. SE+ and LE add the interactive Wild West Shootout topper; LE differences otherwise are cosmetic. [S1][S3] ([Pinside][2])
* The optional **Lyman Sheats Complete Upgrade** is *not* merely cosmetic: it adds a physical saloon/spinner and substantially rewrites Gunfight, Showdown, High Noon and other modes. This brief covers **stock/default “Extended” rules, without the Lyman upgrade**, because the requested catalog entry is the base Remake Special. [S2][S4] ([Chicago Gaming Company][1])
* **Rules basis: `code`.** The latest publicly downloadable remake image I can verify is **v1.1.5**; however, I could not verify a trustworthy exact YYYY-MM-DD release date, so I will not invent one. That software can retain/select the Extended ruleset even on upgrade-equipped games. [S4][S5] ([IFPA Pinball][3])
* Main multiballs under Extended rules: **Gold Mine Multiball, Showdown Multiball, Stampede Multiball**; **High Noon** is the wizard multiball. [S6] ([Cactus Canyon Remake][4])

## Thirty-second game plan

1. **Observed strategy:** attack **Bart/Bionic Bart** aggressively. Extended-code owners and tournament players report Bionic Bart as one of the disproportionately large scoring sources in the base remake. [S7] ([Pinside][5])
2. Prepare **Gold Mine Multiball** while progressing Quick Draws toward **Showdown**. Gold Mine’s jackpots build the **Mother Lode**, whose multiplier rises as full jackpot sets are completed. [S6][S8] ([Cactus Canyon Remake][4])
3. **High-risk/high-reward route:** stack Gold Mine with Showdown. Classic high-level strategy deliberately leaves the final Quick Draw ready, starts Gold Mine, then triggers Showdown inside it. [S9] ([Digital Pinball Fans][6])
4. Build **Bonus X and rank** along the way. There is no general playfield-X system; rank raises Quick Draw and High Noon value, while large Bonus X can make end-of-ball bonus meaningful. [S6][S10] ([Cactus Canyon Remake][4])
5. **Alternate objective:** complete the five Sheriff’s Badge stars and reach **High Noon**—especially attractive if most requirements are already banked rather than starting from scratch. [S6] ([Cactus Canyon Remake][4])

## Core rules and persistence

The five **High Noon** requirements are: defeat the Bart sequence through **Bionic Bart**, start Showdown MB, collect Mother Lode in Gold Mine MB, complete Stampede MB’s required shots, and make **10 combos**. Completing all five lights High Noon at the Gold Mine. Rank increases High Noon and Quick Draw scoring. [S6] ([Cactus Canyon Remake][4])

**Gold Mine Multiball:** lock three balls in the Mine. Major shots score jackpots; after jackpot progress, the Mine collects **Mother Lode**. Completing full sets raises the Mother Lode multiplier, making delayed collection potentially far more valuable. [S6][S8] ([Cactus Canyon Remake][4])

**Showdown Multiball:** win all four Quick Draws. In the stock Extended rules, it retains the older repeating drop-target structure. The later Lyman upgrade explicitly removes the famous “never-ending Showdown” exploit, which means commentators must not import upgrade behavior into a stock Extended machine. [S4][S9] ([Tilt Forums][7])

**Bionic Bart:** after defeating the preceding Bart brothers, the next Saloon/Bart encounter starts Bionic Bart. **Observed strategy:** players can build jackpot value with other shots before cashing at Bart; draining loses the uncollected build, creating a genuine push-your-luck decision. [S6][S7] ([Cactus Canyon Remake][4])

Mode/High-Noon progress is substantially game-long, while some immediate values—especially Bionic-Bart build and ordinary ball bonus—are lost on drain. [S6][S7]

## Skill shots

**Normal Skill Shot:** on plunge, the display names a target/award; make that indicated shot to collect it. Material awards include:

* **Lasso Skill Shot:** lights Bounty and can **increase rank**.
* **Light Lock:** shortcuts Gold Mine MB qualification.
* **Light Extra Ball**.
* **+3 Bonus X**.
* Awards that immediately complete one of the five major shot objectives, such as Bronco/Polly progress.

No separate Super/Secret Skill Shot was verified for the stock Extended rules; the **Runaway Stagecoach Super Skill Shot belongs to the optional Lyman upgrade**. [S6][S2] ([Cactus Canyon Remake][4])

## Secondary features

* **Bounty Award:** qualified by Lasso Skill Shot or winning Quick Draw and collected at Bart. It can light Lock, Extra Ball, Quick Draw or Gun Fight; raise rank; give +5 Bonus X; or award points. [S6] ([Cactus Canyon Remake][4])
* **Extra Ball:** can be lit from Save Polly, Skill Shot, Showdown start, Bounty and Beer Mug progress, but must then be **collected at the Gold Mine**. Tournament policy may instead disable it; I found no universal CGC competition point-conversion value. [S6] ([Cactus Canyon Remake][4])
* **Wild West Shootout:** absent on the base Special Edition unless a topper is installed; SE+ and LE have the interactive topper version. The Lyman upgrade later adds another display-playable version, which is outside this rules basis. [S3][S4] ([Pinside][2])
* No player action button, Magna-Save or conventional stock video mode was found.

## What to watch

1. **Sheriff’s Badge stars:** immediately tells the commentator which High-Noon requirements are already complete. [S6]
2. **Mother Lode lit at the Mine:** the player has a major Gold Mine cash-out available and may delay it to increase the multiplier. [S6][S8]
3. **Quick Draw lamps / raised Bad Guy target:** the player is progressing toward Showdown; the fourth completed Quick Draw starts it. [S6]
4. **Bionic Bart running:** watch whether the player keeps shooting lit jackpots instead of Bart—that means they are deliberately building the eventual cash-out. [S7]

## Match strategy

**Playing ahead:** **Strategic inference:** cash Bionic Bart at a reasonable value instead of endlessly building it; take a prepared Gold Mine MB without insisting on the difficult Showdown stack; lean on safer ramp/combo progress and Bonus X.

**Playing behind:** pursue **Gold Mine + Showdown**, or take a much greedier Bionic Bart build before cashing. The historical stacked Showdown route can become extraordinarily long because drop completion can replenish balls/ball save in the pre-Lyman rules. [S9] ([Digital Pinball Fans][6])

**Key recurring decision:** **cash the value now, or keep building?** That appears in both Mother Lode and Bionic Bart, and it is the clearest reason two strong players may shoot differently in the same state.

## Danger zones

* **Bad Guy drop targets:** Quick Draw/Gun Fight often demands a fast direct target shot; misses or solid rebounds come straight back into uncontrolled center play.
* **Bart hit/eject:** setup-dependent deflections can send the ball onto ramps or laterally across the game rather than cleanly to a flipper. [S11] ([Pinball Info][8])
* **Center Train ramp:** very strong flippers can cause high-speed rejects or airballs off the ramp cover. [S11] ([Pinside][9])
* **Pop area:** looping/orbit play gives up control and may return from the bumpers with little warning.
* **Greedy Bionic Bart:** the hazard is strategic as much as physical—**all uncashed built value disappears on drain**. [S7] ([Pinball Info][10])

## Trivia

* The original 1998 Cactus Canyon was the **last conventional Bally/Williams pinball before Pinball 2000** and shipped with unfinished software; only **903** originals were built. [S2] ([Chicago Gaming Company][1])
* CGC’s remake finally implemented **Bionic Bart**, one of the notable unfinished concepts from the original development. [S3] ([Pinside][2])
* The remake replaces the DMD with a **19.4-inch color display** while intentionally preserving the visual language of the original animations. [S1] ([Chicago Gaming Company][11])
* Its redesigned clear **RGB-lit Bad Guy drop targets** realize a look the original team reportedly wanted but could not manufacture reliably in 1998. [S3] ([Pinside][2])
* Lyman Sheats’ later, optional expansion became his final pinball software project; Josh Sharpe and Sam Zehr completed it from his work and design documents after his death. [S2] ([Chicago Gaming Company][1])
