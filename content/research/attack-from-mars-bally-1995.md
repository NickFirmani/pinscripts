## Identity and versions

**Attack From Mars** — Bally/Midway, December 1995; Williams **WPC-95** platform. Design: **Brian Eddy**; software: **Lyman F. Sheats Jr.**; art: **Doug Watson**; music/sound: **Dan Forden**; mechanics: **Robert C. Friesl**. Approximately 3,450 original machines were produced. [S1][S5] ([GameFAQs][1])

Major multiballs useful to commentary: **regular Multiball** (3-ball), **Martian Multiball** (2-ball), **Total Annihilation** (4-ball), and **Strobe Multiball** (3-ball). **Rule the Universe** is the deep wizard multiball. [S1] ([GameFAQs][1])

**Code sensitivity is unusually important.** The latest *official* CPU release I verified is **V1.1, June 10, 1996**. Later Williams beta **1.13** and coin-capable **1.13B** exist and are common upgrades; they materially alter tournament-mode Stroke of Luck behavior. The 2017 Chicago Gaming remake is different hardware and does not simply imply 1.13B rules. [S3][S4] ([Matt's Basement Arcade][2])

## Thirty-second game plan

1. **Open the saucer safely.** If the forcefield is up, the Super Skill Shot can lower it with one center hit instead of grinding the dangerous 3-bank. [S1][S2]
2. **Build regular Multiball while the saucer is active.** Three virtual locks start it; strong tournament play commonly uses multiball/ball-save time to attack the dangerous center saucer. **Observed strategy.** [S2][S7] ([Kineticist][3])
3. **Progress the four outer shots toward Total Annihilation (TA).** Three completions of a ramp/orbit starts a saucer hurry-up. Stack them: the second jumps to **300M**, third to **500M**, and completing all four awards **1B** and starts TA. [S1] ([GameFAQs][1])
4. In regular Multiball, complete all five jackpots for the roaming **Super Jackpot**; in TA, keep collecting outer jackpots and cash the accumulating **Annihilation Jackpot** at the lock ramp. [S1]
5. **Alternate tournament route:** if the center is murderous, avoid saucer grinding and score through TA/hurry-ups plus regular Multiball. Martian Attack is particularly useful when it can be stacked into another multiball. **Observed strategy.** [S2][S7]

**Big picture:** Rule the Universe is the long-form objective, but a tournament player does **not** need to chase it blindly; saucers, stacked hurry-ups, multiball supers, and TA can decide a match much earlier. [S1][S2]

## Core rules and persistence

* **Saucer:** clear the three forcefield targets, then damage the exposed saucer until the drop target stays down; shoot the hole to destroy it. First saucer requires 4 hits at 50M and pays 200M to destroy; subsequent waves require four additional hits and increase the destruction award by 100M. After five Earth saucers, **Conquer Mars** requires 10 center-hole shots; the final shot awards **2.5B**. [S1] ([GameFAQs][1])
* **Regular Multiball:** locks are virtual—no lock stealing. On default 3-ball settings, one lock-ramp completion lights all three locks for the first Multiball; subsequent Multiballs require relighting locks individually. Default 5-ball settings are harder from the first Multiball. [S1]
* Five one-shot jackpots start at **60M**, increasing 10M each. Completing all five lights a roaming Super Jackpot beginning at **250M**. After a Super, jackpot base value carries into the next Multiball. Super Jackpot progression is ROM-sensitive. [S1]
* **TA progress carries until TA is started**, then all 12 lamps reset afterward. A live hurry-up survives into multiball until collected or timed out. [S1]
* **Martian Attack:** spell M-A-R-T-I-A-N, start it at Stroke of Luck, then hit all four jumping Martians within 30 seconds for Martian Multiball. Martian Bombs carry across attacks. [S1]
* Martian Multiball can stack with other multiballs if sequencing permits; regular Multiball, TA, and Strobe are otherwise mutually exclusive. [S1] ([GameFAQs][1])
* **End-of-ball bonus can matter enormously:** switch/Martian bonus is multiplied by Bonus X, which can reach **250X**; billion-point bonuses are possible. Normally Bonus X resets at end of ball unless Hold Bonus has been awarded. [S1] ([GameFAQs][1])
* Rule the Universe requires: Super Jackpot, TA, Martian Multiball, Conquer Mars, Super Jets, and a 5-Way Combo. Scoring **5B during the mode awards another 5B**. [S1] ([GameFAQs][1])

## Skill shots

**Normal Skill Shot — confirmed rule.** Launch normally into the top Bonus X lanes and steer the flashing lane with the flippers. Awards **10M + 5X Bonus**, with the point value increasing 10M each successful skill shot. Particularly attractive when the forcefield is already down or Bonus X is strategically valuable. [S1]

**Super Skill Shot — confirmed rule.** Hold the **left flipper** while pressing Launch. The ball travels around to the flippers; the first qualifying shot can either:

* hit any ramp/orbit for **50M + all three TA lamps for that shot**, or
* hit a forcefield target for **50M and instantly lower the forcefield**. [S1]

A Super Skill Shot immediately consumes the opening ball saver; the normal top-lane skill shot preserves it somewhat longer. [S1] ([GameFAQs][1])

## Secondary features

* **Ball saves:** brief opening saver; Super Skill Shot generally extinguishes it immediately. Multiballs have their own saves; Strobe has an especially long opening save plus a short post-mode save. Rule the Universe has roughly a 30-second save. Exact operator-adjusted durations were not reliably established. [S1]
* **Video Mode:** normally a Stroke of Luck feature, once per player. Flippers aim; firing is automatic; Launch triggers one **Smart Bomb** clearing the screen. Completing it can score **635M**. Video-mode Extra Ball behavior changes in 1.13. [S1][S3] ([GameFAQs][1])
* **Extra Balls:** native rules include the second saucer, lane completions, Video Mode and Strobe Multiball. Tournament disable/conversion behavior is event-specific and should be confirmed. [S1]
* **Launch button / Martian Bombs:** during Martian Attack, a flashing Launch button spends a bomb to kill one remaining Martian. Critically, killing the fourth Martian this way **still starts Multiball even after the physical ball has entered an outlane, provided it has not reached the trough**. [S1]
* **Stroke of Luck:** ordinary play is pseudo-random. Tournament-mode **V1.1 generally gives 50M**; 1.13/1.13B instead uses deterministic state-based awards such as Start Attack Wave, Video Mode, Martian Bomb, Strobe, Light Martian Attack or Light Lock. [S1][S4] ([GameFAQs][1])

## What to watch

1. **Forcefield position / saucer animation:** shield down means a valuable Attack Wave is active; expect center shots or a Multiball setup. [S1]
2. **Three lamps under each ramp/orbit:** approaching three completions means a hurry-up; several nearly completed shots signals a potential 300M/500M/1B stack. [S1]
3. **Five Multiball jackpot shots / roaming lamp:** after all five jackpots, the Super Jackpot moves shot-to-shot with a distinctive audio “blip.” [S1]
4. **Jumping Martians + flashing Launch button:** Martian Attack is live; flashing Launch means bombs are available and can turn a pending drain into Multiball. [S1]

## Match strategy

**Playing ahead:** favor controlled ramp/lock play, collect an available hurry-up rather than greedily extending its stack, and use Multiball primarily to protect saucer progress. If the center is rejecting badly, there is little reason to volunteer a direct forcefield shot. **Strategic inference from verified scoring/risk.**

**Playing behind:** stacked hurry-ups are the cleanest variance lever—holding a 100M hurry-up while completing more shots can jump it to 300M/500M, with the fourth completion paying 1B plus TA. A prepared Martian Attack + main Multiball stack or deep saucer/Conquer Mars push provides still more upside. [S1][S7]

**Key recurring decision:** **cash the saucer now, or keep setting up safety/leverage?** Good players may either expose themselves to high-value center rebounds immediately or spend time building Multiball/TA first so those center hits occur with multiple balls and a saver. [S2]

## Danger zones

* **Forcefield head-on:** the classic AFM killer—fast center target rebounds can go straight down the middle. [S2]
* **Exposed saucer drop target:** still dangerous; glancing the angled side targets rather than drilling center can produce safer caroms. **Observed strategy.** [S2]
* **Left inlane:** balls can roll unusually far up the lane and find the left outlane. [S1]
* **Lock ramp:** narrow and steep; watch a marginal shot fail to climb and return abruptly. [S1]
* **Orbit → pop-bumper area:** loops frequently dump into the Bonus X lanes/pops, which can then exit into Stroke of Luck or the right orbit with little control. [S1]

## Trivia

* AFM was designed by Brian Eddy and programmed by tournament champion Lyman Sheats; the pair later collaborated on **Medieval Madness**. [S1]
* Eddy originally prototyped a much more elaborate mothership intended to move around over the playfield on a telescoping/pivoting mechanism; it proved impractical. [S6] ([This Week in Pinball][4])
* The original production run was about **3,450 machines**. [S5]
* The famous **“Dirty Pool”** behavior—getting a ball behind the raising forcefield and using it to spot another saucer—became an intentionally recognized rule in V1.1. [S3]
* Despite the timing and similar name, the game was not created as a license for the film *Mars Attacks!*; the concept predated it. [S5][S6]

### Uncertainties and conflicts

* **ROM:** V1.1 is the latest official release I verified; 1.13/1.13B are later beta releases and materially better/different for tournament Stroke of Luck. [S3][S4]
* **Super Jackpot progression:** detailed contemporary documentation reports 250M → 500M → 750M → 1B, but notes some software versions instead reset after 750M. Exact ROM should therefore be known before hard-coding this to the final page. [S1]
* **Video Mode Extra Ball:** percentaged under earlier code; V1.13 changed it so that, when Extra Balls are available, it should reliably appear around 30 saucers. [S1][S3]
* **Ball-save durations:** presence and relative behavior are verified, but I did not find a sufficiently authoritative fixed default duration for ordinary Multiballs; operator settings may matter.
