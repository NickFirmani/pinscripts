## Identity and versions

* **Strikes and Spares** — Bally Manufacturing, **June 1978**, model **1135-E**, early Bally **AS-2518-17** solid-state hardware with chimes. Design by **Gary Gayton**, art principally credited to **Kevin O’Connor**. Production: **12,820**. No multiball. [S3][S4] ([Kineticist][1])
* Two flippers, three pop bumpers, one kickout saucer, one spinner, five **S-P-A-R-E** standups, and ten central rollover “bowling pins.” [S3][S4] ([Pinside][2])
* No meaningful production-code revision history was verified; operator switches and physical setup are much more important competitively.
* Scope follows the supplied commentator-reference specification. 

## Thirty-second game plan

1. **Observed strategy:** shoot **up top all day**—prefer the spinner when lit, otherwise the right orbit—to keep cycling through **A-B-C**. Avoid the central pin rollovers and S-P-A-R-E targets unless their awards matter. [S2] ([Silverball Rules][3])
2. Prioritize the **B lane**. It lights the spinner and center bumper; when Automatic Strike is active it also scores/advances the current Strike value. [S1] 
3. Repeated A-B-C completions raise end-of-ball Bonus X **2× → 3× → 5×**. Base bonus maxes at **29K**, so 5× means **145K waiting at drain**. [S1] 
4. Upper play also feeds the saucer, which scores the current **Strike value** and advances it through **5K / 10K / 15K / 20K / 30K / Special**. [S1] 
5. Alternate strategy only when awards count: complete **S-P-A-R-E** for Extra Ball/Special opportunities—or point conversions as large as **50K**. [S1][S4] 

## Core rules and persistence

* Base bonus starts at **1K**, maxes at **29K**, and advances from A-B-C lanes, S-P-A-R-E targets, return lanes, and three steps at a time from the top saucer. Tilt forfeits it. [S1] 
* A-B-C completion #1 lights **2×**, #2 **3×**, #3 **5×**; later completions reach Special depending on adjustment. [S1] 
* **Bonus-X persistence is adjustable.** Switch #22 explicitly selects **Bonus Multiplier Memory** versus reset. Do not assume the multiplier dies at the end of the ball. [S1] 
* B scores 500, adds bonus, **lights the spinner**, lights the center bumper for 1K, and controls Automatic Strike. Automatic Strike can be **always active or alternating**. [S1] 
* The ten central pins score **1K when lit / 100 unlit**. Their knocked-down state is stored in memory; completing the full set scores the current Strike value, advances it, then resets the pins. [S1] 
* Top saucer likewise scores and advances Strike value. Its starting state is adjustment-sensitive: **5K may be enabled or skipped**. [S1] 
* S-P-A-R-E first lights the return lanes for Extra Ball; later progression lights outlane Special and then scores Special directly. The actual award may instead be replay or points. [S1][S4] 

## Skill shots

**B-lane skill shot** — plunge through the **center B lane**.

* Bob Matthews explicitly identifies B as the skill shot. [S2] ([Silverball Rules][3])
* It immediately lights the spinner and center 1K bumper, advances bonus, and—when Automatic Strike is active—also scores and advances the Strike value. [S1]
* On conservative settings, Automatic Strike **alternates**, making the lit/unlit state especially worth watching before the plunge. [S1] 
* No separate super, alternate, or secret skill shot found.

## Secondary features

* **No conventional ball save or kickback.**
* **Extra Ball / Special:** S-P-A-R-E and later A-B-C/Strike progression can generate awards. Bally provides three broad award modes: replay, Extra Ball, or **Novelty points**. In Novelty, most Specials are worth **50K** and the return-lane Extra Ball is **20K**. [S1] 
* The instruction card specifies a maximum of **one Extra Ball per ball in play**. [S4] ([Pinball Nirvana][4])
* No video mode, player controls, mystery award, or lane-change control.

## What to watch

1. **B / spinner lit:** explains why the player immediately returns to the spinner instead of shooting targets.
2. **2× / 3× / 5× inserts:** at 5×, a maxed 29K bonus is worth **145K**.
3. **Strike-value ladder:** 20K/30K means an incidental saucer or Automatic Strike has become a meaningful cash-out.
4. **Return/outlane award lamps:** if Extra Balls or 50K conversions count, S-P-A-R-E has suddenly become strategically relevant.

## Match strategy

**Playing ahead:** stay with the documented low-risk route—**lit spinner/right orbit → A-B-C**—and accumulate Bonus X rather than shooting exposed central rollovers or standups. A strong 5× bonus is already substantial equity. [S2] ([Silverball Rules][3])

**Playing behind:** exploit a strong lit spinner, push to **5× / 29K bonus**, and advance Strike toward 30K/Special. If the tournament uses Novelty scoring, 50K Specials make deeper A-B-C or S-P-A-R-E progression a legitimate comeback route. [S1]

**Key recurring decision:** **keep shooting safely up top, or leave that route to chase S-P-A-R-E/pins?** On normal tournament settings, expert guidance strongly favors up-top spinner/orbit play; Extra Balls or valuable Special conversions can flip the calculation. [S2]

## Danger zones

1. **Top-saucer kickout:** the key documented feed; some copies eject toward a sling or directly down the middle. [S2] ([Silverball Rules][3])
2. **Saucer lip-out:** a ball circling but failing to settle can fall directly toward center drain. [S2]
3. **Pop-bumper action:** successful upper shots can still exit unpredictably toward danger. [S2]
4. **S-P-A-R-E / other standups:** expert strategy largely avoids them because the rebound is worse than spinner/orbit flow. [S2]
5. **Central pin rack:** progression looks tempting but puts the ball into uncontrolled central rollover/bumper traffic; Bob’s guide specifically recommends ignoring it. [S2]

## Trivia

* Strikes and Spares was a high-volume early Bally solid-state title: **12,820 produced**. [S3] ([Pinside][5])
* It uses Bally's early **AS-2518-17 MPU and mechanical chimes**, rather than the later electronic sound-board package. [S3] ([Pinside][2])
* Designer Gary Gayton later recalled that **Strikes & Spares was only the second game he designed** and specifically remembered it as a flowing, skill-shot-oriented design. [S5] ([Scribd][6])
* Kevin O'Connor said the famous woman on the backglass was **entirely fictional**, not based on a real model; his signature is hidden around the Player 2 score-card area. [S6] ([The Pinball Fix][7])
* The ten central rollover switches are arranged physically like a **ten-pin bowling rack**, turning the bowling motif into an actual rules mechanism. [S3]

### Uncertainties and conflicts

* **1977 versus 1978:** some restoration/database pages display the August 1977 **project date**, but modern archive records identify **June 1978 as date of manufacture/release**. Use *Bally 1978*. [S3][S6] ([Pinside][5])
* Bally's original FO-587 manual notoriously shipped with its nominal **“Game Feature Options” page blank**; Action Pinball notes that some adjustment information was instead printed on cards mounted in the backbox. The surviving manual nevertheless documents the strategically important switches used above. [S1][S7] ([Action Pinball][8])
* Kineticist currently adds **Michele Martinelli** to the art credits [S3], while Pinside/IPDB-derived records list Kevin O'Connor alone [S4]. This does not affect gameplay; credit O'Connor on the compact final page unless stronger primary attribution emerges.
* Spinner effectiveness and saucer kickout behavior are extremely **copy-dependent**. Owner/tournament observations range from ordinary scoring to machines where “B then spinner” dominates virtually everything. [S2][S8] ([Pinside][9])
