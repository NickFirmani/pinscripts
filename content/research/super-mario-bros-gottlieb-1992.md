## Identity and versions

**Super Mario Bros.** — Gottlieb/Premier Technology, 1992, game #733. Designed by **Jon Norris**; software by Allen Edwall and Rand Paulin; artwork by David Moore and Constantino Mitchell. Gottlieb System 3, three flippers, raised upper playfield, rotating castle, and DMD. Approximately **4,200** were produced. [S3][S5]

* Do not confuse it with the smaller **Super Mario Bros. Mushroom World**, released separately in 1992. [S5]
* **Rules basis: `rom` — Game PROM revision 3**, archived as `gprom3.bin`. Four English ROM sets are preserved; their game-PROM files progress through `gprom.bin`, `gprom1.bin`, `gprom2.bin`, and `gprom3.bin`. [S4]
* One principal multiball: **2-ball Multiball normally, 3-ball when started while Super Mario**. [S1][S2]

## Thirty-second game plan

1. **Abuse the ramp loop.** The right inlane lights **Quick Shot** at the left ramp; repeated collections grow into multi-million awards. Contemporary competitive/tutorial discussion bluntly summarizes optimal play as “shoot the left ramp over and over.” **Observed strategy.** [S2][S6]
2. Use the upper playfield/right ramp to advance **S-U-P-E-R**. Becoming **Super Mario** gives defensive help and doubles any Round started while Super. [S1][S2]
3. If pursuing multiball, complete **1-2-3-4 in order**, then shoot the left hole. Starting it Super gives three balls instead of two; castle jackpots climb **5M → 10M → 20M → 40M**. [S1][S2]
4. High-variance alternatives are the stronger Rounds—especially **Luigi's Cave Count-Up**—and, outside Tournament Mode, random Bonus Chance can absurdly award **200M**. [S1][S7]
5. Central decision: keep taking the simple ramp-based value, or expose the ball to upper-playfield/mode progression for Super, multiball and larger jackpots.

## Core rules and persistence

**Super Mario:** complete S-U-P-E-R. Super lights the left kick save and raises/resets the center post between the flippers. Using the kick save or knocking down the center post returns Mario to normal; the Feather target can restore Super once per ball. **A Round started while Super scores double for that entire Round.** [S1]

**Multiball:** qualify shots **1 → 2 → 3 → 4** in sequence: left ramp, Yoshi target, right lane/loop, then upper-playfield ramp. The left hole then lights temporarily to start it. Normal Mario starts two balls; Super Mario starts three. Castle jackpots advance 5M/10M/20M/40M and reset to 5M at each new multiball. [S1][S2]

**Rounds:** light three turtle shells, then collect the lit Key hole and choose between two offered Rounds with the flippers. The important scoring examples are Yoshi Countdown, Luigi Cave Count-Up, Bomb, Exclamation and Mega Bumpers. Round scoring doubles if the Round was entered while Super. [S2]

**Quick Shot:** the right inlane flashes the left ramp. Successive collections grow dramatically; both the exact sequence and maximum are operator-adjustable. The factory manual's shipped-game addendum gives **20M / 10M / 5M** maximums for Easy / Medium / Hard, while Hard also disables the Quick Shot extra-ball award. [S1]

**Tournament Mode materially changes the game:** it disables Auto-Skill and Catchup, forces Bonus Chance to **1M**, makes Mystery always add a shell, and makes each destroyed castle worth **10M with no all-castles Special**. Normal mode instead permits the random **200M three-sevens Bonus Chance**. [S1]

Castle progress normally persists **between games**, an unusual global-state rule. Tournament Mode removes the valuable final Special but the documentation found does not explicitly state that the stored castle counter itself is reset. [S1][S2]

## Skill shots

**None found.**

The machine uses an automatic shooter/button rather than a manual plunge, and neither the factory gameplay documentation nor detailed rulesheet identifies a conventional plunge/launch skill shot. Gottlieb's separate **Auto-Skill** operator setting is adaptive difficulty, not a player skill shot. [S1]

## Secondary features

* **Kick Save / center post:** becoming Super activates both forms of drain protection. Using either generally consumes Super status. [S1]
* **Invincible:** normally available from Mystery. It continuously enables the kick save and center post, while right-outlane drains are replaced by another ball. Tournament Mode's deterministic Mystery behavior effectively removes this random bailout. [S1]
* **Mystery / Bonus Chance:** highly random in normal play; Bonus Chance ranges all the way to **200M**. Tournament Mode fixes it at 1M and normalizes Mystery. [S1]
* **Video Mode:** completing the four Bullet lamps via the left return lane lights the castle; entering starts a side-scrolling Mario mode. Right flipper jumps; holding left makes Mario run. Completing it while running awards **30M rather than 10M**. [S2][S4]
* **Extra balls:** available through Quick Shot, Cave progression and the Castle Extra Ball Round. The factory software does **not provide a general “disable all extra balls” tournament adjustment** in the documentation found; event policy therefore matters. [S1][S8]

## What to watch

1. **SUPER letters / flashing castle:** Super Mario is near or active; expect either castle destruction or an attempt to start a doubled Round. [S1]
2. **Quick Shot flashing at left ramp:** usually explains repetitive left-ramp shooting; the award is escalating and can reach tournament-relevant values. [S1][S6]
3. **1-2-3-4 lamps:** show multiball progress. When all four are complete, watch for the left hole to start multiball. [S1]
4. **Super status at multiball start:** immediately distinguishes **three balls from two**, and therefore a much safer jackpot opportunity. [S1]

## Match strategy

**Playing ahead:**
**Observed strategy:** stay on the straightforward left-ramp / upper-ramp scoring loop rather than chase the entire ruleset. Tournament commentary and player reviews repeatedly describe these two ramps as disproportionately valuable. [S6][S7]

**Playing behind:**
Build **Super + three-ball multiball**, chase escalating castle jackpots, or use the best available Round. Luigi's Cave Count-Up is specifically reported as capable of producing very large scoring from a successful sequence. **Observed strategy.** [S2][S7]

**Key recurring decision:**
**Take repeatable Quick Shots or invest in Super/multiball.** The former is safer and often sufficient; the latter adds balls, doubles Rounds and unlocks 40M repeat jackpots, but demands more varied shots.

## Danger zones

* **Upper-playfield miss:** valuable progression requires the small upper flipper; failure immediately ends that setup opportunity.
* **Right loop:** intentionally sends the ball into pop bumpers, exchanging progress for loss of control.
* **Left-hole kickout:** important starts/awards finish with a forced eject rather than a controlled ramp return.
* **Super protection consumption:** a left-outlane kick save or center-post save can simultaneously strip Super, losing the doubled-Round / three-ball-MB setup. [S1]
* **Tilt during a strong Yoshi bonus:** Yoshi bonus builds by 500K hits and carries between balls, making an unnecessary tilt more expensive than the visible playfield scoring suggests. [S2]

## Trivia

* *Super Mario Bros.* was **Gottlieb's first production DMD pinball machine**. [S3][S5]
* Gottlieb produced about **4,200 machines**, making it one of the company's better-selling System 3 titles. [S3]
* Despite the title, much of its imagery draws from **Super Mario World**, including Yoshi. [S5]
* The rotating blue castle is largely a visual toy—the actual castle scoring is registered by the nearby hole. [S2]
* Gottlieb followed it within months with **Super Mario Bros. Mushroom World**, a substantially smaller and different machine rather than another edition of this game. [S5]
