## Identity and versions

* **Quick Draw** — D. Gottlieb & Co., June 1975; model **380**, two-player electro-mechanical (EM) game with score reels, two flippers, three pop bumpers, two five-bank drop-target banks and two kickout holes. Scope is the game specified in the supplied brief.  [S1][S2] 
* Design: **Ed Krynski**; art: **Gordon Morison**. Quick Draw is the **two-player version of Fast Draw**, the four-player model 379 released earlier in 1975. [S2][S3] ([Pinside][1])
* No multiball, locks, software, ball save or modern multiplier system.
* Production is disputed: Pinside/IPDB-based sources list **2,660**; Kineticist lists **2,767**. [S2][S4] ([Pinside][1])
* **3-ball vs. 5-ball is strategically crucial.** The surviving factory Quick Draw instruction card is explicitly for **5-ball** play. [S1] 

## Thirty-second game plan

1. **UTAD — “up top all day.”** Favor the A-B-C lanes, bumpers and kickout holes rather than directly attacking the dangerous drop banks early. **Observed strategy.** [S3] ([Silverball Rules][2])
2. Each saucer scores **1K + 1K for every A/B/C letter collected**, up to 4K; bank bumper rebounds into those saucers whenever possible. [S3][S5] ([Silverball Rules][2])
3. Drop-target hits build the **end-of-ball bonus to 15K**, but rebounds are dangerous and multiple targets falling too quickly may register only one bonus advance. [S3] ([Silverball Rules][2])
4. On **5-ball**, save aggressive drop-bank work for ball 5: that ball gets **automatic double bonus**, or **triple bonus if A-B-C is completed**. [S1][S3] 
5. Once all ten drops are down, only the two **center black/horseshoe targets reset**, now worth **5K each repeatedly**—the major alternate scoring route. [S1][S3] 

## Core rules and persistence

* **A-B-C:** each letter increases both kickout-hole values by 1K: **1K base → 4K with ABC complete**. The corresponding pop bumper also becomes more valuable when its letter is collected. [S3][S6] ([Pinside][3])
* **Bonus:** drop targets advance end-of-ball bonus, which tops at **15K**. Bonus is scored on drain. [S3] ([Silverball Rules][2])
* **5-ball factory rules:** bonus is single on balls 1–4; ball 5 is automatically **2×**, becoming **3× if ABC is complete**. [S1] 
* **3-ball configuration:** reliable schematic/competitive references report ABC gives **2× bonus on balls 1–2**, with **3× on ball 3** when ABC is complete. [S3][S7] ([Zenfolio | Fun With Pinball][4])
* **Drops and ABC reset after every ball.** This is not a long-term progression game. [S1] 
* Complete **both five-banks** and the game raises only the two center black targets; each is then worth **5K**, and they reset again after both are knocked down. [S1][S3] 
* Easy commentator trap: knocking down two or more targets almost simultaneously can advance bonus only once because the EM scoring mechanism cannot register them independently fast enough. [S3] ([Silverball Rules][2])

## Skill shots

* **B-lane plunge — observed competitive skill shot.** Bob's Guide recommends the center **B** lane: it feeds directly into the center bumper and usually offers better continuation than A or C. [S3] ([Silverball Rules][2])
* This is **not a separate coded scoring award**; it is the strategically preferred plunge lane.
* A/C can sometimes feed cleanly to a flipper, but on some setups they can head straight down the middle. Watch previous plunges before assuming B is universally safest. [S3]

## Secondary features

* **Special:** completing **A-B-C plus either corresponding five-bank** lights that side's kickout hole for Special; factory instructions state the holes light **alternately**. [S1] 
* Special is a replay, not normal tournament score. Unless the event substitutes points for Specials, this progression is usually strategically secondary.
* **No Extra Ball, ball save, kickback, mystery, video mode or player-controlled utility** was verified for stock Quick Draw.

## What to watch

* **A/B/C lamps:** more letters mean higher saucer values and, on 3-ball, potentially doubled EOB bonus.
* **Ten drops nearly complete:** a player may briefly abandon UTAD to finish the banks and unlock repeatable **5K black targets**.
* **Only the two black center drops standing:** the 5K repeat-scoring phase is active.
* **Ball number:** on a 5-ball game, ball 5 radically changes shot selection because the entire bonus is automatically doubled.

## Match strategy

**Playing ahead:** Stay **UTAD**, take saucers and A-B-C progress, and avoid forcing the banks unless only one or two drops remain. **Observed strategy.** [S3] ([Silverball Rules][2])

**Playing behind:** Attack the banks, especially on a double/triple-bonus ball. Finish all ten to unlock the repeatable **5K black targets**, while building toward a maximum 15K ×2/×3 bonus. **Strategic inference** from confirmed scoring.

**Key recurring decision:** **Safe upper-playfield scoring versus dangerous drop-target leverage.** On five-ball, that balance moves sharply toward the drops on ball 5; on three-ball, ABC makes bonus multipliers important much earlier.

## Danger zones

* **Drop-bank rebounds:** primary tournament hazard; direct hits can fire immediately toward the center or an outlane. [S3] ([Silverball Rules][2])
* **Pop-bumper exits:** face-on bumper hits can send the ball straight down the middle or toward an outlane. [S3]
* **A/C plunge lanes:** some copies drop safely to a flipper; others can feed SDTM. [S3]
* **Inlane-divider posts:** a falling ball can rattle on top and bounce either inward to the drain or outward to the outlane. [S3]
* **Slow dead bounces:** Quick Draw can play slowly enough that a ball expected to dead-bounce across may fail to reach the opposite flipper. [S3]

## Trivia

* Quick Draw is the **two-player companion to Fast Draw**, which uses essentially the same playfield/rules in a four-player cabinet. [S2][S3] ([PinRepair][5])
* Designer **Ed Krynski** and artist **Gordon Morison** were one of Gottlieb's defining design/art pairings of the 1970s. [S2]
* Gottlieb returned to essentially the same playfield architecture in **Amazon Hunt (1983)**, a System 80A solid-state game also designed by Krynski. [S8] ([Pinside][6])
* Quick Draw has **no conventional slingshots**; the open lower playfield instead leaves the two long banks exposed along the sides. [S2] ([Pinball Nirvana][7])
* The factory instruction card's artwork/document number is **B-15969-2**. [S1]

### Uncertainties and conflicts

* **Production count:** Pinside/IPDB-derived references give **2,660** [S2], while Kineticist gives **2,767** [S4]. Leave the number off the final one-pager unless a preferred archival source is chosen. ([Pinside][1])
* **3-ball rules:** the surviving factory instruction card located here is explicitly a **5-ball** card [S1]. The 3-ball 2×/3× behavior is supported by competitive guides, owners and schematic analysis [S3][S7], but not that factory card.
* **Skill shot terminology:** B is the documented competitive plunge preference [S3], not a separately programmed/awarded skill-shot feature.
* Physical rebound and plunge behavior is exceptionally copy-dependent.
