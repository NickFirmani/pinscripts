## Identity and versions

* **Hercules** — Atari, May 1979; four-player solid-state game on Atari's Generation/System 2 hardware. Design by **Steve Bicker**, artwork by **Jim Kelly**.  [S2][S3] ([Kineticist][1])
* Famous for its extraordinary scale: roughly **93 in. deep × 40 in. cabinet width × 83 in. tall**, with about **18 square feet of playfield**, oversized flippers/bumpers and a **cue-ball-sized ball**. Atari marketed it as the largest flipper game produced. [S1][S4] ([World Radio History][2])
* No multiball, locks, ramps, drop targets, ball save or software-driven modes. [S3] ([Pinside][3])
* Factory advertising says it is adjustable for **3- or 5-ball play** and liberal/conservative settings. [S4]
* No distinct production/software revision affecting strategy was verified.

## Thirty-second game plan

1. **Shoot up top from the right flipper** to activate and advance **Double Bonus**, Extra Ball and Special progress. **Observed strategy.** [S2] ([Silverball Rules][4])
2. Then favor the left flipper for the long right-to-left **Path of Victory** orbit. That shot advances every feature activated on the current ball and ultimately collects Double Bonus / Extra Ball / Special when ready. [S2]
3. Keep building base bonus toward **49K**, but at 10K/20K/30K/40K you must hit the corresponding large pad target to unlock the next 10K band. [S2]
4. The major reliable payoff is **up to 98K end-of-ball bonus** once Double Bonus is earned; importantly, base bonus carries ball-to-ball while Double Bonus does not. [S2]
5. Alternate when ahead: repeat the relatively safe Path of Victory/upper-lane cycle rather than deliberately attacking dangerous pad standups unless bonus is actually capped. **Strategic inference.**

## Core rules and persistence

* Base bonus advances from lane and pad-target scoring and can reach **49K**. Progress is gated: at 10K, 20K, 30K and 40K, a specific large standup must be hit before bonus can continue increasing. [S2] ([Silverball Rules][4])
* **Base bonus is held from ball to ball. Double Bonus resets each ball.** This is the most important persistence distinction for commentary. [S2]
* Double Bonus is a three-stage process:

  * make its corresponding upper lane to activate progression;
  * accumulate three progress lamps via that lane and/or Path of Victory;
  * make Path of Victory again to award Double Bonus. [S2]
* Extra Ball and Special work similarly when enabled: their upper lanes activate progress, then repeated lane/Path shots advance them; Extra Ball requires five progress steps and Special six, followed by Path of Victory to collect. [S2]
* A single **Path of Victory** can advance multiple activated features simultaneously. If several are ready, one Path shot can complete more than one. [S2]
* For an orbit to register as Path of Victory, the ball must traverse the route cleanly; another switch hit between the orbit's star rollovers invalidates it. [S2]
* Atari's contemporary promotion explicitly advertised **ball-to-ball memory** and Path of Victory awarding Double Bonus, Specials and Extra Balls. [S1][S4] ([World Radio History][2])

## Skill shots

`None found.`

No distinct coded or labeled skill-shot award was verified. The plunge enters the upper-lane area and therefore can immediately seed useful feature progress, but that should not be described as a formal skill shot. [S2]

## Secondary features

* **Extra Ball:** when enabled, activate its upper lane, build five progress steps, then collect with Path of Victory. This is strategically material because tournament setup may disable or neutralize it. [S2]
* **Special:** same basic structure, requiring six progress steps before Path of Victory collects it. Tournament replays are normally irrelevant unless the event assigns a point substitute. [S2]
* **3/5-ball and liberal/conservative adjustments:** documented by Atari's flyer; exact venue configuration should be checked. [S4]
* No verified kickback, outlane rescue, player-controlled save, video mode, mystery award or other optional utility.

## What to watch

* **Yellow Double Bonus lamps in the center:** three lit means Double Bonus is ready; the player's next major objective should be Path of Victory. [S2]
* **Path of Victory flashing:** this becomes the priority shot because it can advance or collect several features at once. [S2]
* **Bonus sitting exactly at 10K/20K/30K/40K:** bonus is stalled until the corresponding large pad target is hit. [S2]
* **Upper feature lane newly made:** that tells you which of Double Bonus / Extra Ball / Special can now be advanced by subsequent Path shots.

## Match strategy

**Playing ahead:** Favor upper-lane and Path-of-Victory loops. The orbit is considered relatively safe, and Hercules' extremely slow ball speed can allow controlled passes afterward. Only attack a pad when bonus is actually capped there. **Observed strategy / strategic inference.** [S2]

**Playing behind:** Build toward the full **49K + Double Bonus = 98K** cash-out, taking the required pad targets despite their rebound danger. If Extra Balls are playable, pursuing the five-step EB path materially raises the ceiling.

**Key recurring decision:** **Keep taking the comparatively safe orbit/up-top cycle, or expose the ball to a pad target to unlock another 10K of bonus?** The pad does little strategically until bonus is sitting at its corresponding threshold.

## Danger zones

* **Two pop-bumper exits:** the primary uncontrolled region; weak Path shots can fall into them halfway around the orbit. [S2]
* **Four giant pad standups:** Bob's competitive guide identifies their rebounds as the other major playfield danger. [S2]
* **Weak Path-of-Victory attempts:** insufficient flipper strength sends the ball into the bumpers rather than completing the valuable orbit. [S2]
* **Ball spin:** at Hercules' unusually slow travel speed, the visible spin of the oversized ball can noticeably alter where it goes after contact. [S2]
* **Oversized flippers:** they can feel loose/sloppy compared with normal pinball; successful control techniques depend strongly on their condition. [S2]

## Spoken commentary cues

* “He's gone up top first—that activates the features he wants to advance through Path of Victory.”
* “Path of Victory is lit now; that one orbit can advance several features at once.”
* “Bonus is stuck at twenty thousand—he has to hit that pad before it can climb any higher.”
* “Base bonus carries to the next ball, but Double Bonus does not, so this Path shot matters before the drain.”
* “He's got Double Bonus ready—that turns a full 49K bonus into a 98K end-of-ball collect.”
* “That Path attempt came up short; instead of completing the orbit it's dropping into the bumpers, which is the dangerous feed on Hercules.”

## Trivia

* Atari promoted Hercules in early 1979 as the **largest flipper game ever produced**, with about 18 square feet of play action. [S1] ([World Radio History][2])
* Its ball is approximately **cue-ball size**, requiring correspondingly gigantic flippers, bumpers and other hardware. [S1][S2]
* The factory flyer gives dimensions of roughly **83 inches tall and 93 inches deep**, and a weight of about **275 lb**. [S4] ([Game Museum Forums][5])
* Hercules was previewed at the **AMOA and IAAPA conventions in November 1978** before its 1979 release. [S1]
* Atari's original operations/service manual is designated **TM-128**; archival Atari corporate records preserve publication materials for it. [S5] ([The Strong National Museum of Play][6])

### Uncertainties and conflicts

* **Production quantity:** reliable databases currently list production as **unknown** [S3], while one secondary historical book reports approximately **500**. Because I did not find Atari production documentation confirming 500, I would leave production quantity off the final page. ([Pinside][3])
* **Formal skill shot:** no reliable rules source identifies one. Upper-lane plunge placement clearly matters, but calling that a coded “skill shot” would be an inference.
* **Extra Ball/Special configuration:** Atari marketed liberal/conservative and 3/5-ball adjustments [S4], while the competitive rules source says EB/Special progression applies **if those features are on** [S2]. The venue settings materially affect commentary.
* **Physical behavior:** Path completion and control are highly dependent on the condition of Hercules' unusual oversized flipper assemblies. [S2]
