## Identity and versions

* **Fallout — Pro**, Stern, **2026**, on **SPIKE 3**. Design: **Keith Elwin**; software/rules: **Andrew Wilkening, Brett Z Rubin, Raymond Davidson, Rick Naegele**; mechanics: **Harrison Drake**; art: **Sam Gilbey and Jeremy Packer / Zombie Yeti**; sound by **Jerry Thompson**. [S1][S2] ([Stern Pinball][1])
* The game combines the Prime Video series with Bethesda’s Fallout games; players choose **Lucy, The Ghoul, or Maximus**. [S1] ([Stern Pinball][1])
* **Pro is materially different from Premium/LE.** Pro has no Magna-Flip Vault lower playfield, Fat Man cannon/upper shooting gallery, moving Vault door, or Deathclaw kickback coil. Its Vault is a stationary bash target; V.A.T.S./Fat Man rules are implemented through main-playfield shots instead. [S1][S5] ([Kineticist][2])
* **Rules basis: `code` — v0.80, released 2026-09-04.** This is current public launch code. [S2] ([Pinside][3])
* The code is visibly incomplete: the maintained rulesheet still marks several Quests, side quests and wizard modes “not implemented yet.” [S3] ([Pinball Rule Sheets][4])
* Major currently documented multiballs: **Deathclaw Multiball**, **The Kings** Quest multiball, and Pro equivalents of later Vault/Wasteland progression. Deathclaw is presently the most strategically established. [S3][S4] ([Pinball Rule Sheets][4])

Research scope follows the supplied commentator-reference specification. 

## Thirty-second game plan

1. **Observed early strategy:** qualify a **Quest**, but delay starting it until **Deathclaw Multiball** is also close. [S4] ([JLP's Pinball Cards][5])
2. Charge or buy **Fusion Cores**, then long-press the action button to activate **Power Armor / 2× playfield** immediately before the scoring stack. [S3][S4] ([Pinball Rule Sheets][4])
3. Start the Quest, then **Deathclaw MB**. First Deathclaw MB gives 20 seconds to build jackpot value, then 12 seconds to bash Deathclaw and cash it; enough bashes qualify a Super Jackpot. [S3] ([Pinball Rule Sheets][4])
4. During multiball, use the **Radroach targets for add-a-ball** and keep Power Armor active as long as Fusion Core charge lasts. [S4] ([JLP's Pinball Cards][5])
5. **Alternate:** concentrate on Quest completion and weapon upgrades. Weapons raise overall playfield-X potential and also improve end-of-ball Bonus X, so a developed single-ball game can still become substantial. [S3]

## Core rules and persistence

**Quests are the main modes.** Food/water shots raise HP; **50 HP** qualifies a Quest at the upper-right scoop. More HP means more Quest time, up to roughly 100 seconds. The left “dead sling” target changes the selected Quest. [S3] ([Pinball Rule Sheets][4])

Three Quests are substantially documented on v0.80: **Radscorpion Attack**, **The Kings** and **Find the Target**. Others remain incomplete or undocumented in public launch code. [S3]

**Deathclaw Multiball:** damage both legs twice, then hit Deathclaw again to start. In the first MB, lit shots build the jackpot for 20 seconds; then Deathclaw becomes the timed cash-out for 12 seconds. Four bashes qualify the first Super, collected at the side loop. [S3] ([Pinball Rule Sheets][4])

**Power Armor:** purchase Fusion Cores from Sundries or charge them via the pop-bumper targets. Long-press action to activate. Early documentation identifies this as **2× playfield**, lasting while core charge remains. It can be switched off early, but doing so costs charge. [S3][S4] ([JLP's Pinball Cards][5])

**Weapons:** junk upgrades weapons; higher weapon levels increase playfield scoring and also add Bonus X. Exact X progression is still incompletely documented publicly. [S3]

**S.P.E.C.I.A.L. persistence is unusual:** logged-in players can carry Strength, Perception, Endurance, Charisma, Intelligence, Agility and Luck advancement **between games and machines**. Reaching 10 in all seven eventually unlocks character-specific modes. This persistent progression is separate from normal within-game state and may be tournament-sensitive. [S1] ([Stern Pinball][1])

## Skill shots

**Cap Stash Skill Shot:** time the plunge so the ball crosses the **flashing right inlane**.

* Scores **3M +1M per previous successful skill shot**.
* Adds **3 seconds of ball save**.
* Also drops/activates the Nuka-Cola bottle-cap spinner for a Cap-Stash opportunity. [S3][S4] ([Pinball Rule Sheets][4])

No other implemented production skill shot is currently documented; the v0.80 rulesheet explicitly says additional skill shots are still pending. [S3]

## Secondary features

* **Stimpak outlane save:** collect white-lit Stimpak shots to relight the outlane save. With sufficient Perception, the right flipper can move the save between outlanes. [S3][S4]
* **Companion action:** short-press Action to use the active companion; examples include Mr. Handy spotting Deathclaw progress and Protectron assisting RadAway/Quest timing. [S3]
* **Power Armor action:** long-press Action for playfield X. This creates a visible player-intent cue: short press = utility, long press = scoring multiplier. [S3]
* **Sundries:** spend bottle caps on items, or attempt theft. Early rules say failed theft forces a 10-second right-ramp escape; failing that loses caps and loot. [S4] ([JLP's Pinball Cards][5])
* **Extra ball:** currently documented from four Radroach targets and collected at the left Radroach target; tournament conversion behavior is not yet publicly established. [S3]
* **Video mode:** None verified.

## What to watch

1. **Power Armor / Fusion Core meter:** if the action button is glowing for Armor, expect the player to save it for a Quest + multiball stack.
2. **HP at 50%+ / yellow scoop:** Quest is qualified; higher HP means more time, so continued food/water shots may be intentional rather than wasted setup. [S3]
3. **Deathclaw weak-point lighting:** both legs completed twice means multiball is one final Deathclaw shot away. [S3]
4. **Green Radroach targets during MB:** these are not incidental—completing them can add a ball. [S4]

## Match strategy

**Playing ahead:** **Strategic inference:** take a qualified Quest and Deathclaw MB without exhausting every possible upgrade; activate Power Armor for the known high-value window, use Stimpak defensively, and avoid unnecessary Sundries theft.

**Playing behind:** maximize the stack: **high-HP Quest + Power Armor + Deathclaw MB**, then aggressively pursue Radroach add-a-ball and Deathclaw Super. Weapon development offers additional multiplier leverage if time permits. [S3][S4]

**Key recurring decision:** **spend Fusion Core charge now or bank it for a better stack?** Power Armor is finite, and using 2× on ordinary shots may cost the player their biggest multiplier window later.

## Danger zones

* **Left outlane / Paragon-style pop:** hands-on testers specifically found this area punishing; the single pop effectively replaces the left sling and can send marginal catches directly out. [S5] ([Kineticist][2])
* **Deathclaw bash:** Pro has no kickback coil, but the direct bash still gives up control and can ricochet toward center.
* **Vault/center jump target:** strong centerline rebound risk after a miss or partial shot.
* **Inverted upper flipper:** unusual geometry means missed redirects can fall unpredictably into lower-playfield traffic; little tournament sample exists yet.
* **Sundries theft escape:** not a physical feed, but missing the required timed right ramp can wipe **all caps and loot**, a major strategic loss. [S4]

## Trivia

* Fallout is a **Keith Elwin** design and Stern describes it as coming from the same team behind *Godzilla, JAWS, Jurassic Park,* and *King Kong*. [S1] ([Stern Pinball][1])
* Its die-cast **Pip-Boy** contains a working playfield LCD built with **The Wand Company**. [S1][S5]
* The Nuka-Cola target is an unusual **vertically moving spinner**, which Stern calls an industry first. [S1]
* All editions use a **“Mutated” inverted upper flipper**, a layout device Stern says has not appeared in pinball for more than 75 years. [S5] ([Stern Pinball][6])
* The game combines assets from the Amazon series—including Seasons 1 and 2—with *Fallout 3, Fallout 4,* and *Fallout: New Vegas*. [S1][S5] ([Stern Pinball][1])
