## Identity and versions

* **Guns N' Roses (Standard Edition / SE)** — Jersey Jack Pinball, released October 2020. The supplied brief specifically requests the Standard Edition. 
* Game design: **Eric Meunier and Slash**; software team includes Keith Johnson, Bill Grupp, Duncan Brown, Joe Katz, JT Harkey and Ted Estes; artwork by **Dayne Henry Jr. and Mark Moliter**. [S2][S4] ([Pinside][1])
* SE has **3 flippers** and lacks the LE/CE Gibson upper playfield and physical six-ball guitar lock. Consequently, **Power Chord is not present on SE**; band-member locks are virtual and handled through the left ramp. [S2][S7] ([Jersey Jack Pinball][2])
* **Latest verified software: V3.03, March 25, 2025.** V3.00 substantially rebalanced Song/Album scoring, so old exact scoring numbers should not be imported from early rulesheets without verification. [S1][S4] ([Jersey Jack Pinball][3])
* Commentary-relevant multiballs include four **Booster Multiballs**—Make Some Noise, Throw the Lights, Ignite the Flames, Turn It Up—plus **COMA Multiball**. Songs themselves can begin as multiballs using up to six previously locked balls. [S3][S5] 

## Thirty-second game plan

1. **Collect band members while preparing Booster Multiballs.** Axl, Slash, Duff, Richard, Dizzy/Melissa and Frank come from different major shots; qualified members allow virtual locks at the left ramp. [S3][S5] 
2. **Bank locks but don't rush the sixth.** On SE, each virtual lock takes repeated left-ramp work; six locks automatically launch the selected Song. Strong players often prepare Booster Multiballs and useful Patches first. **Observed strategy.** [S5][S8] ([Tilt Forums][4])
3. **During the Song, hit lit shots and keep the Rock-It Meter alive.** Song scoring escalates through successive stages; cradling too long actively drains the meter and can end the Song. [S5] ([Tilt Forums][4])
4. **At the scoop, decide cash-out versus push.** In single-ball play after a stage, the player can take the accumulated Song Jackpot and end the Song, or continue for another stage and an add-a-ball—but without a fresh full ball-save window. This is the game's central tournament decision. [S5] ([Tilt Forums][4])
5. **Alternate high-variance route:** enter the Song with multiple Booster Multiballs, strong Patches and a high Song Level, then push toward Encore rather than taking an early cash-out. **Observed strategy.** [S5][S8] ([Tilt Forums][4])

## Core rules and persistence

* Collecting a band member enables its virtual lock. On **SE**, locks are collected through the left ramp rather than the LE/CE physical guitar mechanism. Six locks automatically start a Song; fewer locks can still become balls used when the Song begins. [S2][S5][S7] ([Jersey Jack Pinball][2])
* A Song progresses through stages of lit-shot scoring. Finishing a stage lights the center scoop. **Single ball:** choose cash-out or continue/add-a-ball. **Multiball:** the scoop automatically advances the Song and adds a ball. [S5] ([Tilt Forums][4])
* The accumulated **Song Jackpot** is the main cash-out. The later the Song stage and the stronger the setup, the greater the scoring ceiling; current V3.00+ scoring was explicitly rebalanced from earlier code. [S4][S5] ([Pinside][1])
* The **Rock-It Meter** rises from productive Song shots and falls during inactivity, especially while balls are cradled. Emptying it can end the Song early. Completing a Song with a full meter can award an **Encore**, carrying the scoring state into another song. [S5] ([Tilt Forums][4])
* **Patches** create persistent benefits such as easier band-member collection, slower Rock-It decay, Booster-Multiball benefits and Extra-Ball qualification. Normal carryover is important, but Competition Mode is documented to disable random awards and carry-over features, so venue configuration matters. [S3][S5] ([Moss Distributing][5])
* Easy commentator trap: generic GNR rules discussions often describe **Power Chord** during Songs. That feature requires the physical guitar-lock mechanism and **does not exist on Standard Edition**. [S7] ([Pinside][6])

## Skill shots

* **Skill Shot:** soft-plunge into one of the Crowd Meter inserts. It advances Crowd / **Make Some Noise** qualification. [S6] ([Kineticist][7])
* **Super Skill Shot:** hit the currently blinking Crowd Meter insert. It advances Make Some Noise more strongly and can be especially useful on the opening plunge. [S6] ([Kineticist][7])
* Qualification thresholds are adjustment/code sensitive; use the visible Crowd progress rather than assuming a fixed number of successful plunges.

## Secondary features

* **COMA save / COMA Multiball:** hitting the COMA target can light the left outlane. An apparent drain there can be saved; continued switch and shot progress then develops into COMA Multiball. SE has the rules feature without the CE's physical lock-up choreography. [S3][S5][S7] 
* **Action button:** central to Song/Patch choices and certain timed awards. Most importantly, it is used to take the Song cash-out when the game offers the continue-versus-stop decision. [S5]
* **Mystery:** can supply powerful shortcuts such as ball save, locks, Patches, band-member progress or Extra Ball qualification. Competition Mode removes random behavior. [S3][S5] ([Moss Distributing][5])
* **Extra Balls:** several qualifications exist, including Patch combinations and Song progress. Collection is at the upper center lane. Tournament handling must be verified rather than assumed. [S5][S6] ([Tilt Forums][4])
* Factory normal ball save is **6 seconds**. Song/multiball protection is separate and can be far more strategically significant; both are adjustment-sensitive. [S3] ([Moss Distributing][5])

## What to watch

* **Band-member revolver inserts:** completed members explain why the player suddenly shifts to repeated left ramps—they are converting members into virtual locks. [S3][S5]
* **Five or six virtual locks displayed:** six forces the Song start, so a player sitting at five may intentionally avoid the left ramp while preparing Boosters/Patches. [S5]
* **Center scoop flashing during a Song:** a stage is complete. At one ball, this is the major **cash versus continue** decision. [S5]
* **Rock-It Meter and Song Jackpot on the display:** a falling meter explains why a player stops cradling; a huge jackpot explains why they may abandon further Song progress and cash immediately. [S5]

## Match strategy

**Playing ahead:** Build enough locks to make the Song safe, but cash a healthy Song Jackpot instead of insisting on another stage or Encore. Useful Booster Multiballs provide safer progress without requiring maximum setup. **Strategic inference** from the confirmed cash-or-continue structure.

**Playing behind:** Prepare several Booster Multiballs and Patches before the sixth lock, start with a strong Song Level, then **push through successive stages instead of cashing early**. The scoring grows dramatically, and Band Frenzy/Encore can turn one long Song into a match-changing ball. **Observed strategy.** [S5][S8] ([Tilt Forums][4])

**Key recurring decision:** **“Is this Song Jackpot enough?”** Cashing locks in a guaranteed score; continuing gives another ball and a much higher ceiling, but no equivalent fresh safety net and a drain can erase the uncollected opportunity.

## Danger zones

* **Left-ramp reject:** documented tournament/owner examples can rebound nearly straight down the middle. [S8] ([Pinball Info][8])
* **Center scoop rejection/kickout:** strongly setup-dependent; poor alignment can turn the game's most important cash-out shot into a drain risk. [S8] ([Pinside][9])
* **COMA-side ball movement:** balls can jump or reach the left-outlane area unexpectedly; useful when COMA is lit, disastrous when it is not. [S8]
* **Central spinner/standup area:** repeated attempts to finish missing band members produce direct rebounds with little recovery time.
* **Cradling during Songs:** unusually, safe-looking control has a rules cost—the Rock-It Meter drains while the player waits. [S5]

## Trivia

* **Slash was a hands-on co-designer**, working with Eric Meunier on the game rather than serving only as a licensing participant. [S2][S6] ([Jersey Jack Pinball][10])
* The game contains **21 full-length Guns N' Roses master recordings**, and the rules turn the songs themselves into multiball scoring modes. [S2] ([Jersey Jack Pinball][2])
* Standard Edition has **three flippers**; LE/CE add a fourth flipper and the physical Gibson-headstock upper playfield. [S2]
* JJP paired the large backbox display with a separate **7-inch “stage” display** embedded in the playfield. [S2]
* The lack of Power Chord is a genuine **rules difference**, not merely cosmetic cost reduction: designer Keith Johnson specifically identified it as the principal rule missing from SE. [S7] ([Pinside][6])

### Uncertainties and conflicts

* **Scoring revision:** the maintained detailed rulesheet [S5] is rooted in **V1.20**, while JJP's V3.00 changelog explicitly says Song and Album scoring/rules were extensively rebalanced. Structural concepts such as locks, Song stages, Rock-It Meter and the cash/continue decision remain useful; old precise scoring figures should not be printed as current without on-machine verification. [S4][S5] ([Pinside][1])
* **Power Chord:** generic GNR documentation sometimes describes it without an edition warning. JJP's feature matrix shows SE lacks the physical guitar-lock upper playfield, and Keith Johnson explicitly states **Power Chord is absent on SE**. [S2][S7] ([Jersey Jack Pinball][2])
* **Competition Mode:** JJP documentation says it disables random awards and carry-over features, but the public operator excerpt does not enumerate every individual progression item affected. [S3] ([Moss Distributing][5])
* **Physical feeds:** left-ramp and scoop behavior vary substantially with pitch, alignment and coil strength. [S8]
