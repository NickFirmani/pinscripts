## Identity and versions

* **El Dorado — D. Gottlieb & Co., 1975**, model 370; one-player electro-mechanical wedgehead with four flippers, two pop bumpers and **15 drop targets**: ten across the top and five on the right. The supplied request says “Gottlieb 1976,” but Gottlieb's original El Dorado is a **1975** game; IPDB dates it April 1975 and records 2,875 produced.  [S1] ([IPDB][1])
* Design: **Ed Krynski**; art: **Gordon Morison**. [S1][S5] ([Kineticist][2])
* The 1976 date is plausibly confusion with **Target Alpha** or **Canada Dry**, Gottlieb multiplayer derivatives, or the **Europlay El Dorado** copy. Their rules are materially different. [S1][S5] ([Kineticist][2])
* No multiball, jackpot, end-of-ball bonus, software revision, or playfield multiplier.
* **3-ball versus 5-ball configuration materially changes the rules.** On factory 3-ball settings, completing the **top ten-target bank alone** scores 5K, resets both banks and lights Special; on 5-ball, **both banks must be completed**. [S2] ([Pinside][3])

## Thirty-second game plan

1. **Shoot whichever drop target is lit:** lit drops score **5,000**, dark ones only **500**. This 10× difference is the dominant tournament strategy. **Confirmed rule / observed strategy.** [S3][S4] ([Silverball Rules][4])
2. **Move the lit target deliberately** with the center star rollover or lower standup/bull's-eye when that creates an easier shot. [S4][S6] ([Credit Dot Pinball][5])
3. On the usual **3-ball tournament setup**, completing the upper ten-bank resets **both** banks, giving the player fresh targets to continue farming 5K shots. [S2]
4. Soft-plunge the **lit 5K upper-right lane** whenever possible; it is meaningful relative to a game where ordinary targets score only 500. [S3] ([Silverball Rules][4])
5. **Alternate strategy:** when a particular lit drop is too dangerous, clear useful dark targets—especially in the top bank on 3-ball—toward the 5K bank-completion/reset instead of forcing a bad 5K shot. **Strategic inference.**

## Core rules and persistence

* Drop targets score **500 normally / 5,000 when aligned with the traveling lit spot**. [S2][S3] ([pdfcoffee.com][6])
* The traveling spot can be moved by the **center star rollover** and a lower standup/bull's-eye. Skilled play is therefore not merely “clear the targets”; it is choosing when to move the spot and then hitting the newly lit target. [S4][S6] ([MAACA][7])
* **3 balls:** completing the **top target sequence** scores 5K, resets both target banks and lights Special. **5 balls:** both target sequences must be finished for that reset/award. [S2] ([Pinside][3])
* Factory cards also change incidental scoring: lit pops are **1,000 on 3-ball / 100 on 5-ball**, while the card's “Moving Spot” switch award is **100 on 3-ball / 1,000 on 5-ball**. [S2]
* **Target progress carries across ordinary drains.** The factory operating sequence explicitly resets the banks at **game start**, and separately resets them when the qualifying target sequence is completed; normal outhole handling only advances the ball count. [S5] ([pdfcoffee.com][6])
* There is **no end-of-ball bonus**. Score comes directly from shots, making each 5K lit target conspicuously valuable.
* Easy commentator trap: this is **not Target Alpha**. Target Alpha replaces El Dorado's moving-spot system with end-of-ball bonus and therefore has a substantially different tournament strategy. [S7] ([Silverball Rules][8])

## Skill shots

* **Lit-lane Skill Shot:** soft-plunge into the **lit 5,000-point upper-right lane**. This is the established tournament skill shot. [S3] ([Silverball Rules][4])
* Why choose it: it equals the value of a successful lit drop target without first exposing the ball to target-bank rebounds.
* No separate super, secret, button-controlled or alternate skill shot was found.

## Secondary features

* **Special / replay:** after the qualifying target-bank completion, the banks reset and **Special** lights on the traveling top-target position. Special awards a replay, not an extra ball. [S2][S5] ([Pinside][3])
* The factory has a **liberal/conservative adjustment** controlling whether completion itself awards a replay; the 5K score and reset remain the strategically relevant pieces in tournament play. [S5] ([pdfcoffee.com][6])
* **No extra ball:** do not import the add-a-ball rules from **Gold Strike/Lucky Strike**; those are related but different games. [S4][S5] ([Silverball Rules][9])
* No ball save, kickback, mystery, video mode, player-controlled utility, or Magna-Save.

## What to watch

* **One lit insert underneath a standing drop:** that's the money target—**5K instead of 500**.
* **Center star or lower bull's-eye hit:** watch the light move; the player's intended target may change immediately. [S4][S6]
* **Only a few upper-bank targets standing:** on a 3-ball setup the player may abandon strict 5K hunting and finish the bank to reset everything.
* **Banks suddenly reset during the ball:** the qualifying sequence was completed; Special is now lit and a fresh scoring cycle has begun. [S2]

## Match strategy

**Playing ahead:** Keep taking controllable **lit 5K targets** and avoid forcing long cross-playfield targets merely to complete the bank. If the spot can be moved safely, move it rather than shoot an ugly target. **Observed strategy.** [S3][S4]

**Playing behind:** Be more willing to clear dark upper targets. On **3-ball**, finishing that top bank produces the 5K completion award and resets all 15 targets, reopening the entire moving-spot scoring cycle. **Strategic inference** from the factory rules. [S2]

**Key recurring decision:** **Shoot the currently lit 5K target, or move/clear toward a better target and eventual reset?** The former maximizes immediate value; the latter may improve the next several shots.

## Danger zones

* **Any drop target:** this is fundamentally a target-rebound game; a clean hit can still return center or toward an outlane. [S3]
* **Left upper feed:** may exit left toward the outlane/inlane or kick right toward the flipper/center; learn the copy before nudging automatically. [S3]
* **Slow right-side feed:** can leak toward the right inlane/outlane divider and require an early nudge. [S3]
* **Fast right-side feed:** may naturally cross safely toward the left flipper; unnecessary nudging can make it worse. [S3]
* **Right-side drops:** rarely clean conventional backhands; experienced players sometimes use a moving/flying backhand when trapping is unlikely. [S3] ([Silverball Rules][4])

## Trivia

* El Dorado is one of **Ed Krynski's** best-known drop-target layouts, with **Gordon Morison** artwork. [S1][S5]
* Gottlieb produced **2,875** originals. [S1] ([IPDB][1])
* Its unusual upper playfield omits a conventional arch so a **ten-target bank spans almost the entire top**; another five-bank sits down the right side. [S6] ([MAACA][7])
* Gottlieb reused the layout repeatedly: **Gold Strike, Lucky Strike, Target Alpha, Canada Dry, Solar City**, and eventually the solid-state **El Dorado City of Gold**. [S1][S7] ([Kineticist][2])
* The multiplayer derivatives removed the moving-spot scoring that makes original El Dorado distinctive, replacing it with bonus-oriented rules. [S7] ([Credit Dot Pinball][5])

### Uncertainties and conflicts

* **Year:** “Gottlieb 1976” is not supported. IPDB records **April 1975** [S1]; other historical sources place release in March 1975 [S5]. The month conflicts slightly, but **1975 is well established**. ([IPDB][1])
* **Target persistence:** the factory operating instructions reset the banks at game start and on sequence completion, supporting ball-to-ball persistence [S5]; one museum description says they reset after a drain. The factory documentation should control. ([pdfcoffee.com][6])
* **3-ball/5-ball rules:** these are not cosmetic. The official cards explicitly change the bank-completion requirement and incidental scoring, so tournament ball count must be confirmed. [S2]
* **Replay behavior:** Gottlieb's liberal/conservative adjustment changes whether a bank completion itself awards a replay. That usually has no match-scoring effect unless the tournament has an unusual replay conversion. [S5]
