* **Cirqus Voltaire** — Bally/Midway, October 1997; **Williams WPC-95**, model 50062. Requested scope is the original Bally machine. 
* Design: **John Popadiuk and Cameron Silver**; software: **Cameron Silver**; art: **Linda Deal**; mechanics: **Jack Skalon and Louis Toy**. Production: **2,704**. [S3][S4] ([Pinside][1])
* Distinctive hardware includes the rising **Ringmaster**, disappearing **Boom Balloon** pop bumper, Menagerie captive ball, Highwire magnet/physical lock, playfield-mounted DMD, and neon ramp. [S1][S3] ([Planetary Pinball][2])
* **Latest official revision verified: V1.4, October 14, 1998.** **V2.0H** is later and substantially expanded, but is explicitly an **unofficial Home ROM**, not supported by Williams. It adds rule options including a different skill-shot system and extra Join the Cirqus phases. [S3] ([Matt's Basement Arcade][3])
* Major stackable multiballs include **Highwire, Juggler, Strike-an-Arc**, and the Ringmaster multiballs **Frenzie/Razz/Special**. **Ringmaster Battle does not stack.** [S2] ([GameFAQs][4])

## Thirty-second game plan

1. **Progress Ringmasters while preparing another multiball.** Spell WOW, raise the Ringmaster, hit him five times, then shoot underneath to defeat him. Ringmasters #2–4 start Frenzie, Razz and Special. [S2] ([GameFAQs][4])
2. **Build Highwire or Strike-an-Arc on the left ramp**, or Juggler from repeated left-loop shots, but ideally do not burn them alone. **Observed strategy:** stack a Ringmaster multiball onto another multiball for four-ball play and overlapping jackpots. [S2][S5] ([GameFAQs][4])
3. **Use multiball to collect Marvels safely.** Nine Marvels eventually light all six CIRQUS letters and lead to **Join the Cirqus**, the multi-stage wizard mode. [S2] ([GameFAQs][4])
4. **Ringmaster Battle is the huge alternate scoring route:** on ordinary scoring, repeated Ringmaster hits can generate tens of millions. But **Tournament Mode explicitly lowers Ringmaster Battle scoring**, so on a tournament-configured machine the super-stack / Join-the-Cirqus route becomes relatively more important. [S2][S3] ([GameFAQs][4])
5. Central decision: **cash a ready multiball now, or delay until a Ringmaster is ready so both can start together?**

## Core rules and persistence

* **Highwire Multiball:** light locks using the LIGHT/LOCK standups beside the left ramp, then shoot the ramp to lock three balls. First Highwire allows stacked lock qualifications; later attempts get harder. Three 1M jackpot shots begin at Juggler, Acrobats/right ramp, and the Ringmaster loop, then move. [S2] ([GameFAQs][5])
* **Juggler Multiball:** six left-loop shots total—three to qualify and three to lock/start. Juggler jackpots begin at 500K; two quick Juggler shots can score 1M double jackpot, then a third can score a 2M super. [S2] ([GameFAQs][4])
* **Strike-an-Arc:** repeated left-ramp loops start a 2-ball frenzy; switch hits charge a jackpot starting at 500K and increasing thereafter. Locked Highwire balls count toward the required ramp count. [S2] ([GameFAQs][4])
* **Stacking:** Highwire + Strike-an-Arc + Ringmaster is a documented three-mode stack; Juggler + Ringmaster and several two-way stacks also work. Stacks launch up to four balls and can generate 20M–40M in a strong sequence. [S2][S5] ([GameFAQs][5])
* **Ringmaster Battle:** available after defeating the fifth Ringmaster, provided “Defeat All Ringmasters” was not the final Marvel. It is exclusive—other features cannot start during it. The Ringmaster continually removes progress if not hit; repeated hits score heavily, and defeating him lights an Extra Ball. [S2] ([GameFAQs][4])
* **Bonus:** judges, cannonballs and perfect scores feed end-of-ball bonus; spell **RING** to increase Bonus X up to 4×. Competitive strategy generally treats bonus as secondary to multiball/Ringmaster scoring. [S2] ([GameFAQs][4])
* Easy commentator trap: **spotting the Highwire or Juggler Marvel does not actually start that multiball.** [S2] ([GameFAQs][4])

## Skill shots

* **Normal / simple Skill Shot:** plunge so the ball hits the sideways skill-shot target behind the playfield display area. Scores **200K × successful skill shots**, increasing to 1M and then remaining at 1M. [S2] ([GameFAQs][4])
* **V2.0H complex Skill Shot:** an unofficial-ROM adjustment can replace the simple award with a selectable one-of-five award system. Exact five awards were not sufficiently verified from a primary source here. **Tournament Mode under V2.0H uses the simple skill shot.** [S3] ([Pinside][1])

## Secondary features

* **Ball saves:** normal ball-save count/time and separate multiball saves are operator-adjustable in V2.0H; Highwire, Ringmaster, Strike-an-Arc and Juggler each have configurable saves. [S3] ([Matt's Basement Arcade][3])
* **Ringmaster magnet save:** after WOW, the Ringmaster magnet catches and then throws the ball unpredictably. A short saver can protect against an immediate drain; this behavior itself is adjustable in later code. [S2][S3] ([GameFAQs][4])
* **Spot Marvel:** right inlane briefly lights the left ramp to spot one unfinished Marvel; normally limited to one spotted Marvel per ball. Later code can disable this timed award. [S2][S3] ([GameFAQs][4])
* **Side Show:** Ballyhoo targets light the hidden Side Show scoop. Awards can include **Neon Multiball, Spot Marvel, Light Lock, Extra Ball, 250K/500K**, and the **Amazing Roonie** video mode. Tournament software removes/normalizes some randomness. [S2][S3] ([GameFAQs][4])
* **Extra Balls:** obtainable from several features including Ringmaster progression, Boom Balloon, Side Show and others; tournament handling must be confirmed. [S2] ([GameFAQs][4])
* **Video mode:** Amazing Roonie has the player jump obstacles; completing it is worth about 2.9M in the documented ruleset. [S2] ([GameFAQs][4])

## What to watch

* **Ringmaster physically raised:** the player is in the five-hit attack phase; if another multiball is nearly ready, they may intentionally delay the final defeat to build a stack.
* **Three balls queued along the Highwire:** Highwire Multiball is imminent or ready; the left ramp is about to become the start/cash-out shot.
* **Multiple completed Marvel lamps / CIRQUS letters:** the player is progressing toward Join the Cirqus; three Marvels in a line light the corresponding CIRQUS letter. [S2] ([GameFAQs][4])
* **Boom Balloon rises:** a short timed pop-bumper opportunity is active; its accumulated value also matters later in Party Multiball. [S2] ([GameFAQs][4])

## Match strategy

**Playing ahead:** Start a prepared Highwire/Juggler rather than taking extra single-ball setup risk, then add an already-active Ringmaster when available. On Competition/Tournament settings, do not assume Ringmaster Battle is worth milking indefinitely—the code explicitly lowers its scoring. **Strategic inference** from confirmed rules. [S3]

**Playing behind:** Build a **super-stack**—Highwire + Strike-an-Arc + Ringmaster is the premier example—and attack overlapping jackpots with four balls. If Tournament Mode is off or Ringmaster Battle low-scoring is disabled, reaching Battle creates another enormous high-variance comeback route. **Observed strategy.** [S2][S5] ([Pinside][6])

**Key recurring decision:** **Start the multiball already qualified, or hold it while finishing the next Ringmaster so the Ringmaster multiball can be layered on top?**

## Danger zones

* **Ringmaster loop:** weak counter-clockwise attempts can return directly SDTM. [S2] ([GameFAQs][4])
* **Right ramp:** weak attempts may fall out through the Juggler-side drain instead of completing the safe return. [S2]
* **Ringmaster magnet release:** intentionally random; the game may briefly save a bad throw, but control is lost. [S2]
* **Unusual left inlane/outlane geometry:** there is a large open rubber/post area instead of a normal inlane wireform; nudging matters considerably. [S2] ([GameFAQs][4])
* **Physical lock/diverter system:** failed magnet catches or lock/diverter misbehavior can materially alter feeds; later software contains extensive compensation specifically for this hardware. [S3] ([Matt's Basement Arcade][3])

## Trivia

* Cirqus Voltaire places its **DMD directly on the playfield** rather than in the backbox; Bally marketed this heavily as a major presentation feature. [S1][S4] ([Planetary Pinball][7])
* The **Boom Balloon** is a pop bumper that physically rises out of and disappears back beneath the playfield. [S3][S4]
* The backglass acrobat was modeled on designer **John Popadiuk**. [S2] ([GameFAQs][4])
* There is **no physical replay knocker**; the game uses an electronic sound instead. [S2]
* The neon tube and Menagerie captive ball appeared in multiple colors across production. [S2][S4] ([GameFAQs][4])

### Uncertainties and conflicts

* **Software:** V1.4 is the latest verified official revision, dated October 14, 1998. V2.0H is newer but explicitly **unofficial** and changes enough rules that the installed ROM matters materially. [S3] ([Matt's Basement Arcade][3])
* **Ringmaster Battle strategy:** the classic rulesheet recommends milking it and documents roughly 50M-scale outcomes [S2], but V1.3+ Tournament Mode deliberately activates reduced scoring [S3]. Tournament commentary should not assume the old milk strategy remains dominant. ([GameFAQs][5])
* **Lock stacking:** later software exposes an adjustment controlling how many multiballs allow pre-stacked lock qualifications; venue configuration can therefore change the preferred setup order. [S3]
* **V2.0H skill shot:** the optional complex version allows a choice among five awards, but I did not verify the complete five-award list from a sufficiently strong source.
* **Physical feeds:** magnet behavior, Juggler routing and Highwire lock/diverter reliability are unusually hardware-sensitive on this title. [S2][S3]
