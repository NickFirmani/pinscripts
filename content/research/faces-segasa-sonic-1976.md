## Identity and versions

* **Faces** — Segasa d.b.a. **Sonic**, Spain, 1976; four-player electro-mechanical score-reel game. It has **3 flippers, 2 pop bumpers, 2 spinners, 5 standups, and no multiball**. [S1][S4] ([Museum of the Game][1])
* No designer or artist credit was found in reliable archival sources; I would leave those fields blank rather than infer them.
* Production quantity is **uncertain**: Pinside reports 1,500, while IPSND explicitly lists the production run as unknown. [S4][S6] ([Pinside][2])
* As an EM, there is no software revision. Mechanical adjustment and tournament handling of Extra Balls/Specials are the important configuration variables.
* Scope follows the supplied commentator-reference specification. 

## Thirty-second game plan

1. **Observed strategy:** send the ball **up top** and build the separate **red and green bonus ladders**, each to **100K**. The top saucer and corresponding colored standups advance them. [S2][S3] 
2. Keep the two bonuses reasonably balanced: **only whichever color is lit when the ball drains is collected**. Having 100K green and 20K red is dangerous if red is selected. [S2] 
3. Once substantial bonus is built, hit **A + B** to light **Double Bonus**. With both colors maxed, the drain is worth **200K regardless of which color is selected**. [S2]
4. Once a color reaches 100K, its spinner lights for **1K per spin**; this gives a strong alternate scoring route instead of continuing to expose the ball to standups. [S2]
5. **F-A-C-E-S is secondary in normal tournament scoring** unless Extra Balls/Specials have tournament value. [S2][S5] 

## Core rules and persistence

* There are **two independent bonus ladders**, red and green, each maxing at **100K**. Green standups/left-side features advance green; red standups/right-side features advance red. [S2] 
* **Only the currently selected color pays at end of ball.** This is the central rule to explain on stream. Double Bonus doubles that selected payout. [S2]
* The mid-right **Change Bonus Selection** target changes which color is active. The top saucer can also alter/select a bonus depending on which of its cycling awards is lit when collected. [S2]
* **A + B lights Double Bonus.** Expert strategy recommends building significant bonus first because those targets introduce much more rebound risk than simply playing up top. [S2]
* Once a color bonus is maxed, that side's spinner becomes worth **1K per spin**. [S2]
* The upper-left controlled gate is **not a ball-save gate**. When open, a right-orbit shot can travel fully around the top and down the left rather than being stopped and fed toward the saucer. [S2]
* **Persistence:** surviving strategy documentation strongly treats bonus and Double Bonus as ball-local, but I did not find a primary manual proving the reset details. Mark this **verification-required** on the venue copy.

## Skill shots

**Yellow Top-Saucer Skill Shot** — plunge so the ball reaches the top saucer while its cycling indicator is on the **yellow circle**.

* Awards **5K**.
* Advances **both red and green bonuses**.
* Opens the upper-left gate.
* The award cycles with switch hits, so plunge strength—and how many switches the ball touches beside the saucer—affects which award is active. [S2][S3] ([Kineticist][3])
* Do **not** simply full-plunge: expert guidance warns an over-hard plunge can produce a fast, dangerous rebound through the upper gate area. [S2]

No separate super or alternate skill shot verified.

## Secondary features

* **Ball save:** none found.
* **Extra Ball / Special:** completing the five **F-A-C-E-S rollover buttons** advances an EM progression. The **first completion** is verified to light the bumpers and the 5K right-side exit lane; later progression is associated with Extra Ball and Special. [S2] 
* Exact later F-A-C-E-S award order was **not sufficiently verified from a primary source**. A surviving recreation confirms lit lower lanes can award Extra Balls, but that is not strong enough evidence to specify the complete sequence as a confirmed rule. [S5] ([VPForums.org][4])
* In tournament play, F-A-C-E-S is usually de-emphasized when Extra Balls/Specials have no scoring value. Owner/tournament commentary specifically describes modifying Faces to substitute points for Extra Balls. [S5] ([Pinside][5])
* No video mode, mystery award, lane-change control, Magna-Save, or comparable player utility.

## What to watch

1. **Red/green bonus ladders:** immediately tells how much equity the player has built on each side.
2. **Which bonus arrow/color is lit:** this is the actual end-of-ball payout; a huge unselected bonus does not help if the wrong color is lit. [S2]
3. **Double Bonus lamp:** A and B have both been collected; the selected bonus now has major leverage. [S2]
4. **Lit spinner:** that color has reached 100K, turning its spinner into a meaningful **1K-per-spin** scoring shot. [S2]

## Match strategy

**Playing ahead:** keep the two bonuses balanced and use upper-playfield action rather than forcing dangerous A/B shots too early. Once a spinner is lit, repeated spinner scoring can be preferable to attacking standups. **Observed strategy.** [S2]

**Playing behind:** pursue the full **100K bonus + Double Bonus** package. A 200K end-of-ball payout is enormous relative to ordinary switch scoring; a lit 1K spinner can add another rapid scoring burst. [S2]

**Key recurring decision:** **balance the weak bonus, or exploit the already-maxed side?** Switching to the weak color protects against a disastrous low end-of-ball collect; staying with the strong side enables the lit spinner and immediate scoring.

## Danger zones

1. **B target:** documented as the riskier half of A-B because rebounds expose the right outlane. [S2]
2. **Pop-bumper exits:** balls leaving along the outer lower rubbers can head directly toward the center drain. [S2]
3. **Right side of the central triangle:** balls rolling off it can leak into the right outlane. [S2]
4. **Over-hard plunge:** can produce a violent cross-top rebound involving the controlled gate. [S2]
5. **Open-gate orbit:** whether the around-the-world feed is desirable is highly copy-dependent; some players explicitly prefer the right spinner over relying on the gate feed. [S2][S5] ([Pinside][5])

## Trivia

* Faces was manufactured in **Spain by Segasa under the Sonic trade name** in 1976. [S1][S6] ([Museum of the Game][1])
* Its layout is repeatedly documented as closely related to or influenced by **Williams' Grand Prix (1976)**, but Faces adds its own gate and third-flipper geometry. [S2][S7] ([New York Pinball][7])
* The unusual **upper-left flipper** gives the player a direct attack on the right-side spinner. [S2]
* The upper-left gate is **relay-controlled**—an earned change to ball routing rather than an outlane rescue. [S2][S4] ([Pinball Nirvana][8])
* No reliable designer or artist credit appears in the major machine archives I found. [S4]

### Uncertainties and conflicts

* **Production:** Pinside reports **1,500** [S4], but IPSND lists the production run as **unknown** [S6]. Do not quote 1,500 as certain. ([Pinside][2])
* **Date:** archival databases disagree between January and December 1976. “Sonic, 1976” is safe. [S4][S6]
* **F-A-C-E-S progression:** first-completion effects are documented, but I did not find a primary rules source that cleanly establishes the complete subsequent Extra Ball/Special sequence. [S2]
* **Persistence:** I found no primary manual confirming precisely which bonus/doubler states reset between balls. Tournament strategy sources treat the main bonus build as ball-local; verify the physical machine.
* Physical risk—especially the gate feed—is strongly machine-dependent.
