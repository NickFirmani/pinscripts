## Identity and versions

**Aztec** — Williams Electronics, 1976, model 466. The normal production game is a 4-player **electro-mechanical (EM)** machine with score reels; 10,150 were produced. Design is credited to **Gordon Horlick**; artwork to **Christian Marche**. [S2] ([IPDB][1])

Williams also built **10 solid-state Aztec prototypes** late in 1976. These were early System 2 hybrids: digital scoring, but the gameplay logic remained largely electromechanical and they retained the EM chimes/credit reel. Unless the venue has an exceptionally rare prototype, assume the production EM. [S6] ([PinWiki][2])

No software revision applies to the production EM.

## Thirty-second game plan

1. **Plunge the center top lane** to collect A and light the spinner for **1,000 per spin** instead of 100. [S1][S3]
2. **Shoot the left spinner repeatedly**—“up top all day”—to build the end-of-ball bonus toward its **50K base maximum** while scoring the lit spinner. [S1][S3]
3. Hit **Z or T**, then make the **left orbit/spinner lane or upper-right U-chute** to turn on **Double Bonus**; a max 50K bonus then pays 100K at drain. [S1][S3]
4. The **U-chute/right bonus lane** is the other major scoring shot: it scores **5K, or 10K when doubled, for each lit AZTEC letter** and kicks the ball back into play. A repeatable kickout can produce huge strings. [S5][S3] ([GameFAQs][3])
5. **Alternate tournament route:** if the U-chute feed is hostile, ignore most standups and grind the safer lit spinner. [S3]

## Core rules and persistence

**Confirmed rule:** the center top rollover scores 1K, collects **A**, and changes the spinner from **100 to 1,000 per spin**. [S1] ([Pinball Australia][4])

**Confirmed rule:** bonus begins at **5K**, advances in 5K steps, and tops out at **50K**. Double Bonus can therefore make the end-of-ball payout **100K**. [S1] ([Pinball Australia][4])

**Important two-step Double Bonus rule:** hitting **Z or T qualifies** the relevant lanes; the player must then make the left lane/orbit or right U-chute to energize and hold the Double Bonus relay. Merely hitting Z/T is not the entire cash-out. [S1][S3] ([Pinball Australia][4])

**Persistence:** bonus and the A-Z-T-E-C feature relays are **ball-specific**. The outhole sequence resets the letter relays when the ball ends; the next ball starts again with 5K bonus. [S1][S7] ([Pinside][5])

**Source correction:** Bob's otherwise excellent EM guide says the “maximum base” bonus is 100K. The Williams manual and physical 5K–50K bonus unit establish that this is a mistake: **50K is the base maximum; 100K is the doubled payout.** [S1][S3] ([Pinball Australia][4])

## Skill shots

**Center top rollover**

* **Shot:** plunge through the center of the three top lanes.
* **Award:** 1K, lights A, and lights the spinner for 1K/spin.
* **Why choose it:** it immediately enables the game's safest repeatable scoring engine.
* **Persistence:** A/spinner qualification resets at the end of the ball.
* **Cue:** **A** and **1000 SPINNER** lamps illuminate. [S1][S3][S7] 

The lit left/right top rollovers are useful secondary plunge outcomes because they advance bonus, but no separate alternate/super skill-shot rule was found.

## Secondary features

**Extra Ball:** collecting AZTEC letters can light the dangerous **center standup** for Extra Ball. Qualification is operator-adjustable; documented configurations include stricter and more liberal letter combinations. [S1][S5] ([Pinball Australia][4])

There is **no verified built-in tournament point conversion** for the Extra Ball. Whether it is active, disabled, or handled by event rule therefore matters.

**Special:** completing the required AZTEC condition plus reaching 50K bonus can light the alternating bottom outlane Special. Its useful tournament value depends on the credit/extra-ball and tournament setup. [S1][S5] ([Pinball Australia][4])

No stock ball save, Magna-Save, action button, mystery award, video mode, lane-change flipper control, or multiball was found.

## What to watch

* **1000 SPINNER lit:** A has been collected; repeated spinner shots are now materially valuable. [S1]
* **50,000 bonus lit:** base bonus is maxed; a Double Bonus cash-out represents a 100K drain payout. [S1]
* **DOUBLE BONUS lamp:** Z/T setup plus a qualifying lane has succeeded; the current bonus will pay twice. [S1]
* **Several AZTEC letters lit in the right chute:** each successful U-chute visit can now be worth tens of thousands, making its kickout especially important. [S5]

## Match strategy

**Playing ahead:** favor the **lit spinner** and avoid unnecessary C/E/center-target shots. Once bonus is healthy, whether to pursue Double Bonus depends heavily on how safely that particular machine returns the U-chute/orbit. **Strategic inference** based on the documented risk profile. [S3]

**Playing behind:** pursue **50K + Double Bonus**, and exploit the U-chute if its kickout is repeatable. Multiple letters plus doubled chute scoring and a 100K end-of-ball bonus can change the match quickly. [S1][S3][S5]

**Key recurring decision:** **spinner safety versus feature value.** The spinner offers controlled repeat scoring; Z/T and especially the U-chute offer much more leverage but expose the ball to dangerous standups and kicker rebounds.

## Danger zones

* **U-chute kickout:** can strike the upper-right bumper, glance off the chute rubber and head straight down the middle. [S3]
* **U-chute outside graze:** misses around the outside are specifically documented as center-drain hazards. [S3]
* **Upper bumper exits:** unpredictable and particularly dangerous toward the **left outlane**. [S3]
* **AZTEC standups:** square hits can rebound directly toward either side drain. [S3]
* **Center Extra Ball target:** valuable only when lit and enabled; otherwise it adds center-shot risk for little strategic return. [S3]

## Trivia

* Williams produced **10,150 EM Aztecs**, a very large run for the period. [S2] ([IPDB][1])
* The rare **10-machine SS prototype run** was part of Williams' transition toward solid-state pinball; the computer handled scoring rather than the full rules engine. [S6] ([PinWiki][2])
* Full-production Williams solid state would not arrive until **Hot Tip** in 1977. [S6]
* IPDB credits **Gordon Horlick** based in part on confirmation from Barry Oursler, who worked on Aztec's production before becoming a Williams game designer himself. [S2] ([IPDB][1])
* IPDB notes that the backglass imagery appears to draw from illustrations in the 1955 National Geographic book *Indians of the Americas*. [S2]

### Uncertainties and conflicts

* **Bonus maximum:** [S3] contains an internal error stating a 100K *base* maximum. Manufacturer documentation [S1] establishes **50K base / 100K doubled**.
* **Extra-Ball qualification:** it is adjustment-sensitive. Sources consistently identify AZTEC-letter qualification but descriptions differ depending on machine adjustment. Confirm the venue's conservative/medium/liberal setting before putting a specific combination on the final sheet. [S1][S5]
* **SS prototype:** rules differences from the EM were not found to be competitively significant; [S6] says the electronics primarily replaced scoring. If the venue somehow has an SS prototype, it warrants direct physical verification.
