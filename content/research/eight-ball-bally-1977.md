## Identity and versions

**Eight Ball** — Bally Manufacturing, **1977**, model **1118-E**. Four-player early solid-state game on Bally MPU **AS-2518-17**, with two flippers, three pop bumpers, spinner, left-outlane kickback and no multiball. Design: **George Christian**; artwork credited to **Paul Faris and Margaret Hudson**. Production: **20,230 machines**. [S1][S2] ([Pinball Serial Number Database][1])

There are no meaningful editions or software revisions to distinguish. One unusual rules distinction matters constantly: **Players 1/3 shoot solids 1–7; Players 2/4 shoot stripes 9–15.** Both are functionally the same seven-ball rack followed by the shared 8-ball. [S1]

## Thirty-second game plan

1. **Hit the 8-ball target early even when it isn't qualified.** It lights the **1,000-point spinner** and the left-side Break Shot/kickback. [S1][S3] 
2. Work the upper-right **Bank Shot/candy-cane horseshoe** to advance toward **2X → 3X → 5X bonus**. Completing the four top lanes can also light a center top lane for another advance. [S1][S3]
3. Once **5X + lit spinner** are established, strong tournament strategy is largely **spinner all day** from the right flipper: it scores 1K/spin, returns the ball up top, and naturally collects rack balls. [S3] ([Silverball Rules][2])
4. Let the awkward **7-ball right return lane** happen naturally. If only 5/6 remain, consider their standups; after all seven are lit, shoot the lit 8-ball to complete the rack. [S3]
5. Completing a rack is a long-term payoff: it establishes **24K Super Bonus on following balls**, before multiplier and new-rack additions. On the final ball, immediate spinner/5X scoring may therefore be more valuable. [S1]

## Core rules and persistence

Each collected numbered ball scores **500 immediately** and adds **3,000 to end-of-ball bonus**. Bonus can be multiplied by **2X, 3X or 5X**. [S1] 

**Rack progress persists between balls for that player.** When 1–7 or 9–15 are complete, the 8-ball target lights. Hitting the lit 8-ball completes the rack; that rack resets on the player's **next turn**, not immediately. [S1]

Completing the rack establishes **Super Bonus beginning on the following turn**: **24,000 + 3,000 for every newly lit rack ball**, all multiplied by the current bonus multiplier. At 5X, the locked-in 24K alone is already **120K at drain**, before new rack progress. [S1]

Easy commentary mistake: **hitting the 8-ball target while unqualified is still useful.** It lights the spinner and Break Shot lane/kickback; qualification only determines whether the hit also completes the rack, lights the right-outlane Special, and establishes Super Bonus. [S1]

The Bank Shot sequence advances through point awards and **2X / 3X / 5X**, then an **Extra Ball or 15K** depending adjustment. [S1] 

## Skill shots

**Observed tournament plunge / “skill shot” strategy:** [S3]

* **Ball 1:** aim for the **4 lane**, whose exit can favor an 8-ball-target hit or clockwise candy-cane feed.
* **Later balls:** plunge whichever numbered top lane is still missing.
* If all four top numbers are already collected, aim for the currently lit **2 or 3 center lane**, which awards the lit Bank Shot value/advance.

There is **no separate manufacturer-defined skill-shot bonus**; this is competitive terminology for optimizing the plunge through existing rules. [S1][S3]

## Secondary features

**Break Shot / left-outlane kickback:** hitting the 8-ball target lights it. A ball entering while lit scores **5K and is kicked back into play**; unlit, the lane is a drain. Competitive documentation notes that using the kickback extinguishes both the kickback and lit spinner, making another 8-ball-target hit an immediate priority. [S1][S3] 

**Extra Ball:** the Bank Shot progression reaches an X-ball award after 5X. Operator switch S31 can replace that with **15,000 points**. Factory instruction-card configurations limit awards to **one Extra Ball per ball in play**. [S1][S4] 

**Special:** completing a rack lights the **right outlane**. Operator adjustments can make that award either a replay or **15K**, and can make the lamp persist only for that ball or for the player's remaining turns. This materially affects tournament strategy. [S1] 

No timed ball save, mystery, video mode or player-controlled feature was found.

## What to watch

* **Spinner / left kickback lit:** somebody has hit the 8-ball target; spinner grinding becomes the obvious play. [S1]
* **Bank Shot showing 2X / 3X / 5X:** tells you how valuable the eventual drain bonus is and whether the player still needs the candy-cane shot. [S1]
* **Seven numbered pool balls lit:** the 8-ball is now qualified; hitting it establishes future Super Bonus. [S1]
* **SUPER BONUS lit:** that player has already completed a rack on an earlier ball; their drain bonus now starts with the locked 24K base. [S1]

## Match strategy

**Playing ahead:** get the kickback lit, establish 5X, then favor the **lit spinner**. Avoid deliberately blasting the 5/6 standups or trying to manufacture the 7-ball return unless rack completion is very close. **Observed strategy.** [S3]

**Playing behind:** earlier in the game, completing a rack is worth pursuing because Super Bonus can transform every later drain into a six-figure award at 5X. If only 5 or 6 remain, aggressive standup shots become justified. [S1][S3]

On the **final ball**, the equation changes: a newly completed rack's Super Bonus does not begin until the next turn. Without an earned Extra Ball, immediate 5X spinner scoring can be much more useful than forcing rack completion. This is a **strategic inference from the confirmed persistence rule**. [S1]

**Key recurring decision:** **finish the rack or stay on spinner?** The spinner is repeatable immediate scoring; rack completion is harder and partly dependent on the 7 lane, but creates enormous future-ball bonus leverage.

## Danger zones

* **Pop-bumper exits:** both primary upper shots feed this area; balls can rocket toward the left outlane. [S3]
* **8-ball target rebound:** a soft square hit can simply dribble straight down the middle. [S3]
* **Weak spinner attempts:** especially a marginal backhand, can roll back through the spinner and head center-drain. [S3]
* **5/6 standups:** rebounds are dangerous enough that competitive guidance says to avoid them unless needed to finish the rack. [S3]
* **Right return / 7 ball:** the desired switch sits beside the right outlane; trying to manufacture it adds substantial drain risk. [S3]

## Trivia

* Bally produced **20,230 Eight Balls**, and it remained the highest-production flipper game until *The Addams Family* surpassed it in 1992. [S2] ([Pinside][3])
* Despite being solid-state, the game uses an **MPU-controlled four-note chime box** rather than a conventional electronic sound board. [S2][S5] ([Pinball Nirvana][4])
* George Christian designed the game; Paul Faris and Margaret Hudson are credited with its memorable pool-hall artwork. [S2]
* The rules exploit multiplayer electronics cleverly: odd players see **solids**, even players **stripes**, while using exactly the same physical switches. [S1]
* The game has no drop targets at all—its “rack” is remembered electronically from rollovers and standups, an especially visible use of solid-state memory in 1977. [S1][S2]

### Uncertainties and conflicts

* **Tournament award settings are highly material.** Bally explicitly allows the Bank Shot Extra Ball to become 15K and the right-outlane Special to become 15K rather than a replay; the Special's persistence is also adjustable. [S1]
* **Plunge “skill shot”:** [S3] calls the optimized top-lane plunge a skill shot, but the manufacturer manual documents only the underlying lane awards. Treat “skill shot” as competitive terminology, not an official named rule.
* **Spinner value after kickback:** the competitive guide [S3] states that a successful kickback extinguishes both kickback and spinner. The manufacturer manual confirms the two are jointly lit by the 8-ball target but does not explicitly describe that post-save extinguishing behavior in its feature summary.
* No meaningful ROM revision or alternate-edition rules conflict was found.
