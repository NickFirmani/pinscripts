## Identity and versions

**Stranger Things — Premium**, Stern Pinball, **2019/2020**, SPIKE 2/LCD. Lead design **Brian Eddy**; software/rules **Lonnie D. Ropp, Mike Vinikour and Eddy**; art **Bob Stevlic**; mechanics **Robert Blakeman**; sound/music **Ken Hale**. [S1][S5]

Premium/LE add three strategically relevant mechanisms absent from Pro: the **playfield video projector**, animated/shaking Demogorgon, and Eleven's **physical magnetic Telekinesis lock** above the left ramp. [S1]

Latest verified Premium code: **LE/PRE v1.12.0, November 26, 2024**. v1.12 is primarily system/support work; v1.11 added a strategically relevant **Telekinesis maximum locked-balls adjustment** plus left-ramp diverter/flipper-power configuration. [S2]

Major multiballs include **Telekinesis, Demogorgon Battle multiballs, Total Isolation, Send It Back, Light the Fire, and Final Showdown**. [S3]

## Thirty-second game plan

1. **Start a Chapter, then bring in Telekinesis Multiball.** Chapter modes cannot normally be started once TKMB is running, so setup order matters. [S3]
2. For a bigger stack, go **Chapter → Demodog Attack → Telekinesis MB**. This is the documented high-leverage stacking order. [S3]
3. Work **Burn It Back** whenever convenient: it eventually lights **Spell of Protection** at the left outlane and **60 seconds of 2X all scoring**. [S3]
4. On Premium, lock one/two balls on Eleven's magnet during TKMB to make the Super Jackpot **2X/3X**; 2X scoring can double it again. [S3]
5. Alternate strategy: attack **Demogorgon progression**, especially the second Battle multiball; competitive players disagree whether elaborate TK stacks outperform taking strong Demogorgon opportunities immediately. **Observed strategy conflict.** [S3][S4]

## Core rules and persistence

**Chapters:** three shots to a ramp/orbit qualify its Chapter. Four different Chapter starts qualify **Total Isolation I**; eight qualify Total Isolation II. Six Season-1 Chapters lead to **Send It Back**; six Season-2 Chapters to **Light the Fire**. [S3]

**Telekinesis Multiball:** complete the two green LIGHT LOCK standups flanking the left ramp, then shoot the left ramp for locks; three locks start MB. First TKMB is easy to qualify—all three locks light after one standup completion. Later starts require progressively more work. [S3]

During TKMB, five jackpots light a **6M Super Jackpot** at the left eject. On Premium/LE, locking one or two balls on the magnet raises that Super to **2X/3X**. Burn-It-Back 2X scoring applies again, so the same Super can effectively score at up to **6X base**. [S3]

**Swipe-a-Ball — Premium/LE only:** if Telekinesis balls are already physically locked when *another* multiball begins, the action button can steal one into the active MB. That costs one stored TK lock afterward. There is no documented per-game usage limit. [S3]

**Demogorgon Bomb:** once per game, the action button instantly kills the current Demogorgon for reduced value; saving it for a difficult later Demogorgon is usually more valuable than spending it on the easy first one. [S3]

**Bonus is unusually important.** Major achievements add permanent components to end-of-ball bonus; Chapter scoring contributes 10% of that ball's Chapter points, and Bonus X can reach **5X**. Strong tournament players specifically warn **not to tilt** after a productive ball. [S3][S4]

## Skill shots

* **Skill Shot:** plunge into the flashing center drop target — **5M +1M per prior success**. [S3]
* **Super Skill Shot:** soft plunge to the flippers, then hit the left eject/kicker — **10M +2.5M per success**. [S3]
* **MXV Super Skill Shot:** make both on the same plunge via a drop-target rebound into the kicker. Awards both underlying shots plus **25M +5M per MXV**, and a **Drawing**. [S3]

The MXV is the materially important high-risk option because Drawings add to wizard-mode jackpots and bonus.

## Secondary features

**Spell of Protection:** Burn It Back lights a one-use **left-outlane save**. Verified thresholds include 3, 12, 25, then every 14 loops. [S3]

**2X Scoring:** Burn It Back also lights 2X at the left eject; collect it for **60 seconds of double scoring**. Verified thresholds include 7, 18, then every 14 loops. This is one of the game's biggest tournament leverage mechanisms. [S3]

**Mystery:** pseudo-random normally but **deterministic in Competition Mode**. Important awards include Add-A-Ball, Light Lock, Start Chapter, Spell of Protection and Add Time. [S3]

**Extra Balls:** multiple qualifications exist; disabled EBs score **15M** in the documented rules. Stern's current **Competition Install sets NO EXTRA BALLS**. [S2][S3]

## What to watch

* **Three balls suspended on Eleven's magnetic wall:** Telekinesis MB is ready; fewer locked balls may also become Swipe-a-Ball ammunition. [S1][S3]
* **Burn It Back / 2X lit at left eject:** expect the player to time that collect around a multiball or lucrative Chapter.
* **Demogorgon ramp drops and mouth exposed:** a Demogorgon Battle is ready; center becomes the critical high-risk shot.
* **Bonus X approaching 5X:** a drain may produce a huge score jump; a tilt becomes especially costly. [S3]

## Match strategy

**Playing ahead:** prioritize **Spell of Protection**, safe Chapter progress, and a straightforward Chapter + TKMB stack. Protect a large accumulated bonus; don't force Demogorgon drops solely for progression when safer ramps/orbits are available. **Observed strategy / inference.** [S3][S4]

**Playing behind:** build **2X**, then launch a larger Chapter → Demodog → TK stack, use Premium's 3X TK Super potential, or pursue a lucrative Demogorgon multiball. Deep scoring is enormous: *Can't Stop This!* offers repeated Supers up to **100M base** during a 60-second unlimited-save phase, while Upside Down 2 can approach **80–90M before 2X**. [S3]

**Key recurring decision:** **how much setup before multiball?** Some tournament players advocate the full three-feature stack; others deliberately take Demogorgon/TK opportunities earlier because one extra setup shot—especially at center or the Premium left ramp—can simply end the ball. [S3][S4]

## Danger zones

* **Center drop targets:** repeatedly documented as severe SDTM/rebound hazards; use multiball protection when practical. [S4]
* **Premium Telekinesis ramp reject:** the added lock hardware requires more momentum; weak/rejected shots can return straight down the middle. [S4]
* **Telekinesis magnet failure/drop:** setup-sensitive; an unsuccessful physical lock can dump the ball back down the left ramp. [S4]
* **Left eject kickout:** some machines feed the sling dangerously; learn it before treating Mystery/2X as a safe collect. [S4]
* **Demogorgon mouth alignment:** machines vary materially in how reliably a clean center shot enters the mouth. [S4]

## Trivia

* *Stranger Things* marked **Brian Eddy's return to production pinball design** after classics including *Attack from Mars* and *Medieval Madness*. [S1][S5]
* Premium/LE introduced Stern's **first playfield video projector**, projecting animations directly onto the Demogorgon screen, ramps and targets. [S1]
* The Premium's wall-mounted magnetic lock physically suspends balls to represent **Eleven's telekinesis**. [S1]
* **David Harbour** recorded custom Jim Hopper speech for the game. [S5]
* The machine's main code is based on the show's **first two seasons**, with six Chapter modes for each season. [S3]

### Uncertainties and conflicts

* **Current rulesheet revision:** the detailed community rulesheet still labels itself **v1.09** [S3], while current Premium software is **v1.12.0** [S2]. v1.12 is system-focused; v1.11 changed Telekinesis lock/diverter adjustments, so venue configuration matters more than a major rules rewrite.
* **Dominant tournament route:** documented competitive opinions conflict. The rulesheet recommends **Chapter → Demodog → TKMB** for maximum stacking [S3], while experienced tournament discussion includes players who prefer taking Demogorgon #2 or simpler mode/MB routes rather than risking extensive setup [S4].
* **Telekinesis physical behavior:** v1.11 introduced hardware-related lock, diverter and flipper-power adjustments [S2]; older and newer production machines may therefore play materially differently even on identical code.
* **Upside Down:** current rules document potentially large scoring, especially Upside Down 2 [S3], while several tournament players still recommend ignoring it because the required drops are too dangerous [S4]. Treat that as risk preference, not a rules conflict.
