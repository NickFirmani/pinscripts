## Identity and versions

* **Banzai Run** — Williams Electronic Games, May 1988; model 566, **Williams System 11B**. Design by **Pat Lawlor and Larry DeMar**, software by **Larry DeMar and Ed Boon**, art by **Mark Sprenger**, mechanics by **John Krutsch**, music/sound by **Brian Schmidt**. Approximately 1,750 units were produced. [S1][S2] ([Pinside][1])
* Its defining mechanism is **Banzai Hill**, a playable vertical second playfield occupying the backbox; a magnet lifts the ball from the main playfield to it. [S1][S3] ([Pinside][2])
* Latest normal production ROM I could verify: **L-3**. Tournament-modified ROMs/settings exist and can radically alter strategy; notably, INDISC 2020 fixed Cliff Jumps at 10K and disabled Freestyle. [S1][S5] ([Kineticist][3])
* One major **2-ball multiball**, culminating in the **King of the Hill** 1M jackpot and then unlimited 250K Victory Laps. [S3] ([Pinball.org][4])

## Thirty-second game plan

1. **Observed strategy:** challenge one rider—**Green Machine is generally the safest**—to open Banzai Hill, then repeatedly return to the Hill rather than immediately defeating every challenged rider. [S4][S5] ([Kineticist][5])
2. On the Hill, shoot **A-B-C** to light **2× Cycle/Cliff Jump**, then make the upper-left Cliff Jump. Its base value grows with successive jumps and persists ball-to-ball. [S3][S4] ([Pinball.org][4])
3. **Keep at least one challenged rider undefeated.** Clearing everyone currently flashing closes the Hill and forces more dangerous main-playfield qualification. This is the central tournament exploit/strategy. [S3][S4][S5] ([Pinball.org][4])
4. Alternate route: defeat all four riders → make the Hill lock → start multiball → Time Lock at the center saucer → Hill captive ball for **1M King of the Hill** → loop the main ramp for **250K Victory Laps**. [S3][S4] ([Pinball.org][4])
5. If Cliff Jumps have been nerfed by tournament ROM/settings, that alternate route becomes the **primary strategy**. [S6] ([Scout's Scoop][6])

## Core rules and persistence

* Four normal opponents—**Green Machine, Blue Beard, Yellow Belly, Red Hot**—must first be **challenged** on the lower playfield, then defeated at their matching Hill shots. A challenged-but-undefeated rider keeps the Hill open. [S3] ([Pinball.org][4])
* Before the first multiball, the main **ramp spots one opponent target**; the center Bumper Saucer also spots opponent progress. Ramp spotting itself is operator-adjustable by number of races. [S2][S3] 
* **Cycle Jump value persists between balls.** A-B-C doubles only the Jump collected on that Hill visit; completing A-B-C also extends the Hill center-post protection. [S3] ([Pinball.org][4])
* After all four riders are defeated, the Hill lock starts 2-ball multiball. Shoot the center saucer for **Time Lock**, then reach Banzai Hill and hit the upper captive ball for **1M**. The Time Lock is roughly 20 seconds in the rulesheet and can allow one or two Hill attempts depending on adjustment. [S2][S3] 
* After the jackpot, the main ramp scores **250K Victory Laps** for the remainder of multiball. [S3] ([Pinball.org][4])
* Laps and Cycle Jump value carry ball-to-ball; rider progress carries until multiball, then defeated riders reset after that ball. There is **no bonus multiplier**. [S3] ([Pinball.org][4])

## Skill shots

**Super Cycle Stunt / Cycle Stunt** — plunge directly into the **Hill Entrance** rather than the normal top lanes. It awards 100K on the first success, increasing by 100K to a 500K maximum, and normally sends the ball directly to Banzai Hill. [S3] ([GameFAQs][7])

It can also immediately challenge rider(s), but this is **operator-adjustable from zero through four riders**, making the venue setting highly significant. [S2] 

On the plunge that begins multiball, the skill shot remains available for points but does not send that ball to the Hill. [S3] ([GameFAQs][7])

No separate verified alternate/secret skill shot found.

## Secondary features

* **No conventional ball save found.** Multiball instead has **Instant Rematch**: if the jackpot is missed and multiball ends, the center saucer can restart it during an operator-adjustable window of roughly **23 or 28 seconds**. [S2][S4] 
* **Left outlane kickback:** center Bumper Saucer relights it. Its initial/persistent behavior is highly adjustable, from always-on through off-every-ball. [S2][S3] 
* **Extra balls:** configurable lap threshold at the lower captive ball; Finish Line also awards one, and A-B-C before the King captive-ball shot can add one if enabled. Tournament treatment therefore matters. [S2][S3] 
* **Freestyle:** a largely uncontrolled mini-playfield can spot/defeat riders after repeated qualification. Strategically secondary and sometimes disabled for competition. [S3][S6] ([Pinball.org][4])
* Flipper buttons lane-change the Green Machine top rollovers. [S3] ([Pinball.org][4])

## What to watch

1. **Flashing rider inserts:** at least one means Banzai Hill is available; solid means that rider has been defeated. [S3]
2. **2× Cliff Jump lamp / A-B-C completion:** the next Jump this Hill visit is doubled. [S3]
3. **Hill LOCK arrow:** all four regular riders are defeated and multiball is ready. [S3]
4. **TIME LOCK flashing at the center saucer:** during multiball, this is the setup shot for the 1M King captive-ball jackpot. [S3]

## Match strategy

**Playing ahead:** **Observed strategy:** light one relatively safe rider—typically Green Machine—and milk doubled Cliff Jumps while preserving access to the Hill. Avoid unnecessary rider standups, Freestyle qualification, and the more elaborate multiball path. [S4][S5] ([Kineticist][5])

**Playing behind:** finishing the four riders and attacking **1M King of the Hill + repeatable 250K Victory Laps** gives a higher-variance comeback route. If multiple high-value Cliff Jumps are already built, repeatedly doubling those may instead be the fastest catch-up. [S3][S4]

**Key recurring decision:** **Do I defeat this rider, or deliberately leave them alive to keep Banzai Hill open?** That tension between race progress and renewable Cliff Jumps defines tournament play.

## Danger zones

1. **Vertical-playfield physics:** every missed shot comes back almost immediately; hesitation is punished. [S4] ([Kineticist][5])
2. **Hill upper mini-flipper:** miss the Cliff Jump/captive ball and the nearby drain returns the ball to the main playfield. [S3]
3. **Main ramp return:** drops rapidly toward the right-flipper/slingshot area and varies substantially between machines. [S3][S4]
4. **Center saucer eject:** on some examples it kicks dangerously toward the middle. [S4]
5. **Lower rider standups:** direct target shooting is riskier than spotting progress via ramp/saucer; tournament strategy often minimizes them. [S3][S4]

## Trivia

* Banzai Run was **Pat Lawlor’s first released pinball playfield design**. [S1][S4] ([Pinside][2])
* The vertical Banzai Hill replaced the conventional backglass with a functional second playfield. [S1]
* The design evolved from a construction-themed prototype called **Wreck’n Ball**. [S1] ([Pinside][2])
* The racers’ jerseys contain names from the development team, including Krutsch, Lawlor, Boon, Sprenger and Schmidt. [S1] ([Arcade History][8])
* Banzai Run introduced an early **multiball restart / Instant Rematch** feature. [S4] ([Kineticist][5])

### Uncertainties and conflicts

* **Cycle/Cliff Jump starting value:** Bowen Kerins’ older rulesheet says **75K**, increasing by 25K to 200K [S3]; Kineticist’s updated guide says **50K**, likewise increasing by 25K [S4]. I found no primary documentation resolving the discrepancy. ([GameFAQs][7])
* **Finish Line scoring:** [S3] reports **75K + extra ball**, while [S4] reports **750K + extra ball**. The Williams manual verifies that the threshold is operator-adjustable from 20–39 laps, but the section inspected does not establish the point award. Treat the point value as **uncertain**. ([GameFAQs][7])
* Tournament ROM/settings can completely reverse the normal Cliff-Jump-first strategy; INDISC 2020 is a verified example. [S6] ([Scout's Scoop][6])
