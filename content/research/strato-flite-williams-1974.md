## Identity and versions

* **Strato-Flite** — Williams Electronics, **July 1974**, model **445**; four-player electro-mechanical game with score reels, two flippers, three DC pop bumpers, one kickout hole and two ball-return gates. Design: **Norm Clark**; art: **Christian Marche**; **5,977 produced**. [S1][S3]  ([manualzz.com][1])
* No multiball, software, locks or conventional mode system.
* Williams also made **Super-Flite** (1974), essentially the two-player version; Segasa/Sonic derivatives also exist. [S2][S3] ([Kineticist][2])
* Factory-adjustable for **3- or 5-ball play**. [S1] ([manualzz.com][1])

## Thirty-second game plan

1. **UTAD — “up top all day.”** Bank shots off the side letter targets and let the fast pops help complete A/B/C/D groups rather than attacking everything directly. **Observed strategy.** [S2] ([Silverball Rules][3])
2. Complete **any one 3-letter group** to light a star and **2× bonus**. Complete a second group for **3× bonus**. [S1] ([manualzz.com][1])
3. The second star also lights the **left-bottom inside rollover for Extra Ball**. If EBs are live, that becomes the largest immediate leverage in the game. [S1] ([manualzz.com][1])
4. With 3× established, stop forcing letter targets and keep the ball upstairs; base bonus reaches **10K**, so maximum drain bonus is **30K**. [S4][S2] ([Pinside][4])
5. Alternate payoff: use the top saucer to open a **ball-return gate**; traversing the lit gate scores 5K and returns the ball to the shooter lane for another trip upstairs. [S2]

## Core rules and persistence

* Each A/B/C/D group contains three letter targets. Completing all three lights that group's star. **One star = Double Bonus; two stars = Triple Bonus.** [S1] ([manualzz.com][1])
* Completing a third group adds **no further bonus multiplier**. Completing **all four** lights the dangerous center target for Special. For tournament scoring, strong players normally stop caring about groups three and four unless Special/EB has event value. **Observed strategy.** [S1][S2] ([manualzz.com][1])
* Base bonus runs from **1K to 10K** and counts down at end of ball, multiplied by 1×/2×/3×. [S4] ([Pinside][4])
* **Letter/star progress resets after each ball.** A player must re-earn the multiplier each ball. [S5] ([Pinside][5])
* The upper saucer award cycles among useful states as nearby slings/switches are hit: **500, 5K, open upper gate, open lower gate**. The right-side small sling also advances bonus. [S2] ([Silverball Rules][3])
* Easy commentator trap: **two stars is the strategic ceiling for multiplier.** Four stars does not mean 4×; it merely lights Special. [S1]

## Skill shots

* **Top-saucer skill shot:** plunge so the ball settles into the upper kickout hole while its best award is lit. Competitive preference is:

  1. **Open bottom gate**
  2. **5K**
  3. Open upper gate
  4. 500 points. [S2] ([Silverball Rules][3])
* The small slings beside the saucer cycle its award, so a few controlled bounces before entering can actually improve the plunge result. [S2]
* No separate super/secret skill shot found.

## Secondary features

* **Lower ball-return gate:** when opened by the upper saucer, a ball entering it scores **5K and returns to the shooter lane**, effectively creating another plunge rather than ending the ball. This is why Bob's Guide ranks “open bottom gate” above raw 5K on the skill shot. [S2] ([Silverball Rules][3])
* **Upper gate:** similar 5K/reshoot behavior, but competitive guidance says balls encounter it much less often. [S2][S6] ([Pinside][6])
* **Extra Ball:** two stars lights the left-bottom inside rollover for an EB. Factory rules limit players to **one Extra Ball per ball in play**. [S1] ([manualzz.com][1])
* **Special:** all four stars light the center target. Operator adjustment can make that award **one or two replays or one Extra Ball**. [S1] ([manualzz.com][1])
* No automatic ball save, kickback, mystery, video mode or player control button.

## What to watch

* **One star / Double Bonus:** player has completed one A-D group; the next sensible objective is usually whichever group is closest to completion.
* **Two stars / Triple Bonus:** main setup is done; expect safer UTAD play rather than more target hunting.
* **Extra Ball lamp at left-bottom inside lane:** an ordinary-looking lower rollover has become potentially match-changing.
* **Top saucer showing “Open Bottom Gate”:** ideal plunge result; the player has acquired a physical 5K/reshoot escape route.

## Match strategy

**Playing ahead:** Complete two convenient groups for 3×, then quit chasing letters. Keep the ball upstairs, exploit an open lower gate, and bank the large EOB bonus. **Observed strategy.** [S2]

**Playing behind:** Two-star progression is still the first goal, but if Extra Balls are live the lit left-bottom lane becomes worth substantial risk. An open bottom gate can also create repeated 5K/reshoot opportunities. If Specials convert to something meaningful, all-four-groups becomes a legitimate high-variance extension.

**Key recurring decision:** **After two stars, keep playing safe or chase the remaining groups?** Under ordinary tournament rules, groups three/four add no multiplier, so the center Special route is usually inferior to protecting 3× bonus.

## Danger zones

* **Center Special target:** the clearest direct SDTM rebound on the layout. [S2] ([Silverball Rules][3])
* **Pop-bumper exits:** almost every plunge/flipper shot reaches the three fast DC pops, after which control is limited. [S2]
* **Right sling / left sling / outlane sequence:** experienced owners specifically call this combination a “death knell” on fast copies. [S5] ([Pinside][7])
* **Letter-target rebounds:** the wide-open playfield stays extremely fast; direct target work can immediately send the ball sideways toward slings/outlanes. [S5]
* **Left return lane:** fast feed, but often intentionally transferable across to the right flipper; commentator should distinguish a deliberate pass from loss of control. [S2]

## Spoken commentary cues

* “One letter group complete—that's double bonus.”
* “Second star gets him triple bonus **and** lights the Extra Ball lane.”
* “He's done with the multiplier now; the third and fourth groups only lead toward Special.”
* “That top saucer opened the bottom gate, so he's got a five-thousand-point reshoot available.”
* “He's going UTAD here—up top all day—letting the pops and bank shots finish the letters.”
* “Center Special is lit, but that's a nasty direct target; there's no bonus multiplier left to gain from it.”

## Trivia

* Strato-Flite was designed by **Norm Clark**, whose Williams work also includes *Spanish Eyes*, *Gulfstream* and *Star Pool*. [S3] ([Pinside][8])
* Its backglass art by **Christian Marche** features a delta-wing military aircraft against a stylized purple/space background. [S3]
* Williams produced **5,977**, followed a few months later by **1,901 Super-Flites**, the two-player counterpart. [S3][S6] ([Pinside][8])
* The game uses **DC-powered pop bumpers**, contributing to its reputation as an exceptionally fast EM. [S5] ([Pinside][7])
* Williams' surviving project archive at The Strong includes Strato-Flite notes, sketches, wiring diagrams and bills of materials dating from **1972–1974**. [S7] ([Strong Museum of Play][9])

### Uncertainties and conflicts

* **Target/star reset:** the factory manual clearly describes the star rules but does not state the reset timing in its headline instructions. Owner/repair evidence confirms the letter-target relay bank resets **after every ball**. [S5] ([Pinside][5])
* **Bonus maximum:** the 1K–10K playfield bonus lamps are documented photographically/restoration-side [S4]; the primary manual confirms the Double/Triple Bonus circuitry but does not state “10K maximum” in its short game-operation paragraph.
* **Gate behavior is mechanical-copy dependent.** The factory hardware has separate top/bottom gate relays [S1], while competitive sources strongly prefer the bottom gate because it is encountered more frequently. [S2]
* The factory Special can be adjusted to replay or Extra Ball, so completing all four groups can range from nearly worthless to extremely important depending on tournament setup. [S1]
