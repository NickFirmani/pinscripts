## Identity and versions

* **Medieval Madness** — Williams Electronic Games, 1997; WPC-95 platform. Designer **Brian Eddy**, software **Lyman F. Sheats Jr.**, art **John Youssi / Greg Freres**, sound/music **Dan Forden**. Production is documented at 4,016 games. [S5] ([Pinball Archive][1])
* This brief targets the **original Williams game**, not Chicago Gaming's later remake editions. The remakes reproduce the core game but use different electronics/display hardware, and physical feeds should not be assumed identical. [S7]
* **Official production software verified:** v1.0, July 25, 1997. Later **1.09 / 1.09B / 1.09C** ROMs exist but were unofficial beta-derived releases. [S6] ([Matt's Basement Arcade][2])
* Relevant multiballs: **Castle Multiball**, **Multiball Madness**, and the wizard mode **Battle for the Kingdom**. Royal Madness is important but is single-ball. [S2]
* **Version-sensitive:** v1.0 documentation says Merlin-target progress resets between balls; 1.09 explicitly changed those scored targets to carry over. [S2][S6] ([Pinball Nirvana][3])

## Thirty-second game plan

1. **Observed tournament strategy:** pound the **center castle**. Castle scoring rises predictably and is one of the game's strongest low-complexity scoring engines; the first two castles make an excellent establishing score. [S2][S4] ([Skill Shot][4])
2. Start **Castle Multiball** through the narrow castle-side lock: 3 locks first time, 6 thereafter. Use the multiball either for valuable ramp jackpots or, often, as insurance while continuing to attack the castle. [S2] ([GameFAQs][5])
3. If pursuing **Multiball Madness**, qualify several Madnesses *before* shooting Merlin's Saucer. More qualified Madnesses means more balls, more Super Jackpot shots, and higher values. [S2]
4. A particularly strong setup is **Trolls! + Multiball Madness**: completing Trolls! during the multiball adds Troll Madness, restores lost balls, and refreshes ball save. [S2] ([GameFAQs][5])
5. **Alternate/high-variance route:** build toward Royal Madness and ultimately Battle for the Kingdom rather than repeatedly cashing the predictable castle value.

## Core rules and persistence

* **Castle progression — confirmed:** for castle number *N*, hit the drawbridge *N* times, then the gate *N+1* times, then enter the castle. Castle destruction is 2M × castle number for castles 1–5; the sixth is 20M. Gate values also escalate sharply. [S2] ([GameFAQs][5])
* Castle progress can be made **during multiball**, making multiball a safer time to attack later castles. [S2]
* **Castle Multiball:** virtual locks; 3 lock shots first time, 6 thereafter. Either ramp scores the 750K base Jackpot; 5 ramp jackpots light a 1.5M Super Jackpot at the lock. Completing the later Super Jackpot stage permanently raises its jackpot base for subsequent cycles. [S2] ([GameFAQs][5])
* **Multiball Madness:** one qualified Madness starts 2 balls; 2–4 start 3; all five start 4. Corresponding qualified shots become Super Jackpots; the castle lock is a **Double Super Jackpot**, up to 2M at five Madnesses. [S2]
* **Royal Madness:** 20-second shot timer, reset by making a required shot. Completing all ten required shots can award an extra ball or a points substitute depending on configuration. [S2] ([GameFAQs][5])
* Major achievement/title progress carries toward **Battle for the Kingdom**; bonus multiplier normally does not, except via a Hold Bonus X award.
* Easy commentator trap: a lit **Multiball Madness** does *not* mean the player necessarily wants to start it immediately. Starting a weak one can throw away considerable scoring leverage. [S2]

## Skill shots

* **Normal Skill Shot:** plunge through the flashing top rollover; flippers can change the selected lane. Awards 50K +10K per successive skill shot plus **+5 Bonus X**. Safer choice, and the bonus multiplier can make it more valuable than it first appears. [S2] ([Pinball Nirvana][3])
* **Super Skill Shot:** hold the **left flipper while launching**. The ball travels around to the flipper; make a flashing major shot for 100K and start a Castle Hurry-Up. [S2][S7] ([Chicago Gaming Company][6])
* **Strategic choice:** normal = safer / bonus-building; super = immediate controlled shot plus hurry-up opportunity.

## Secondary features

* **Ball save:** roughly five seconds after the opening plunge has cleared the bumpers; also provided at multiball starts and Royal Madness. Completing Trolls! during Multiball Madness can restore balls and restart protection. [S2]
* **Troll Bombs:** launcher button destroys an active troll; particularly valuable in Battle for the Kingdom, where trolls obstruct the castle. They can be earned from troll-target progress, troll combinations, or Merlin's Magic. [S2]
* **Merlin's Magic:** right saucer mystery award. Possible awards materially include locks, Trolls!, Madnesses, castle progress, points, bonus-X effects, and extra ball. Tournament Play is designed to equalize random features/global values between players. [S2][S7] ([Chicago Gaming Company][6])
* **Video Mode — Save the Children:** qualified through repeated FIRE completions and started at Merlin's Saucer. Primarily secondary tournament scoring; extra-ball availability is operator-adjustable. [S2][S7]
* **Extra balls:** available from several systems including castles, Castle Multiball and Royal Madness. Their enablement/conversion is highly tournament-sensitive and must be venue-checked. [S2][S7]

## What to watch

1. **Castle hardware:** drawbridge up → gate exposed → gate raised/open. This immediately tells you which kind of center hit the player needs.
2. **Five Madness inserts at Merlin:** the count of flashing Madnesses predicts the quality and ball count of an upcoming Multiball Madness.
3. **Raised trolls:** Trolls! or Troll Madness is active; they alter shot geometry and may signal a valuable Trolls!/multiball stack.
4. **Six blue achievement inserts / Battle for the Kingdom insert:** these show whether the player is merely scoring or approaching the wizard mode.

## Match strategy

**Playing ahead:** favor castle progress and controlled Castle Multiball. Bank predictable value instead of forcing the Catapult, trolls, or an elaborate five-Madness setup. [S4]

**Playing behind:** build several Madnesses, ideally combine Trolls! with Multiball Madness, attack Double Super Jackpots, or push Royal Madness/Battle for the Kingdom. Those routes create much larger variance.

**Key recurring decision:** **cash the castle now, or invest shots in a stronger Madness stack?** Castle points are fixed, repeatable and immediately available; Madness requires setup and careful avoidance of Merlin's Saucer but has greater multiball leverage. [S2][S4]

## Danger zones

1. **Castle gate rebound:** perhaps the signature warning situation — a hard gate rejection can head straight toward the center. [S2][S3]
2. **Direct troll hits:** expert strategy guides describe the troll standups/figures as dangerous direct targets; grazing them while shooting nearby lanes is often preferable. [S3] ([GameFAQs][7])
3. **Partial Peasant-ramp reject:** a weak climb that comes back down can become a center-drain threat. [S3] ([Digital Pinball Fans][8])
4. **Castle-lock miss:** the narrow lane's surrounding rubber/targets can produce abrupt center rebounds.
5. **Fast orbit returns:** controllable on a dialed-in game, but trying to flip them immediately rather than loop-pass/drop-catch can turn a safe feed into chaos. [S3]

## Trivia

* Medieval Madness was designed by **Brian Eddy**, who had previously designed *Attack from Mars*; the games share obvious structural similarities while having substantially different scoring priorities. [S2][S5]
* The original is a **Williams WPC-95** game with a production run documented at **4,016** units. [S5]
* The castle is not merely decorative: its **motorized drawbridge and moving portcullis** physically change the center shot as the player advances. [S1]
* Credited voices include future *Saturday Night Live / 30 Rock* performers **Tina Fey and Scott Adsit**. [S5] ([Pinball Archive][1])
* Chicago Gaming later recreated the title as Medieval Madness Remake, beginning a series of licensed Williams/Bally reproductions. [S7]
