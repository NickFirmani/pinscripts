## Identity and versions

* **Sinbad — solid-state version** — D. Gottlieb & Co., February 1978, model 412, **System 1**, four players, four in-line flippers, 10 drop targets, center swing/spinner target. Design: **Ed Krynski**; art: **Gordon Morison**. Gottlieb produced **12,000 SS games**. [S1][S2]  ([Pinside][1])
* **Important ambiguity:** Gottlieb also produced about **950 four-player electro-mechanical Sinbads** in 1978. They look similar but have **substantially different bonus rules**. The brief below covers the much more common **solid-state System 1 Sinbad**. [S2][S4] ([Pinside][2])
* No multiball. No numbered stock ROM revision affecting strategy was verified.
* SS headline rule: base bonus up to **15K**, multiplied as high as **5× = 75K**; completing all ten drops immediately collects that bonus and resets the targets. [S1][S3] ([GameFAQs][3])

## Thirty-second game plan

1. **Attack the drop colors in progression order:** white → yellow → purple → red. White establishes **2×**, white+yellow **3×**, +purple **4×**, and all four groups **5×**. **Observed strategy.** [S3] ([Silverball Rules][4])
2. Simultaneously build the **15K base bonus**, principally from the center spinner and lit upper-left star rollovers. [S3]
3. Finishing all ten drops **immediately cashes the accumulated bonus** at its current multiplier, then resets the drop/bonus cycle. A full collect is **75K**, enormous on this scoring scale. [S1][S3]
4. After the multiplier is established, favor **UTAD (“up top all day”)** through the left orbit / center spinner rather than continuing unnecessary dangerous drop attacks. **Observed strategy.** [S3]
5. If Extra Balls are live, purple becomes an alternate priority: complete purple, then send the ball through the now-lit purple top lane for the Extra Ball. [S3]

## Core rules and persistence

* The multiplier hierarchy is **conditional, not simply “number of drops hit.”** The white target must be down before 2× exists; white + both yellow = 3×; add all purple = 4×; add all red = 5×. Nine targets with **white still standing = only 1×**. [S3] ([Silverball Rules][4])
* Each completed color also upgrades its matching top rollover:

  * white: **100 → 1K**
  * yellow: **500 → 5K**
  * purple: **1K → Extra Ball**
  * red: **5K → Special**. [S3]
* The center swing/spinner advances base bonus **1K per five spins**. Lit green star rollovers on the upper-left also advance bonus 1K. [S3]
* Base bonus tops at **15K**. Completing all ten drops scores the accumulated bonus immediately at the multiplier, then resets base bonus and multiplier so the player can build another cycle **on the same ball**. [S1][S3] ([GameFAQs][3])
* End-of-ball also scores the current bonus. Thus a long ball can contain one or more completed-drop cash-outs **plus** another partial/full bonus on drain.
* **Easy commentator trap:** physical hit order does not have to be white-yellow-purple-red. The game evaluates which complete color sets exist. The strategic reason to shoot them in order is insurance against draining before all ten are down. [S3]
* On **3-ball versus 5-ball settings**, individual target/spinner values and some reset/Special behavior change. The multiplier structure remains strategically recognizable, but venue configuration should be checked. [S5] ([Pinball Nirvana][5])

## Skill shots

* **Leftmost top lane:** plunge for the **5K lane**. Bob's competitive guide explicitly identifies this as the preferred skill shot. [S3] ([Silverball Rules][4])
* More important than the 5K itself: avoid missing the upper lanes and falling down the far-left side, whose feed can become an immediate right-outlane threat. [S3]
* No alternate/super/secret skill shot found.

## Secondary features

* **Extra Ball:** complete the purple drop group, then collect the lit purple top rollover. If EBs are enabled, this can justify attacking purple earlier than pure multiplier order would suggest. [S3]
* **Special:** complete the red group to light its top rollover for Special/replay. Normally irrelevant in tournament play unless converted to points. [S1][S3]
* No ball saver, kickback, video mode, mystery award or player-controlled utility was verified.

## What to watch

* **2×/3×/4×/5× bonus lamps:** instantly tells you how much the current bonus cash-out is worth.
* **One color bank incomplete:** particularly the single white target. A nearly cleared playfield can still mean **1× bonus** if white is standing.
* **15K bonus lit:** now a full drop completion means a **75K immediate collect**.
* **Purple top lane lit:** Extra Ball is available; if tournament EBs are live, the player's priorities may change immediately.

## Match strategy

**Playing ahead:** Get a useful multiplier, then reduce variance by sending the ball **up top through the left orbit/spinner** and building bonus rather than repeatedly attacking raw drop-target rebounds. **Observed strategy.** [S3]

**Playing behind:** Pursue the full **15K × 5 = 75K** completion. Because the completion cashes immediately and resets the cycle, a long ball can repeat this payoff multiple times. If EBs are live, purple → top-lane Extra Ball adds another high-variance path.

**Key recurring decision:** **Finish another dangerous multiplier color, or stop attacking drops and build the base bonus safely?** A 5× multiplier means little with a tiny bonus; a large bonus at 2×–4× may already be worth protecting.

## Danger zones

* **Far-left plunge/upper-lane miss:** ball can rebound from the lower post across toward the **right outlane**, producing a near-house-ball. [S3] ([Silverball Rules][4])
* **Center-spinner descent:** a ball falling straight through the **left side of the spinner gap** is a documented center-drain feed. [S3]
* **White/yellow drop rebounds:** specifically called out by competitive references as dangerous early targets. [S3]
* **In-line four-flipper arrangement:** a ball can slip between the upper and lower flippers if the player reflexively flips both rather than letting it fall to the lower bat. [S4] ([Scribd][6])
* **Drop-target greed:** multiplier progress is valuable enough that players repeatedly expose the ball to direct rebounds; many drains occur one target short of the next multiplier.

## Spoken commentary cues

* “White and yellow are complete, so that's triple bonus now.”
* “He's at fifteen thousand base with five-times lit—that's a seventy-five-thousand collect if he finishes the drops.”
* “All ten down: the game cashes the bonus **right now**, resets the targets, and he can build another one.”
* “Multiplier is established, so now he's going UTAD—up top all day—to build the base bonus more safely.”
* “Purple is complete, so that top rollover is now an Extra Ball if the tournament allows it.”

## Trivia

* Sinbad was one of Gottlieb's earliest **System 1 solid-state** titles and the company's second production SS game after *Cleopatra*. [S2] ([Pinside][7])
* It was a direct promotional tie-in to Columbia Pictures' **1977 *Sinbad and the Eye of the Tiger***; Columbia had recently acquired Gottlieb. [S1][S4] ([GameFAQs][3])
* Gottlieb made three closely related versions: **12,000 SS Sinbads**, roughly **950 four-player EM Sinbads**, and a rarer two-player EM **Eye of the Tiger**. [S2][S4]
* The rotating center target depicts swords in opposing orientations, creating a **crossed-swords effect while it spins**. [S2] ([PinWiki][8])
* *Sinbad* ranked **second among pinball earners** in Play Meter's 1978 operator survey, behind Bally's *Eight Ball*. [S6] ([Museum of the Game][9])

### Uncertainties and conflicts

* **SS versus EM is critical:** both are named *Sinbad* and were produced in 1978. The SS game has progressive **2×–5× bonus and immediate bonus collection/reset on all ten drops**; the EM version only reaches **2×** and does not use the same immediate-reset bonus structure. [S3] ([Silverball Rules][4])
* **3-ball versus 5-ball:** secondary transcriptions of the factory rules report different raw target/spinner values and different 5× reset/Special behavior. [S5] This should be verified on the actual venue game before those fine details make the final page.
* Pinside currently labels the latest software revision **unknown**; no authoritative numbered original ROM revision was found. [S2]
* Physical spinner and left-side feeds are highly setup-dependent even though both hazards are repeatedly documented by competitive sources. [S3]
