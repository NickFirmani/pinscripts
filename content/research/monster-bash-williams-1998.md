## Identity and versions

**Monster Bash** — Williams Electronic Games, **1998**, model **50065**, WPC-95/DMD. Design: **George Gomez**; software: **Lyman F. Sheats Jr.**; art: **Kevin O'Connor**; mechanics: **Chris Shipman**; music/sound: **Vince Pontarelli**. Production: **3,361**. [S1][S2]

Latest official Williams ROM verified: **v1.0, August 16, 1998**. Williams' archived technical-support page still identifies 1.0 as current. Unofficial/home-use 1.06/1.06B ROMs exist, so tournament machines should be checked. [S1][S6]

Do not confuse this with **Chicago Gaming Company's 2018 Monster Bash Remake**, which is a separate machine generation despite closely reproducing the original. [S2]

Major multiballs: **Frankenstein Multiball, Mosh Pit Multiball, Monster Bash**, and **Monsters of Rock**. [S3][S4]

## Thirty-second game plan

1. **Start monsters; don't obsess over finishing them one at a time.** Six started monster features light **Monster Bash**, a powerful four-ball wizard multiball. [S3][S4]
2. Before starting **Frankenstein Multiball**, bring in two or more timed monster modes. **All active mode timers stop during multiball**, making mode completion dramatically easier. **Observed tournament strategy.** [S3][S4]
3. Completing a monster earns its **instrument**. All six instruments light **Monsters of Rock**, substantially more valuable than Monster Bash and worth at least **55M just to start** on the first trip. [S3]
4. The central setup-versus-cash-out decision is **start Frank now or keep stacking modes?** More setup raises scoring enormously but risks draining or timing out before multiball.
5. Alternate route: if the dangerous center spinner is dialed in, six shots light **Mosh Pit Multiball**, a strong standalone scoring feature. [S4][S5]

## Core rules and persistence

The six monster features are **Creature Feature, Full Moon Fever, Ball & Chain, Frankenstein, Mummy Mayhem, and Drac Attack**. Their qualification progress is player-specific and persists through balls. Starting the feature counts as collecting that monster; **completing** its objective earns its instrument. [S3][S4]

The signature rule is that **timed monster modes freeze while any multiball is active**, but their shots remain live. You also cannot meaningfully build new monster qualifications during multiball, so preparation happens beforehand. [S3][S4]

**Frankenstein Multiball:** collect Frankenstein's body parts, then shoot the exposed center ramp for three-ball play. Major shots score jackpots; hitting Frank scores a Super, upgraded to Double/Triple Super after making other jackpots first. Enough Super-Jackpot progress awards his keyboard. [S3][S7]

**Monster Bash:** starting all six monsters lights the scoop for four-ball multiball. Each monster shot scores **2M** initially; the center spinner collects a growing Bash Jackpot. [S3]

**Monsters of Rock:** six instruments light the scoop. Start bonus is **5M per instrument collected +25M per complete set**, minimum **55M** on the first visit. The four-ball mode then lights each monster for 4M and awards escalating Monsters of Rock jackpots. [S3]

A famous advanced route is the **Big Six**: get all six monster modes running simultaneously, worth a **15M bonus**, with Frankenstein last so his multiball freezes all five timed modes. Competitive PAPA strategy specifically recommends this when achievable. [S5]

## Skill shots

**Top-lane Skill Shot**

* Use flipper lane-change to put the flashing light under the plunging ball in the **D-I-G** lanes.
* Awards escalating points and a **Monster Bomb** for one specific monster.
* Monster Bombs are spent with the launch button during that monster's active mode to spot one required hit/shot.
* This makes the skill shot strategically useful even when its raw points are small. [S3][S4]

No separate verified Super Skill Shot or secret plunge shot was found.

## Secondary features

**Monster Bombs:** six themed bombs—one per monster—spot progress during the associated active mode. They come from the skill shot, periodic spinner progress and scoop awards. They are especially useful for dangerous or time-sensitive final mode shots. [S3][S4]

**Phantom Flip:** blue standup hits qualify an automatic computer-controlled flip from a lit return lane. The game chooses and attempts a shot itself. Competitive sources regard it as unreliable, and tournament setups often disable it. Phantom Flips can stack and carry between balls on normal software. [S3][S4][S7]

**Extra Ball:** lit from monster progression, **12 Mosh Pit shots**, or a scoop award; collected at the scoop. Williams' archived page confirms the Mosh-Pit-based qualification and shows this as operator-adjustable. Tournament treatment must be checked. [S3][S7]

**Ball save:** the original rulesheet states the **factory beginning-of-ball save is OFF**. Multiballs themselves have substantial dedicated ball-save windows. [S3]

## What to watch

* **Monster portraits lit vs. instruments lit:** portrait = feature started; instrument = feature completed. Six portraits means Monster Bash; six instruments means Monsters of Rock. [S3][S4]
* **Exploding-clock animation during multiball:** confirms mode timers are frozen; this is why the player built the stack first. [S4]
* **Frankenstein target bank lowered:** his ramp is exposed and multiball is ready; delaying the shot usually means the player wants more modes running.
* **Scoop flashing Bash / Monsters of Rock:** a major scoring transition is ready; Monsters of Rock supersedes the ordinary Bash opportunity. [S3]

## Match strategy

**Playing ahead:** take a practical two- or three-feature stack into Frankenstein rather than gambling the ball on the perfect six-mode setup. Creature + Full Moon Fever are especially attractive because their shots overlap broad multiball shooting. Once Monster Bash is lit, taking it is generally safer than continued setup. **Observed strategy / strategic inference.** [S4]

**Playing behind:** push for a larger stack, the **Big Six**, or a long Monsters of Rock. Ball & Chain becomes especially explosive after completion because its remaining ramp/spinner scoring is tripled while its timer is frozen during multiball. A dialed center spinner also makes Mosh Pit a viable high-variance comeback route. [S3][S5]

**Key recurring decision:** **How much more do I build before starting Frankenstein?** Every additional mode adds overlapping scoring and instrument progress, but the multiball shot is already available and one bad setup shot can waste everything.

## Danger zones

* **Mosh Pit spinner:** aligned almost perfectly with the center drain; competitive guidance explicitly warns that a miss can be fatal. [S4]
* **Weak left-ramp shot:** rolls back, clips a Phantom Flip target and often reaches the right slingshot. [S3]
* **Orbit returns:** standard-play orbits feed the bumpers; exits toward the slings/outlanes can become uncontrolled quickly. [S4]
* **Frankenstein bank:** repeated center standup hits are necessary but expose the player to hard rebounds.
* **Phantom Flip:** if enabled, an automatic missed shot can remove player control and directly produce an outlane drain. [S4]

## Trivia

* Monster Bash was one of Williams' final conventional WPC games; **Cactus Canyon** followed before the company moved to Pinball 2000. [S2][S3]
* The six Universal Monsters form a rock band: Creature on sax, Wolf Man on drums, Frankenstein on keyboard, Bride on vocals, Dracula on guitar and Mummy on bass. [S3][S4]
* **Phantom Flip** was an evolution of *The Addams Family's* Thing Flips, using learned shot timing to flip automatically. [S7]
* The backglass includes hidden appearances of **Elvira and Wonder Woman**, and the software contains numerous Williams cow jokes. [S3]
* The easter egg **Lyman's Lament** replaces normal music/callouts with programmer Lyman Sheats complaining about the game; the cheat-code route is inappropriate for tournament play. [S3][S4]

### Uncertainties and conflicts

* **Official ROM:** Williams' archived support page identifies **v1.0** as the final/current factory ROM [S1]. Later **1.06/1.06B** ROMs circulate for home/free-play use [S6] but are not the official production ruleset.
* **Monster Bash wording:** Williams' short promotional rules page says “complete all six Monster Features” to reach Monster Bash [S7], while the detailed contemporary production rulesheet and actual tournament documentation distinguish **starting six → Monster Bash** from **completing six → instruments / Monsters of Rock** [S3][S4]. Use the latter.
* **Extra Ball qualification:** Williams' dedicated Mosh Pit page states **6 spinner-lane shots light Mosh Pit, 12 light Extra Ball** [S7]. Another archived Williams Extra Ball page loosely says EB after enough spinner shots with “factory set at six.” The detailed production rulesheet agrees with **12 Mosh Pit shots**. [S3][S7]
* **Frankenstein parts:** archived Williams pages say six body parts, while some later community summaries count seven visible/progression states. For commentary, avoid stating a part count unless the venue's installed ROM/playfield behavior is confirmed.
* Physical feeds—and especially Phantom Flip accuracy—are strongly machine-specific. [S4]
