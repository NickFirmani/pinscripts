## Identity and versions

* **Meteor** — Stern Electronics, **September 1979**, model **113**, Stern **M-200 MPU** solid-state. Design by **Steve Kirk**, software by **Alan McNeil**, art by **Leslie Kaip and Jorge Obregon**. [S1][S4]
* Production is usually listed as **7,939 complete games**; another archival total of **8,362** includes 423 knock-down/export games for Australia. [S4]
* Three flippers, one spinner, **15 drop targets**, including the unusual six-bank **memory** targets spelling M-E-T-E-O-R. No multiball. [S1][S4]
* Two original-code revisions matter: the first U1 ROM has a documented bonus-countdown bug; the later **U1a factory bug-fix ROM** corrects it. [S5]
* Modern replacement code such as *Meteor 2021/Strike* materially changes the rules; this brief covers **original Stern rules**.

## Thirty-second game plan

1. **Observed strategy:** knock down **M and R plus three of the four middle E-T-E-O targets**, leaving one middle target standing. That creates the best sustainable spinner state: **2,000 points per spin** without completing/resetting METEOR. [S1][S2]
2. Then **rip the spinner from the left flipper**. Meteor is fundamentally a spinner game; strong tournament guidance largely ignores the lower 1 and 3 banks unless collected incidentally. [S2]
3. Completing all six METEOR targets is still good: the bank resets, spinner drops back to 200/spin, but Bonus X advances one step toward **7×** and the six targets themselves score 20K total. [S1][S2]
4. Build the three **missile/1-2-3 banks** for up to **21K base bonus**; at 7× that is **147K end-of-ball bonus**. [S1][S2]
5. Alternate route when the spinner feed is poor: deliberately work the numbered banks and Bonus X rather than gambling on repeated around-the-world spinner returns. **Strategic inference.**

## Core rules and persistence

* The spinner starts at **200**. Each middle METEOR target down adds **200/spin**; each end target **M and R adds 600/spin**. Completing all six immediately resets the bank and spinner value. [S1]
* Therefore the practical farm state is **M + R + three middle letters = 2,000/spin**. Contemporary Stern publicity advertised a theoretical 2,200 maximum, but the completed bank resets immediately. [S1][S2]
* Every METEOR completion advances Bonus X: **1× → 2× → 3× → 4× → 5× → 6× → 7×**. [S1]
* **Primary manual persistence:** multipliers through **6× carry ball-to-ball**; after reaching 7× and draining, the next ball starts at **3×**. This conflicts with Bob’s Guide, which says 6×/7× reset to 1×; use the Stern manual as authoritative. [S1][S2]
* The six METEOR targets have **player-specific memory** and restore their down/up state on that player's next ball. The three numbered missile banks reset each ball. [S1]
* Each numbered target advances its corresponding missile row and adds 1K to bonus, up to **7K per row / 21K total**. End-of-ball bonus therefore tops at 147K with 7×. [S1][S2]
* Lit star rollovers, return lanes, and the center standup can **spot METEOR targets**, so a player can accidentally complete and reset an ideal spinner setup. [S1]
* Tilting forfeits bonus entirely. [S1]

## Skill shots

**None found on original code.**

The plunge can interact with the upper star-rollover/METEOR area, but neither the Stern manual nor contemporary rules defines a distinct plunge award. Do not confuse this with modern *Meteor 2021* code, which explicitly adds skill shots. [S1][S5]

## Secondary features

* **Ball save:** none on stock code.
* **WOW / Extra Ball:** whenever all three missile rows show the same value, a rotating **WOW** can light; operator settings determine whether this begins at 1K–6K or includes 7K. [S1]
* Deep METEOR progression can also light a moving WOW on the six-bank at either **6× or 7×**, operator selectable. [S1]
* Extra Balls can be globally disabled. Special/WOW awards are highly configurable: original hardware supports Extra Ball, replay, no award, and large point-award configurations. This can materially alter tournament scoring. [S1]
* Slingshots can either **move the lit WOW/Special/spot lamps** or leave them stationary, another strategically meaningful adjustment. [S1]
* No player-controlled lane change, video mode, mystery, Magna-Save, or similar feature.

## What to watch

1. **Which METEOR targets are down:** M and R plus three middles means the player has the ideal **2K spinner** setup.
2. **Bonus-X lamps:** 5× or 6× is valuable persistent equity; completing METEOR again may push toward 7×.
3. **Missile rows:** three rows approaching 7K means both a huge bonus and potential WOW/Special opportunity.
4. **Moving lit spot/WOW lamp:** sling hits may move the desired award depending on the venue adjustment. [S1]

## Match strategy

**Playing ahead:** establish a strong spinner value and keep shooting the repeatable spinner rather than forcing lower drop targets. Preserve a 4×–6× multiplier if possible, especially because stock code carries it to the next ball. **Observed strategy.** [S2]

**Playing behind:** repeatedly complete METEOR for Bonus X, rebuild the spinner, and aggressively pursue 2K spinner rips. A developed 21K bonus at 7× contributes another **147K**, large enough to swing a close classic-game match.

**Key recurring decision:** **leave one METEOR target standing to preserve spinner value, or finish the bank for another Bonus-X step?** Spinner farming maximizes immediate points; completing the bank sacrifices the current spinner setup for multiplier and later-ball equity.

## Danger zones

1. **Around-the-world spinner return:** the primary documented danger; a strong spinner shot can return into either drain path. [S2]
2. **Threading the upper-right gap:** misses can strike the bumper/post under the upper flipper and rebound downward toward center. [S2]
3. **Aggressive shatz/inlane transfer:** too hard and the ball can travel up the inlane and directly down the outlane. [S2]
4. **Numbered drop banks:** useful for bonus but carry more direct rebound risk than spinner play. [S2]
5. **Center “Kirk Post”:** its presence, rubber, or removal substantially changes center-drain saves and transfer techniques. [S2][S5]

## Trivia

* Meteor is licensed from the **1979 disaster film** starring Sean Connery and Natalie Wood, although the actors do not appear on the machine artwork. [S3][S4]
* The backglass/flyer image derives from a film advertising painting by noted space artist **Robert McCall**. [S4]
* Meteor was the **first Stern game to use the SB-300 sound board**, adding continuous background sound whose pitch rises during play. [S4]
* Steve Kirk was heavily involved in organized competitive pinball; some Meteors even carried **“selected for tournament play”** apron stickers. [S4]
* The small post between the flippers was internally nicknamed the **“Kirk Post”** after designer Steve Kirk. [S4]

### Uncertainties and conflicts

* **Bonus-X persistence conflict:** Stern's original manual says **1×–6× carry**, while draining after reaching 7× starts the next ball at **3×** [S1]. Bob’s Guide says 6×/7× reset to 1× [S2]. Use the manufacturer rule unless the venue copy demonstrably behaves differently.
* **ROM revision matters:** original U1 has a race-condition bug that can produce an apparent **255× bonus countdown** when several events coincide around the drain. U1a fixes it. [S5]
* **Production:** 7,939 is the confirmed conventional production run [S4]; 8,362 includes 423 knock-down/export units. Do not present these as contradictory.
* Extra Ball/WOW/Special and spot-lamp movement are operator-adjustable and can materially alter the value of deep progression.
* Spinner-return danger and the usefulness of the Kirk Post are highly copy-dependent.
