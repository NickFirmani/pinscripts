## Identity and versions

* **Attack From Mars (Remake)** — Chicago Gaming Company, 2017; a licensed recreation of Bally/Williams’ 1995 game. Original design: **Brian Eddy**; software: **Lyman F. Sheats Jr.**; art: **Doug Watson**. [S1][S8] ([Chicago Gaming Company][1])
* Editions: **Classic (CE), Special (SE), Limited (LE)**. Verified differences are display, audio, lighting, topper, trim, shaker, etc.; **no gameplay-rule difference was found**. [S1]
* **Rules basis: `code`**. Latest downloadable Chicago Gaming software is **v1.0.0**. CGC’s page is undated; the earliest verifiable public availability I found is **2017-09-05**, when the AFMr owners thread added the firmware link. [S2][S3] ([Chicago Gaming Company][2])
* Do not confuse CGC **v1.0.0** with Williams AFM ROM numbering. Community investigation indicates the stock remake runs legacy 1.1-era gameplay internally and does **not** support the later 1.13 tournament ROM. [S7] ([tiltforums.com][3])
* Major multiballs: regular **Multiball** (3-ball), **Martian Attack Multiball** (2-ball), **Total Annihilation** (4-ball); **Strobe Multiball** is a Stroke of Luck award. **Rule the Universe** is the 5-ball wizard mode. [S4]

Research scope follows the supplied commentary brief.

## Thirty-second game plan

1. **Observed strategy:** A common tournament route is **Total Annihilation (TA)**: complete the two ramps and two loops three times each. It is substantially safer and more repeatable than pounding the center saucer. [S5][S6] ([Kineticist][4])
2. **Confirmed rule:** A **Super Skill Shot** can instantly complete all three TA inserts for one ramp/loop. [S4]
3. Each naturally completed third TA shot starts a **100M hurry-up** at center. Complete another before it expires and it becomes **300M**, then **500M**; completing all four awards **1B automatically and starts TA**. [S4]
4. In TA, repeatedly shoot the four majors for jackpots and the lock ramp for the accumulating **Annihilation Jackpot**. [S4]
5. **Viable alternate:** start an **Attack Wave before starting multiball**, then use the extra balls to safely bash the lucrative but dangerous center saucer. [S5]

The core strategic tension is therefore **safe major-shot progress versus high-value center risk**.

## Core rules and persistence

* Four TA shots are **Capture** (left loop), **Big-O-Beam** (left ramp), **Tractor Beam** (right ramp), and **Atomic Blaster** (right loop). Three shots to each starts TA. Progress resets after TA, not at end of ball. [S4]
* **Important trap:** Super Skill Shot completing a TA lane **does not start its 100M hurry-up**. [S4] ([Pinball.org][5])
* Attack Waves require lowering the 3-bank forcefield, damaging the saucer, then shooting its hole. Five Earth waves lead to **Conquer Mars**: ten 100M hole shots, with the last worth **2.5B**. [S4] ([Pinball.org][5])
* An already-running Attack Wave can be damaged during multiball, but a **new** wave cannot normally be started until multiball ends. [S4]
* Regular locks are **virtual**: the ball is immediately returned, so opponents cannot steal locked balls. Five distinct jackpots light a roaming Super Jackpot beginning at **250M**. [S4]
* Martian Attack lasts **30 seconds**. Martian Attack Multiball can stack with another multiball when started in the proper order; regular MB, TA, and Strobe are otherwise mutually exclusive. [S4]
* End-of-ball bonus is real leverage: Bonus X reaches **250×**, and a developed bonus can reach **billions**. [S4] ([Pinball.org][5])
* Rule the Universe requires Super Jackpot, TA, Martian MB, Conquer Mars, Super Jets, and a 5-way combo. Scoring 5B during it awards **another 5B**, then opens high-value Victory Laps. [S4]

## Skill shots

* **Normal Skill Shot — confirmed rule:** Launch without holding left flipper; steer the flashing upper Bonus X lane with the flippers. Awards **10M + 5× Bonus**, with the point award increasing 10M on successive normal skill shots. It preserves the opening ball-save longer than the Super. [S4]
* **Super Skill Shot — confirmed rule:** Hold **left flipper while pressing Launch**. The ball bypasses the top lanes to the flipper. A ramp/loop scores **50M and spots all three TA inserts** for that shot; a forcefield hit scores 50M and lowers the shield. Fixed at 50M. Completing it does **not** give the corresponding TA hurry-up and effectively sacrifices the longer opening safety window. [S4] ([Pinball.org][5])
* **Observed choice:** experts commonly use the Super on the forcefield when it is up, or on a TA major when committing to TA. [S5]
* No separate additional secret/alternate skill shot was verified.

## Secondary features

* **Ball save:** brief opening save. A Super Skill Shot—or a failed Super attempt into a non-awarding shot—ends it early. Strobe has its own substantial start/end protection. [S4]
* **Martian Bombs:** when available, the Launch button flashes. During Martian Attack, one press kills one remaining Martian. Bombs stack and carry between attacks; using the last needed bomb even after an outlane drain can trigger Martian Multiball before the ball reaches the trough. [S4] ([Pinball.org][5])
* **Stroke of Luck (SOL):** normally a pseudo-random scoop award including locks, multiball, Video Mode, Strobe, etc. **On stock-remake tournament mode it awards 50M every time.** The later Williams 1.13 deterministic tournament logic is not stock-AFMr behavior. [S4][S7]
* **Video Mode:** SOL-only; flippers aim and Launch gives one Smart Bomb. Full completion is **635M**, thus tournament-relevant when enabled—but stock-remake tournament mode's 50M SOL removes the route. [S4]
* **Extra balls:** multiple qualifications exist, including the second Attack Wave and rollover progress. Tournament mode itself does **not** disable them. No stock AFMr point-conversion value was verified; conversion/disable handling is competition policy. [S4][S7] ([Tiltforums][3])
* Flippers also select the flashing skill lane, lit inlane/outlane lamps, and the next country before an Attack Wave. [S4]

## What to watch

* **TA insert ladders:** a third lamp on a ramp/orbit means a center hurry-up has started; several nearly-complete ladders telegraph a 300M/500M/1B stack. [S4]
* **Forcefield drops:** the saucer is exposed; expect aggressive center bashing, especially if a multiball is about to start. [S4][S5]
* **Five regular-MB jackpots completed:** the Super Jackpot begins visibly roaming among the five major shots, with a distinctive audio “blip.” [S4]
* **Six blue lamps above the flippers:** all Rule-the-Universe prerequisites are complete; the scoop becomes the massive wizard-mode start. [S4]

## Match strategy

**Playing ahead:**
**Strategic inference:** lean toward ramps/orbits, controlled returns, TA progress, and automatic TA hurry-up cash-outs. Avoid repeated naked center bashes unless the value is already compelling.

**Playing behind:**
Accept center variance: stack TA hurry-ups toward **500M/1B**, attack an exposed saucer during multiball, or pursue deep saucer/Rule-the-Universe progress. A mature Bonus X also means a drain can itself produce a major score swing. [S4]

**Key recurring decision:**
**Cash a live hurry-up at the dangerous center now, or complete another TA lane to multiply it and potentially auto-cash it at TA?** This setup-versus-cash-out choice frequently explains apparently different shot choices.

## Danger zones

* **Forcefield:** repeated straight-up-the-middle rebounds are the game's signature drain threat. [S5]
* **T / I Martians:** dangerous direct standup hits; experienced players may carom into them from the outer Martians. [S5]
* **Lock ramp:** narrow and steep; underpowered shots reject back toward the flippers. [S4]
* **Right ramp:** explicitly the smaller/tighter of the two ramps. [S4]
* **Left inlane:** balls can roll *up* past its switch and migrate into the left outlane. [S4] ([Pinball.org][5])

## Trivia

* AFMr was Chicago Gaming/Planetary Pinball's **second Bally/Williams remake** and was sold as Classic, Special and Limited editions. [S1]
* Doug Watson, original AFM artist, also sculpted/designed the remake LE's animated Martian topper. [S1]
* The remake replaces the WPC hardware with modern electronics while deliberately recreating the original game's feel and rules presentation. [S1]
* The same saucer-shooting Video Mode later appears in **Junk Yard**. [S4]
* AFM contains a cow-themed Video Mode Easter egg triggered through ramp/callout interactions; it changes the targets to larger cows without special scoring. [S4]
