## Identity and versions

**White Water** — Williams Electronic Games, **January 1993**, model **50018**, **WPC Fliptronics II / DMD**. Design **Dennis Nordman**; software **Mike Boon**; art **John Youssi**; mechanics **Win Schilling**; music/sound **Chris Granner**. Production: **7,008**. [S1]

Latest official ROM verified: **L-5, May 20, 1993**. Its changes from L-4 were service/bug fixes rather than a rules rewrite; L-4 and earlier revisions do have some strategically relevant differences around multiball/Whirlpool behavior. [S2]

Major multiballs: **White Water Multiball** (3-ball), **Whirlpool Challenge** (2-ball), and **Gold Rush** (2-ball). [S3][S4]

## Thirty-second game plan

1. **Prioritize White Water Multiball:** light locks at the green targets, then lock three balls at **No Way Out**. [S3][S4]
2. During the three-ball launch, hit **Insanity Falls with two or preferably all three balls**; that establishes Double or Triple Jackpot for the rest of the multiball. [S3]
3. The huge tournament setup is **5X Playfield → Multiball**. Collect the 25-second 5X Whirlpool Award with two balls already locked, then immediately start MB. **Observed strategy.** [S4]
4. Keep advancing rafts/hazards naturally; deeper raft progress raises the base multiball jackpot and eventually reaches **Wet Willie's**, itself a major score. [S3]
5. **Do not chase Vacation Jackpot as the default tournament strategy.** Its 200M base is usually inferior to repeated 5X jackpots and requires awkwardly delaying multiball. [S4]

## Core rules and persistence

**White Water Multiball:** hit both green targets around No Way Out to light a lock, then lock three balls there. Later multiballs become harder to qualify. At MB start all three balls are fed to the upper-right flipper; making Insanity Falls with two opening balls establishes Double Jackpot, all three establishes Triple Jackpot. Base jackpot is **10M + 2M per raft advance**. [S3]

**5X Playfield:** the sixth Whirlpool Award gives **25 seconds of 5X almost all scoring**. On normal later rules, MB's ordinary Single/Double/Triple Jackpot multiplier does **not multiply again by 5X**: jackpots become 5X during the timer, then revert to the previously established tier. Older ROM behavior can differ. [S3][S4]

**Hazards/Rafts:** flashing orange Hazard shots advance the raft once the current set is completed. Hazard value is **500K × River Class**. Raft progress increases MB jackpot value and eventually reaches Wet Willie's. [S3]

**River Class:** spell **R-I-V-E-R** via the left targets or lit inlane to advance toward Class VI. River Class multiplies Hazard value and end-of-ball bonus but **resets to Class I each ball**; partial R-I-V-E-R letters persist. [S3][S4]

**Wet Willie's:** after reaching the end of the raft progression, hit all seven Hazards for 10M each, then shoot one rapidly travelling Hazard for **100M**. Stage 1 survives a drain; the travelling-shot stage does not. [S3][S4]

## Skill shots

**Insanity Falls Skill Shot**

* Gentle plunge onto the upper playfield, then make **Insanity Falls** with the upper flipper.
* Scores **1M**, increasing by 1M per successful skill shot to **5M**.
* A plunge that is too hard crosses into Spine Chiller instead.
* It is often still strategically preferable to choose **Bigfoot Bluff** rather than the Skill Shot if a valuable Whirlpool Award is already available. [S3][S4]

No separate verified Super or Secret Skill Shot.

## Secondary features

**Bounceback:** left-outlane kickback. Complete the three red center targets to relight it; completing them while already lit instead spots a Hazard. Regular White Water MB also relights it. [S3][S4]

**Whirlpool Awards:** Insanity Falls lights Bigfoot Bluff to collect the currently flashing award; pop hits move that selection. The six awards are **Whirlpool Challenge, Man Overboard, Light Extra Ball, Advance Raft, Mystery Canyon, 5X Playfield**. [S3]

**Mystery Canyon:** normally random and can be enormously useful—Class VI or multiple raft advances—but in documented **Tournament Mode it always awards 5M**. [S3]

**Extra Ball:** can be lit from the Whirlpool ladder and ramp progression; memory and thresholds are operator-adjustable. The factory manual allows Extra Ball percentage to be disabled. [S5]

**No conventional ball save:** neither ordinary single-ball play nor main multiball has a standard timed saver. [S4]

## What to watch

* **5X is the flashing Whirlpool Award:** expect the player to stop progressing awards and prepare two locks before collecting it.
* **Two balls locked + third Lock lit:** ideal 5X→Multiball staging state.
* **Opening MB volley:** count how many balls make Insanity Falls—one = Single, two = Double, three = Triple Jackpot afterward. [S3]
* **River Class lamps:** high Class means Hazards and end-of-ball bonus are temporarily much more valuable; it disappears at drain. [S3]

## Match strategy

**Playing ahead:** take the first Multiball promptly, establish whatever Jackpot tier is safely available, and avoid forcing the dangerous green lock targets solely to perfect a later 5X stack. Keep Bounceback lit and take easy Hazards. **Strategic inference.**

**Playing behind:** set up **two locks + 5X next at the Whirlpool**, start 5X, immediately lock ball three, and attack Insanity Falls repeatedly. Even though later code caps the simultaneous multiplier at 5X rather than 15X, this remains the game's defining comeback window. **Observed strategy.** [S4]

**Key recurring decision:** **take Multiball now, or hold it for 5X?** The first MB is comparatively easy; every extra setup shot risks wasting it, but a successful 5X stack can overwhelm ordinary scoring.

## Danger zones

* **Lite Lock targets:** mandatory but dangerous direct standups; strong players prefer grazing them en route to No Way Out. [S4]
* **Lost Mine/Whirlpool kickout:** machine-specific; can feed the right flipper cleanly or “moonwalk” up the inlane into the outlane. [S4]
* **Boomerang Bend:** notoriously difficult and often the last Hazard preventing raft progress. [S4]
* **Upper playfield:** generally only one good upper-flipper attempt; missing Insanity Falls wastes a premium scoring opportunity.
* **No MB ball save:** a bad opening volley can end White Water Multiball almost immediately. [S4]

## Trivia

* White Water was a **Dennis Nordman** design with John Youssi's distinctive caricature art and Mike Boon's software. [S1]
* Its upper playfield uses a separately controlled **upper-right flipper**; the second stage of the right flipper button activates it. [S3]
* The machine's Bigfoot physically turns to divert balls into the Whirlpool; during **Bigfoot Hotfoot** he instead allows repeated cave shots. [S3]
* The famous **Vacation Jackpot** begins at 200M and grows by 10K for every game in which it is not collected. [S3]
* Williams produced **7,008** machines. [S1]

### Uncertainties and conflicts

* **5X + Multiball Jackpot behavior is ROM-sensitive.** Normal L-3-and-later-style rules make jackpots 5X during the timer instead of stacking Double/Triple into 10X/15X [S3][S4]. Some older ROMs permit stronger stacking. Verify ROM before making a categorical call.
* **Tournament settings have two related concepts:** the factory **Tournament Play** adjustment equalizes multiplayer multiball/jackpot state [S5], while the rules documentation's **Tournament Mode** fixes Mystery Canyon at 5M [S3]. Verify exactly what the event has enabled.
* **Physical lock stealing:** player lock qualification is remembered, but the three actual locked balls are shared hardware. Another player's multiball can empty them, requiring a player with previously qualified locks to shoot No Way Out again. [S4]
* **Lost Mine kickout and upper-playfield feeds** vary dramatically by machine and are strategically important. [S4]
