## Identity and versions

* **Stars** — Stern Electronics, **March 1978**, model **103**, Stern **M-100** solid-state platform. Design by **Steve Kirk**, art by **Jorge Obregon**. Production: **5,127**. [S3][S4]
* Two flippers, two 3-bank drop-target banks, five colored Star standups, two spinners, one pop bumper, and a small center post. **No multiball.** [S1][S4]
* The game can be configured for **2- or 4-player operation**; some markets received a dedicated two-player backglass. This does not change the core rules. [S1][S4]
* Factory ROM pairs **P3/P4 and P3A/P4A** are documented, but I found no reliable gameplay-rule difference between them. [S5]
* Scope follows the supplied commentator-reference specification.

## Thirty-second game plan

1. **Check the Special setting first.** If Special is worth **100K and open-ended**, hit all five colored Star targets, then repeatedly shoot whichever Star is lit red for Special. That overwhelms every other scoring route. **Observed strategy.** [S1][S2]
2. If only one 100K Special is allowed, collect it, then favor the **right spinner**, now worth **1K/spin** after all five Stars. [S1][S2]
3. If Specials have no tournament value—or the Star standups are unreliable—attack the two **drop banks** for **2× then 3× bonus** and improved left-spinner scoring. [S1][S2]
4. Base bonus reaches **19K**; with 3×, drain bonus reaches **57K**. [S1][S2]
5. Central decision: **Stars/right spinner versus drop banks/bonus**. The correct tournament strategy changes dramatically with Special rules and spinner strength. [S2]

## Core rules and persistence

* Bonus starts at **1K** and maxes at **19K**. Star targets and lit bonus rollovers advance it; lit return lanes/outlanes give three advances. Bonus is collected at drain. [S1]
* The two 3-banks are the Bonus-X ladder. First qualifying bank completion awards **Double Bonus**. The next qualification awards **Triple Bonus**; whether that requires either bank or both banks is operator-adjustable. [S1]
* Double/Triple Bonus are **ball-local**: the manual explicitly says their lamps go out when the ball reaches the outhole and bonus is collected. [S1]
* A later both-bank completion can award **WOW = Extra Ball**; another both-bank completion awards Special, with Specials available on subsequent completions. WOW can be disabled. [S1]
* Each drop target scores 500 and advances bonus. If only the **center target** of a bank remains standing, it is worth **7K**, a meaningful incidental cash-out. [S1]
* The **right spinner** starts at 200/spin and rises by 200 for each Star collected; all five Stars make it **1K/spin**. Every ball begins with one random Star already spotted. [S1]
* The **left spinner** increases with the center drop-target state; its increased-value lamps disappear when the two banks reset. [S1]
* Completing all five Stars **during one ball** lights a rotating Special on one Star target. Ordinary 500-point scoring moves which Star is lit. [S1]

## Skill shots

**Star Target Skill Shot** — on the plunge, hit one of the accessible upper Star standups that is **not already spotted**.

* One random Star is automatically spotted at the beginning of each ball; Bob Matthews specifically recommends plunging for the unlit **white or green** upper Star. [S1][S2]
* Award: another Star toward the right spinner's 1K value and, if relevant, the rotating Special.
* Visible cue: one Star lamp is already lit before the plunge.

No separate alternate, super, or secret skill shot found.

## Secondary features

* **WOW / Extra Ball:** deep drop-bank progression can award an Extra Ball. Operator switch #26 can disable WOW entirely. [S1]
* **Special:** operator-configurable as a free ball, replay, both, or **100,000 points**. [S1]
* **Star Special limit:** independently adjustable to **one Special per ball or open-ended**. This is the single most strategically important venue setting. [S1][S2]
* High-score thresholds can also award Extra Balls rather than replays, but these should normally be irrelevant on a tournament setup. [S1]
* No conventional ball save, kickback, player control, mystery feature, or video mode.

## What to watch

1. **All five Star lamps lit:** right spinner is now **1K/spin** and the rotating Star Special is qualified.
2. **One Star glowing red for Special:** if Specials score 100K, this is immediately the most valuable target on the machine.
3. **2× / 3× Bonus lamp:** tells whether the player is pursuing the drop-bank strategy and how much the 19K bonus is worth.
4. **Drop-bank state:** two outside targets down with the center standing means that center target is worth **7K**.

## Match strategy

**Playing ahead:** if a 100K Special is already collected or has no value, favor whichever spinner makes the cleanest full orbit. Avoid unnecessary standup/drop rebounds; protect an established 3× bonus.

**Playing behind:** with **open-ended 100K Specials**, finish all five Stars and chase the moving red target repeatedly—the highest-variance comeback route by far. Otherwise build 3× and exploit whichever spinner is strongest. [S2]

**Key recurring decision:** **Star targets or drop targets?** Stars build the safer high-value right spinner and may unlock gigantic Specials; drops build Bonus X and the left spinner. Machine condition and Special configuration decide which route is actually correct. [S2]

## Danger zones

1. **Right-spinner short shot:** Bob Matthews specifically warns that partial spinner shots falling back are dangerous. [S2]
2. **Right side of the exposed pop bumper:** balls dropping just to its right have a high drain percentage. [S2]
3. **Drop-target rebounds:** generally feed directly into sling action and can turn a good setup shot into an immediate drain. [S2]
4. **Star standups:** chasing a moving Special requires repeated direct target shots with similar sling/rebound danger.
5. **Center post configuration:** with rubber it can enable passive flipper-to-flipper transfers; bare or removed, the same feed behaves very differently. [S2][S4]

## Trivia

* *Stars* was **Steve Kirk's first of three designs for Stern Electronics**. [S4]
* Its artwork is by **Jorge Obregon**, who contributed art to several early Stern titles. [S4]
* Although fully solid-state, *Stars* has **no electronic sound board**; it still uses traditional chimes. [S4]
* Stern provided a switch allowing **two-player instead of four-player operation**, and special two-player backglasses are known to exist. [S1][S4]
* The little post between the flippers became informally known inside Stern as the **“Kirk Post,”** after Steve Kirk. [S4]

### Uncertainties and conflicts

* **Special configuration is decisive.** Stern permits 100K, replay, Extra Ball, or replay+Extra Ball [S1], and separately permits one-per-ball versus open-ended Star Specials. [S1][S2] The commentator page cannot name one dominant strategy until this is known.
* The factory manual says left-spinner value rises with **center drop targets down** and resets with the banks [S1]; Bob's shorthand says drop-bank work raises the left spinner [S2]. These are compatible strategically, but avoid saying that a completed bank itself permanently raises the spinner.
* Factory ROM pairs **P3/P4 and P3A/P4A** both exist [S5]. No authoritative rules difference was found; do not infer one.
* The 2-player configuration changes player capacity/backglass, not verified scoring rules.
* Spinner strength, Star-target sensitivity, and the Kirk Post dramatically affect route choice. [S2]
