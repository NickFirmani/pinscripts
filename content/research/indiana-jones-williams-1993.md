## Identity and versions

* **Indiana Jones: The Pinball Adventure** — Williams Electronics, **August 1993**, model 50017; widebody **WPC-DCS** “SuperPin.” Design by **Mark Ritchie and Doug Watson**, software by **Brian Eddy**, art by Watson, mechanics by **Jack Skalon**, music by **Chris Granner**, sound by **Rich Karstens**. Production: **12,716**. [S1][S4]
* Latest verified Williams production software: **L-7, November 22, 1993**. Earlier P-series code differs materially in multiball and Super Jackpot rules. [S2][S4]
* Commentary-relevant multiballs: **Idol Multiball** (3-ball), **Lost Treasure / Quick Multiball** (2-ball), **Well of Souls** (6-ball mode), and deep wizard **Eternal Life** (6-ball). [S1][S2]
* Tournament Play is a real operator adjustment: in multiplayer games it **equalizes multiball and Jackpot state instead of carrying it between players**. [S1]

## Thirty-second game plan

1. **Observed strategy:** the simplest tournament route is **Idol Multiball**. Complete the center **E-N-T drops**, shoot the center hole to lock a ball, and repeat for three locks. [S1][S2][S3]
2. In multiball, shoot the **left ramp to light Jackpot**, then the **right ramp to collect**. The first three base Jackpots are **20M / 30M / 40M**. [S2]
3. The huge leverage is the **center lock shot during the Jackpot timer**: park one ball in the Idol for **2× Jackpot**, two for **3×**. A 40M Grail Jackpot can therefore become 120M before any Raise Jackpot additions. [S2]
4. After all three Jackpots, shoot left ramp to light **Super Jackpot**, then hit the captive ball: bases are **80M / 90M / 100M** on production L-code. [S2]
5. Alternate strategy: modes and **Path of Adventure** can be excellent when those shots are safer. The Path's Pit can reach **150M**, Friends/Loop Jackpot **200M**, and completing all 12 modes reaches the **1B Eternal Life** wizard payoff. [S2][S3]

## Core rules and persistence

* **Idol locks:** E-N-T completion normally lights Lock; center hole stores the ball in the rotating Idol. Three earned locks start Idol Multiball. The game also permits a difficult “**You cheat, Dr. Jones!**” center shot with the drops standing. [S1][S2]
* **Jackpot multiplier:** during Idol Multiball, one ball parked in the Idol makes the current Jackpot 2×; two makes it 3×. The hold lasts for an operator-adjustable timer—factory-medium documentation shows **15 seconds**. Collecting the Jackpot releases the held balls and resets the multiplier. [S1][S2]
* **Jackpot progress persists across multiballs** until the player has reached Super Jackpot territory; subsequent multiball behavior then resets to the Ark sequence. [S2]
* **I-N-D-Y:** flipper-controlled top lanes advance Bonus X **2× → 4× → 6× → 8×**, light Friends, and eventually Extra Ball. Bonus X normally resets next ball after the maximum/Extra Ball cycle. [S2]
* **Friends / Loop Jackpot:** light all five Friends, then score lit loops; right ramp collects the accumulated Jackpot, capped at **200M**. If lit but uncollected at drain, its built value/Friends state resets. [S2]
* **Path of Adventure:** complete A-D-V-E-N-T-U-R-E, then right ramp enters the tilting upper playfield. Lit paths score up to **10M each**; repeated completions ultimately light the Pit, which increases to **150M**. [S1][S3]
* **Lost Treasure:** captive-ball progression starts 2-ball play; treasure progress carries across attempts. After the five treasures, further captive-ball hits score **40M**. [S2][S3]
* Easy commentary trap: **Path of Adventure can remain active during Lost Treasure**, where its values are doubled. Starting other multiball while Path is lit can also create a difficult simultaneous control problem. [S2][S3]

## Skill shots

**None found.**

The detailed contemporary rulesheet explicitly states that Indiana Jones has **no normal skill shot**. The plunge enters the I-N-D-Y/pop area, but there is no separately defined plunge award. [S2]

## Secondary features

* **Eternal Life ball save:** exists in single-ball play, Idol Multiball and Lost Treasure, with separate operator-adjustable timers from **Off to 60 seconds**. Do not assume a venue game's duration. [S1]
* The game also has optional **~2-second outlane saves** after an Idol release on the right and after hitting the center drop bank on the left. [S1]
* **Hand of Fate:** an inlane lights the mode saucer for a random award. Possible awards include points, Extra Ball, Eternal Life, Quick Multiball, Bonus X, Dogfight, a Friend, an Idol lock, Path of Adventure, and Super Jets. [S1][S2]
* **Video modes:** Raven Bar, Escape in the Mine Cart and Choose Wisely use the flipper buttons; they are part of the 12-mode progression toward Eternal Life. [S1][S2]
* **Extra Balls:** available from I-N-D-Y, Path of Adventure, certain modes and Hand of Fate. Maximum Extra Balls, lit-state persistence and novelty/point substitution are operator-adjustable. [S1]
* Flipper buttons steer both the **Path of Adventure playfield** and video modes. The gun handle/trigger also participates in some mode/buy-in interactions. [S1]

## What to watch

1. **Balls visible inside the rotating Idol:** in single-ball play these are locks; during Jackpot scoring, one/two held balls mean the current Jackpot is **2×/3×**.
2. **Left-ramp “Lite Jackpot” → right-ramp “Jackpot” lamps:** instantly explains the player's two-shot multiball sequence. [S1]
3. **Path of Adventure flashing on the right ramp:** the player has completed ADVENTURE and may abandon normal scoring to play the tilting mini-playfield.
4. **Mode inserts:** twelve completed adventures mean the mode saucer is about to start **Eternal Life**, a six-ball wizard mode with a possible **1B Mega Jackpot**. [S2][S3]

## Match strategy

**Playing ahead:** take straightforward Idol Multiball, prioritize clean single-multiplied Jackpots, and avoid unnecessarily parking two balls in the Idol if doing so leaves only one uncontrolled ball alive. A ready mode or safe Path may be preferable to constructing a perfect 3× Jackpot. **Strategic inference.**

**Playing behind:** pursue **3× Idol Jackpots**, then Super Jackpots. A prepared Friends Jackpot or high-value Path/Pit is also capable of swinging the match; deep players can chase Eternal Life, but that is a survival-dependent long game. [S2][S3]

**Key recurring decision:** **cash the Jackpot now, or park another ball to raise it to 2×/3×?** Every extra multiplier dramatically increases value but temporarily removes a controlled ball from multiball and concentrates the game on the remaining ball(s).

## Danger zones

1. **Center E-N-T drops:** direct target shots can produce violent rebounds before the lock hole is exposed.
2. **Left/right ramp rejects:** both ramps are steep; short shots return quickly toward the lower playfield. [S2]
3. **Right outlane:** unusually wide on some setups, although the one-way Idol-return gate and “Narrow Escape” post can occasionally save it. [S2]
4. **Captive-ball target:** hard hits can produce unpredictable rebounds; historical rules documentation even notes occasional airballs over nearby targets. [S2]
5. **Path of Adventure during multiball:** attention must shift to steering the tilting upper field while other balls remain live below—a major control trap. [S2][S3]

## Trivia

* Indiana Jones was the **first Williams pinball machine to use the DCS digital sound system**. [S4]
* It was one of Williams' **SuperPin widebodies**, alongside *The Twilight Zone* and *Star Trek: The Next Generation*. [S4]
* **John Rhys-Davies reprised Sallah** and recorded new dialogue specifically for the machine. [S4]
* The rotating Golden Idol physically stores and releases the three balls used for the main multiball. [S1][S4]
* The Path of Adventure is a mechanically **tilting mini-playfield controlled with the flipper buttons**, rather than a conventional upper flipper playfield. [S1]

### Uncertainties and conflicts

* **Production L-code versus prototype P-code:** Super Jackpot values and multiball drop-target behavior differ. The standard L-code values used here are **80M / 90M / 100M**; older prototype documentation may show **100M / 125M / 150M**. [S2]
* **Jackpot multiplier timer is adjustable** from 5–120 seconds; factory/difficulty presets vary substantially. [S1]
* **Path Extra Ball/Pit persistence is adjustable:** Easy can hold it for the game; Hard can make it ball-local. [S1]
* **Hand of Fate remains random.** Tournament Play's documented function is to equalize multiplayer multiball/Jackpot state; I found no authoritative evidence that it makes Hand of Fate deterministic. [S1]
* Indiana Jones contains numerous documented L-code quirks, particularly around Path of Adventure and deep mode interactions. For a one-page tournament reference, avoid promising edge-case behavior not visible on the specific machine. [S2]
