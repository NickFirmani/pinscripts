## Identity and versions

**Rick and Morty — Blood Sucker Edition**, Spooky Pinball, **2020**. Solid-state P3-ROC/LCD game designed by **Scott Danesi**, with rules input/software work including **Bowen Kerins and Eric Priepke**; artwork by **Josh Freydkis** and music by Danesi. [S1][S5] ([Spooky Pinball][1])

The **Blood Sucker Edition (BSE) is not a rules/mechanism upgrade**: Spooky explicitly said Standard and BSE games have the same playfield features. BSE adds collector cosmetics such as special armor/powdercoat, plaque/art treatment and related trim. The total production run was about **750 games**. [S5] ([Pinside][2])

Latest public code actually verified: **Build 2023.03.22**. Spooky's current support page still displays 2022.09.02 as its download, but the code-history page linked by Spooky identifies 2023.03.22 as the latest public release. [S1][S2] ([Spooky Pinball][3])

Major multiballs: **Gromflomite Battle Multiball** and **Meeseeks Mania**; they can overlap, and Adventures can run during multiball. [S3]

## Thirty-second game plan

1. **Think beyond Adventures.** Adventures are the obvious progression, but rules designer Bowen Kerins says expert/high-score play is really about **Dimensions and Megaseeds**. Each seed adds **5% of that ball's score to end-of-ball bonus**, and collected seeds persist for the entire game. [S1][S4] ([Spooky Pinball][1])
2. Hit ramps to qualify an **Adventure**, while hitting the **Flooble Crank** to light locks and keep the right-outlane Anti-Gravity save charged. [S1][S3]
3. Charge the Portal Gun—especially via the upper spinner—then enter the **Garage** to change dimensions and expose Megaseeds. Garage entry lights five seed shots versus three through the easier right-orbit route. [S1]
4. Spawn **Meeseeks immediately before valuable shooting**: one/two/three active blue Meeseeks make their lit shots **2X/3X/4X**. Don't wait too long; they decay to purple **divisors**. [S1][S3]
5. Prepare an Adventure + **Gromflomite Battle MB** stack; behind, add Meeseeks Mania and/or a favorable positive Dimension for maximum variance. [S3]

## Core rules and persistence

**Adventures:** first Adventure normally needs one ramp; later Adventures require both. Start at the center scoop except **Pickle Rick**, which starts at the Garage. Every played Adventure stamps the Morty Adventure Card; wins are **green stamps**. After nine stamps, the tenth start gives **Morty's Choice**; unfinished Adventures receive scoring multiplied according to green-stamp progress. [S1][S3] ([Spooky Pinball][1])

**Dimensions/Megaseeds:** charge the Portal Gun with spinner/regular-shot activity, then enter the Garage/right portal route. Dimensions can positively or negatively alter scoring/mechanics. Each collected Megaseed permanently adds **5% of current-ball scoring to end-of-ball bonus on every later ball**. Tilting therefore destroys potentially enormous accumulated bonus. [S1][S4] ([Spooky Pinball][1])

**Meeseeks:** up to three blue Meeseeks can coexist, creating **2X → 3X → 4X** on their lit shots. Each has its own timer; expired Meeseeks become purple and divide scoring instead. Enough collected Meeseeks automatically starts **Meeseeks Mania**. [S1][S3]

**Gromflomite Battle:** Flooble Crank lights one physical lock; after locking it, another Crank lights the second. Two locks light multiball at the scoop. Any jackpot lights a Garage Super Jackpot; later Supers require the difficult left-Garage path. During MB, three Crank hits earn the first Add-A-Ball plus 10 seconds of save. [S1][S3]

Locks are physically shared but player progress is remembered; an opponent can **steal locked balls**, with extra Crank hits required as compensation. Easy commentary mistake: seeing balls physically locked does not mean the next player automatically owns those locks. [S1]

## Skill shots

**Normal Skill Shot — Noob Noob**

* Auto-plunge feeds the upper flipper; hit **Noob Noob**.
* Starts at **200K** and increases after successful skill shots.
* Safer/easier choice. [S1]

**Super Skill Shot — Garage**

* From the same upper-flipper feed, shoot the difficult **left Garage entrance**.
* Starts at **400K**, also increasing on later successes.
* Higher-value but substantially harder. [S1]

**Side-loop extension**

* Loop the upper-flipper side loop before collecting the Skill Shot to **multiply its value**; repeated loops can increase it further.
* High-risk tournament choice because every extra upper-flipper exchange risks losing the opportunity. [S1]

## Secondary features

**Anti-Gravity save:** Flooble Crank charges a battery powering a player-operated magnet at the **right outlane**. Use the extra right-side button to pull the ball back; careful pulsing consumes little battery, while holding it continuously is easier but expensive. [S1] 

**SLAM Save:** complete S-L-A-M at the in/outlanes. A subsequent drain caused immediately by the unusual left **Slam bumper** can be fired back into play; the detection window is short and the save is disabled during multiball. [S1]

**Mystery:** complete Beth/Summer/Jerry then shoot the scoop. Useful awards include Light Lock, Charge Portal Gun, Anti-Gravity charge, Meeseeks and Megaseeds. Tournament Rules alters variable gameplay elements. [S1]

**Extra Ball:** default qualification includes **three Dimension changes**, collected at the spinner loop; both qualification count and EB limits are adjustable. No verified built-in tournament points conversion was found. [S1]

## What to watch

* **Blue vs. purple Meeseeks arrows:** blue = multiplied shot; purple = that shot is now being divided. Three blue Meeseeks means **4X**. [S1]
* **Portal Gun meter full / Garage flashing:** player is about to leave C-137 and hunt Megaseeds.
* **Orange seed arrows:** collect now—each one permanently increases future end-of-ball bonus. [S1]
* **Two balls physically in the Danesi Lock + green scoop:** Gromflomite Battle is ready; if Adventure is also lit, expect the player to combine them. [S1]

## Match strategy

**Playing ahead:** keep Anti-Gravity charged, take available seed collections, and avoid letting blue Meeseeks expire while over-preparing. A straightforward Adventure + Gromflomite stack provides scoring plus combined ball-save protection. **Strategic inference.**

**Playing behind:** lean into the systems Bowen identifies as expert scoring: repeatedly change Dimensions, accumulate Megaseeds, spawn three Meeseeks, and attack a **4X high-value Adventure/jackpot**. Gromflomite and Meeseeks Mania can coexist, creating a very high-variance multiball window. [S3][S4] ([Kineticist][4])

**Key recurring decision:** **take the feature now or add another modifier?** Rick and Morty rewards layering Adventure + Dimension + Meeseeks + multiball, but the game is brutally short; one additional setup shot may mean never reaching the stack.

## Danger zones

* **Portal kickout:** explicitly documented to fire the ball **fast toward the left flipper**; be ready before Garage ejects. [S1]
* **Slam bumper:** replaces the left slingshot and intentionally sends the ball wildly out of control toward quick drains. [S1]
* **Left Garage entrance:** very late upper-flipper shot; misses can end near the **left outlane**. [S1]
* **Right outlane:** survivable only if sufficient Anti-Gravity charge remains and the player executes the magnetic rescue.
* **Steep left ramp:** short/rejected shots surrender control into the game's already chaotic lower playfield. [S1][S6]

## Trivia

* Rick and Morty was **Scott Danesi's second Spooky design** after *Total Nuclear Annihilation*. [S1][S4]
* Rules designer **Bowen Kerins** deliberately structured the game in layers: Adventures for baseline players, Meeseeks for intermediate play, and Megaseeds/Dimensions as the expert scoring system. [S4] ([Kineticist][4])
* The **Danesi Lock 2.0** combines a horseshoe/U-turn geometry with two physical ball locks and multiplayer lock-stealing logic. [S1]
* The left sling is replaced by an actual **pop bumper**, the “Slam bumper,” helping give the game its famously chaotic lower playfield. [S1]
* Blood Sucker Edition was explicitly sold as a **collector/cosmetic upgrade**, not a mechanically superior rules edition. [S5] ([Pinside][2])

### Uncertainties and conflicts

* **Latest code:** Spooky's current support page still labels **2022.09.02** as the downloadable version [S1], while the public-code page it links to explicitly identifies **2023.03.22 as Latest Public Release** [S2]. Use 2023.03.22 unless the venue reports otherwise. ([Spooky Pinball][3])
* **Production split:** reliable sources agree on roughly **750 total games**, but archives disagree on the BSE count—PinWiki reports **714 BSE + 36 Standard**, while Pinside reports 751 BSE. Avoid printing an exact BSE production number. [S5]
* **Tournament Rules:** the factory manual only says it “alters variable gameplay elements.” Build 2022.08.18 added **Tournament Shuffle**, which randomizes Adventure/Dimension ordering per game, but no concise authoritative final list of every tournament alteration was found. [S1][S2]
* **Skill-shot loop multiplier:** factory documentation confirms looping before collection multiplies the Skill Shot and can be repeated, but does not state the exact multiplier formula. [S1]
* Physical difficulty is extremely machine-sensitive; contemporary location reports specifically emphasize Garage consistency, steep-ramp behavior and chaotic pop-bumper feeds. [S6]
