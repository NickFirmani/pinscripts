## Identity and versions

**FunHouse** — Williams Electronic Games, **1990**, model 50003; early **WPC alphanumeric** solid-state game. Design: **Pat Lawlor and Larry DeMar**; software: DeMar/Brian Eddy; art: **John Youssi**; mechanics: John Krutsch; music: Chris Granner; Rudy voiced by **Ed Boon**. Approximately **10,750** produced. [S1][S5]

Latest factory game ROM verified: **L-9, December 11, 1992**. L-9 adds Tournament-mode behavior, Smart Trap Door, and an adjustable **Million Plus Carryover** rule. [S2]

Major multiballs: **Midnight Multiball** (3-ball, primary scoring feature) and **Quick Multiball** (2-ball Mystery Mirror award). [S3]

## Thirty-second game plan

1. **Advance the clock to 11:30.** The safe tournament route is repeated **center ramps**, which advance 15→20→25→30 minutes per ball and return controllably toward the left flipper. [S3][S4]
2. At **11:30**, shoot the **Hidden Hallway** twice: first lock moves to 11:45; second reaches midnight and puts Rudy to sleep. [S1][S3]
3. Shoot **Rudy's open mouth** to start Midnight Multiball. The Trapdoor immediately lights for **2M**. [S1][S3]
4. During multiball alternate **Trapdoor → center ramp → Trapdoor**: Million Plus rises 2M → 3M → 4M … up to **10M per shot**. This is the game's match-breaking scoring engine. [S3]
5. **Alternate strategy:** when Extra Balls count, work Mystery Mirror/Gangways for EBs; when behind, take riskier Trapdoor loops for 500K–1M while advancing the clock faster than pure safe ramp play. [S3][S4]

## Core rules and persistence

**Clock:** factory-medium play starts at **8:00** and clock progress carries between balls. Reaching 11:30 lights the lock; ordinary play cannot advance farther without Hidden Hallway locks. After completed Midnight Multiball, the clock normally resets to **5:00**. Difficulty settings materially change these times: 9/10 are easier; 7/6 harder. [S3]

**Midnight Multiball:** first Hidden Hallway lock → 11:45; second → midnight; Rudy sleeps; shoot his mouth for 1M and start 3-ball play. Trapdoor begins at **2M**, then closes after each collect. Center ramp reopens it for the next value, increasing 1M at a time to 10M. [S1][S3]

If multiball ends **without collecting any Million Plus**, the Hidden Hallway briefly relights for a last-chance re-lock and 2-ball restart. If at least one Million Plus was made, normal play resumes. [S3]

**L-9 adjustment:** `MIL. + CARRYOVER = YES` makes a later Midnight Multiball resume the Million Plus value from the previous one instead of restarting the ladder. This can radically change late-game scoring. [S2]

**Tournament Mode:** L-9 makes Mystery Mirror begin on **Quick Multiball**. Tournament lock handling also prevents physical lock stealing: after draining with multiball/lock progress, the clock can reset to **11:30**, forcing the player to earn the lock shots again. [S2][S6]

**Rudy bonus:** jaw hits build end-of-ball bonus at **50K × Rudy hits**; the accumulated hit count carries between balls. [S3]

## Skill shots

**Rudy's Hideout Skill Shot**

* **Plunge:** use less than full power so the ball travels around the top loop, stalls, and rolls backward into the Hidden Hallway/Hideout kicker behind Rudy.
* **Award:** **250K**, then 500K, 750K, then **1M** on subsequent successes.
* **Why choose it:** meaningful guaranteed scoring plus normal plunge clock advancement.
* **Persistence:** value advances across plunges for the entire game.
* **Cue:** display calls the Hideout award after the rollback. [S3]

No separate verified super/alternate/secret skill shot was found.

## Secondary features

**Steps left-outlane rescue:** when **Steps Gate Open** is active, either the center ramp **or a left-outlane drain** sends the ball to the secondary left plunger instead of ending the ball. The Steps plunger can collect Light Frenzy, 500K, Light Extra Ball, or Superdog depending on lit progress. [S3]

**Plunge compensation:** FunHouse has the unusually generous “**You Can Have It Back**” behavior; a plunge that immediately drains can be returned, sometimes even after weak plunges touch upper switches. [S3]

**Extra Balls:** always collected at the **Hidden Hallway**. They can be lit from Mystery Mirror, Gangway progression, or Steps. No universal tournament point conversion is built into the documented rules; event configuration therefore matters. [S3]

**Mystery Mirror:** six awards include Extra Ball, Million, max jets, Superdog, Steps Gate Open and Quick Multiball. Jets change which award flashes. On L-9 Tournament Mode the game merely **starts** with Quick Multiball selected; it does not make the entire Mirror progression fixed. [S2][S3]

## What to watch

* **Clock at 11:30 + LOCK flashing:** ordinary clock-building is finished; Hidden Hallway is now the only meaningful progression shot.
* **Rudy asleep / mouth hanging open:** midnight is reached; the next mouth shot starts Midnight Multiball.
* **Trapdoor flashing Million Plus:** the biggest scoring shot is available; after collection, expect an immediate center-ramp attempt to relight it.
* **Mirror award flashing:** explains why a player may detour to the Wind Tunnel—especially for Quick Multiball or Extra Ball. [S3]

## Match strategy

**Playing ahead:** **observed strategy** favors the center ramp: repeat it under control until 11:30, then concentrate on Hidden Hallway locks. In Midnight Multiball, take the available 2M Million Plus rather than wasting the opportunity trying to establish elaborate side features. [S4]

**Playing behind:** use the more volatile scoring routes—Trapdoor loops, repeated Million Plus laddering, and potentially Super Frenzy. If Extra Balls are live, Mirror and Gangway qualification can justify significant additional risk. [S3][S4]

**Key recurring decision:** **safe clock progress via center ramp, or faster/high-value Trapdoor and side-feature play?** The ramp preserves control; the Trapdoor route adds meaningful immediate points but its failed returns are much more dangerous.

## Danger zones

* **Weak Trapdoor-loop reject:** documented classic FunHouse drain—can roll toward the left outlane or hit the left sling first. [S4]
* **Slingshots:** uncontrolled sling action quickly opens both outlanes; avoid loose feeds when a safe ramp is available. [S4]
* **Rudy jaw reject:** direct center/upper-flipper shots can return sharply toward the lower playfield.
* **Steps targets:** competitive guidance discourages deliberately bashing them because direct target play sacrifices control. [S4]
* **Wind Tunnel eject:** often catchable, but setup-specific coil strength can turn it from a controlled feed into sling/outlane danger.

## Trivia

* FunHouse was one of the first games built on Williams' **WPC platform** and still used alphanumeric displays rather than a DMD. [S1][S5]
* **Ed Boon**, later co-creator of *Mortal Kombat*, voices Rudy. [S5]
* Rudy's moving eyes, eyelids and jaw let him physically watch and react to the ball, making him one of pinball's defining interactive toys. [S5]
* The game has **two manual plungers**: the normal shooter and the left-side Steps shooter. [S1]
* L-9 arrived more than two years after release and included meaningful tournament and Trapdoor behavior fixes rather than merely bug cleanup. [S2]

### Uncertainties and conflicts

* **Normal Trapdoor bonus values:** the original November 1990 Williams manual describes a **250K → 500K → 750K** sequence [S1], while Mark Phaedrus's 1991 corrected rulesheet and modern L-era documentation describe **500K → 750K → 1M** [S3]. The latter is appropriate for later production/L-9 behavior; verify an unusually early ROM before quoting these values.
* **Tournament lock persistence:** later competitive documentation consistently says Tournament Mode resets the player's clock to 11:30 after a drain with lock progress, requiring re-locks [S6]. The L-9 change list confirms tournament-specific changes but does not itself enumerate this lock behavior.
* **Smart Trap Door:** L-9 adds an operator adjustment that changes when the Trapdoor opens after the loop and specifically reduces rollback problems. A venue setting can therefore materially alter one of the game's signature danger feeds. [S2]
* No evidence of a factory ROM later than **L-9 (December 11, 1992)** was found.
