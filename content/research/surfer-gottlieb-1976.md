## Identity and versions

**Surfer** — D. Gottlieb & Co., **October 1976**, two-player electromechanical game, IPDB #2465. Design **Ed Krynski**; artwork **Gordon Morison**. It has two flippers, two pop bumpers, five drop targets, five central star rollovers, a center spinner and one upper-right eject hole. About **2,700** were produced. [S1][S2]

Surfer is the **two-player counterpart to four-player Surf Champ**; the competitive rules/layout are substantially the same. No multiball or software revisions. [S1][S3]

## Thirty-second game plan

1. **Build the eject hole.** Roll across the five central star rollovers; each raises the upper-right hole from **1K to 6K**. [S3]
2. Once the hole is valuable, **shoot the saucer repeatedly from the left flipper**; tournament guidance calls this the primary scoring route. [S3]
3. Completing all five stars lights the two side lanes; make one to turn on **Double Bonus**. On the final ball, Double Bonus is already lit, so the same side-lane collect reaches **Triple Bonus**. [S4]
4. Build bonus toward **15K**, making a final-ball 3X bonus worth as much as **45K**—huge relative to normal scoring. [S3]
5. Alternate route: finish **all five stars plus all five drops** for 5K, a drop-bank reset, and lit outlane awards; useful, but usually riskier than a dialed saucer strategy. [S5]

## Core rules and persistence

**Star rollovers → saucer value:** the upper-right eject hole begins at **1K** and increases by 1K for each central star rollover collected, max **6K**. Completing all five stars also lights the two mid-playfield side lanes for Bonus-X advancement. [S3][S5]

**Bonus:** top lanes and the upper-left advance lane build bonus to **15K**. Bonus and most feature state reset after each ball. [S3][S5]

**Double/Triple Bonus:** after all five stars, either lit side lane activates Double Bonus. The **last ball starts with Double Bonus already active**; hitting a lit side lane then advances to Triple Bonus. Factory-card wording confirming this behavior survives in collector/technical documentation. [S4]

**Drops + stars:** completing both the five-bank drops and all five star rollovers scores **5K**, resets the drop bank and lights the outlane features: left for Extra Ball, right for Special. [S5]

**3-ball vs. 5-ball matters:** pop bumpers are **1K in 3-ball but only 100 in 5-ball**. Experienced owners consequently describe 3-ball as much more pop-oriented, while 5-ball emphasizes bonus. [S5][S6]

## Skill shots

No manufacturer-defined scored Skill Shot was found.

**Observed tournament plunge:** if its return is safe, plunge/shoot the **left orbit**; otherwise aim for the currently useful top lane. Bob's Guide explicitly makes the choice dependent on the physical feed. [S3]

## Secondary features

**Extra Ball:** after completing all stars and all drops, the **left outlane** lights for an Extra Ball. The player must drain through that outlane to receive it, so tournament handling materially changes its value. [S5]

**Special:** the same completion lights the **right outlane** for Special/replay. No verified built-in tournament points conversion was found. [S5]

No ball save, kickback, mystery, player-controlled save or video feature exists.

## What to watch

* **How many central stars are lit:** directly indicates the eject-hole value; all five means **6K** and side lanes available. [S3]
* **Side lanes lit:** the next successful side-lane feed advances Bonus X.
* **Double/Triple Bonus lamps:** especially important on the final ball, where 3X can turn a full 15K bonus into 45K. [S4]
* **Drop bank reset / outlane lamps:** signals that the stars+drops completion has paid and EB/Special are now available. [S5]

## Match strategy

**Playing ahead:** once the saucer is at 5K–6K, stop forcing difficult star/drop geometry and repeatedly take the safer eject-hole shot if its kickout behaves. On the final ball, prioritize preserving a large **3X bonus** over chasing marginal features. **Strategic inference supported by observed tournament strategy.** [S3]

**Playing behind:** finish the five stars, aggressively take a side lane for 2X/3X bonus, and attack the 6K saucer. In 3-ball configuration, strong pop-bumper action is also much more attractive because each hit is worth 1K. [S3][S6]

**Key recurring decision:** **keep building features or start farming the saucer?** Good players generally bias the right flipper while building star/saucer value, then shift toward the left flipper once the eject hole becomes lucrative. **Observed strategy.** [S3]

## Danger zones

* **Second star from the right:** its continuation runs into the right pop; Bob's Guide specifically warns about the rebound. [S3]
* **Side-lane exits:** can return SDTM or clip the top of a sling mount and become uncontrolled. [S3]
* **Center spinner:** owner reports flag slight misses as capable of ugly center ricochets. [S6]
* **Drop-bank rebounds:** tight geometry behind the pop makes deliberate drop shots hazardous. [S3][S6]
* **Saucer kickout:** normally into the right-bumper area; whether it is truly safe is machine-specific. [S3]

## Trivia

* **Ed Krynski** designed Surfer; **Gordon Morison** supplied the surfing artwork. [S1][S2]
* Surfer is the **two-player version of Surf Champ**, not merely another surf-themed Gottlieb. [S1][S3]
* The two pop bumpers are reported as being **wired together**, so hitting one activates both mechanisms—a distinctive cost-saving/behavioral quirk. [S5]
* Gottlieb produced roughly **2,700 Surfers**. [S1]
* Its center spinner is positioned unusually low and centrally, deliberately complicating access to the five scoring stars and upper drop bank. [S6]

### Uncertainties and conflicts

* **Production count:** Pinside/IPDB-derived records report about **2,700** [S1], while Flipcommons currently lists **2,809**. Use “about 2,700” unless primary factory production records surface.
* **Exact release date:** sources agree on **October 1976** but differ between October 1 and October 18. Month/year is sufficient. [S1][S2]
* **3-ball star wiring:** recreation documentation states star rollovers **2 and 4 are tied together in 3-ball play** [S5], but I did not find a directly readable factory card/schematic image confirming that detail. Omit it from the final one-pager unless verified on the venue game.
* **Saucer kickout safety:** Bob's Guide treats it as generally manageable, but physical examples vary. Do not describe it as guaranteed safe. [S3]
