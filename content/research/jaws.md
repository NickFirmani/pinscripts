## Identity and versions

**Canonical identity.** JAWS, Stern Pinball, January 2024. Lead game design is Keith Elwin; mechanics/engineering Harrison Drake; software/rules Rick Naegele and Elizabeth Gieske; artwork Michael Barnard; sound Jerry Thompson. It is a Stern SPIKE 2 LCD game. ([Pinside][1])
  
  **Models.** Original production comprises Pro, Premium, and Limited Edition. The LE was limited to 1,000 machines. Premium/LE add the Orca upper playfield and associated wheel rules; Pro lacks those features. Stern added a cosmetically redesigned **JAWS 50th Anniversary Premium Edition** in July 2025; Stern does not state a production count in the sources reviewed. ([Stern Pinball][2])
  
  **Current software.** As of August 31, 2026, the latest public release located is **v1.02.0, July 1, 2026**. The major rules-completion milestone was v1.00.0 in July 2025, which added the final “Smile, You Son of a…” wizard mode. v1.01.0 added shared progress for Co-op/Team Play and fixes. v1.02.0 is mainly fixes and system upgrades, including Life Ring behavior and Search Multiball intro skipping rather than a wholesale scoring rewrite. ([Pinside][3])
  
  **Relevant multiballs.** The important families are the three sequential Jaws Multiballs; the individual Bounty Hunt multiballs followed by Celebration Multiball; Night Search; Search/Rescue Multiball; and deeper mini-/wizard multiballs such as Great White, Super Cast 'n Catch and Super Celebration. Premium/LE additionally have Say Ah! through the Orca wheel rules. ([Tilt Forums][4])
  
  ## Thirty-second game plan
  
  **Core plan.**
  
  1. **Chum the water:** hit the chum bucket, then chase the moving red arrows to fill the chum line.
  2. **Attach a barrel:** once the chum line is complete, make a lit yellow setup shot, then hit the moving shark fin.
  3. **Start Jaws Multiball:** make a lit return/feed shot after the required barrel or barrels are attached.
  4. **Build Shark Encounters in parallel:** bash the boat/captive-ball area to qualify an Encounter, start it at one of the five beach shots, then finish its **Encounter Jackpot at the fin**. A successful completion permanently raises base Machete by +1x.
  5. **Run a Bounty Hunt in the background:** start it via the right-ramp/reel progression, complete its objectives during single-ball play, then hit the fishing reel to start Bounty Hunt Multiball and capture the shark.
  6. **Cash Machete on something consequential:** a completed Encounter jackpot, Beach Closed hurry-up or other large award can suddenly become match-changing. ([Tilt Forums][4])
  
  **Tournament interpretation.** Do not assume the first Jaws Multiball itself is the jackpot strategy. A well-regarded strategy guide specifically treats it as a **utility multiball**: its own scoring is relatively modest, so use the protection to progress suitable Shark Encounters or other objectives. That guide identifies Encounter and bounty completion as major contributors to large scores. This is strategy judgment, not a programmed rule. ([Kineticist][5])
  
  **Central decision.** Keep investing in permanent/compound value—Machete, bounty perks, gear, later Jaws Multiballs—or stop building and take the score currently available before a dangerous fin/boat/target-bank shot ends the ball.
  
  ## Core rules and persistence
  
  **Jaws Multiball progression.** For Jaws Multiball 1, one chum-bucket hit starts a sequence of moving red arrows; their scored values contribute to the multiball jackpot base. Complete the line, qualify the fin, attach one barrel, then shoot a qualifying feed to start multiball. Jaws Multiballs 2 and 3 require progressively harder chum/barrel work and two then three attached barrels. ([Tilt Forums][4])
  
  **Shark Encounters.** These are the five principal timed modes. Enough boat/captive-ball hits qualify one; each starts at a different major beach shot. Completing the required shots lights the fin for an Encounter Jackpot. **Making that fin jackpot is important:** it completes the Encounter and permanently adds +1x to base Machete. Missing it can produce a lesser backup award without the permanent multiplier increase. ([Tilt Forums][4])
  
  **Machete.** Complete the left target bank when Light Machete is available to light a return lane. Rolling through that lane makes the next qualifying shot score at the current Machete multiplier. Base Machete starts at 2x. Permanent +1x increases come from completed Shark Encounters, first-attempt captures of non-Mako bounty sharks, and completing Night Search Multiball; other rules can provide temporary boosts. A lit Machete can be carried into multiball even though it generally cannot be newly lit there. ([Tilt Forums][4])
  
  **Bounty Hunts persist.** They are unusual because they are **untimed background objectives**, progress only during single-ball play, and their objective progress is maintained from ball to ball. Once all objectives are satisfied, the fishing reel starts the shark's Bounty Hunt Multiball. If a capture fails, the hunt can be revisited under reduced requirements but with reduced reward conditions. ([Tilt Forums][4])
  
  **Celebration value persists.** Capturing a bounty shark leads into Celebration Multiball; its jackpot value is maintained across the player's game, so early successful captures can improve later Celebration scoring. ([Tilt Forums][4])
  
  **Beach Closed.** Three rescued beachgoers at one major shot start its hurry-up. Collecting it adds a persistent scoring boost to later lit shots there, subject to Search/Rescue reset rules and the Hammerhead perk. The **fifth Beach Closed hurry-up rolls the base values of the previous four into its starting value**, has a ball saver, and leads into Search or Rescue Multiball. It cannot be stacked with an Encounter or another multiball. ([Tilt Forums][6])
  
  **Quickshots.** A lit harpoon-lane shot followed quickly by the moving fin scores a Quickshot. Consecutive successes build its multiplier up to 7x; missing resets that Quickshot multiplier. Every successful Quickshot lights Gear at the right ramp. ([Tilt Forums][4])
  
  **Per-player state.** In ordinary competitive multiplayer, do not treat one player's progress as another's. v1.01 explicitly introduced sharing of chum line, barrels, Jaws Multiballs, bounties, Encounters, gear and other progress **only for Co-op/Team Play teammates**. ([Pinside][3])
  
  ## What to watch
  
  **Moving red arrows / chum LEDs.** Red major-shot arrows mean the player is building the chum line toward Jaws Multiball. Once those are finished, yellow setup shots and then the moving fin indicate the barrel-conversion phase. ([Tilt Forums][4])
  
  **Machete return lane.** A lit Machete inlane is an immediate tactical tell. Watch the player lane-change it and ask what valuable shot is coming next—the next made shot can be multiplied, potentially at substantially more than 2x after completed Encounters. ([Tilt Forums][4])
  
  **Bounty status.** A player may appear to be shooting unrelated ramps, targets and spinners while actually satisfying a background bounty checklist. When those objectives are finished, the **fishing reel becomes the conversion shot into Bounty Hunt Multiball**. ([Tilt Forums][4])
  
  **Life Ring / action button.** When Life Ring is qualified, watch the left-outlane insert and player's action-button hand. The player must activate it around the time the ball threatens the left outlane; v1.02 specifically changed grace-period behavior on Easy difficulty. ([Tilt Forums][4])

  ## Match strategy
  
  **Playing ahead.** Prefer conversion over greed. Complete the relatively accessible progression already in front of you, use the first Jaws Multiball as protection for useful mode work, and avoid taking additional direct-target/fin shots solely to inflate a multiplier you do not need. A Mako bounty is a defensible early choice because its capture makes later bounty qualification easier; this is a strategy recommendation, not a mandatory optimal line. ([Kineticist][5])
  
  **Playing behind.** Raise variance through **Machete plus a real cash-out**, not simply random multiball. Complete an Encounter to increase base Machete, stack an appropriate Encounter with Jaws Multiball 1, chase a bounty capture/perk, or exploit a large Beach Closed/Feeding Frenzy award. Quickshots also permit a growing multiplier but punish a miss by resetting that sequence. ([Kineticist][5])
  
  **Most important recurring decision.** “Do I shoot dangerous setup now for a larger future multiplier, or cash what I have?” The target bank, center target area and fin build enormous leverage, but the point of building Machete is eventually to multiply a **large award**, not simply to collect 2x on an incidental shot.
  
  ## Danger zones
  
  * **Moving fin rejects.** The fin settles near the central captive-ball area and can send unsuccessful hits rapidly back toward the lower playfield. It is nevertheless a mandatory conversion shot for barrels and successful Encounter completion. ([Kineticist][5])
  * **Boat/captive-ball center.** Repeated direct hits are strategically necessary for Encounters and shark damage but expose the ball to uncontrolled central rebounds. Tournament commentary should distinguish “safe progression” from deliberate center-target risk. ([Pinside][7])
  * **Mini-flipper/right-inlane feed.** Strategy documentation notes that on some physical copies, balls passing the mini-flipper can find the right outlane rather than a clean inlane feed. Stern later added an adjustable ball saver for a bad right-inlane-gate exit, so this is especially machine-dependent. ([Kineticist][8])
  * **Left outlane.** A qualified Life Ring is not passive protection: the player normally must deploy it with the action button. Watch for the button press before calling the drain final. ([Tilt Forums][4])
  * **Spinner/setup sensitivity.** The fishing reel is strategically important enough that a weak or sticky reel materially changes how attractive Pond Attack and bounty progression are. Treat its behavior as a venue-specific strategic input rather than assuming all Jaws machines shoot alike. ([Tilt Forums][4])

  * “Those red arrows are the chum line—finish that, attach the barrel at the fin, and Jaws Multiball is close.”
  * “The important part of this Encounter is still ahead: hitting the fin jackpot permanently raises his Machete multiplier.”

  * “Watch the action button here—if Life Ring is ready, that left-outlane ball may not actually be dead yet.”
  
  ## Trivia
  
  * JAWS debuted at CES in January 2024 and was designed by Keith Elwin. ([Stern Pinball][9])
  * Michael Barnard created the game's hand-drawn cabinet/playfield artwork; the Pro translite and LE mirrored backglass also draw on Roger Kastel's iconic original Jaws poster art. ([Stern Pinball][2])
  * Stern incorporated footage and audio from **all four Jaws films**. ([Stern Pinball][2])
  * Richard Dreyfuss recorded custom speech for the game, and it uses John Williams's original Jaws theme music. ([Stern Pinball][9])
  * The original Limited Edition was capped at **1,000 machines worldwide**; Stern later released the separate 50th Anniversary Premium in 2025. ([Stern Pinball][2])
  
  ## Tournament and venue checks
  
  **Model first.** Determine Pro versus Premium/LE/50th Premium. Premium-family machines have the Orca upper playfield, wheel awards, additional shots and Say Ah! rules that simply do not exist on Pro. ([Stern Pinball][2])
  
  **Software version.** Record it. Current is v1.02.0 from July 1, 2026. The detailed community rulesheet is explicitly maintained against v1.01, although the published v1.02 change list does not show a rewrite of the core Encounter/Jaws MB/Bounty/Machete economy. ([Pinside][1])
  
  **Extra balls.** A Stern Competition Install disables extra balls. Under current documented rules, an otherwise-awarded extra ball becomes **15M points**, and that 15M compensation **can be multiplied by Machete**. Early code used 50M; v0.84 changed the compensation to 15M, making old launch-era references incorrect. ([Tilt Forums][10])
  
  **Competition Install versus arbitrary local setup.** The documented Competition Install includes two tilt warnings, no game restart/lost-ball recovery, no extra balls and other standard tournament adjustments. A director can still modify individual adjustments afterward, so “Competition Install” should not substitute for physically checking the machine. ([Tilt Forums][10])
  
  **Ball-save adjustments.** v1.01 introduced adjustable savers for a bad Quickshot-lane exit and bad right-inlane-gate behavior, defaulting to enabled. v1.02 also fixed Life Ring's Easy-difficulty grace period. Confirm these locally before commentary because they can make apparent drains unexpectedly return. ([Pinside][11])
  
  **Mechanical scouting.** Test the fin reject, central captive-ball rebound, mini-flipper feed and fishing-reel strength. These materially change which strategic lines are actually safe on a tournament copy. ([Kineticist][5])
  
  ## Uncertainties and conflicts
  
  **Rulesheet revision lag.** The strongest detailed public rules reference found, the Tilt Forums community rulesheet, states that it is based on **v1.01**, while Stern/Pinside show **v1.02.0** as current. The v1.02 notes reviewed contain Life Ring/Search Multiball fixes and system changes but no listed restructuring of Jaws Multiball, Shark Encounters, Bounty Hunts or Machete. Those core rules are therefore high-confidence but not independently documented in a v1.02-specific official rulesheet. ([Tilt Forums][6])
  
  **Strategy versus rules.** Claims such as “Mako first,” “Scars/Raft Attack are preferable Encounters,” and “Jaws Multiball 1 is utility multiball” are informed competitive strategy judgments from Noah Crable's guide, not programmed requirements. The guide states a v0.98 rules basis despite an April 2026 update date, so use it for tactical framing, not version-sensitive numeric rules. ([Kineticist][8])
  
  **Physical risk.** Reel strength, fin rejects and mini-flipper/right-outlane behavior vary by setup. Treat danger ratings as observations that must be confirmed during venue scouting rather than universal properties of every copy. ([Kineticist][8])
